# Pitch Deck Section Guide

Detailed guidance for each of the 12 sections. For each: what to include, what to avoid, and how to make it land.

---

## 1. Purpose & Vision

**Include:** One sentence. The change you're creating in the world. Not the product — the outcome.
**Avoid:** Mission statement committee language. Buzzwords. Anything longer than 15 words.
**Example:** "Every property manager makes data-driven decisions without hiring a data team."
**Common mistake:** Confusing vision (where the world is going) with mission (what you do day-to-day).

## 2. Target Audience

**Include:** Specific roles, company sizes, industries, geographies. The more specific, the more credible.
**Avoid:** "Everyone who..." or "All businesses that..." — if your TAM is everyone, you have no strategy.
**Example:** "Mid-market property management companies (50-500 units) in the US Southeast, where the GM makes technology decisions."
**Common mistake:** Describing the market instead of the buyer. Name the person who signs the check.

## 3. The Problem

**Include:** Three dimensions — financial cost, operational friction, personal frustration. Quantify at least one.
**Avoid:** Leading with your solution disguised as the problem ("The problem is they don't have our product").
**Example:** "$2.4M average annual waste from manual lease processing. 12 hours/week per manager on data entry. GMs promoted for gut instinct, punished when it's wrong."
**Common mistake:** Making the problem too abstract. The audience should wince.

## 4. The Solution

**Include:** Direct mapping from Problem → Solution. One-to-one. Show the product doing the thing.
**Avoid:** Feature lists. Technical architecture. "We leverage AI/ML to..." — say what it does for the human.
**Example:** "Automated lease processing → saves $1.8M/year. Dashboard replaces manual reports → recovers 10 hours/week. Predictive alerts → GM makes the right call before the board asks."
**Common mistake:** More solution slides than problem slides. If you need 5 slides to explain the solution, the product is too complicated or you haven't found the core value.

## 5. Commercial Model

**Include:** Pricing tiers (who pays what), unit economics (CAC, LTV, margins), revenue model (subscription, transaction, hybrid).
**Avoid:** Showing only the price — show the business model. Why this pricing works for this market.
**Example:** "Three tiers: Starter ($500/mo, <100 units), Growth ($1,500/mo, 100-500 units), Enterprise (custom). 85% gross margin. 14-month CAC payback."
**Common mistake:** No unit economics. Investors care about the machine, not just the price tag.

## 6. Market Analysis (TAM/SAM/SOM)

**Include:** Bottom-up math. Show your work. TAM from industry data, SAM from your actual reach, SOM from realistic capture rate.
**Avoid:** Top-down only ("The property management market is $X billion, we just need 1%..."). That's not analysis, it's hope.
**Example:** "TAM: 320K US property management companies × $18K avg annual software spend = $5.8B. SAM: 48K mid-market companies in target regions = $864M. SOM: 2% capture in 3 years = $17.3M ARR."
**Common mistake:** Making SOM too large. If your SOM is >5% of SAM with no existing traction, it's not credible.

## 7. Market Viability

**Include:** Evidence the market is real and growing. Competitor presence (validates demand). Regulatory or behavioral tailwinds.
**Avoid:** "No competition" — that means no market, not a blue ocean.
**Example:** "5 funded competitors in adjacent segments validates demand. New HUD reporting requirements (2027) create compliance urgency. 73% of GMs surveyed want to replace current tools."
**Common mistake:** Treating competition as a threat instead of validation.

## 8. Evidence of Success

**Include:** Traction metrics in order of strength: revenue → paying customers → pilots → LOIs → waitlist. Include logos if permitted. Growth rate matters more than absolute numbers early on.
**Avoid:** Vanity metrics (downloads, page views, social followers) unless they directly convert.
**Example:** "$380K ARR, 22 paying customers, 180% net revenue retention, 3 enterprise pilots closing Q2. NPS: 72."
**Common mistake:** Burying the strongest metric. Lead with the number that makes them lean forward.

## 9. Future Growth

**Include:** 3-year revenue projection with key assumptions stated. Margin trajectory. Inflection points (when you hit profitability, when growth accelerates).
**Avoid:** Hockey stick with no explanation. If the curve bends, say why.
**Example:** "Y1: $800K ARR (current trajectory). Y2: $3.2M (enterprise tier launch + Southeast expansion). Y3: $9.6M (national expansion + platform revenue). Gross margin expands from 78% to 88% as infrastructure amortizes."
**Common mistake:** Projections disconnected from the business model in Section 5.

## 10. Unique Advantage

**Include:** What you have that's defensible. Proprietary data, network effects, switching costs, regulatory moats, team expertise that can't be hired away.
**Avoid:** "First mover advantage" (rarely real), "better UX" (subjective), "our team" (unless truly unique).
**Example:** "Proprietary dataset of 2.1M lease transactions creates prediction accuracy competitors can't replicate without 3+ years of market presence. Integration with top 4 PMS systems creates switching cost."
**Common mistake:** Listing advantages that any well-funded competitor could replicate in 6 months.

## 11. Horizon Mapping

**Include:** Three distinct time horizons with specific initiatives.
- **H1 (0-12 months):** What you're executing now. Should be concrete and underway.
- **H2 (12-24 months):** Adjacent expansion. New segments, geographies, product lines.
- **H3 (24-48 months):** Transformative bets. Platform plays, new business models, category creation.

**Avoid:** Making H1 aspirational (it should be operational) or H3 too conservative (it should be ambitious).
**Common mistake:** All three horizons are just "more of the same but bigger."

## 12. The Team

**Include:** Key people with ONE line on why they're right for THIS problem. Advisors and board with relevant credentials. Key planned hires.
**Avoid:** Full bios. LinkedIn summaries. Headshots that don't look like the person.
**Example:** "CEO: 12 years in property management technology (built and sold PMS to Yardi). CTO: Former ML lead at Zillow (built Zestimate v3). Advisor: Former COO of Greystar (managed 500K units)."
**Common mistake:** Including everyone. Only show people who move the needle on credibility.
