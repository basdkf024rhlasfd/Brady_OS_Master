# Reward Doctrine

The reward system is strict by design. Recognition must be earned, not inflated.

## Medals (Money-Tied Only)

No medals are awarded unless there is an actual **monetary win** — revenue, profit, clearly realized financial upside.

Medals represent financial outcomes, not effort or aesthetics.

## Ribbons (Non-Monetary Achievement)

Ribbons mark successes of all sizes that may or may not be tied to money. They come in tiers:

| Tier | Scope | Rarity |
|------|-------|--------|
| **The Colossus** | Lifetime-defining achievement | A few times in a lifetime. Mentioned sparingly, if at all. |
| **Yearly Ribbons** | Annual performance and impact | Targets for year-level wins |
| **Project Ribbons** | Project-scale or quarter-scale wins | Targets for project-level wins |

## Stickers (Everyday Recognition)

If an outcome doesn't rise to quarterly-level recognition, it can still earn a sticker.

Stickers are quota-based:
- Entry-level: ~1 sticker/month
- Higher-level: higher budget
- Commissioner quarterly budget: ~30% of organization size

This makes stickers meaningful but still somewhat scarce and unexpected.

## Why This Matters

The constraint prevents recognition inflation. When everything is celebrated, nothing is. The three tiers create clear thresholds:
- **Did money move?** → Medal
- **Was something meaningful achieved?** → Ribbon (at appropriate tier)
- **Was something worth noticing?** → Sticker (if budget allows)
