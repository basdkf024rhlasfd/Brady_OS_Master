# Kaizen + Gemba

**Source:** Toyota Production System; Masaaki Imai *Kaizen* (1986) and *Gemba Kaizen* (1997)
**Core principle:** Continuous small improvements at the place where work actually
happens beat occasional big initiatives from headquarters. Go see for yourself.

## When to apply

- Improvement initiatives come from HQ and fail at the front line
- Frontline workers have ideas that never surface
- "Process improvement" is a once-a-year consulting project
- Managers can't describe what actually happens at a store/plant/site
- Small problems are ignored until they become big ones

## The framework

**Kaizen (改善) — change for the better:**

- Small, incremental improvements made by the people doing the work
- Many small improvements compound; one big initiative rarely sticks
- Everyone contributes every day — not just "improvement teams"
- Cost of change is low; authority to change is pushed to the front line

**Kaizen cycle (PDCA):**

1. **Plan** — identify a specific waste or problem; hypothesize a fix
2. **Do** — try the fix on a small scale
3. **Check** — measure what happened
4. **Act** — standardize if it worked; try something else if it didn't

Repeat. Daily.

**Gemba (現場) — the actual place:**

- The "real place" where work is done — the factory floor, the kitchen, the sales
  counter, the patient room
- "Gemba walks" are structured observations by leaders
- Three rules of the gemba walk:
  1. Go see
  2. Ask why (5 Whys until you find the root cause)
  3. Show respect (talk WITH the workers, not AT them)

**Five S (5S) as the gemba foundation:**

- **Sort** (seiri) — remove unneeded items
- **Set in order** (seiton) — a place for everything
- **Shine** (seiso) — clean daily; cleaning is inspection
- **Standardize** (seiketsu) — make the above consistent
- **Sustain** (shitsuke) — make it a habit

5S isn't about neatness; it's about making abnormalities instantly visible.

**Kaizen events vs. daily kaizen:**

- **Daily kaizen:** Small improvements by operators during normal work; suggestion
  boards; weekly team huddles
- **Kaizen events (kaizen blitz):** 3-5 day focused improvement of a specific
  process, cross-functional team, measurable before/after

Both matter. Daily kaizen builds the culture; kaizen events drive step changes.

## Canonical example

Toyota's suggestion system: in peak years, ~2 suggestions per employee per month, ~99%
adopted. Aggregate impact: billions in cost savings, but more important — a
continuously improving operating system.

A Japanese Toyota plant will have tens of thousands of small improvements per year,
most invisible to visitors. A typical US plant might have 1-2 "continuous improvement"
projects running. The gap is cultural, not tooling.

## Applied to QSR / retail / ops

- **Gemba walk for ops leaders:** Every executive should spend N hours per week at a
  store/site/plant. Not as an inspection. As observation + questions.
- **Daily kaizen at the store level:** 15-minute shift huddle. What broke yesterday?
  What will we try today? What did we learn last week?
- **5S in the back of house:** Most QSR back-of-house kitchens fail 5S. Tools in the
  wrong place, prep stations different across stores, cleaning standards drift.
- **Suggestion system that actually works:** Crew submits ideas; response time <1 week;
  adopted ideas credited publicly. Without fast response + credit, suggestions stop.
- **Kaizen events for specific pain points:** 3-day event with the drive-thru team
  to cut cycle time. Measurable before/after. Spread learnings to nearby stores.

## Common failure modes

- Gemba walks that become inspections. Workers hide problems. Culture collapses.
- Kaizen events without follow-through. Gains evaporate within weeks.
- Only HQ-led improvements. Removes the frontline from ownership.
- 5S as an aesthetic exercise, not a problem-visibility tool.
- Leaders who "know their operations" from the office. They don't.
