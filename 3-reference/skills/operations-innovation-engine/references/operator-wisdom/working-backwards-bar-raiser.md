# Working Backwards + Bar Raiser

**Source:** Bryar & Carr, *Working Backwards* (2021). Amazon's internal practices.
**Core principle:** Design from the customer backward (output metric). Operate by
moving the few inputs that actually drive the output. Protect the hiring bar with a
trained outsider who has veto power.

## When to apply

- Teams chase output metrics (revenue, NPS, retention) without a lever to pull
- Hiring quality degrades as the company grows
- Activity metrics proliferate; few correlate with outcomes
- Decisions happen without a clear owner
- Ops leaders report lag metrics and have no actionable levers

## The framework

**Input metrics vs. output metrics:**

- **Output metrics** are outcomes — revenue, profit, NPS, share price. You can't
  directly move them.
- **Input metrics** are the controllable behaviors that drive outputs. Amazon's
  examples: product selection, price, availability, shipping speed. If you know
  what drives the output, you know what to measure daily.

**Steps to find the right input metrics:**

1. Pick the output metric that matters most
2. Brainstorm the inputs that drive it
3. Test each input: does moving it actually move the output? (Measure correlation;
   better, run a controlled test.)
4. Keep 3-5 inputs that actually move the output. Kill the rest.
5. Make inputs highly visible in daily operating rhythm.

**The Weekly Business Review (WBR):**

- Same day every week, same format, same attendees
- Metrics in order: customer experience → input → output
- Read the deck in silence; discuss exceptions, not everything
- The chart anomalies are the agenda

**The Bar Raiser:**

- A trained interviewer from *outside* the hiring team
- Present at every final-round loop
- Has **veto power** over any offer
- Job isn't to staff this team — it's to keep the hiring bar higher than the current
  team average
- Trained and certified (weeks of shadowing); the title is earned, not assigned

**Single-threaded leaders and "two-pizza teams":**

- A team small enough to be fed by two pizzas (5-8 people)
- Single-threaded means one person, one priority, one team — no matrix distractions
- The team owns its metrics end to end
- Anti-pattern: a person managing four initiatives at 25% each

**The six-page memo:**

- No PowerPoint, ever
- Narrative, tight prose, limited citations
- Distributes one source of truth for a decision
- Read in silence for 20-30 min at the start of the meeting
- Q&A focuses on specifics — no "how does this interact with X"

## Canonical example

Amazon's Retail business ran on four input metrics for years: in-stock rate, price
competitiveness, selection, and delivery speed. Executives could be awakened at 3am
and asked about any of them. When one slipped, the team responsible knew to act
before the output (revenue, NPS) moved.

The Bar Raiser program kept hiring quality up during Amazon's 10x headcount growth
(2001-2010). Every offer had an accountable outsider. Teams couldn't lower the bar
to close requisitions.

## Applied to QSR / retail / ops

- **Find your 3-5 input metrics.** For a QSR: speed of service, order accuracy,
  food temperature at delivery, cleanliness score, mystery shop score. These lead
  sales by weeks.
- **WBR cadence:** One meeting per week. Same format. Charts only. Exceptions drive
  the conversation — not the read-out.
- **Bar Raiser for GM hiring:** A District Manager from a different region sits on
  every GM final loop. Has veto. Keeps the quality of the management bench from
  sliding.
- **Single-threaded ownership:** Every ops initiative has one name. "We need a
  throughput team" is a bad answer; "We need one leader for throughput" is the right
  one.
- **Memos, not decks.** The Panda innovation workshop is a working example —
  narrative beats bullets for ops decisions.

## Common failure modes

- Measuring too many inputs. 3-5, not 30.
- Input metrics that don't actually move the output. Test before adopting.
- "Bar Raiser" title without training. The power comes from trust, and trust comes
  from demonstrated skill.
- Treating single-threaded leadership as "project lead." It's full P&L and career
  ownership, not a side task.
- Six-page memos becoming sloppy six-page decks with text. The writing discipline is
  the point.
