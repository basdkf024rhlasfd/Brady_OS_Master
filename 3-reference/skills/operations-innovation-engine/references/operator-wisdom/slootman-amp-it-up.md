# Slootman — Amp It Up

**Source:** Frank Slootman, *Amp It Up* (2022). CEO of Data Domain, ServiceNow, Snowflake
**Core principle:** Raise standards, narrow focus, pick up the pace, align priorities.
Do all four without adding resources. Intensity is an operating system.

## When to apply

- The company has drifted into comfortable mediocrity
- Priorities have multiplied; focus has blurred
- Pace of decisions has slowed even though markets are moving faster
- "Alignment" is assumed but not enforced
- Leadership tolerates underperformance because replacing people feels hard

## The framework

**Four levers (all four, at once):**

**1. Raise Standards**

- Stop celebrating average. Stop rationalizing it ("we're improving").
- Benchmark against the best, not against last quarter.
- Promote the top 20% visibly. Move out the bottom 10% quickly.
- Leaders model the standard. If you tolerate B work, you've set the standard at B.

**2. Narrow Focus**

- Most companies have too many priorities. "We're focused on 20 things" = zero focus.
- Kill projects. Kill products. Kill initiatives. The list should hurt.
- One or two top priorities per team per quarter. Defend them.
- "If we can't be #1 or #2 in this, we shouldn't do it" (GE-era framing, still useful).

**3. Pick Up the Pace**

- Decisions in hours or days, not weeks
- Ship on date. If you can't, re-scope — don't slip.
- Replace long meetings with short check-ins. Replace 40-page decks with 1-page memos.
- Set tight deadlines. Pace compounds: a 2x pace team against a 1x pace team widens
  the gap every cycle.

**4. Transform / Align**

- Incentives, comp, org design, and priorities must point the same direction
- Publicly re-commit the team to the new standards; name names
- If a leader can't get on the program, they go. Fast.
- Culture change requires some visible personnel change. Without it, the words
  aren't real.

**Slootman's principles (from the book):**

- "Mission is a better word than strategy"
- "Declare war on status quo"
- "Hire drivers, not passengers"
- "Replace consensus with conviction"
- "Stop tolerating"
- "Compress cycle time"

## Canonical example

Snowflake under Slootman (2019-): took over mid-hypergrowth, sharpened focus onto
cloud data warehouse, narrowed the value prop, accelerated hiring in the top 20%,
moved out the bottom. IPO'd in 2020 at the highest software IPO valuation ever.
Operating cadence — weekly revenue reviews, daily escalations — was faster and
sharper than typical enterprise software.

Data Domain (earlier): grew from startup to $1B+ revenue and acquisition by EMC,
beating IBM, HP, and Symantec in the dedupe market. Same playbook.

ServiceNow: took the company from IPO to ~$50B market cap; focus on platform, not
verticals, was a "narrow focus" decision that defined the next decade.

## Applied to QSR / retail / ops

- **Raise standards at the store level:** Top-quartile stores get resources,
  recognition, and promotions. Bottom-quartile stores get help — then, if no
  improvement, new GMs.
- **Narrow focus:** If a chain is "working on" 15 format initiatives, pick 2. The
  others wait.
- **Pace:** A pilot approval should take days, not a quarter. A format decision
  should be decided in one exec meeting, not three. Compress the cycle.
- **Transform / align:** GM incentives should reflect the new standards. If comp
  still rewards volume while the mission is margin, nothing changes.
- **Tolerance:** The underperforming GM who's been "working on it" for two years is
  a signal about the culture, not the individual.

## Common failure modes

- Raising standards without narrowing focus. Team drowns; morale collapses.
- Narrowing focus without raising pace. Better priorities moving at the same speed
  still loses.
- Picking up pace without aligning incentives. Team races toward the wrong goal.
- "Amp it up" as a slogan without a 90-day action plan. Change requires specific
  decisions with owners.
- Replacing people indiscriminately. Slootman is clear: the goal is standards, not
  turnover. Replace only to raise the average.
