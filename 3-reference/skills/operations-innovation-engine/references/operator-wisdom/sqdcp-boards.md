# SQDCP Boards — Shop-Floor Visual Management

**Source:** Toyota / lean tradition; widespread in manufacturing, healthcare, QSR ops
**Core principle:** Five numbers — Safety, Quality, Delivery, Cost, People — on one
board, reviewed daily with the team, catch problems in hours instead of weeks.

## When to apply

- Problems surface in monthly P&L reviews instead of at shift level
- Different shifts/sites report different metrics
- "Dashboards" exist but operators don't look at them daily
- Management reports lag the operating reality by weeks
- Incidents repeat because learning doesn't spread

## The framework

**The five dimensions (SQDCP):**

1. **S — Safety.** Incidents, near-misses, OSHA recordables. Always first (literally,
   in ordering). A safety issue always interrupts any other conversation.
2. **Q — Quality.** Defects, rework, customer complaints, mystery shop scores, health
   inspection results.
3. **D — Delivery.** On-time, in-full, cycle time, throughput, speed of service.
4. **C — Cost.** Labor cost %, food cost %, waste, utility consumption.
5. **P — People.** Attendance, turnover, training hours, engagement.

**The board:**

- **Physical** (preferred) or digital. Physical makes the data local and visceral.
- **One column per metric** — today, week to date, month to date, target.
- **Red/yellow/green** visual status for each metric.
- **Action items** listed under any red — who owns, by when.
- **Updated daily** by the team that owns the work, not by HQ.
- **Located where the work happens** — behind the counter, at the plant, at the
  nurse's station.

**Daily stand-up (huddle) around the board:**

- Same time every day, 10-15 minutes, standing
- Each metric: green → move on; yellow → watch; red → owner commits to countermeasure
- No problem-solving during the stand-up — just surfacing and assigning
- Problem-solving happens afterward, with the owner + relevant people, offline

**Tiered reviews:**

- **Tier 1:** Shift team, daily, at their board
- **Tier 2:** Store/site lead + team leads, daily, rolling up Tier 1s
- **Tier 3:** District/region, weekly, rolling up Tier 2s
- **Tier 4:** Corporate ops review, monthly, rolling up Tier 3s

Information flows up fast; problems get escalated with context and countermeasures
already in motion.

## Canonical example

Alcoa under Paul O'Neill (1987-1999): made worker safety the obsessive top metric.
Boards in every plant, reported daily, tracked ruthlessly. Safety improved ~95%. Side
effect: plants with improving safety ALSO saw quality and productivity improve. The
discipline of measuring and acting carried across metrics.

Toyota plants run SQDCP (or a close variant) at every station. A production manager
can walk any plant globally and read the same board.

Chick-fil-A uses variants of this at the store level. Every store GM knows their
speed-of-service numbers daily. Corporate reviews them weekly. The consistency of
ops metrics across 2,800 stores is a direct output.

## Applied to QSR / retail / ops

- **Store-level SQDCP board behind the counter:** 4x6 feet. Crew updates it at shift
  start. GM reviews with shift lead at open. DM sees it on store visit. It's the
  single-page truth about the store.
- **S:** slip-and-fall incidents, knife cuts, burns; every incident sparks a walk
- **Q:** mystery shop score, health score, guest complaint count
- **D:** speed of service (drive-thru and front counter), order accuracy
- **C:** labor %, food cost %, waste
- **P:** attendance, on-time shift starts, training hours
- **Huddle:** 10 min at open, all shift crew. GM runs it. Exceptions only.

## Common failure modes

- Boards that become wallpaper. Update daily or kill the board.
- Metrics that measure activity, not outcome. "Hours trained" beats "training sessions
  held."
- Board updated by HQ, not by the team. Loses ownership immediately.
- Using the board for blame. Reds are countermeasure triggers, not punishment triggers.
- Tier rollups without tier stand-ups. If Tier 2 doesn't huddle, the escalation dies.
