# Greg Foran — Radical Simplification

**Source:** Greg Foran's tenure running Walmart US (2014-2019) and Woolworths NZ /
The Warehouse Group NZ; public interviews, earnings commentary, and case studies
**Core principle:** Clean, fast, friendly. Strip complexity out of the store — fewer
SKUs, fewer steps, fewer signs, fewer decisions for the associate and the customer.
Operations innovation as radical subtraction.

## When to apply

- Retail or QSR at scale with accumulated complexity
- Stores look cluttered; associates look overwhelmed; customers complain
- Initiatives stacked on initiatives; "the list of things we're doing" is too long
- New CEO or COO looking for operational reset
- "We have 1,200 promotions running this quarter" territory

## The framework

**The three words:**

- **Clean** — store appearance, shelf appearance, aisle width, associate presentation
- **Fast** — checkout speed, restocking speed, associate decision speed
- **Friendly** — associate-customer interaction quality, recovery from problems

These are the three metrics a store is judged on. Everything else subordinates.

**Radical SKU reduction:**

- Foran's Walmart cut ~5-15% of SKUs from most categories during his tenure
- Logic: every added SKU extends shelf, slows restocking, adds supply chain cost,
  confuses the customer
- Target: stock the items customers actually want, in the sizes they actually want
- Private label emphasized; slow-movers pruned mercilessly

**Out-of-stock reduction:**

- Walked stores asking "why is this shelf empty?" and tracing root cause
- Most out-of-stocks are process problems (restocking cadence, incoming truck
  scheduling, POS data lags), not demand problems
- Every empty shelf is a lost sale and a customer trust erosion

**Associate empowerment:**

- Simplified associate roles (fewer tasks per role = fewer ways to fail)
- Cut corporate requirements for reporting / tagging / signing
- Let GMs run their stores with clear metrics, not micromanagement
- Pay and recognition tied to the three metrics (clean, fast, friendly)

**Store reset (Walmart US, 2015-2017):**

- Removed ~50% of the signage that had accumulated over 15 years
- Widened aisles in many stores (literally: pulled fixtures)
- Brighter lighting
- Cleaner queuing systems at checkout
- Associates promoted from task-lists to "make this store feel good"

**Numbers (Walmart US under Foran):**

- Cleanliness scores up materially
- Shelf-availability up (out-of-stocks down)
- Speed-of-service / checkout times down
- Customer satisfaction (both internal and external measures) up
- Foran's Walmart tenure coincided with the start of Walmart US's comp-sales
  recovery from Amazon pressure

## Canonical example

Walmart US pre-Foran (pre-2014): shelves overstocked but with wrong products. Signage
everywhere — sale signs, regulatory signs, associate-of-the-month signs, clearance
signs stacked on clearance signs. Associates spread thin across 20 distinct roles per
store. Customer satisfaction at peer lows. Sales flat despite category tailwinds.

Walmart US mid-Foran (2017-2018): visibly cleaner stores, fewer signs, wider aisles,
fewer SKUs on shelf, associates with clearer roles. Satisfaction rising; comp sales
positive for the first time in years; out-of-stock rate dropping.

Woolworths NZ (Foran before Walmart): similar playbook; turned around a struggling
Australasian grocery retailer with radical simplification.

## Applied to QSR / retail / ops

- **Clean / Fast / Friendly as daily operating lens.** GM walks the store each
  morning asking those three questions. Everything else is downstream.
- **SKU rationalization in QSR:** Every chain's menu has a long tail. Cut the tail.
  Free up prep capacity, simplify training, tighten supply chain.
- **Signage reduction:** Most QSRs have more signs than customers can read. Kill
  corporate-mandated signs that don't drive behavior. The wall becomes clean.
- **Cut manager task lists:** The average QSR GM has too many corporate reporting
  obligations. Every 15 minutes on reporting is 15 minutes off the floor.
- **Out-of-stock as a root-cause question:** Every OOS has a root cause in ordering,
  receiving, stocking, or forecasting. Don't just restock — fix the cause.
- **Pay and recognition aligned:** GMs who deliver clean/fast/friendly get promoted.
  Ones who don't, don't. Visible and consistent.

## Common failure modes

- Simplification as a slogan; "clean, fast, friendly" said but not measured daily.
- SKU cuts without removing associated shelf / prep / training complexity. Savings
  only realize when the downstream stuff is cut too.
- "Empower associates" without giving them authority to make actual decisions. Cut
  the script; let them recover from problems without manager escalation.
- Signage reduction without filling the space with something better (clear
  categorization, clean shelves). Just removing signs leaves a hole.
- Treating radical simplification as a one-time project. It's a continuous discipline
  — complexity grows back every year if you're not cutting it.
