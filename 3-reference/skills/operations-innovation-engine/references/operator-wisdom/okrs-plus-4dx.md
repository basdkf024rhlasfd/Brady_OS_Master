# OKRs + 4DX — Lag and Lead Measures

**Source:** John Doerr *Measure What Matters* (OKRs, Andy Grove lineage); McChesney,
Covey, Huling *The 4 Disciplines of Execution* (4DX)
**Core principle:** Objectives are what you want; Key Results are how you measure
getting there. But KRs are usually lag measures — to actually move them, you need lead
measures (the few weekly behaviors that drive the outcome).

## When to apply

- Goals are set annually but nothing moves in the first 6 months
- Teams hit their KRs but the business doesn't improve (proxy problem)
- Leaders review lag metrics weekly and have no levers to pull
- Priorities compete; no single focus emerges
- Accountability cadence is monthly or quarterly — too slow

## The framework

**OKRs (Objectives and Key Results):**

- **Objective:** Qualitative, ambitious, inspiring. "Become the fastest QSR drive-thru
  in the country."
- **Key Results:** 3-5 measurable outcomes that together mean the Objective is
  achieved. "Cut average drive-thru cycle time from 5:30 to 3:30. Top-decile rank in
  QSR drive-thru speed survey. 95%+ customer satisfaction on speed attribute."
- **Cadence:** Quarterly OKRs nested inside annual OKRs. Weekly check-in on progress.

KRs should stretch. Hitting 70% of a stretch KR usually beats hitting 100% of a safe one.

**4DX (The 4 Disciplines of Execution):**

1. **Focus on the Wildly Important Goal (WIG).** One, maybe two, per team. Not five,
   not twelve. The WIG is the thing that changes the business if you achieve it.
2. **Act on lead measures.** Lag measures (revenue, margin, satisfaction) are
   outcomes you can't directly control. Lead measures are the 2-3 behaviors that
   predict the lag will move. For drive-thru speed: headset occupancy rate, mobile
   order conversion, percentage of orders completed at the order point.
3. **Keep a compelling scoreboard.** A simple visual that shows lead + lag measures
   for the WIG. Everyone on the team can update it. Kept visible in the workplace.
4. **Create a cadence of accountability.** Weekly WIG session (15-30 minutes). Each
   team member reports: what I committed last week, what I accomplished, what I
   commit this week. Nothing else on the agenda.

**Where OKRs and 4DX meet:**

- Objectives = WIGs (picking the right one is the hardest step)
- Key Results often = lag measures (outcome metrics)
- The missing link: 4DX's insistence on **lead measures** for each KR
- OKRs tell you what to measure; 4DX tells you what to *do* every week

## Canonical example

Intel's original OKR practice (Grove): each quarter, executives wrote their OKRs on
a single page. Each direct report wrote theirs. The cascade was explicit — your KR
was your manager's lead measure.

4DX case study — a hospital lowering patient fall rates (the lag). Lead measures:
percentage of at-risk patients visually marked, frequency of hourly rounding,
communication of fall risk at shift change. Hourly rounding rate was the big lever.
Fall rate dropped 40% in 6 months.

Google (2000s-): famous OKR adopter. Teams set OKRs publicly; achievement rates and
stretch levels were part of performance reviews.

## Applied to QSR / retail / ops

- **One WIG per store, per quarter.** Speed. Cleanliness. Or new-item adoption. Not
  all three at once.
- **Lead measures matter more than the WIG.** If the WIG is drive-thru speed (lag),
  the lead measures are headset fit, order-point staffing during peak, and mobile
  order share.
- **Scoreboard on the wall.** Literally — a physical or digital board in the back of
  house, updated daily by crew.
- **Weekly WIG session with GM + shift leaders.** 15 minutes. What did we commit? Did
  we do it? What do we commit next week?
- **Don't stack 5 OKRs on a store GM.** One WIG. The quarter's entire focus.

## Common failure modes

- Too many OKRs. If every team has 5 objectives, the org has 500. Nothing moves.
- OKRs as performance reviews. They're goal-setting, not compensation tools. Mixing
  them creates sandbagging.
- Picking lag measures as lead measures. "Revenue this week" is still a lag.
- Skipping the weekly WIG session during busy periods. That's exactly when the
  cadence matters.
- Setting OKRs without catchball (see Hoshin Kanri). Imposed goals are not committed
  goals.
