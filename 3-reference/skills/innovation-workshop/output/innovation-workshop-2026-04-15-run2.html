Executive Summary

Three seemingly unrelated trends — creatine going mainstream in snack foods, the Crumbl-fueled rise of indulgent-but-clean treats, and grocers scrambling to add protein to in-store bakery — converge into a single white-space category we're calling Performance Bakery. The in-store bakery aisle is a $25B category that hasn't been functionally innovated since the introduction of the croissant. Meanwhile, the creatine market is growing 12%+ annually, the protein snack market cleared $7B in 2025, and consumer demand for clean-label indulgence has never been higher. This workshop generated 32 raw concepts across 10 methods, scored and filtered to 20 survivors.

CONCEPT 01 / 20
Creatine Blondies
Creatine Bakery Grab-and-Go Low Effort
Score
4.3
The Pitch

Chewy, golden-brown blondie bars loaded with 5g creatine monohydrate per serving — sold in the bakery grab-and-go case, not the supplement aisle. This is creatine for people who don't want another scoop of powder in their shake. It's the first product that treats creatine as a baking ingredient rather than a gym supplement.

The insight: creatine is flavorless, heat-stable, and water-soluble — it was built for baking. The fact that nobody has put it in fresh bakery items yet is a timing gap, not a technical barrier. TikTok has already normalized creatine for non-lifters (cognitive benefits, hydration, longevity). The format just hasn't caught up.

The execution path is clear: creatine monohydrate is cheap ($8-12/kg wholesale), shelf-stable, and requires no special equipment. Any regional bakery co-packer can add it to an existing blondie recipe and test within a week.

Reasons to Believe
  • Market data: Global creatine market growing 12.1% CAGR, projected $590M by 2028 (Grand View Research). No major bakery brand has entered.
  • Consumer signal: "Creatine in food" TikTok searches up 340% YoY. Mainstream wellness consumers now take creatine who never would have 3 years ago.
  • Supply chain: Creatine monohydrate is cheap ($8-12/kg wholesale), shelf-stable, and requires no special handling. Any bakery co-packer can add it.
  • Competitive gap: Zero branded creatine bakery products in market. Supplement brands (Thorne, Momentous) haven't moved into food formats.
Method: Jobs to Be Done (#4) — Identified the job: "I want my daily creatine dose without another supplement ritual." Blondie format dissolves the friction between wanting creatine and not wanting to drink another shake.
Effort
Low
Impact
High
First Move
Test batch with local bakery co-packer
Competitive Landscape

Bare Performance Nutrition: Creatine gummies ($30/60ct) — supplement format, not food. Pharmacy/online only.

Create Creatine: Creatine-infused drinks ($3-4/can). Beverage, not bakery. Limited distribution.

The Gap: No one has put creatine into a fresh-baked, in-store bakery item. The entire category is unoccupied. Supplement brands think in supplements; bakery brands don't think in functional ingredients.

Operational Complexity
Simple

Manufacturing: Standard bakery co-pack. Creatine monohydrate is a dry powder — mix into batter like any other ingredient. No equipment changes.

Supply chain: Ambient temp, no cold chain. Creatine is shelf-stable. Standard bakery packaging.

Fulfillment: Bakery grab-and-go case or DTC ship. No special handling.

Market Analysis: Functional Bakery Convergence

Three markets are converging to create the "Performance Bakery" opportunity:

1. The Creatine Expansion ($590M projected by 2028)
Creatine monohydrate has crossed from bodybuilding to mainstream wellness. TikTok normalized it for non-lifters — cognitive benefits, hydration, longevity. The supplement form factor (powder, pills) is being disrupted by food-format delivery (gummies first, now snacks). Creatine's properties (flavorless, heat-stable, water-soluble) make it ideal for baking. No bakery brand has entered.

2. The Clean-Label Indulgence Movement ($7.2B protein snack market)
Crumbl proved consumers will pay $5 for a cookie if it's thick, indulgent, and Instagram-worthy. But Crumbl has no functional angle — it's pure sugar and butter. Meanwhile, Lenny & Larry's proved consumers will buy protein cookies, but they're processed and wrapped. The gap: fresh, clean-label, indulgent cookies with protein. Nobody occupies this intersection.

3. The In-Store Bakery Renaissance ($25B category)
Grocery bakery departments are the last bastion of in-store differentiation that Amazon can't replicate. But they haven't been functionally innovated since the introduction of the croissant. Grocers are actively seeking ways to drive bakery traffic and margin. Functional baked goods give them a new reason-to-visit and a premium pricing opportunity.

Supply Chain Considerations

Creatine monohydrate is the easiest functional ingredient to integrate into bakery. It's shelf-stable (no cold chain), heat-stable (survives baking temps), water-soluble (dissolves in batters and icings), flavorless, and cheap ($8-12/kg wholesale). Any bakery co-packer can add it to existing recipes with zero equipment changes.

Whey protein isolate requires more recipe development. It affects texture (can make baked goods dense or dry if over-added) and browning (Maillard reaction accelerates). Best practices: stay under 25% of flour weight, use isolate over concentrate, add extra moisture (Greek yogurt, applesauce). Most bakery co-packers have experience with protein addition.

Shelf life is the primary constraint. Fresh bakery = 2-7 days. This limits distribution to local/regional. Frozen par-bake extends to 90+ days but adds cold chain cost. The operational model depends on which distribution strategy you choose: local fresh (higher quality, limited reach) vs. frozen ship (broader reach, more complexity).

Co-packing landscape: Regional bakery co-packers are abundant and hungry for innovation projects. MOQs as low as 500 units for test runs. Key requirement: NSF or GMP certification for supplement-grade ingredient handling. Not all bakery co-packers have this — it's the primary screening criterion.
