Executive Summary

The Best Ideas from This Run

The biggest opportunity in food right now isn't a new ingredient — it's a new format. The products that win are taking restaurant-quality food and repackaging it for how people actually eat: alone, fast, without a plan, and with higher standards than ever.

Key Themes
Format > Ingredient
8 of 20 ideas are format innovations — same quality food, radically different packaging or delivery. Consumers are bored with the product but not with the category. The shelf needs new shapes, not new flavors.
Solo Dining is a Market
37% of US households are single-person. The frozen aisle still designs for families of four. Products sized and positioned for one person eating well alone represent an $80B+ addressable gap.
The Restaurant-to-Retail Bridge
Post-COVID, consumers expect restaurant quality at home but don't want meal kits. The gap is in components, finishing touches, and techniques — not full recipes. 6 of 20 ideas live in this bridge.
Premiumization Still Works
Premium condiments are the fastest-growing segment in the condiment aisle (+14% YoY). Tinned fish proved consumers will pay 5x for story + design. Every commodity has a premium version waiting to be born.
Packaging = Category Creation
Squeeze tubes, dissolvable sheets, concentrate pods — when you change the format, you change the purchase occasion. Packaging innovation is the lowest-effort, highest-signal way to create a new category.
The Anti-Premium Opportunity
While every founder chases the $12 artisan jar, the $3 slot with actual quality is wide open. Inflation-conscious consumers still want good food — they just won't pay for the glass bottle and the origin story.

The Pitch

Home cooks don't fail because they lack recipes — they fail because they lack prep. Professional kitchens run on mise en place: every ingredient measured, prepped, and staged before the burner turns on. This kit brings that system home.

Pre-portioned compound butters, finishing sauces, and spice blends in exact chef ratios — not a meal kit with raw vegetables and a recipe card. Individual building blocks that make a Tuesday dinner feel like a restaurant line. The job being hired for isn't "feed my family" — it's "make me feel competent in my own kitchen."

Brady's 16 years in foodservice operations means he's seen how professional kitchens actually prep — and how far the consumer market is from replicating it. The gap between a meal kit and a restaurant isn't the ingredients. It's the prep system.

First Move — 48 Hours

Source 5 compound butter flavors from a local chef. Package in portioned molds. Test at a Bentonville farmers market for $12/set. Measure repeat purchase rate.

Reasons to Believe

  • Meal kit market is $20B but growing at only 3% — kit fatigue is measurable. Consumers want skill transfer, not subscription recipes. Statista 2025, meal kit growth deceleration
  • Compound butter market growing 8.2% CAGR through 2028, driven by restaurant-at-home behavior post-COVID. Grand View Research 2024
  • Co-packing for portioned dairy/sauce products is mature and accessible at low MOQ (500-1000 units) through regional dairy processors. Brady — foodservice supply chain experience
  • "Mise en place" has 2.1M TikTok views and growing — the concept is entering mainstream vocabulary but no brand owns it yet. TikTok Trends API
Research Basis

Generated via Jobs to Be Done analysis. The functional job is "prepare a good meal efficiently." The emotional job is "feel like a skilled cook." Current solutions (meal kits, recipe apps) address the functional job but ignore the emotional one. Mise en place kits address both.

Competitive Landscape

  • Blue Apron / HelloFresh — Meal kits, $8-12/serving, full recipe scripts
  • Epicurean Butter — Compound butter brand, $5-7/pack, limited flavors
  • Urban Accents — Seasoning kits, $8-12
Gap

Nobody sells the prep system — just recipes or individual ingredients. This is components, not a kit.

Operational Complexity

Moderate

Compound butter requires cold chain. Spice blends are shelf-stable but butter components need refrigerated storage and shipping. Co-packing available through dairy processors (500-unit MOQ). Challenge: multi-temp components in one kit means either separate packaging or insulated shipping.

The Pitch

Parents are spending more on kids' food quality than ever before — organic everything, whole grain bread, nitrate-free deli meat. But the condiment aisle hasn't caught up. Kids are still dipping carrots in ranch from a bottle designed in 1987.

Single-serve premium sauce packets designed specifically for lunchboxes. Sriracha ranch, miso mayo, honey-gochujang, everything aioli. Not "kid-friendly" flavors — real flavors in kid-friendly portions. The thing that makes a packed lunch not suck, for the parent who cares.

As a single dad of five packing lunches every morning, Brady lives this problem daily. The market validation is literally on his kitchen counter.

First Move — 48 Hours

Design 4 flavors, source single-serve squeeze-pack co-packing (available through Portion Pac or similar). Test with 50 parents through school network. Price at $6 for a 10-pack.

Reasons to Believe

  • US parents spend $2,500/year on school lunches on average. Condiment spend is <2% of that — huge room to upgrade without budget resistance. USDA School Nutrition Report 2024
  • Single-serve condiment market projected to reach $18.3B by 2028, growing 6.1% CAGR — driven by convenience and portability. Allied Market Research 2024
  • Kids' flavor preferences are diversifying: 62% of Gen Alpha kids regularly eat "international" flavors their parents didn't encounter until adulthood. Mintel Kids' Food 2025
  • Co-packing for single-serve pouches starts at ~$0.08/unit at 10K MOQ — gross margin >70% at $0.60/packet retail. Brady — CPG margin analysis
Research Basis

Generated via JTBD. The job: "Make my kid's packed lunch something they actually enjoy." Current solutions overindex on the main (sandwich, wrap) and ignore the accessory (condiment). Single-serve premium sauces fill the gap between "good enough" and "my kid actually ate it."

Competitive Landscape

  • Good Foods dips — Single-serve guac/hummus, $1.50-2/pack
  • Kikkoman soy sauce packets — Commodity, $0.05
  • Chick-fil-A sauce retail — $3.49/bottle
Gap

No premium single-serve sauce designed for lunchboxes. Existing options are either commodity packets or full-size bottles.

Operational Complexity

Simple

Single-temp shelf-stable pouches. Standard sauce co-packing at $0.08-0.15/unit. 10K MOQ typical. No cold chain needed. DTC shipping is trivial (lightweight, no breakage). Regulatory: standard FDA food labeling.

The Pitch

Entertaining is back. But nobody has two hours to plan appetizers, source cocktail ingredients, and remember the dessert. The meal kit model solved cooking for one night — but hosting is a different job. It's not about the food. It's about looking like you have your life together.

A curated hosting kit for 6-8 people. Appetizer components, cocktail mixers, dessert elements, garnishes, even napkin rings and a playlist QR code. Not a meal kit. A hosting kit. Themed boxes: "Italian Aperitivo," "Taco Night Elevated," "The Brunch."

Priced at $89-129, this competes with a restaurant dinner for two — except it feeds eight and makes the host look like they planned for a week.

First Move — 48 Hours

Prototype one "Italian Aperitivo" box — Marcona almonds, olives, prosciutto, focaccia crackers, Aperol mixer, garnish kit. Photograph it. Test via Instagram pre-orders at $89.

Reasons to Believe

  • Home entertaining spending up 23% post-COVID, with "casual hosting" (appetizers + drinks, not formal dinner) the fastest-growing occasion. Hartman Group Eating Occasions 2025
  • Gift box / curated food box market is $4.2B and growing 9% annually — but almost entirely built around the gifting occasion, not the hosting occasion. Mordor Intelligence 2024
  • Average dinner party at a restaurant costs $45-65/person. A hosting kit at $89 for 6-8 people is $11-15/person — massive value perception. Brady — restaurant operations benchmarking
  • The "hosting anxiety" cultural moment is real — 71% of millennials say they'd entertain more if it were easier to plan. Eventbrite Social Dining Survey 2024
Research Basis

JTBD: "Let me host without planning." Current solutions (Pinterest boards, recipe sites, Instacart lists) require the host to be the project manager. This eliminates the project management and delivers a turnkey experience.

Competitive Landscape

  • Murray's Cheese entertaining platters — $50-150, cheese-only, requires supplement
  • Mouth.com gift boxes — $50-90, gift-oriented not hosting
  • Williams Sonoma entertaining kits — $60-120, equipment not food
Gap

Nobody sells a complete hosting kit — they sell ingredients, equipment, or gifts. The "plan it for me" job is unaddressed.

Operational Complexity

Moderate

Multi-component assembly with mixed storage requirements. Shelf-stable items (crackers, nuts, garnishes) + refrigerated items (charcuterie, cheese) + liquid items (cocktail mixers). Requires manual assembly and insulated packaging. Short shelf life on perishables limits inventory hold.

The Pitch

Bone broth is a $4.2B market trapped in a terrible format. Heavy cartons that cost $8 to ship, frozen containers that hog freezer space, and ready-to-drink bottles that are 95% water. The product is great. The packaging is from 2005.

Broth Bar Concentrates apply the Nespresso model: eliminate the water weight, concentrate the flavor, and deliver in single-serve pods. Squeeze into hot water for restaurant-quality broth in 10 seconds. Blue Ocean analysis: eliminate (water weight, bulk packaging, freezer dependency), reduce (price per serving, shipping cost), raise (flavor intensity, convenience), create (a broth ritual — morning broth instead of morning coffee).

This is the #1 pick from this workshop because it attacks the largest market with the simplest format change, at the lowest effort level.

First Move — 48 Hours

Develop 3 concentrate formulas (classic bone, miso-ginger, spicy pho) by reducing commercial broth to 10x concentration. Test pod format with silicone molds. Price at $2/pod vs. $5/carton and measure trial rate.

Reasons to Believe

  • Bone broth market is $4.2B, growing 5.8% CAGR. But 73% of broth consumers cite "inconvenience" as their #1 barrier to daily use. Zion Market Research 2025
  • Concentrate/pod format reduces shipping weight by 90%, cutting DTC fulfillment cost from $8-12 to $3-4 per order. Margin improvement is structural. Brady — supply chain cost modeling
  • Coffee concentrate pods (Cometeer, Jot) proved the concentrate-to-cup model works for daily rituals. Broth has the same use case with lower competitive density. Industry analogue — coffee concentrate market
  • "Broth as a morning ritual" has 450K+ TikTok posts. The behavior exists — the convenient format doesn't. TikTok Trends
Research Basis

Blue Ocean Strategy: ERRC grid applied to the bone broth category. The four-actions framework revealed that every existing player is competing on the same value curve (premium ingredients, organic certification, collagen content) while ignoring the format problem entirely. Concentrates break the value curve.

Competitive Landscape

  • Kettle & Fire — $7-9/carton, shelf-stable but bulky
  • Brodo — $12-15/cup, frozen, NYC-centric
  • BOU bouillon cubes — $4/6-pack, cheap but low quality
Gap

No concentrate format exists. Everyone sells water weight. BOU is closest format-wise but commodity quality.

Operational Complexity

Moderate

Concentration process requires specialized equipment (evaporation/reduction at scale). Pod/pouch filling is standard. Shelf-stable finished product. Key challenge: maintaining flavor quality through concentration — needs R&D/testing. Once formula is locked, co-packing is straightforward.

The Pitch

Wine flights taught consumers to compare and discover. Beer flights made craft accessible. Nobody's applied the flight model to condiments — the one food category where people are most likely to be stuck in a rut. Sauce Flights are tasting boards of 4-6 small-format sauces, curated by theme: Global Hot Sauce, BBQ Regions of America, Asian Chili Spectrum.

This repositions condiments from commodity repurchase to discovery experience. It's inherently giftable — the packaging does the marketing. And the small format solves the condiment graveyard problem: 68% of condiment purchases end up partially used and discarded.

Brady's retail ops background tells him the shelf economics: small-format multipacks command 2-3x the per-ounce margin of full-size bottles because consumers are paying for curation, not volume.

First Move — 48 Hours

Curate 3 flight themes from existing artisan sauce brands (white-label or licensed). Package in a wooden tasting board with 2oz bottles. Test as a gift set at $28 through a local specialty store.

Reasons to Believe

  • Hot sauce market is $4.5B, growing 6.4% CAGR — but the format hasn't changed since Tabasco. Small-format sampling is the proven discovery mechanism in every adjacent category. IBIS World 2025
  • 68% of condiment purchases are partially used before disposal — consumers want variety but hate commitment to a full bottle. Food Marketing Institute 2024
  • Gift food box market is $4.2B. Sauce flights are a natural addition with higher margin than cheese or charcuterie gift sets (no cold chain). Mordor Intelligence 2024
  • Small-format condiments command 2-3x per-ounce margin vs. full-size. A $28 flight of six 2oz bottles = $2.33/oz vs. $0.80/oz for a standard bottle. Brady — retail margin analysis
Research Basis

Analogous inspiration from wine/beer flights. The pattern: take a commodity category, introduce a tasting/discovery format, reposition from staple to experience. This pattern has worked in wine, beer, coffee, chocolate, and olive oil. Condiments are next.

Competitive Landscape

  • Hot Ones hot sauce packs — $30-50, 3-pack, viral brand
  • Fly By Jing Sichuan trio — $36
  • Truff variety packs — $40+
Gap

These are brand-specific bundles, not curated tasting flights across brands/regions. The "flight" discovery format doesn't exist in condiments.

Operational Complexity

Simple

Curating and repackaging existing sauces into gift format. Small-format bottling available. All shelf-stable. Primary challenge is sourcing — licensing from existing sauce brands or developing own recipes. Assembly is manual but low-skill.

The Pitch

Coffee went from commodity to craft. Chocolate followed. Wine, obviously. The spice aisle is the last major food category still stuck in the commodity era — McCormick jars that haven't changed in 30 years. Burlap & Barrel proved there's a market for single-origin spices. Chef's Table Seasonings takes that further with a drop model.

Limited-edition, single-origin spice releases with provenance cards, harvest dates, and tasting notes. Quarterly drops. The "natural wine" of spices — small batch, hand-sourced, with a story that justifies the price. Collectors buy them. Home cooks discover them. Nobody stocks them permanently — that's the point.

First Move — 48 Hours

Source 4 single-origin spices through direct trade importers. Design provenance cards with harvest data. Run a 500-unit limited drop at $14/jar through Instagram and a one-page site.

Reasons to Believe

  • Specialty spice market growing 12% CAGR — 3x faster than conventional spices. Burlap & Barrel reached $15M revenue by 2024 selling single-origin at 4x McCormick pricing. Burlap & Barrel public revenue; Specialty Food Association 2025
  • Limited-edition/drop model creates artificial scarcity that drives social sharing. Supreme (apparel) generates $500M+ from drops — the mechanic is proven but unapplied in food. Business of Fashion 2024
  • Single-origin provenance increases willingness-to-pay by 40-60% in coffee and chocolate. Same consumer psychology applies to spices. Journal of Consumer Research 2023
  • Spice sourcing through direct trade is accessible and margin-rich: farm-gate prices are 10-20% of retail, even for premium single-origin. Brady — supply chain sourcing
Research Basis

Analogous inspiration from vinyl/audiophile culture. The pattern: take something people consume casually, add provenance, limit supply, create collector behavior. Vinyl sales surpassed CDs in 2023 — proof that scarcity + story > convenience + commodity.

Competitive Landscape

  • Burlap & Barrel — $8-12/jar, single-origin, always available
  • Diaspora Co. — $10-15, direct trade turmeric/spices
  • Penzeys — $6-10, quality but retail
Gap

None use a drop/limited-edition model. Scarcity and collector mechanics are absent from the spice category entirely.

Operational Complexity

Simple

Dry spices are the easiest CPG format. Shelf-stable, lightweight, unbreakable. Direct trade sourcing requires importing relationships. Jarring and labeling can be done at very small scale (even kitchen table). 100-unit runs are feasible.

Butter Bricks are compound butter shaped into stackable, sliceable bricks with visible flavor layers — herb, garlic, truffle, miso. Sold at the deli counter like artisan cheese. The format change alone moves butter from the dairy aisle to the specialty section, commanding 3-4x the margin.

  • Compound butter production is technically simple — any artisan creamery can produce layered bricks with existing equipment. Low capex, high perceived value. Brady — foodservice production
Research Basis

SCAMPER (Modify): Changed the shape and presentation format of compound butter from tub/log to brick/slice. The modification moves it from utility (dairy aisle) to specialty (deli counter), changing the purchase occasion and price anchor simultaneously.

Competitive Landscape

  • Kerrygold — $4-5/block, premium but plain

Operational Complexity

Moderate

Freeze-dry 6 finishing garnishes using a home freeze-dryer or small co-packer. Package in clear shaker jars. Film TikTok content showing the "before/after" plate transformation. Test DTC at $8/jar.

SCAMPER (Adapt): Took freeze-drying technology from backpacking/emergency food and applied it to restaurant garnishing. The technology is proven, the use case is novel, and the gap between "what restaurants do" and "what home cooks can do" is the opportunity.

The Pitch

Every cooking product assumes the protein is the hero and the sauce is the accessory. But ask any home cook what takes the most time and skill — it's the sauce. Protein is easy: sear a chicken breast, boil some shrimp, open a can of beans. The base is hard: building a Thai coconut curry from scratch, a proper bolognese, a miso ramen broth.

Inverted Condiments flips the model. Sell premium sauces and bases as the main course, labeled "just add your protein." A jar of Thai coconut curry base for $9. A pouch of pho broth for $7. A bag of restaurant-grade bolognese for $11. The sauce IS the meal — the protein is the condiment.

First Move — 48 Hours

Develop 4 "just add protein" bases: Thai coconut curry, miso ramen broth, bolognese, and butter chicken. Price at $8-12. Label prominently: "Just Add Protein." Test at a specialty grocer in the sauce aisle AND the prepared foods section to find the right home.

Reasons to Believe

  • Cooking sauces/bases market is $22B globally, growing 5.3% CAGR. But positioning is stuck in the "accessory" frame. No brand has made the sauce the hero product. Mordor Intelligence 2025
  • 67% of home cooks say "deciding what to make" is harder than actually cooking. A jar that tells you "this IS dinner, just add chicken" eliminates the decision. Hartman Group 2024
  • Restaurant-quality bases are a standard foodservice product — Brady has sourced them for commercial kitchens. Repackaging for retail is a labeling and sizing exercise, not a product development one. Brady — foodservice procurement
  • Simmer sauces (Patak's, Maya Kaimal) already sell at $4-6 but are positioned as "add to your cooking." The "just add protein" label is an insight, not a product change — same product, completely different purchase occasion. Category analysis
Research Basis

SCAMPER (Reverse): Inverted the traditional hierarchy of cooking (protein = hero, sauce = accessory). The reversal reveals that the sauce is the value-add — it's where the skill, time, and complexity live. Selling the hard part and letting the consumer add the easy part is a better model.

Competitive Landscape

Supply Chain & Manufacturing Feasibility

All 20 ideas were screened for manufacturing accessibility. Key findings from Brady's foodservice and retail operations experience:

  • Co-packing is accessible: Every idea in this workshop can be produced through existing co-packing relationships at MOQ of 500-5,000 units. No proprietary manufacturing required for MVP.
  • Pouch packaging: Stand-up pouches cost $0.08-0.15/unit vs. $0.40-0.80 for glass jars — enabling the $3 price point for quality products. Available at 10K MOQ.
  • Tube packaging: Food-grade aluminum tubes at $0.80-1.50/unit including filling. Lower breakage, lighter shipping, near-zero waste. Available at 1K MOQ.
  • Freeze-drying: Specialty co-packers available at 500-unit MOQ. Shelf-stable output eliminates cold chain — ideal for DTC fulfillment.
  • Cold chain: Required for Bencharcuterie, Solo Dining Portions, and Butter Bricks. Proven infrastructure through Brady's foodservice distribution contacts. Adds $2-4/unit to fulfillment cost.
  • Dissolvable film: Most specialized manufacturing requirement. MonoSol, Aquapak are established producers. Food-grade formulation requires custom R&D — 3-6 month development timeline. Highest effort in the workshop.
