## Bottom Line Up Front

JB Hunt FMS all-in revenue per stop runs **~$210** (2024 blended average across their full book of business). For pure white-glove furniture delivery — 1915 South's service level — the rate runs higher, likely **$225–275/stop** before any volume negotiation. At 29 stores with an estimated 50K–120K annual delivery stops, a JB Hunt contract would run approximately **$11M–$33M/year at list rates**, versus 1915 South's estimated **$15M–18M fully-loaded owned-delivery cost**. The economics are close — meaning the real value drivers of outsourcing are capital liberation and HR simplicity, not unit cost savings.

**Important caveats on this $210 figure:**

**Working estimate for 1915 South:** Budget **$225–275/stop** for white-glove in-home furniture delivery through JB Hunt FMS, before any negotiated discounts for volume commitment or geographic concentration.

## 2. JB Hunt FMS — What They'll Actually Offer 1915 South

JB Hunt sells two distinct models to furniture retailers (per HFA Solution Partner page):

**A. Final Mile Commingle ("nationwide delivery network"):**
- 1915 South's deliveries share trucks with other shippers' freight in the same geographic zone
- Best for: lower-volume markets, geographic outliers, overflow capacity
- Pro: no volume minimums, immediate coverage across 99%+ of US
- Con: less brand control, scheduling tied to route density, potential longer windows in rural markets

**B. Dedicated Final Mile ("dedicated and customized, for retailers with market density"):**
- JB Hunt provides dedicated routes/trucks exclusively for 1915 South
- Best for: markets where 1915 South has high stop density (e.g., core FL markets, NC corridor)
- Pro: consistent crew, brand alignment, tighter scheduling windows
- Con: requires volume floor to justify economics (likely 50-75+ stops/week per market minimum)
- Note: JB Hunt does NOT publish dedicated program minimums

**Recommendation for 1915 South:** A hybrid contract — dedicated in the 4-5 highest-density markets, commingled in outlier markets. This mirrors how City Furniture operates (in-house for FL core, 3PL for outlying markets).

## 3. JB Hunt FMS — Southeast Coverage Assessment

**Network:** 274 locations, 99.4% contiguous US coverage, largest commingled cross-dock network in the US.

**Geographic DNA:** JB Hunt's furniture delivery expertise came from two acquisitions:
- **Cory 1st Choice Home Delivery** ($100M, Feb 2019) — New Jersey-based, 14 warehouses, 1,000+ contractors, 2M deliveries/year, strong Northeast/Mid-Atlantic
- **RDI Last Mile Co.** (Dec 2019, ~$35M revenue) — East Coast specialist, large-format delivery, added Southeast capacity

The Cory heritage is Northeast-origin. The Southeast network is real but built partly through the RDI acquisition and organic expansion. It is not as deep as their Northeast/Midwest corridors.

**1915 South's specific markets — risk assessment:**

| Market | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Florida core (Orlando, Tampa, Jacksonville, South FL) | Strong — high density, JB Hunt has established presence, multiple cross-dock locations |
| North/Central Florida (Tallahassee, Gainesville, Ocala) | Good — serviceable, moderate density |
| FL Panhandle (Pensacola, Panama City, Destin) | Moderate risk — lower density, likely 2-3 day delivery windows rather than daily, possible rural premium |
| Georgia (Thomasville, Albany, Valdosta) | Good — close to JB Hunt's Southeast network, manageable density |
| North Carolina (Greensboro, Charlotte market) | Strong — I-85/I-40 corridor, good density |
| Alabama/Mississippi (Birmingham, Biloxi, Gulf Coast) | Moderate risk — thin market nationally for big-and-bulky 3PL, Biloxi specifically is a known coverage gap for several 3PLs |
| Roanoke VA / SW Virginia | Moderate — I-81 corridor is serviceable, but Roanoke is a secondary market; JB Hunt likely serves it from their Mid-Atlantic network |

**The rural coverage question is the most important thing Brady should ask Justin.** What percentage of 1915 South's delivery volume is in markets under 100K population? If >30%, JB Hunt's commingled network will struggle to match owned-fleet service levels, scheduling windows, and customer satisfaction scores.

## 7. In-House vs. Outsourced Economics Framework

**Industry benchmarks:**
- Delivery cost as % of furniture retail sales: **6–10%** average; top performers at **4.5–6%**
- Last-mile = **53%** of total shipping cost for bulky furniture items
- Owned fleets outperform 3PL on unit cost once volume exceeds **~3,000–3,500 daily consignments** in a fixed zone (this is US-generic; Southeast density makes the threshold slightly lower)
- 3PL reduces total logistics costs by **10%+ on average** — but this is for operations transitioning from poorly-optimized owned fleets, not well-run ones

**Maine Pointe case study (unnamed furniture retailer, 2022):**
Most relevant published analog — client had poorly managed 3PL contracts, branded trucks with 3PL name, no pricing discipline.
- Restructured to 90% client-branded, dedicated delivery
- Achieved **21% savings on final-mile, 15% on linehaul, 27% total delivery cost reduction**
- EBIT improved **23%**
- Key insight: the directional move was *toward* dedicated/owned delivery, not away from it

**The crossover math for 1915 South:**
| Scenario | Annual Cost |
|---|---|
| Current owned fleet (est., from SFDR-015) | ~$15-18M (13% of $137M est. revenue) |
| JB Hunt at $210 blended/stop × 70K stops | $14.7M |
| JB Hunt at $250 white-glove/stop × 70K stops | $17.5M |
| JB Hunt at $250 white-glove/stop × 100K stops | $25.0M |

At 70K annual delivery stops, JB Hunt pricing at $210 (blended) is essentially a wash against owned fleet. At $250/stop (white-glove), owned fleet wins on unit cost. The outsourcing value is entirely in capital liberation and headcount reduction — not P&L economics at the per-stop level.

## 8. JB Hunt Financial Health & Negotiating Posture

**JB Hunt FMS is in a volume contraction cycle but margin expansion mode:**
- 2024 full-year: $910M revenue (-1% YoY), $60M operating income (+29% YoY) — **record FMS operating income**
- Stops fell from 4.60M (2023) → 4.32M (2024) → running ~4.0M annualized rate in 2025
- Q1 2025: Revenue $201M (-12% YoY), Operating Income $4.7M (-69% YoY)
- Q1 2026: Revenue $188M (-6% YoY), Operating Income $7.2M (+53% YoY)

**What this means for negotiation:**
- JB Hunt is *not* desperate for any volume at any price — they shed unprofitable accounts to hit record margins in 2024
- But at -12-15% stop decline, they are actively replacing lost volume
- A 29-store committed network with predictable stop volume in an underserved Southeast geography is exactly the kind of new contract they want
- The CEO specifically called out "multiple new customer contracts implemented over the past year" as a growth driver
- **Best negotiating window in 5 years** — JB Hunt won't cut margin to win business, but they will compete aggressively on service commitment and account management
