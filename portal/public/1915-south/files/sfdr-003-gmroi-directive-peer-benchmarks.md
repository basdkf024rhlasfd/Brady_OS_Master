# SFDR-003 — Ashley GMROI Directive & Peer Benchmarks

**Date:** 2026-04-25
**Depth:** Standard
**Sources analyzed:** 22
**Research type:** Competitive Analysis / Market Research
**Prepared for:** Brady Smallwood — Justin Woods call prep

## Executive Summary

Ashley Furniture Industries published a formal GMROI-focused press release on December 9, 2024, positioning GMROI as "a cornerstone of retailer success" and tying its $1.5B+ manufacturing investment directly to licensee profitability outcomes. The release is strategic framing, not a hard-number mandate — no specific GMROI floor, target, or timeline was published externally. The actual enforcement mechanism appears to be commercial (ordering dynamics, inventory stocking requirements, preferred retailer status) rather than contractual. Peer GMROI benchmarks show a wide spread: Havertys runs ~4.95 (high-margin, lean-inventory operator), Arhaus runs ~1.82 (premium-price, higher-inventory model), and industry consensus pegs a "satisfactory" furniture retailer GMROI at $2.00–$3.00 per dollar invested. No public statements from peer Ashley licensees responding to the directive have surfaced in trade press — the franchisee response is opaque externally.

## Key Findings

1. **The December 2024 directive is a public positioning statement, not a published numeric mandate.** Ashley's December 9, 2024 press release ("Ashley Furniture Industries Drives Retail Success Through GMROI Excellence and Manufacturing Innovation") frames GMROI as foundational to the licensee value proposition but names no specific targets. [1]

2. **Ashley won the inaugural HFA Manufacturer of the Year award specifically for GMROI education.** The citation explicitly credits Ashley for "educating its retail partners about GMROI, throughput, and turn rate" — signaling that GMROI is the lens through which Ashley is positioning its entire manufacturer-retailer relationship externally. [2]

3. **The "factory floor to front door" phrase is Ashley's tagline for the GMROI push.** Woodworking Network coverage of the December release used this framing and described the strategy as running from manufacturing investment through to retailer inventory optimization — vertically owned argument for why Ashley products generate better returns than competitors. [3]

4. **No specific GMROI targets or compliance thresholds were published to the licensee network.** The ashley.biz licensee FAQ and the operator-facing inventory article both promote GMROI as an important metric without stating any numeric floor or enforcement mechanism. [4][5]

5. **Havertys GMROI (calculated): ~4.95.** FY 2024: $439.1M gross profit ÷ $88.7M average inventory. Gross margin of 60.7%. This is an outlier driven by Havertys' unusually high gross margin for furniture retail, lean inventory posture, and geographic concentration. [6]

6. **Arhaus GMROI (calculated): ~1.82.** FY 2024: $501.2M gross profit ÷ $275.65M average inventory. Gross margin of 39.4%. Arhaus intentionally carries more inventory (up 16.8% YoY) to support its premium custom order model. [7]

7. **Industry consensus GMROI benchmark for furniture retail: $2.00–$3.00.** Retalon (furniture-specific retail analytics source) states "most furniture retailers would be happy to get a GMROI between $2–$3." Broader retail benchmarking sources corroborate this as the satisfactory range. [8][9]

8. **No peer Ashley licensee has publicly commented on the directive.** Trade press search across Furniture Today, Home News Now, Woodworking Network, and HFA content surfaces zero named licensee operators responding to the GMROI push. The response, if any, is happening behind closed doors.

9. **Ashley's leverage is commercial, not contractual.** The licensee structure requires no fees and no revenue sharing — operators "have the freedom to run your business as you see fit." [5] This means GMROI compliance is enforced through product access, inventory stocking programs, and co-op marketing eligibility, not through contractual performance requirements.

10. **Todd Wanek's December 2024 positioning ties domestic manufacturing investment directly to retailer turns.** His stated logic: Ashley's 60% domestic production + near-500-robot automation = faster replenishment cycles = licensees can carry leaner inventory = higher turns = better GMROI. This is the underlying rationale for the GMROI push. [10]

## Detailed Analysis

### 1. What the December 2024 GMROI Directive Actually Said

The primary source is Ashley Furniture Industries' press release dated December 9, 2024, titled "Ashley Furniture Industries Drives Retail Success Through GMROI Excellence and Manufacturing Innovation." It was published at ashleyfurnitureindustriesllc.com and picked up by Woodworking Network under the "factory floor to front door" framing.

Core claims from the release:
- "Ashley has built its foundation and legacy upon the methodology of Gross Margin Return on Investment (GMROI)"
- GMROI is described as "a cornerstone of retailer success"
- The mechanism: "through expert forecasting and inventory management, Ashley helps retailers optimize their largest investment — inventory. By maintaining tight, lean stock levels while ensuring product availability, retailers achieve higher turn rates and increased profitability"
- Todd Wanek: "When we invest in our manufacturing and distribution network, we're really investing in our retailers' success"

**What it did not say:** No specific GMROI targets (e.g., "licensees must achieve GMROI of 2.5"), no compliance timeline, no measurement period, no consequences for underperformance. This is a public brand/trade positioning document, not an operator directive. The actual operational specifics — if any exist — would be in private communications to the licensee network.

The simultaneous context: Ashley won the inaugural HFA Manufacturer of the Year award (presented at the Fall 2024 market cycle) specifically citing GMROI education as a key criterion. This suggests December 2024 was a coordinated public moment to cement the GMROI narrative with the broader trade.

### 2. Peer Ashley Licensee Responses

No public responses from named Ashley HomeStore licensees have appeared in trade press. The search covered Furniture Today, Home News Now, Woodworking Network, Home Accents Today, HFA publications, and LinkedIn through April 2026.

What this silence likely means: The Ashley licensee model is commercially structured, not franchise-structured. There is no Franchise Disclosure Document, no formal franchise association, and no structured forum for licensee pushback that generates press. Operators who disagree manage it through private conversations or by quietly expanding non-Ashley SKUs in their stores.

The relevant structural fact: Ashley's licensee agreement requires operators to purchase products from Ashley (the only financial obligation) and grants "the freedom to run your business as you see fit." This means GMROI pressure from Ashley is soft power — product prioritization, inventory program access, co-op advertising, market event invitations — not hard contractual requirements.

### 3. GMROI Improvement Strategies Furniture Retailers Are Discussing

From trade press and industry sources (2024–2025):

**Assortment rationalization (SKU reduction):** Applying the 80/20 rule — 80% of sales from 20% of SKUs — to eliminate low-velocity items. The practical application in furniture: auditing floor sample counts, reducing SKU count in underperforming categories (e.g., dining), concentrating investment in bedroom and upholstery where turns are higher.

**Order frequency vs. order size:** Ashley's MOQ-of-1 policy is specifically positioned to enable this. Rather than buying deep on a few SKUs, licensees are encouraged to buy wider and shallower — more styles on floor at lower per-SKU investment. This directly improves turns without requiring margin improvement.

**Lean floor model inventory:** Reducing the number of physically stocked floor samples while using digital merchandising (3D rendering, room visualizers) to present a broader catalog without the carrying cost. Arhaus is the premium example of this — high floor investment, but tied to custom order model that captures client deposits before production.

**Promotional clearance cadence:** Using structured promotional events (clearance events, end-of-season sales) to clear aging inventory rather than holding at full price. The goal is converting slow-turning floor samples to cash before they erode margin.

**Category mix shift:** Shifting assortment toward higher-margin, faster-turning categories. In furniture, this typically means accessories, accent pieces, and case goods (dressers, nightstands) over large-ticket upholstery, which has slower turns but higher gross margin per transaction.

### 4. GMROI Benchmarks — Havertys and Arhaus (Calculated from Public Filings)

**Havertys (HVT) — FY 2024**
- Net sales: $722.9M
- Gross profit: $439.1M (60.7% gross margin)
- Inventory (end of year): $83.4M
- Inventory (prior year end): $94.0M
- Average inventory: $88.7M
- **Calculated GMROI: $439.1M ÷ $88.7M = 4.95**
- Inventory turns: $283.8M COGS ÷ $88.7M avg inventory = **3.20 turns/year**
- Context: Havertys operates 107 stores in 16 states (Southeast/Mid-Atlantic), high-end positioning, 60%+ gross margin is exceptional for furniture. The GMROI of ~5.0 is well above the industry $2–$3 benchmark — driven primarily by the extraordinary gross margin, not by turns. Revenue declined 16.1% YoY in 2024 due to housing market softness.

**Arhaus (ARHS) — FY 2024**
- Net revenue: $1,271.1M
- Gross profit: $501.2M (39.4% gross margin)
- COGS: $769.9M
- Inventory (end of year): $297.0M
- Inventory (prior year end): $254.3M
- Average inventory: $275.65M
- **Calculated GMROI: $501.2M ÷ $275.65M = 1.82**
- Inventory turns: $769.9M COGS ÷ $275.65M avg inventory = **2.79 turns/year**
- Context: Arhaus sells premium custom-order furniture through 95 showrooms. Its GMROI of ~1.82 is below the "satisfactory" $2–$3 range because the custom order model requires carrying raw material and in-transit inventory. Client deposits ($221M, up 27% YoY) partially offset this — the model is economically sound even at lower GMROI because deposits fund inventory.

**Industry consensus benchmark (non-calculated):**
- Retalon (furniture retail analytics): "Most furniture retailers would be happy to get a GMROI between $2–$3"
- Broad retail GMROI databases: Home goods/furniture averages ~2.0–2.5 satisfactory range
- Floor covering specialty retail (a comparable premium category): 4.42 average GMROI
- Source: Retalon.com, multiple retail analytics providers

## Implications & Recommendations for Justin Call

- **The directive is leverage framing, not a hard mandate.** Justin should engage with the GMROI push on his own terms — agree with the metric, demonstrate what 1915 South's current GMROI is, and frame any gap as a roadmap conversation rather than a compliance issue.

- **Havertys' ~5.0 GMROI is not a realistic peer benchmark for an Ashley licensee.** Havertys achieves it through 60%+ gross margin — a function of its premium positioning and private label mix, not superior inventory management. A more honest peer for Justin is the industry consensus $2–$3 satisfactory range.

- **Arhaus' ~1.82 GMROI, below the satisfactory range, is instructive.** Arhaus is profitable, growing, and well-capitalized despite a sub-$2 GMROI — because the custom order model (client deposits) changes the cash flow dynamic. If 1915 South has special order business, that same argument applies.

- **Ashley's leverage is the product catalog and supply chain, not the contract.** The "freedom to run your business as you see fit" language in the licensee agreement means Justin can push back on any implied mandate with "show me the contractual basis." The real question is what access to product, programs, or inventory terms is tied to GMROI performance — and that's a private conversation with his territory rep.

- **The right offensive posture for Justin:** quantify 1915 South's current GMROI, identify the 2–3 operational levers within his control (assortment rationalization, order frequency, promotional clearance cadence), and present a roadmap to move from current state to the $2–$3 industry benchmark. This turns a compliance conversation into a strategic planning conversation.

- **What specific numeric GMROI targets, if any, Ashley communicated privately to licensees.** The December press release is public; the operator-level communication is not. Justin will know whether he received a specific number.
- **1915 South's actual current GMROI.** Without their gross margin % and average inventory balance, the benchmark comparison is directional only.
- **Whether Ashley has a tiered licensee program tied to GMROI performance** (e.g., preferred retailer status, co-op advertising eligibility, priority on new product launches). No public documentation exists on this.
- **HFA member-only benchmarking data.** The Retail Owners Institute and HFA member surveys likely contain category-specific GMROI ranges, but these are paywalled.
- **Peer licensee responses.** No named Ashley HomeStore operators have commented publicly. If Justin has relationships with other large licensees (Broad River, American Signature, etc.), their informal responses would be more useful than anything in the public record.
