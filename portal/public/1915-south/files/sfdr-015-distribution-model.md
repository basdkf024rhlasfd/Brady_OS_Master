# SFDR-015 — 1915 South Distribution Model (CLOSED)

**Date researched:** 2026-04-24
**Status:** Closed — confirmed from public sources (Thomasville Times-Enterprise, Home News Now, Furniture Today, 1915south.com)
**Unlock:** Distribution is the largest single cost lever in the business. Justin's JB Hunt comment signals a potential outsourcing of owned last-mile — a 5-6 point EBITDA margin expansion opportunity.

---

## The Model: Fully Vertically Integrated Last-Mile

1915 South does NOT pull-and-ship from Ashley corporate DCs for end-customer delivery. They operate their own distribution network end-to-end.

**Supply chain:**
1. Ashley manufactures at US facilities (Brandon FL, Advance NC, Ecru/Ripley/Verona MS, Arcadia WI)
2. Ashley ships to 1915 South's regional DCs (domestic 2-3 week lead time via "Quick Ship Program")
3. 1915 South receives, processes, and assembles at its DCs
4. 1915 South's own trucks deliver white-glove (in-home, with assembly) to customers
5. Customers can also pick up at DCs (dedicated pick-up area confirmed in Thomasville DC)

**Most relevant Ashley supply DCs for 1915 South's geography:**
- Brandon, FL → Florida stores (12 stores, biggest market)
- Advance, NC → NC and VA stores
- Ecru/Ripley/Verona, MS → AL and MS stores

Previous analysis underestimated distribution at 6% of revenue. Revised based on actual DC network size:

| Cost Item | Annual Est. | % of $182M Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| DC/delivery staff (300 employees × $52K loaded avg) | ~$15.6M | ~9% |
| DC occupancy (4 facilities, lease/mortgage) | ~$4-5M | ~2.5% |
| Truck fleet D&A + fuel + maintenance | ~$3-4M | ~2% |
| **Total distribution/delivery** | **~$23-25M** | **~13%** |

This is the single largest operating cost line after product cost. Note: 1915 South's actual EBITDA is ~14% ($25.5M on $182M per Justin's stated figures) — not the 6-8% compressed furniture retail average. Their owned distribution infrastructure is already priced into that margin.

## The JB Hunt Signal — Most Important Finding

Justin's April 20 email mentioned: "a partnership with JB Hunt (or similar) for final mile distribution" as the vision for the new VP Distribution role.

Given that 1915 South CURRENTLY OWNS its full last-mile operation, this is not a status-quo management hire. **This VP is being hired to execute a strategic outsourcing of the delivery function.**

### JB Hunt Final Mile Services (FMS):
JB Hunt FMS specializes in white-glove, in-home furniture and appliance delivery. Already used by major furniture retailers. The service covers scheduling, delivery crews, assembly, damage handling — everything 1915 South currently self-operates.

### Economic case for the JB Hunt transition:

| Item | Current (owned) | JB Hunt model | Annual Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery crew labor (~120-160 delivery-specific of ~280-340 DC staff) | ~$6.2-8.3M | Transferred to JB Hunt; ~100-150 retained for receiving/assembly | -$7.3M gross (offset by retained staff staying on payroll) |
| Truck fleet (est. $8-10M delivery fleet, 5yr life) | ~$1.6-2M D&A + $1.5M fuel/maint = ~$3.1M | Sell delivery fleet, one-time cash | -$3.1M/yr + ~$5-8M one-time |
| DC delivery bays | ~$0.5-1M/yr | Consolidate space | -$0.75M |
| JB Hunt per-delivery fee (est. 10-12K deliveries/yr × $450-600/delivery) | — | +$4.5-7.2M | +$5.9M (midpoint) |
| **Net annual P&L benefit** | | | **~$2-3M net cost reduction** |
| **One-time capital freed** | | | **~$5-8M from delivery fleet** |

**P&L impact: ~1-2 points of EBITDA margin expansion.** Current EBITDA: ~$25.5M (14% of $182M, Justin-confirmed). Post-transition: ~$27.5-28.5M (~15-16%). The primary value of the JB Hunt transition is capital liberation and operational simplicity — not pure P&L transformation.

**This is the highest-value strategic decision in the company — not the exec layer, not GMROI.**

### What to retain (even under JB Hunt model):
- DC facilities: keep for receiving/processing inbound from Ashley + customer pick-up
- Routing Manager and scheduling function: either keep internal or integrate with JB Hunt's platform
- DC staff for receiving/assembly: retain ~100-150 people (down from 300-340)

### Risks of JB Hunt transition:
1. Customer experience: last impression of the brand is the delivery crew — losing control of that is real
2. JB Hunt coverage: do they cover all 1915 South's rural/secondary markets well? FL panhandle, Biloxi MS, etc.
3. Employee relations: transitioning 120-160 delivery employees is complex — this is a Southern community employer
4. Volume pricing: at 29 stores, 1915 South may not have the leverage for best JB Hunt rates

### Staging recommendation (from research):
Phase 1: Pilot JB Hunt in 1-2 markets where DC is weakest or M&A target geography has no existing DC
Phase 2: Evaluate after 6 months; if customer scores hold, expand to full network
The Zeidan/AFHSVA acquisition (8-9 stores in VA/Roanoke market) is a natural pilot — 1915 South has no DC there, would need to build one or use JB Hunt. **JB Hunt as the default for new acquisitions = smart staging.**
