## Before the call — Brady's decisions

Brady must arrive at this call with answers to these three questions. Don't improvise them.

If yes: this call is an offer negotiation. Panda Kang engagement either pauses or ends.
If no: Brady is interested in an advisory-to-hire path (90-day paid evaluation that could convert to full-time). This is an honest answer, not a dodge — and it lets Brady keep optionality with Panda.

**2. What comp range am I targeting?**

See SFDR-012 for benchmarks. Expected range for this scope: $250-380K base depending on how much equity/performance comp is in the structure. Know your walk-away number. Don't name it first — but don't leave the call without a range surfaced.

If Justin hires both, the scope division is probably: Brady owns CFO + CAIO + operating system; she owns Corp Dev + M&A + integration. That's a clean split. Brady should be prepared to say: "I can see how that works. ## 5 Questions — Phase 3

**1. When you say "Applied AI Roadmap" in the CFO scope — what does that mean to you practically? Is that Brady owning the Zapsight relationship, or is that Brady building something separate on top of it?**

Why: SFDR-001 is answered but Brady needs Justin's language for it, not Brady's. This also sets up the Zapsight-as-vendor framing cleanly.

Listen for: "owning the relationship" (Brady is decision layer above) vs. "building something new" (Brady is architect). Either is fine, but the framing determines how Brady talks about Zapsight publicly.

---

**2. The GE woman — have you talked to her about this specific scope yet, or are you still figuring out the two-role picture?**

Why: Brady needs to know if there's already a scope agreement with her or if Justin is designing it in real-time. If she's already committed to a scope, Brady's scope is whatever's left. If Justin hasn't had that conversation yet, Brady and Justin can design it together.

Listen for: "we've been talking generally" vs. "she's close to a decision." The more committed she is to a specific scope, the more Brady needs to be clear about what he owns.

---

**3. What does "entity design and structure" actually look like in your day-to-day right now — are those decisions being made by Russell, the current CFO, outside counsel, or nobody?**

Why: This is the sleeper scope item. Aviation, trust, real estate, new ventures — if those decisions are currently being made by Russell alone or not at all, Brady's role in that area could be significant. If outside counsel is running it, Brady's role is coordination and strategic input.

Listen for: Russell doing it himself (means Brady needs to build trust with Russell faster), outside counsel running it (means Brady's role is integrator + owner of the operating model), nobody doing it well (means Brady can show wins fast).

---

**4. The 3-month CFO removal timeline — does that assume I start after the removal, or is there a period where both of us are in the building?**

Why: This is the practical handover question. Brady needs to know whether he's inheriting a clean break or a messy transition. Also tells Brady how much time he has before the other company's Monday deadline matters.

Listen for: "I want you there for the overlap" (messy, but shows Justin wants Brady's imprint on the transition) vs. "you start fresh after he's out" (cleaner but Brady is flying blind on some institutional knowledge).

---

**5. You mentioned discussing this with Russell today — how did that go?**

Why: The most important signal in this call. Justin's answer will tell Brady whether this is a Justin idea (requires selling Russell) or a company decision (Russell is ready to meet Brady). Don't skip this question.

Listen for: "Russell's on board" → move toward a Russell conversation fast. "Russell wants to meet you before deciding" → that's Phase 4, and Brady should offer to set it up. "Russell has questions" → what are the questions? Those are Brady's prep items.

- Outsourcing last-mile to JB Hunt = net ~$2-3M/year savings + $5-8M in freed truck capital; primary value is capital liberation and operational simplicity, not pure P&L lift

Brady should surface this with exactly one question — don't deliver a presentation:

Because I think the Roanoke market — if the Zeidan conversation goes anywhere — might be the cleanest place to test it. No existing DC there, no legacy operations to unwind. Why this works:

If Justin lights up on this topic: that's the thread to pull. The JB Hunt transition is a multi-year program and it needs an owner. Brady could be that owner as part of the CFO/CAIO scope — or the VP Distribution is specifically hired to run it under Brady's oversight.
