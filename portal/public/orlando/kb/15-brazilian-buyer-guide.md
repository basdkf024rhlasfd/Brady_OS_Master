# Brazilian Buyer Guide — Orlando Metro

End-to-end guide for Brazilian nationals purchasing real estate in Orlando. Covers visa eligibility, financing, taxes, currency logistics, STR zoning, and community geography.

---

## Overview & Scale

- **Brazil is the #1 international buyer group** in Orlando — estimated $1.6B/year in transactions
- **55% of Brazilian buyers in the US choose Florida** (NAR International Transactions Report 2025)
- Orlando metro has ~66,000 Brazilian residents (Census-based), ~190,000 per Brazilian Embassy estimates
- 100+ Brazilian companies operate 115+ branch locations in Florida
- Four buyer segments: STR investor, relocating family, second/vacation home, HNW diversification

### Why Brazilians Choose Orlando
- No state income tax
- Relative affordability vs. Miami, NYC, Boston ($400K buys a 4BR pool home)
- Disney/Universal brand recognition — familiar from childhood
- Established Brazilian community infrastructure (grocery, churches, schools with Portuguese speakers)
- Tourism provides STR income opportunity
- Direct flights: Azul (VCP-MCO), LATAM (GRU-MCO), GOL (seasonal)

## Full Cost Stack — Moving Money from Brazil

The true cost of purchasing a $400K Orlando property from Brazil is **not** $400K. Currency conversion adds 3.6–9% overhead before you touch the property.

### Cost Comparison: $400K Purchase

| Cost Component | Bank Wire (Banco do Brasil, Itau) | Wise / Remessa Online |
|----------------|----------------------------------|----------------------|
| IOF tax (Imposto sobre Operacoes Financeiras) | 1.1% ($4,400) | 1.1% ($4,400) |
| FX spread over PTAX mid-rate | 2.5–4.0% ($10,000–$16,000) | 0.5–1.5% ($2,000–$6,000) |
| Wire/transfer fee | $40–$80 | $0–$20 |
| Receiving bank fee (US side) | $15–$25 | $0 |
| **Total FX overhead** | **$14,500–$20,500 (3.6–5.1%)** | **$6,400–$10,400 (1.6–2.6%)** |
| Plus US closing costs (2.5–3.5%) | $10,000–$14,000 | $10,000–$14,000 |
| **Total cost to close** | **$424,500–$434,500** | **$416,400–$424,400** |

- **IOF is unavoidable** — Brazilian federal tax on foreign exchange operations
- IOF rate: 1.1% for property purchases/investments, 0.38% for transfers to own account abroad, up to 3.5% for credit card transactions
- **Banco Central reporting**: Transfers over $10,000 require Declaracao de Capitais Brasileiros no Exterior (DCBE/CBE)
- **FinCEN/BOI**: US Corporate Transparency Act requires beneficial ownership reporting for LLCs

### Recommendation
Use Wise or Remessa Online for transfers. A $400K purchase saves ~$8,000–$10,000 vs. traditional bank wire. Split into multiple transfers if needed (FinCEN CTR threshold: $10,000 cash — wire transfers have no threshold but are reported).

## Tax Obligations — No US-Brazil Tax Treaty

**Critical**: The US and Brazil have **no bilateral tax treaty**. This means no automatic relief from double taxation. Brazilian buyers must file in both countries and use unilateral credits to offset.

### Side-by-Side Tax Obligations

| Obligation | US (IRS) | Brazil (Receita Federal) |
|-----------|----------|--------------------------|
| Rental income reporting | Form 1040-NR | Carne-Leao (monthly), IRPF (annual) |
| Tax on net rental income | 10–37% graduated (NRA with ECI election) | 7.5–27.5% graduated |
| FIRPTA withholding at sale | 15% of gross price (10% if ≤$1M + buyer residence) | N/A |
| Capital gains at sale | 15–20% on net gain | 15% (GCAP program) |
| Estate tax on US property | 40% above $60,000 NRA exemption | Varies by state (ITCMD 4–8%) |
| Double taxation relief | Foreign tax credit (Form 1116) | Compensacao via IRPF (limited) |

### Worked Example: $30K/Year Net Rental Income (NRA)

| Step | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Gross rental income | $48,000 |
| Less: expenses (management, insurance, taxes, maintenance) | -$18,000 |
| Net rental income (US) | $30,000 |
| US federal tax (NRA, ECI election, ~15% effective) | $4,500 |
| Brazilian tax on same income (Carne-Leao, ~15% effective) | $4,500 |
| Less: US tax credit on Brazilian return | -$4,500 |
| **Combined effective tax** | **$4,500–$6,000 (15–20%)** |

The ECI election (treating rental income as Effectively Connected Income) allows expense deductions. Without ECI election, NRAs pay **30% flat on gross rental income** — almost always worse.

### Key Filing Deadlines
- **US**: 1040-NR due April 15 (automatic extension to June 15 for NRAs, Oct 15 with Form 4868)
- **Brazil**: Carne-Leao due monthly (last business day of following month), IRPF due April 30

## Estate Tax Exposure

This is the single biggest financial trap for Brazilian buyers. Most don't know about it until it's too late.

### The Math

| Scenario | US Citizen/Resident | Non-Resident Alien (NRA) |
|----------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| Estate tax exemption | $13.61M (2024) | **$60,000** |
| Tax rate above exemption | 18–40% graduated | 18–40% graduated |
| $500K property, estate tax | $0 | **(500K - 60K) x 40% = $176,000** |
| $800K property, estate tax | $0 | **(800K - 60K) x 40% = $296,000** |

### Mitigation Strategies

| Strategy | Cost | Effectiveness | Notes |
|----------|------|---------------|-------|
| US domestic LLC | $125 filing + $138/year (FL) | **Low** — IRS looks through single-member LLCs | Common mistake; does NOT avoid estate tax alone |
| Foreign corporation | $2,000–$5,000 setup | **Moderate** — complex, may trigger PFIC/CFC rules | Requires ongoing compliance ($3,000–$5,000/year) |
| Irrevocable trust | $5,000–$15,000 setup | **High** — removes asset from estate | Must be properly structured; no control retained |
| Life insurance (ILIT) | $2,000–$8,000/year premium | **High** — covers liability directly | Simplest for most buyers; policy held outside estate |
| Joint ownership (JTWROS) | Free | **Partial** — only shifts 50% | Delays problem, doesn't eliminate it |

### Recommendation
For properties under $500K, a properly structured life insurance policy (ILIT) is usually the most cost-effective solution. For $800K+, consider a foreign corporation or irrevocable trust — consult an international tax attorney.

## STR Zoning Reality Check

Many Brazilian investors are sold the dream of "buy near Disney, put it on Airbnb, make 10%." The reality is more nuanced — and the zoning determines everything.

### STR Status by Area

| Area | STR Allowed? | Key Restriction | Brazilian Buyer Trap |
|------|-------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| City of Orlando | Owner-occupied only (51%+ of year) | Max 4 guests, 1 booking at a time | **NRAs cannot meet owner-occupancy requirement** |
| Unincorporated Orange County | 180-day minimum | Effectively banned for STR | Agents may not mention this — always verify zoning |
| Kissimmee (tourist corridor) | Yes — zoned areas only | Must be in STR-zoned area | Not all Kissimmee is zoned — check parcel specifically |
| Osceola County (unincorp.) | Yes — overlay districts | Tourist Development Tax 6% | Must verify overlay district on GIS map |
| ChampionsGate / Reunion | Yes | Resort community, designed for STR | Highest management fees (25–30%), HOA/CDD $5K–$8K/year |
| Davenport / Four Corners | Yes | STR-zoned communities | Oversupplied — occupancy dropping, revenue declining |
| Seminole County | Mostly restricted | Very limited STR activity | Not a viable STR market |

**Bottom line**: If a Brazilian NRA investor wants to do STR, the only viable areas are **Osceola County STR zones, ChampionsGate/Reunion, and Davenport**. Anything in Orange County or City of Orlando is off-limits for non-resident STR.

## Real Net Yield — Killing the "10% Return" Myth

Brazilian buyers are frequently told they'll earn 8–10% returns on Orlando STR properties. Here's what the numbers actually look like.

### Pro Forma: $450K ChampionsGate 4BR Pool Home (Cash Purchase, NRA)

| Line Item | Annual |
|-----------|--------|
| Gross STR revenue (65% occupancy, $250/night avg) | $59,313 |
| Less: STR management (25%) | -$14,828 |
| Less: cleaning ($200 x 95 turnovers) | -$19,000 |
| Less: utilities (owner-paid, $450/mo) | -$5,400 |
| Less: pool/lawn ($350/mo) | -$4,200 |
| Less: supplies/consumables ($150/mo) | -$1,800 |
| Less: maintenance/repairs ($300/mo) | -$3,600 |
| Less: insurance (STR policy) | -$6,000 |
| Less: property tax (non-homestead, ~1.0%) | -$4,500 |
| Less: CDD | -$3,500 |
| Less: HOA | -$2,400 |
| Less: FL sales + tourist dev tax (12.5% on gross) | -$7,414 |
| Less: STR license + business tax | -$500 |
| **Net Operating Income** | **-$13,829** |
| Less: US federal tax (ECI election, net loss) | $0 |
| Less: FX cost to repatriate (1–2%) | -$0 (nothing to repatriate) |
| **True net cash flow** | **-$13,829** |

- **Gross yield**: 13.2% (this is the number agents quote)
- **Net yield after all expenses**: **-3.1%** (this is reality)
- **Break-even occupancy**: ~78% (vs. market average of 60–70%)

### Where the Returns Actually Come From
The investment thesis for Orlando vacation homes is **not** cash flow — it's:
1. **Appreciation**: 4–6% annual in tourist corridors ($18K–$27K/year on $450K)
2. **Personal use**: 2–4 weeks/year of free vacation (imputed value $5K–$10K)
3. **Currency hedge**: BRL-denominated asset diversification
4. **5-year total return** (appreciation + personal use - operating loss): Potentially positive, but not guaranteed

## Entity Setup Sequence

Brazilian NRAs should follow this order. Starting out of sequence creates delays.

| Step | What | Timeline | Cost |
|------|------|----------|------|
| 1 | **Apply for ITIN** (Form W-7 + tax return or treaty exception) | 7–14 weeks | $0 (free) |
| 2 | **Form Florida LLC** (Sunbiz.org) | 1–3 days | $125 filing fee |
| 3 | **Get EIN** for LLC (IRS Form SS-4, by fax — online not available for foreign-owned) | 2–4 weeks by fax | $0 (free) |
| 4 | **Open US bank account** (requires EIN + LLC docs + passport; Chase, Wells Fargo, or Bank of America) | 1–2 weeks | $0 |
| 5 | **Wire purchase funds** from Brazil | 2–5 business days | IOF + FX spread (see Cost Stack above) |
| 6 | **Close on property** | 30–45 days from contract | Closing costs 2.5–3.5% |

### ITIN Notes
- ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) is required for any NRA who needs to file a US tax return
- Can apply in advance with a 1040-NR "dummy" return (showing no income) or wait until first filing
- **Acceptance agents** (certified by IRS) can verify passports without mailing originals to IRS
- Many Orlando-area CPAs serve as ITIN acceptance agents — search IRS.gov

## Buying Process — Where Brazilian Buyers Differ

The standard Orlando buying process is in `kb/13-buying-process.md`. Here are the steps where Brazilian buyers diverge:

| Step | Standard US Buyer | Brazilian NRA Buyer |
|------|------------------|-------------------|
| Pre-approval | Local lender, 2–3 weeks | Foreign national lender (limited options), 4–6 weeks, OR cash (no pre-approval needed) |
| Down payment | 3.5–20% | 30–40% minimum (foreign national mortgage) or 100% cash |
| Interest rate | 6.5–7.5% | 7.5–9.0% (foreign national premium 0.5–1.5%) |
| ITIN | N/A (has SSN) | Required — apply early, 7–14 weeks |
| Entity | Buy in personal name | Often LLC for liability + estate planning |
| Earnest money source | US bank account | Must wire from Brazil (IOF + FX cost) or from US account if already established |
| Title | Personal name | LLC name (requires EIN, operating agreement) |
| Remote closing | Rare | Common — Power of Attorney (POA) must be apostilled at Brazilian Cartorio |
| Homestead exemption | File by March 1, save ~$500–$1,500/year | **Not eligible** unless FL resident (domiciled + legal status) |

### Power of Attorney (POA) for Remote Closing
1. Attorney drafts POA in English
2. Translate to Portuguese (sworn translator)
3. Execute at Brazilian Cartorio (notary)
4. Obtain Apostille from Cartorio (Hague Convention)
5. Send original apostilled POA to closing agent in Orlando
6. Timeline: 2–4 weeks, $200–$500 total cost

## Insurance for Absentee Owners

Brazilian NRAs purchasing investment properties face higher insurance costs than owner-occupants.

| Coverage Type | Annual Cost Range | Notes |
|--------------|------------------|-------|
| Owner-occupied homeowners | $2,275–$4,600 | Standard Florida rates |
| **Non-owner-occupied (LTR)** | **$4,000–$7,000** | 50–80% premium over owner-occupied |
| **STR / vacation rental policy** | **$5,000–$8,000** | Requires specialized carrier; liability coverage critical |
| Flood (if in flood zone) | $700–$3,000 | Required with federally-backed mortgage; optional but recommended for cash buyers |
| Umbrella liability | $300–$600 | Recommended for all rental properties |

- Brazilian buyers unfamiliar with US insurance should know: **there is no Brazilian equivalent** of the Florida insurance landscape — premiums, deductibles, hurricane deductibles, and claims processes are all different
- Hurricane deductible is typically 2–5% of dwelling coverage (on a $450K home, that's $9,000–$22,500 out of pocket before insurance pays)

## Property Management

| Management Type | Fee Range | What's Included |
|----------------|-----------|-----------------|
| Long-term rental (LTR) | 8–10% of collected rent | Tenant placement, maintenance coordination, rent collection |
| Short-term rental (STR) | 20–30% of gross revenue | Listing management, guest communication, cleaning coordination, supplies |

### Red Flags in Property Managers
- No clear fee breakdown (hidden charges for maintenance markup, lease renewal, etc.)
- Unwillingness to share occupancy data or financials monthly
- No vacation rental license in their own name
- Promises of >70% occupancy without market data to back it
- "We handle everything" with no written management agreement

### What Brazilian Owners Need
- **Portuguese-speaking contact** — not required but makes problem resolution easier
- **Monthly financial reporting** with P&L (not just a rent check)
- **Tax document support** — 1099 issuance, expense categorization for 1040-NR
- **Insurance claims assistance** — coordinating with carrier, contractor access
- **Annual property inspection** with photos/video (for absentee owners)

## Top 10 Mistakes Brazilian Buyers Make

1. **Buying in an STR-restricted zone** — Orange County and City of Orlando effectively ban non-resident STR. Verify zoning before offering.
2. **Using bank wire instead of Wise/Remessa Online** — Costs $8,000–$10,000 extra on a $400K purchase.
3. **No ITIN before closing** — Delays closing by weeks. Apply as soon as you decide to buy.
4. **Ignoring estate tax** — $60K NRA exemption means a $500K property creates $176K exposure. Get insurance or structure properly.
5. **Expecting 10% cash returns** — Gross yields of 10–13% collapse to 0–3% (or negative) after all expenses. Buy for appreciation + personal use, not cash flow.
6. **Single-member LLC for "estate tax protection"** — IRS disregards single-member LLCs. Does not avoid estate tax.
7. **Not filing Carne-Leao monthly** — Receita Federal audits are increasing on Brazilian-owned foreign assets.
8. **Skipping CBE/DCBE filing** — Mandatory for foreign assets over certain thresholds. Penalties are severe.
9. **Buying sight-unseen based on agent's photos** — Always inspect or hire a buyer's inspector. Agent has commission incentive.
10. **No property management plan** — Managing an STR from Brazil via WhatsApp does not work. Budget 20–30% management fee into your pro forma.
