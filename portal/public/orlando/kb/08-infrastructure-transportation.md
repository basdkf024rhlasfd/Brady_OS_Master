# Infrastructure & Transportation

How people move around Orlando and the projects shaping future growth.

---

## Current Transportation Network

### Expressway System
Orlando has an extensive toll road network (one of the most tolled metro areas in the US):

| Road | Route | Key Connections |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| **I-4** | Tampa ↔ Daytona Beach | Primary east-west spine through metro |
| **SR 408 (East-West Expy)** | West Orlando ↔ East Orlando | Downtown Orlando bypass |
| **SR 417 (Central FL GreeneWay)** | Apopka ↔ Kissimmee | Eastern beltway loop |
| **SR 528 (BeachLine)** | I-4 ↔ Cocoa Beach | Airport connector, coast access |
| **SR 429 (Western Beltway)** | Apopka ↔ Four Corners | Western bypass, Horizon West access |
| **SR 414 (John Land Apopka)** | Apopka ↔ I-4 | North Orlando connector |
| **Florida Turnpike** | Miami ↔ I-75 | North-south toll corridor |
| **US-27** | Clermont ↔ Haines City | Lake/Polk county access |

### Toll Costs
- Daily commuter toll costs can range from **$5-$15/day** depending on route
- SunPass or E-Pass transponders required (cash tolls mostly eliminated)
- Toll costs should be factored into housing decisions — cheaper home in Clermont may cost $200+/month in tolls

### Commute Patterns
| From → To | Distance | Typical Commute Time |
|-----------|----------|---------------------|
| Horizon West → Downtown | 25 mi | 35-55 min |
| Lake Nona → Downtown | 20 mi | 25-40 min |
| Clermont → Downtown | 35 mi | 45-75 min |
| Winter Park → Downtown | 5 mi | 10-20 min |
| Kissimmee → Downtown | 20 mi | 25-45 min |
| Sanford → Downtown | 25 mi | 30-50 min |
| Windermere → Downtown | 15 mi | 20-40 min |

### Traffic Reality
- I-4 is consistently ranked among the **most dangerous highways** in the US
- Rush hour: 7-9 AM and 4-7 PM — I-4 is brutal
- Tourist traffic adds unpredictable congestion (I-Drive, US-192, I-4 near Disney)
- Orlando is fundamentally a **car-dependent city** — public transit is limited

## Rail Transit

### SunRail (Commuter Rail)
- **Opened**: 2014
- **Route**: Debary (Volusia County) → Poinciana (Osceola County)
- **Stations**: 16 stations along 49-mile route
- **Key stations**: Sand Lake Rd, Church St (Downtown), Maitland, Altamonte Springs, Sanford, Lake Mary
- **Frequency**: Every 30 min during peak, limited midday/weekend service
- **Fare**: $2-$5 per trip depending on zones
- **Ridership**: Growing but still modest (~4,000-6,000 daily riders)
- **Impact on RE**: Properties near SunRail stations have seen **5-15% premium** over comparable homes

### SunRail Expansion — "Sunshine Corridor"
- **Major planned expansion** connecting airport, Convention Center, I-Drive, Disney Springs
- Agreement in principle between SunRail and Brightline for shared corridor
- Projected cost: **$4 billion**
- Would connect 125,000 jobs in airport/I-Drive area
- Could boost ridership to **9.4 million passengers by 2040**
- Transformative for real estate along the corridor
- Timeline: Still in planning/engineering phase

### Brightline (High-Speed Rail)
- **Current service**: Miami ↔ Orlando International Airport (opened 2023)
- **Travel time**: ~3.5 hours Miami to Orlando
- **Orlando station**: Inside MCO airport (Terminal Intermodal Center)
- **Ridership**: Growing, economic impact exceeding $6.4 billion
- **Tampa extension (Phase 3)**:
  - Route: Along I-4 median
  - $15.875 million federal grant for preliminary engineering
  - Timeline: Under development — projected completion late 2020s
  - Would make Tampa-Orlando commuting viable
  - **RE impact**: Could boost demand in both metros and along corridor

## Orlando International Airport (MCO)

### Current State
- **Passengers**: 57+ million annually (2024) — one of busiest US airports
- **Terminal C**: Brand new terminal opened 2022 ($2.8B project)
  - Designed for international flights
  - Brightline station integrated
  - 15 new gates
- **Airlines**: 45+ carriers, extensive international service
- **Expansion**: Continuous growth, south terminal development planned

### Impact on Real Estate
- Airport proximity drives demand in Lake Nona, Meadow Woods, Narcoossee corridor
- Flight path noise affects some neighborhoods (Belle Isle, parts of Meadow Woods)
- Airport employment: 35,000+ direct jobs
- International connectivity supports foreign buyer market
- Cargo operations growing — logistics/warehouse demand near airport

## Road Projects & Infrastructure

### I-4 Ultimate (Completed 2024)
- $2.3 billion reconstruction of 21-mile stretch through Orlando
- Added express toll lanes (variable pricing)
- Improved interchanges at all major crossroads
- **Impact**: Improved commute times but express lanes are expensive ($1-$10+ one way depending on demand)

### Wekiva Parkway / SR 429 Extension
- Completes the western beltway loop
- Connects Apopka area to Mount Dora / Sanford
- **Impact**: Opens up northwest Orange County and south Lake County for development
- Driving growth in Apopka, Mount Dora, Sorrento

### Horizon West Infrastructure
- **Horizon West Connector**: New road connecting Horizon West to Western Beltway
- Multiple road widenings in progress
- Infrastructure lagging behind residential development
- **Impact**: Traffic improvements critical for home values in area

### Other Key Projects
- **Narcoossee Road widening**: Supporting Lake Nona / Sunbridge growth
- **US-192 improvements**: Kissimmee corridor upgrades
- **Colonial Drive (SR-50) improvements**: East-west corridor upgrades
- **John Young Parkway extension**: South Orange County connectivity

## Growth Corridors

### Corridor 1: Lake Nona → Sunbridge (Southeast)
- **Direction**: Southeast from Orlando toward St. Cloud
- **Driver**: Medical City, Tavistock Development, VA Hospital
- **Status**: Active buildout, 30-year development horizon
- **RE impact**: Premium appreciation, long-term play
- **Infrastructure**: Narcoossee Road widening, new interchanges planned

### Corridor 2: Horizon West (West)
- **Direction**: West from Orlando toward Clermont
- **Driver**: Population growth, affordability relative to core
- **Status**: Massive buildout, 50,000+ homes planned
- **RE impact**: Volume market — lots of inventory, moderate appreciation
- **Infrastructure**: Horizon West Connector, SR 429 access

### Corridor 3: International Drive Transformation
- **Direction**: Central tourist corridor
- **Driver**: Epic Universe, Convention Center expansion, I-Drive 2040 plan
- **Status**: Major commercial and hospitality development
- **RE impact**: Commercial RE boom, workforce housing demand
- **Infrastructure**: SunRail extension, pedestrian improvements, transit study

### Corridor 4: Creative Village / Downtown
- **Direction**: Downtown Orlando core
- **Driver**: UCF downtown campus, mixed-use development, urban revitalization
- **Status**: 68-acre development in Parramore — academic, residential, commercial
- **RE impact**: Downtown condo/apartment demand, neighborhood transformation
- **Infrastructure**: SunRail Church St station, pedestrian improvements

### Corridor 5: Sanford / Lake Mary (North)
- **Direction**: North along I-4 into Seminole/Volusia counties
- **Driver**: Corporate offices, SunRail terminus, Sanford airport
- **Status**: Steady suburban growth, tech corridor emerging
- **RE impact**: Good value, improving appreciation
- **Infrastructure**: SunRail service, I-4 express lanes

### Corridor 6: Clermont / South Lake County (West)
- **Direction**: West past Horizon West into Lake County
- **Driver**: Affordability, outdoor recreation, Turnpike access
- **Status**: Rapid suburban growth, mostly new construction
- **RE impact**: Affordable entry, strong growth, long commutes
- **Infrastructure**: FL Turnpike, US-27, limited transit

### Corridor 7: Osceola Innovation District / NeoCity
- **Direction**: South into Osceola County
- **Driver**: Semiconductor/advanced manufacturing (BRIDG facility), innovation hub
- **Status**: Long-term growth play, still early
- **RE impact**: Could transform Kissimmee corridor long-term
- **Infrastructure**: Dedicated innovation district planning

## Utilities & Services

### Water & Sewer
- **Orlando Utilities Commission (OUC)**: Serves city of Orlando and parts of Orange County
- **Toho Water Authority**: Serves Osceola County
- **Seminole County Utilities**: Serves Seminole County
- **Well water**: Still common in some rural/semi-rural areas (Apopka, Geneva, parts of Lake County)
- Reclaimed water widely available for irrigation (separate meter, lower cost)

### Electric
- **OUC**: City of Orlando
- **Duke Energy**: Most of Orange, Seminole, Osceola counties
- **Average electric bill**: $150-$300/month (AC is major cost in summer)
- Solar panels increasingly common — net metering available

### Internet
- **Spectrum (Charter)**: Primary cable internet provider
- **AT&T Fiber**: Available in many areas, expanding
- **Lake Nona**: Gigabit fiber community-wide
- **Brightline/fiber**: Expanding fiber infrastructure
- **Starlink**: Popular in rural Lake County areas
