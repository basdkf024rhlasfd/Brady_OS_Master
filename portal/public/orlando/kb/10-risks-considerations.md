# Risks & Considerations

What every buyer, seller, and investor should understand about the risks of Orlando real estate.

---

## Natural Hazards

### Hurricane Risk
- **Historical frequency**: 3 direct hits since 1974 (Charley 2004, Frances 2004, Irma 2017)
- **Orlando advantage**: ~40 miles inland reduces storm surge risk to zero
- **Primary risks**: Wind damage (60-100+ mph gusts possible), heavy rain flooding, tornadoes, power outages
- **Building code**: Post-Hurricane Andrew (1992) Florida Building Code is among the strictest in the US
  - Homes built after 2002 have significantly better hurricane resistance
  - Impact-rated windows, reinforced roof connections, concrete block construction
- **Older homes** (pre-2002): May lack hurricane-rated features — factor retrofit costs ($10K-$50K for impact windows/doors)
- **Power outages**: Can last 1-7+ days after major storms. Generator or battery backup recommended.
- **Preparation**: Hurricane shutters, impact windows, generator, 7-day supply kit
- **Insurance**: Hurricane deductible is separate from standard deductible (typically 2-5% of dwelling coverage)

### Flood Risk
- **FEMA flood zones** exist throughout Orlando, particularly near lakes
- **Zone AE/A**: High-risk flood areas (100-year flood plain) — flood insurance REQUIRED with federally backed mortgage
- **Zone X**: Moderate-to-low risk — flood insurance recommended but not required
- **Orlando has 100+ lakes** — many homes are near water with elevation concerns
- **Post-Hurricane Ian (2022)**: Lakes in Central Florida rose dramatically; homes in flood plains experienced first-floor flooding
- **Always check**: FEMA flood maps at msc.fema.gov before purchasing
- **Flood insurance cost**: $700-$3,000/year in Zone AE; $300-$500/year in Zone X (preferred)
- **New development**: Generally built at higher elevations but still check lot-specific grading

### Sinkhole Risk
- Central Florida sits on **limestone karst geology** — sinkholes are a real concern
- **Most common areas in Orlando metro**: West Orange County, parts of Apopka, some Winter Park areas
- **Sinkhole vs. settling**: Minor ground settlement is common; catastrophic sinkholes are rare but do happen
- **Insurance**: Standard policies cover "catastrophic ground cover collapse" but NOT gradual sinkhole damage
  - Full sinkhole coverage available as endorsement ($500-$2,000/year additional)
  - Not all carriers offer it
- **Due diligence**: Ask for sinkhole activity reports for the area; check FGS (Florida Geological Survey) database
- **Signs of sinkhole**: Cracks in walls/foundation, doors/windows not closing properly, depressions in yard, circular patterns in vegetation

### Lightning
- Central Florida is the **lightning capital of the US**
- Orlando averages 80+ lightning days per year
- **Impact on RE**: Surge protectors, lightning rods, and proper grounding recommended
- Home insurance covers lightning damage but frequent claims can raise premiums

## Insurance Crisis

### The Situation (2020-2026)
- Florida homeowners insurance is the **most expensive in the nation**
- Average Florida premium: $4,200-$4,600/year (2-3x national average)
- **Root causes**:
  - Hurricane exposure and reinsurance costs
  - Litigation abuse (Florida had 8% of US claims but 76% of insurance litigation)
  - Roof replacement lawsuits (contractors soliciting homeowners to file claims)
  - Insurer insolvencies: 6+ carriers went insolvent 2020-2023
  - Fraud: Inflated claims, organized fraud rings

### Legislative Reforms
- **SB 2-A (2022)**: Major litigation reform — eliminated one-way attorney fees, assignment of benefits restrictions
- **SB 7052 (2023)**: Additional reforms, Citizens depopulation incentives
- **Impact**: Reforms are slowly working — rate increases flattening, new carriers entering market
- **Citizens Insurance** reducing policy count from 1.4M to under 1M

### What This Means for Buyers
- **Budget $3,000-$6,000/year** for insurance on typical Orlando home
- **Get insurance quotes BEFORE** making an offer — some homes are uninsurable or very expensive
- **Roof age matters enormously**: Insurers may deny coverage for roofs >15 years old
  - Roof replacement: $8,000-$25,000 depending on size and material
  - Factor this into purchase negotiations
- **Wind mitigation inspection** ($75-$150): Can save $500-$2,000/year on premiums
- Shop multiple carriers — pricing varies dramatically
- Consider higher deductibles to reduce premiums

### Condo Insurance Crisis
- Particularly acute for older condos (20+ years)
- SB 4-D (post-Surfside) requires structural inspections and fully funded reserves
- Special assessments of $10,000-$100,000+ per unit for deferred maintenance
- Some older condos becoming effectively unmarketable
- **Due diligence**: Request condo association financials, reserve study, pending assessments, insurance policy details

## Market Risks

### Oversupply Concerns
- **New construction oversupply**: Some submarkets (Horizon West, Clermont, Davenport) have very high new inventory
- **Apartment oversupply**: 15,000+ new apartment units delivered 2023-2025, pushing up vacancy rates
- **Condo oversupply**: Downtown and I-Drive corridor have significant condo inventory
- **Vacation home oversupply**: Osceola County resort communities are heavily saturated
- **Watch**: If population growth slows while construction continues, prices could decline

### Affordability Squeeze
- Median home price ($400K) requires ~$115K+ household income
- Orlando median household income: ~$65K — significant gap
- If wages don't keep pace with prices, demand could soften
- Already seeing price declines in some segments (Celebration -10% YoY)
- First-time buyers increasingly priced out of many submarkets

### Interest Rate Sensitivity
- Orlando's market is particularly rate-sensitive because of affordability constraints
- At 7% rates: $400K home = ~$2,130/month P&I
- At 5% rates: Same home = ~$1,720/month P&I (saving $410/month)
- Rate decreases could reinvigorate demand; rate increases could further slow market
- Many buyers waiting on sidelines for rate relief

### Tourism Dependency
- Tourism directly and indirectly supports ~450,000 jobs
- Economic downturn reducing travel could impact housing demand
- However: Orlando has diversified significantly (medical, tech, defense, education)
- Epic Universe adds diversification within tourism (Universal vs. Disney)
- Convention business provides more recession-resistant tourism segment

### Climate Risk Perception
- Even though Orlando is inland, Florida's overall climate narrative affects the market
- National media coverage of hurricanes, flooding, and insurance crisis can cool demand
- Could affect appreciation rates long-term as buyers factor in climate risk
- Insurance costs are the most tangible impact of climate concerns

## Legal & Regulatory Risks

### HOA/Condo Reform
- **SB 4-D compliance**: Driving massive cost increases at older condos
- Future legislative changes could further impact ownership costs
- HOA reform bills introduced regularly — could affect investor rights, rental restrictions

### STR Regulation Changes
- Short-term rental regulations continue evolving
- State preemption protects existing rights but local governments pushing boundaries
- If regulations tighten in Osceola County, entire vacation home investment thesis changes
- Always verify current zoning and regulations before purchasing for STR

### FIRPTA and International Tax Changes
- International buyer demand sensitive to FIRPTA rules and home-country tax treaties
- Changes to FIRPTA could reduce international demand (significant for Orlando)

### Environmental Regulations
- Wetland protection can limit development (impacting land supply)
- Water management district regulations affect stormwater/drainage
- Endangered species considerations in some development areas

## Due Diligence Checklist

### Before Making an Offer
- [ ] Check FEMA flood zone (msc.fema.gov)
- [ ] Get homeowner insurance quotes
- [ ] Research sinkhole history for area
- [ ] Verify school zone assignments
- [ ] Check HOA/CDD fees and financial health
- [ ] Research STR rules (if buying for investment)
- [ ] Review property tax history and exemptions
- [ ] Check for special assessments (condos)
- [ ] Verify roof age (critical for insurance)
- [ ] Research neighborhood crime statistics
- [ ] Check flight path (if near MCO)
- [ ] Research future development plans nearby (Orange County Zoning Atlas)
- [ ] Verify utility providers and costs
- [ ] Check for pending road projects that could affect property

### After Contract (Inspection Period)
- [ ] Full home inspection by licensed FL inspector
- [ ] Wind mitigation inspection (for insurance savings)
- [ ] 4-point inspection (if home >30 years for insurance eligibility)
- [ ] WDO (termite/wood-destroying organism) inspection
- [ ] Sinkhole evaluation (if in sinkhole-prone area)
- [ ] Pool inspection (if applicable)
- [ ] Roof inspection (especially if >10 years old)
- [ ] Survey (verify boundaries, setbacks, encroachments)
- [ ] Title search (verify clean title, no liens)
- [ ] Review HOA/condo documents thoroughly
- [ ] Verify HVAC age and condition (AC is critical in Florida)
