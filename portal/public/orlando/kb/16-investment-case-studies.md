# Investment Case Studies — Orlando Metro

Five worked pro formas with real numbers derived from current market data. Each shows acquisition, financing, annual operating costs, year 1 cash flow, 5-year projection, and a risk scenario.

> **Note on cap rates**: This file distinguishes **gross yield** (annual rent / purchase price) from **cap rate** (NOI / purchase price). Many Orlando agents use these interchangeably — they are not the same. At 7%+ interest rates, most financed cases show negative year 1 cash flow. This is accurate. The 5-year total return (appreciation + principal paydown + rent growth) tells the real story.

Source data: `kb/06-investment-rentals.md` for rents, cap rates, and STR revenue. `kb/03-taxes-insurance-costs.md` for taxes and insurance.

- Cash buyers fare better (no $25K/year mortgage payment), but still only break even after expenses

- MetroWest's proximity to Universal/I-Drive ensures strong room rental demand
- **Not eligible for NRAs** — requires owner-occupancy and legal residency

### Risk Scenario: Oversupply + Occupancy Drop to 50%

Revenue drops to $43,800. NOI becomes -$34,956/year. At this point the property is a pure cash drain. Exit triggers FIRPTA (15% of $420K = $63,000 withheld, refunded partially 6–12 months later).

### Key Takeaways
- **Cash purchase eliminates debt service but does NOT make this profitable** on a cash flow basis
- The real returns come from **appreciation in USD** and **BRL depreciation** — a currency play, not an income investment
- Total compliance cost (US CPA + Brazilian CPA + life insurance + LLC) is ~$6,500/year — often overlooked in pro formas
- **If BRL depreciates against USD** (as it has historically), the investment looks much better in BRL terms
- FIRPTA at exit withholds 15% of gross price — significant cash flow impact on sale
- This is a **wealth preservation and diversification** strategy, not a cash flow strategy
