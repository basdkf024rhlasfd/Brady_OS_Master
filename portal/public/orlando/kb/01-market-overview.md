# Orlando Real Estate Market Overview

## Metro Area Definition

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA) spans four counties:
- **Orange County** (Orlando, Winter Park, Winter Garden, Windermere)
- **Seminole County** (Sanford, Lake Mary, Altamonte Springs, Oviedo)
- **Osceola County** (Kissimmee, St. Cloud, Celebration, Poinciana)
- **Lake County** (Clermont, Leesburg, Mount Dora) — partially included

Metro population: ~2.94 million (2024), making it the 22nd largest metro in the US.

## Current Market Statistics (2025-2026)

### Median Home Prices
- **Orlando metro median sale price**: ~$400,000 (early 2026)
- **Single-family homes**: $392,000-$427,500 depending on month/source
- **Condos/townhomes**: $250,000-$320,000
- **Year-over-year change**: -2.4% to +3.0% depending on period measured
- **Orlando median is ~3% below the national average**

### Price Per Square Foot
- Metro average: ~$220-$240/sqft
- Premium areas (Winter Park, Windermere): $300-$500+/sqft
- Affordable areas (Kissimmee, Poinciana): $160-$200/sqft

### Inventory & Supply
- **Active listings (Jan 2026)**: ~11,741 homes
- **Months of supply**: 7.19 months (Jan 2026) — up dramatically from pandemic lows of <1 month
- **Inventory growth**: +146% year-over-year (Jan 2026 vs Jan 2025)
- **New listings**: 597 new homes in January 2026 alone (+92% YoY)
- Highest inventory level since July 2015

### Days on Market
- **Average days on market**: 64 days (2025)
- Up significantly from pandemic-era averages of 7-14 days
- Luxury properties ($1M+): 80-120+ days
- Entry-level (<$350K): 30-45 days

### List-to-Sale Price Ratio
- Currently ~96-97%, meaning buyers are negotiating 3-4% below asking
- Down from 100%+ during the 2021-2022 frenzy
- Indicates a balanced-to-buyer-favorable market

### 5-Year Trajectory
| Year | Median Price | YoY Change |
|------|-------------|------------|
| 2020 | ~$270,000 | +6% |
| 2021 | ~$320,000 | +19% |
| 2022 | ~$390,000 | +22% |
| 2023 | ~$400,000 | +3% |
| 2024 | ~$405,000 | +1% |
| 2025 | ~$400,000 | -1% to +3% |

### Key Takeaways
- Prices roughly doubled from 2015-2023
- The market has plateaued/slightly corrected since mid-2023
- No crash — just normalization after pandemic frenzy
- 2025 was described as "a year of normalization" by ORRA president
- Buyers now have more time, options, and negotiating room

## Market Segmentation

### Entry-Level / Starter Homes ($200K-$350K)
- Locations: Kissimmee, Poinciana, east Orlando, parts of Sanford/Apopka
- Property types: Older single-family, townhomes, condos
- Demand: Still strong from first-time buyers and investors
- Competition: Moderate — these move faster than luxury
- Typical buyer: Young professionals, first-time buyers, service industry workers

### Mid-Range ($350K-$600K)
- Locations: Horizon West, Clermont, St. Cloud, Winter Garden, Lake Nona townhomes
- Property types: Newer single-family homes, master-planned communities
- This is where the bulk of new construction is priced
- Typical buyer: Growing families, move-up buyers, remote workers

### Upper Mid-Range ($600K-$1M)
- Locations: Winter Park, Baldwin Park, Lake Nona single-family, Windermere (entry)
- Property types: Newer construction with upgrades, established neighborhoods
- Market: More inventory, longer days on market
- Typical buyer: Dual-income professionals, executives, relocating families

### Luxury ($1M+)
- Locations: Windermere, Winter Park estates, Lake Nona estates, Isleworth, Golden Oak
- Median luxury sale price: $1.37M (April 2025), up 7.4% YoY
- Market: Rising prices but lower sales volume — affluent buyers cautious
- Typical buyer: Executives, athletes, international wealth, remote tech workers

## Affordability

### Income Requirements
- To afford the median home (~$400K) with 20% down at 7% rate:
  - Monthly payment: ~$2,130 (P&I only)
  - With taxes/insurance: ~$3,200-$3,500/month
  - Required household income: ~$115,000-$130,000/year
- Orlando median household income: ~$62,000-$68,000
- **Significant affordability gap** — median household cannot afford median home

### Orlando vs. Other Florida Metros
- Orlando is more affordable than Miami (~$600K median), Tampa (~$420K), and Naples ($700K+)
- More expensive than Jacksonville (~$350K) and parts of the Panhandle
- No state income tax provides effective 4-13% raise vs. states with income tax

### First-Time Buyer Reality
- Typical starter home: $280K-$350K
- Required down payment (3.5% FHA): $10,000-$12,250
- Monthly payment with FHA: $2,200-$2,800 (including PMI, taxes, insurance)
- Down payment assistance available (see financing section)

## Foreclosure & Distressed Market

- Foreclosure rates in Orlando are near historical lows
- Florida is a judicial foreclosure state — process takes 6-12+ months
- Very limited REO (bank-owned) inventory
- Short sales are essentially nonexistent in current market
- Some distress emerging in condo market due to insurance/HOA cost increases
- Watch: Older condos facing special assessments for building safety compliance (SB 4-D)

## Market Outlook

### Bull Case
- Population growth of 76,000/year sustains demand
- Epic Universe opening (2025) creates 17,500+ jobs
- No state income tax continues attracting migration
- Brightline/SunRail expansion enhances connectivity
- Lake Nona Medical City creating high-paying jobs

### Bear Case
- Insurance costs continue rising, eroding affordability
- Inventory buildup could push prices down further
- Remote work slowdown could reduce in-migration
- Rising property taxes on non-homestead properties
- Climate risk perception increasing

### Consensus View
- Modest price appreciation of 1-3% annually expected through 2027
- No crash anticipated — too much population growth
- Market favoring buyers for first time since 2019
- New construction oversupply in some submarkets (Horizon West, Clermont)
