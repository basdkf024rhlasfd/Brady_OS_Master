# Property Taxes, Insurance & Costs

Everything about the ongoing costs of owning property in Orlando.

## Property Taxes

### How Florida Property Tax Works
- Florida has **no state income tax** but relies heavily on property taxes
- Property tax is calculated by multiplying the **taxable value** by the **millage rate**
- Millage rate = tax per $1,000 of taxable value
- Total millage includes county, city, school district, water management, and special districts
- Tax bills are mailed in November, due by March 31
- **Discount for early payment**: 4% in November, 3% in December, 2% in January, 1% in February

### County Tax Rates (Effective Rates)
| County | Effective Rate | Notes |
|--------|---------------|-------|
| Orange County | ~1.01% | Highest in metro, includes city of Orlando millage |
| Seminole County | ~0.88% | Lower than Orange |
| Osceola County | ~0.91% | Plus frequent CDD fees |
| Lake County | ~0.85-0.95% | Varies by municipality |

### Example Tax Calculation (Orange County)
For a $400,000 home with homestead exemption:
- Market value: $400,000
- Less homestead exemption: -$50,000
- Taxable value: $350,000
- Approximate tax at 1.01%: **~$3,535/year**
- Without homestead: **~$4,040/year**

### Non-Homestead Properties (Investment/Second Homes)
- No homestead exemption ($50,000 difference in taxable value)
- No Save Our Homes 3% cap — assessed value can increase to market value annually
- 10% cap on annual assessed value increase for non-homestead properties
- This means investor-owned properties pay significantly more tax over time

## Homestead Exemption

### What It Is
- Available to **Florida residents** who own and occupy a property as their **primary residence** as of **January 1**
- Reduces taxable value by up to **$50,000**
  - First $25,000: applies to ALL taxes (including school district)
  - Additional $25,000: applies to assessed value between $50,000-$75,000, excludes school taxes

### How to Apply
- File with your county Property Appraiser by **March 1**
- Required documents: Florida driver's license/ID, Social Security number, property deed
- Once approved, it auto-renews annually (unless you move or change status)
- Online filing available for all Orlando metro counties

### Save Our Homes (SOH) Cap
- **Constitutional amendment passed in 1992**
- Limits annual increase in assessed value to the **lesser of 3% or CPI**
- Only applies to homesteaded properties
- Creates a growing gap between assessed value and market value over time
- **Example**: A home bought for $200K in 2015 might have a market value of $400K in 2025 but an assessed value of only $280K — saving thousands annually

### Portability
- When you sell a homesteaded property and buy a new one in Florida, you can **transfer your SOH benefit** (up to $500,000)
- Must establish new homestead within **3 years** of losing previous one
- **Upsizing**: Full dollar amount of SOH benefit transfers
- **Downsizing**: Proportional amount transfers (based on ratio of new to old market value)
- Must file **Portability Application (DR-501T)** with your new county's Property Appraiser
- This is one of the most valuable and misunderstood tax benefits in Florida

### Additional Exemptions
- **Senior exemption**: Additional $50,000 for homeowners 65+ with household income under ~$36,000
- **Veteran exemption**: Varies by disability rating (100% disabled = full exemption)
- **Widow/widower exemption**: $5,000
- **Disability exemption**: $5,000-$500+ depending on type

## CDD Fees (Community Development Districts)

### What They Are
- A CDD is a **special-purpose government** created to finance and manage infrastructure
- Developer creates the CDD, issues bonds to build roads, utilities, parks, amenities
- Homeowners repay bonds through annual CDD assessments
- **Not technically a tax**, but appears on your property tax bill and is collected the same way

### Two Components
1. **Bond/Debt Service**: Fixed annual payment toward the original infrastructure bonds (20-30 year term)
2. **Operations & Maintenance (O&M)**: Variable annual cost for maintaining community amenities

### Typical CDD Fee Ranges in Orlando
| Community Type | Annual CDD Fee |
|---------------|---------------|
| Basic new construction | $1,000-$1,500/year |
| Mid-range master-planned | $1,500-$2,500/year |
| Premium master-planned (Lake Nona, Horizon West) | $2,000-$4,000/year |
| Resort/vacation communities | $2,500-$5,000/year |

### Key Things to Know
- CDD bonds typically mature in **20-30 years**, then that portion drops off
- O&M portion can (and usually does) **increase annually**
- CDDs are extremely common in Orlando new construction — rare in older neighborhoods
- Some CDDs can be **paid off early** (prepay the bond portion)
- **CDD fees are NOT tax-deductible** (unlike property taxes)
- Always ask: "What is the CDD fee?" and "Can the bond be prepaid?"

### Communities with Notable CDD Fees
- Horizon West villages: $2,000-$3,500/year
- Lake Nona: $2,500-$4,000/year
- Storey Park: $2,000-$3,000/year
- Sunbridge: $2,000-$3,500/year
- Many Osceola County resort communities: $3,000-$5,000/year

## Homeowners Insurance

### The Florida Insurance Landscape
- Florida has the **most expensive homeowners insurance** in the United States
- Average Florida premium: ~$4,200-$4,600/year for $300K dwelling coverage
- **Orlando specifically**: $2,275-$4,600/year depending on ZIP, coverage, and home characteristics
- Orlando benefits from being inland — cheaper than coastal metros

### Why Insurance Is So Expensive
- Hurricane exposure (even inland)
- Roof replacement costs (Florida requires full roof replacement for claims in many cases)
- Litigation abuse (Florida accounts for ~8% of US homeowners claims but ~76% of litigation)
- Insurer insolvencies (multiple carriers have gone bankrupt since 2020)
- Reinsurance costs passed to consumers

### Recent Trends (2024-2026)
- Insurance costs have **stabilized** after years of steep increases
- 2018-2023: Average rate increase of **42.1%** statewide
- 2024: Rates increased smaller ~14%
- 2025-2026: Rates flattening, some carriers reducing rates
- Legislative reforms (SB 2-A, SB 7052) targeting litigation abuse are slowly working

### Citizens Property Insurance
- Florida's **insurer of last resort** (state-run)
- Historically cheaper but intended as safety net, not primary option
- **Depopulation program**: Actively transferring policies back to private market
  - Private insurer can take your policy if their rate is within **20%** of your Citizens rate
- Citizens policies decreased from 1.4M to under 1M as private market stabilizes

### Insurance Cost by Area
| Area | Relative Cost | Reason |
|------|--------------|--------|
| Downtown Orlando | Lower | Inland, newer construction |
| Winter Park | Moderate | Older homes, large trees |
| Windermere | Higher | Larger homes, higher replacement cost |
| Lake Nona | Lower | Newest construction, hurricane-rated |
| Kissimmee/Osceola | Moderate | Mix of old and new |
| Near lakes/flood zones | Higher | Flood risk, additional flood policy needed |

### Flood Insurance
- **Not included** in standard homeowners policies
- Required if in FEMA **Special Flood Hazard Area (Zone A or AE)** with a federally-backed mortgage
- FEMA flood maps available at msc.fema.gov
- Average flood insurance cost: $700-$3,000/year depending on zone and elevation
- Many Orlando properties near lakes are in flood zones — always check
- Preferred risk policies (outside flood zones): $300-$500/year

### Sinkhole Coverage
- Florida is sinkhole-prone, especially in certain geological areas
- Standard policies must cover **catastrophic ground cover collapse**
- Full **sinkhole coverage** is available as additional endorsement — not all carriers offer it
- Expect $500-$2,000/year additional for sinkhole coverage
- Areas with higher sinkhole risk: west Orange County, parts of Apopka, some Winter Park areas

### Tips to Reduce Insurance Costs
1. **Upgrade roof** — newer roofs (< 10 years) get significantly better rates
2. **Hurricane shutters/impact windows** — discounts of 10-30%
3. **Wind mitigation inspection** — $75-$150 inspection can save hundreds/year
4. **Raise deductible** — 2% hurricane deductible vs 5% saves premium
5. **Bundle policies** — auto + home discount
6. **Shop annually** — Florida's market is volatile, rates change
7. **Newer construction** generally costs less to insure

## HOA Fees

### Orlando HOA Fee Averages
| Property Type | Average Monthly HOA |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Single-family home | $150-$250 |
| Townhome | $200-$350 |
| Condo | $400-$600 |
| Luxury condo | $600-$1,200 |

### What HOA Fees Cover (Typical)
- **Single-family HOA**: Common area maintenance, community amenities (pool, fitness, clubhouse), landscaping of common areas
- **Condo HOA**: All of above PLUS exterior building maintenance, roof, structural insurance, water/sewer, sometimes cable/internet
- **Master-planned communities**: May have both an overarching HOA + sub-HOA (double fees)

### Florida HOA Laws
- Governed by **Chapter 720** (HOAs) and **Chapter 718** (Condos)
- HOA boards are elected by homeowners
- Annual budgets must be approved by membership
- **Estoppel letter**: Required at sale — shows outstanding balances, violations, pending assessments ($250 fee typical)
- New law (effective 2025): Condo associations must conduct **structural inspections** and fund reserves for repairs — driving up condo fees statewide

### Red Flags in HOA Documents
- Underfunded reserves (especially condos — new SB 4-D requirements)
- Pending special assessments
- Active litigation
- Deferred maintenance
- High percentage of investor-owned units
- Rental restrictions (if buying as investment)
- Excessive rules (some Orlando HOAs are notoriously strict)

### Notable HOA Fee Increases
- Condo HOA fees up **15%+ year-over-year** in Orlando (2024-2025)
- Driven by: insurance cost pass-through, SB 4-D structural reserve requirements, inflation
- Some older condos seeing **special assessments of $20,000-$100,000+** per unit for deferred maintenance

## Closing Costs

### Buyer Closing Costs (Typical: 2-5% of purchase price)

| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| Loan origination fee | 0.5-1% of loan amount |
| Appraisal | $400-$600 |
| Home inspection | $350-$500 |
| Survey | $350-$500 |
| Documentary stamps on note | $0.35 per $100 of mortgage |
| Intangible tax on mortgage | 0.2% of mortgage amount |
| Title search | $200-$400 |
| Lender's title insurance | $500-$1,500 |
| Recording fees | $30-$50 per document |
| Escrow prepaids | 2-6 months taxes + insurance |

**Example for $400K purchase with $320K mortgage:**
- Doc stamps on note: $1,120
- Intangible tax: $640
- Lender's title: ~$800
- Appraisal + inspection + survey: ~$1,300
- Prepaids (taxes + insurance escrow): ~$4,000-$6,000
- Other fees: ~$1,500
- **Total buyer closing costs: ~$10,000-$14,000 (2.5-3.5%)**

### Seller Closing Costs (Typical: ~3.25% of sale price)

| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| Documentary stamps on deed | $0.70 per $100 of sale price (0.7%) |
| Owner's title insurance | Varies by sale price (~$1,000-$2,500) |
| Real estate commission | 5-6% (negotiable post-NAR settlement) |
| HOA estoppel letter | $250 |
| Municipal lien search | $200-$300 |
| Satisfaction of mortgage recording | $50-$100 |

**Example for $400K sale:**
- Doc stamps on deed: $2,800
- Title insurance: ~$1,575
- Commission (5%): $20,000
- Other fees: ~$750
- **Total seller costs: ~$25,000-$28,000 (6.25-7%)**

### Who Pays What (Orlando Custom)
- **Seller traditionally pays**: Owner's title insurance, doc stamps on deed, commission
- **Buyer traditionally pays**: Lender's title insurance, doc stamps on note, intangible tax, appraisal, inspection, survey
- Everything is negotiable — in buyer's market, sellers may contribute to buyer closing costs
