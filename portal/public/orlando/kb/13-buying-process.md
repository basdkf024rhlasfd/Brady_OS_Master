# Buying Process

A step-by-step guide to purchasing a home in Orlando, from first research to closing day.

---

## Step 1: Financial Readiness

### Check Your Credit
- Pull free reports from annualcreditreport.com (all 3 bureaus)
- **Target scores**:
  - 740+: Best conventional rates
  - 700+: Good rates, jumbo loan eligible
  - 620+: Conventional minimum
  - 580+: FHA minimum (3.5% down)
  - 500-579: FHA with 10% down
- Dispute errors 60-90 days before applying
- Avoid new credit accounts, large purchases, or job changes during process

### Determine Your Budget
- **Rule of thumb**: Purchase price ≈ 3-4x gross annual income
- **Orlando-specific monthly costs** (on a $400K home):
  | Cost | Monthly |
  |------|---------|
  | Mortgage P&I (7%, 5% down) | $2,525 |
  | PMI | $175 |
  | Property tax | $295 |
  | Homeowner insurance | $375 |
  | HOA/CDD | $150-$400 |
  | Maintenance reserve | $300 |
  | **Total** | **$3,820-$4,070** |
- Most lenders cap DTI at 43-45% of gross income
- Don't forget: moving costs, furniture, utility deposits, immediate repairs

### Save for Down Payment + Reserves
- **Conventional**: 3-20% down ($12K-$80K on $400K)
- **FHA**: 3.5% ($14K on $400K)
- **VA/USDA**: 0% down
- **Closing costs**: 2-4% of purchase price ($8K-$16K on $400K)
- **Reserves**: Lenders want 2-6 months of payments in savings after closing
- **Down payment assistance**: City of Orlando up to $45K, Orange County up to $70K (income-restricted)

## Step 2: Get Pre-Approved

### Pre-Qualification vs. Pre-Approval
- **Pre-qualification**: Quick estimate based on self-reported info. Low value in competitive market.
- **Pre-approval**: Full underwrite review — credit pull, income verification, asset documentation. Required to make serious offers in Orlando.

### Documents Needed
- 2 years W-2s or tax returns
- 2 months bank statements (all accounts)
- 30 days pay stubs
- Photo ID
- If self-employed: 2 years business tax returns, P&L statement, CPA letter
- Gift letter (if using gifted funds for down payment)

### Orlando-Specific Tips
- Get pre-approved with a **local lender** — Orlando listing agents prefer local lenders who know Florida closing customs
- Ask about **builder preferred lender** programs if considering new construction (rate buydowns, closing cost credits)
- Get pre-approved for slightly more than your target to have negotiating flexibility
- Pre-approval letters typically valid 60-90 days

## Step 3: Choose a Real Estate Agent

### Post-NAR Settlement Rules (Effective August 2024)
- **Buyer broker agreements now required** before touring homes
- You must sign a written agreement with your agent specifying:
  - Services the agent will provide
  - Compensation amount or rate
  - How compensation will be paid (by buyer, seller, or split)
- **Seller is no longer required** to offer buyer agent compensation through MLS
- In practice: Most Orlando sellers still offer 2-2.5% buyer agent compensation, but this is negotiable

### What to Look For
- Active Orlando market experience (not just licensed — actively selling here)
- Knowledge of your target neighborhoods
- Familiarity with Florida contracts (FAR/BAR)
- Strong communication style that matches your preference
- Transaction volume: 10+ closings/year minimum
- Ask for references from recent Orlando buyers

### Buyer Agent Compensation in Orlando (2025-2026)
- Typical range: 2-3% of purchase price
- Increasingly negotiable, especially on higher-priced homes
- Can be paid by: seller concession, buyer directly, or split
- New construction: Builder typically pays 2-3% (sometimes with bonuses)
- **Negotiate this upfront** in your buyer broker agreement

## Step 4: Research Neighborhoods

### Orlando's Key Decisions
- **County matters**: Orange, Seminole, Osceola, Lake — different tax rates, schools, insurance costs
- **East vs. West**: Lake Nona/East Orlando (newer, medical city) vs. Windermere/Dr. Phillips (established, theme park proximity)
- **Urban vs. Suburban**: Downtown/Winter Park (walkable, older homes) vs. Horizon West/Clermont (new construction, master-planned)
- **Commute**: Factor in I-4 congestion, toll costs ($100-$250/month), and SunRail access
- **School zones**: Verify at ocps.net — zones change, and quality varies dramatically within the same zip code

### Must-Research Items
- FEMA flood zone (msc.fema.gov)
- HOA/CDD fees (can add $200-$600/month)
- Insurance quotes for the specific area
- Flight path proximity (MCO arrivals/departures)
- Future development (Orange County Zoning Atlas)
- Crime statistics (spotcrime.com, local PD maps)

---

## Step 5: Search for Homes

### Where to Search
- **Stellar MLS** (through your agent): Most accurate, real-time data
- **Zillow/Realtor.com/Redfin**: Good for browsing but data can lag 24-48 hours
- **Builder websites**: New construction often not listed on MLS until later stages
- **Agent pocket listings**: Off-market opportunities your agent may know about

### Orlando Market Dynamics (2025-2026)
- **Inventory**: Rising — 4-6 months supply in many areas (balanced to buyer-favorable)
- **Days on market**: 50-70 average, 80-120 for luxury
- **Price reductions**: 25-30% of listings have at least one price cut
- **Negotiation**: Buyers have more leverage than 2021-2022, especially in oversupplied areas
- **Best opportunities**: Homes sitting 45+ days, new construction with standing inventory, sellers who already purchased next home

## Step 6: Make an Offer

### Florida Contract Basics
- **FAR/BAR "As Is" contract**: Most common in Orlando — buyer can inspect but seller has no obligation to repair
- **FAR/BAR standard contract**: Buyer can request repairs; more negotiation but less common now
- **Earnest money deposit (EMD)**: Typically 1-2% of purchase price, deposited within 3 days of acceptance
- **Escrow**: Held by title company or closing attorney

### Offer Strategy in Current Market
- **List price or below**: Appropriate for most properties in current market
- **Inspection period**: 10-15 days standard (negotiate longer for older homes)
- **Financing contingency**: Included if not paying cash (30-45 days to close)
- **Appraisal contingency**: Protects you if home appraises below purchase price
- **Closing date**: 30-45 days typical for financed purchases, 14-21 for cash
- **Seller concessions**: Ask for 1-3% toward closing costs — common in current market
- **Escalation clauses**: Less necessary now but useful in competitive micro-markets (Winter Park, certain Lake Nona sections)

### What NOT to Do
- Don't waive inspection in Orlando — too many hidden issues (roof, AC, sinkhole, Chinese drywall in some 2004-2009 homes)
- Don't waive appraisal unless you can cover the gap with cash
- Don't use a generic out-of-state contract — Florida has specific requirements

## Step 7: Inspection Period

### Required/Recommended Inspections
| Inspection | Cost | Why It Matters in Orlando |
|-----------|------|--------------------------|
| General home inspection | $350-$600 | Foundation, electrical, plumbing, structure |
| Wind mitigation | $75-$150 | Can save $500-$2,000/year on insurance |
| 4-point inspection | $100-$200 | Required for insurance on homes 30+ years |
| WDO (termite) | $75-$125 | Termites are active year-round in FL |
| Roof inspection | $150-$300 | Roof age determines insurability |
| Sinkhole evaluation | $300-$500 | If in sinkhole-prone area (West Orange) |
| Pool/spa inspection | $150-$250 | Pool equipment, surface, deck condition |
| Septic inspection | $250-$450 | If not on city sewer (rural areas) |
| Mold inspection | $300-$600 | Florida humidity = mold risk |

### Orlando Red Flags
- **Roof over 15 years old**: Many insurers won't write a new policy. Replacement: $8K-$25K.
- **AC system age**: Life span in FL is 8-12 years (not 15-20 like up north). Replacement: $5K-$12K.
- **Polybutylene plumbing** (1978-1995 homes): Known failure-prone. Repipe: $4K-$10K.
- **Cast iron drain pipes** (pre-1975): Corrode in Florida soil. Replacement: $5K-$15K.
- **Stucco cracks**: Can indicate settling, sinkhole activity, or water intrusion.
- **No hurricane shutters/impact windows**: Retrofit cost: $10K-$50K.
- **Chinese drywall** (some 2004-2009 construction): Emits sulfur, corrodes wiring/pipes. Remediation: $100K+.

### After Inspection
- **As-Is contract**: You can cancel during inspection period for any reason (EMD returned) or proceed as-is
- **Standard contract**: Submit repair requests; negotiate credits or repairs
- Common negotiation: Seller provides closing cost credit for known issues rather than doing repairs

## Step 8: Appraisal

### How It Works
- Lender orders appraisal after inspection period passes (if proceeding)
- Licensed appraiser visits property, compares to recent sales (comps)
- Cost: $400-$600 (paid by buyer, usually at order)
- Timeline: 7-14 days to complete

### If Appraisal Comes in Low
- **Renegotiate price** to appraised value (most common in current market)
- **Buyer covers gap** with additional cash above down payment
- **Split the difference** — meet halfway between contract and appraised price
- **Challenge the appraisal**: Agent provides additional comps to appraiser/lender
- **Cancel the contract** using appraisal contingency (EMD returned)

### Orlando Appraisal Notes
- New construction communities: Appraisals can be tricky if few comps exist
- Rapidly appreciating areas: Appraisals may lag actual market value
- Builder incentives (rate buydowns, upgrades) can complicate value assessment
- CDD fees can affect appraised value negatively

## Step 9: Insurance & Title

### Homeowner Insurance (Get This Early)
- **When**: Start getting quotes during inspection period — don't wait
- **Budget**: $3,000-$6,000/year for typical Orlando home
- **Key factors**: Roof age, construction year, flood zone, distance to coast
- **Must-shop**: Get 5+ quotes — pricing varies enormously between carriers
- **Wind mitigation inspection** saves $500-$2,000/year
- **Flood insurance**: Required in Zone AE/A, recommended everywhere near water ($700-$3,000/year)
- **Sinkhole coverage**: Optional endorsement ($500-$2,000/year additional)

### Title Insurance
- **Florida custom**: Seller typically pays for owner's title policy in Orange/Seminole counties
- **Buyer pays**: Lender's title policy (required by lender)
- **Title search**: Verifies clean ownership, no liens, no encumbrances
- **Survey**: $300-$500 — verifies property boundaries, setbacks, encroachments

### Choosing a Title Company
- Your agent will recommend one; you can choose your own
- Compare fees — closing costs vary by title company
- Verify they're licensed and insured in Florida

---

## Step 10: Final Loan Approval

### From Pre-Approval to Clear-to-Close
- Underwriter reviews: appraisal, title, insurance, updated financials
- **Conditions**: Lender may request additional documentation (updated pay stubs, explanation letters)
- **Timeline**: 2-3 weeks after appraisal received
- **Clear-to-close**: Means all conditions satisfied — you're approved to fund

### Don't Do This During Underwriting
- Change jobs or income sources
- Make large purchases (car, furniture, appliances)
- Open new credit accounts
- Move large sums between accounts without paper trail
- Co-sign for anyone else's loan
- Miss any bill payments

---

## Step 11: Closing

### Pre-Closing
- **Final walkthrough**: 24-48 hours before closing — verify property condition, agreed repairs completed, all fixtures present
- **Review closing disclosure**: Received 3 business days before closing — compare to loan estimate
- **Wire funds**: Down payment + closing costs wired to title company (verify wire instructions by phone — wire fraud is real)
- **Bring to closing**: Government-issued photo ID, cashier's check (if not wired), any outstanding documents

### At the Closing Table
- **Location**: Title company office (Orlando standard) — can also be mobile notary or remote online notarization (RON)
- **Duration**: 45-90 minutes
- **Who's there**: Buyer, seller (or their representatives), closing agent, possibly agents
- **Documents signed**: Deed, mortgage, note, settlement statement, tax forms, insurance acknowledgments
- **Keys**: Typically received at closing or shortly after recording

### Florida-Specific Closing Details
- **Deed type**: Warranty deed (most common) or special warranty deed (new construction/REO)
- **Documentary stamps**: Paid by seller in most Orlando transactions ($0.70 per $100 of sale price)
- **Intangible tax**: Paid by buyer on new mortgage ($0.002 per $1 of mortgage amount)
- **Recording fees**: ~$10 per page for deed and mortgage recording
- **Prorations**: Property taxes prorated to closing date (seller pays their share)
- **Homestead exemption**: File immediately after closing for next year's tax savings (March 1 deadline)

## Step 12: Post-Closing

### Immediate (First Week)
- [ ] File homestead exemption at county property appraiser (before March 1 for that tax year)
- [ ] Change locks
- [ ] Set up utilities (OUC, Duke Energy, TOHO Water, etc.)
- [ ] Update address (USPS, DMV, voter registration)
- [ ] Get hurricane supplies if during season (June 1 - November 30)
- [ ] Test/service AC system (critical in Florida)
- [ ] Locate main water shutoff and electrical panel

### First Month
- [ ] Get AC maintenance contract ($150-$300/year — essential in FL)
- [ ] Service pool equipment if applicable
- [ ] Treat for pests (quarterly service: $30-$50/month in FL)
- [ ] Check irrigation system and timer
- [ ] Meet HOA management, get community guidelines
- [ ] File for any applicable tax exemptions beyond homestead

### Ongoing
- **AC maintenance**: Quarterly filter changes, annual professional service
- **Roof**: Annual inspection, clean debris after storms
- **Pest control**: Year-round in Florida — quarterly treatments standard
- **Hurricane prep**: Review plan annually, maintain shutters/impact windows, keep 7-day supply kit
- **Insurance**: Review annually — shop for better rates, verify adequate coverage
- **Property taxes**: Verify assessed value annually, challenge if overvalued
- **SOH cap**: Your Save Our Homes cap grows each year — significant long-term tax savings

## First-Time Buyer Checklist (Orlando Edition)

### Before You Start
- [ ] Credit score 620+ (ideally 700+)
- [ ] Savings for down payment + closing costs + reserves
- [ ] Stable employment for 2+ years
- [ ] Research down payment assistance (City of Orlando $45K, Orange County $70K)
- [ ] Complete HUD-approved homebuyer education course (required for most DPA)

### During the Process
- [ ] Signed buyer broker agreement with agent
- [ ] Pre-approval letter from local lender
- [ ] FEMA flood zone checked for every property you consider
- [ ] Insurance quotes obtained before making offers
- [ ] HOA/CDD documents reviewed thoroughly
- [ ] All inspections completed during inspection period
- [ ] Homestead exemption filed after closing

### Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes in Orlando
1. **Not budgeting for insurance**: $3,000-$6,000/year surprises many transplants
2. **Ignoring CDD fees**: Can add $1,500-$4,000/year on top of HOA
3. **Falling for model home upgrades**: Builder upgrades are marked up 40-60% — negotiate or DIY
4. **Skipping wind mitigation inspection**: $75-$150 that saves $500-$2,000/year
5. **Not checking roof age**: Can make a home uninsurable or add $15K+ to cost
6. **Waiving inspection to "win"**: Orlando market doesn't require this anymore
7. **Not filing homestead exemption**: Miss the March 1 deadline = pay full taxes for a year
8. **Underestimating AC costs**: Florida AC runs 8-10 months/year — budget for maintenance and replacement
