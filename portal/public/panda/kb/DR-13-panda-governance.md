## Executive Summary

Panda Express is a privately held, family-controlled company founded in 1983 by Andrew and Peggy Cherng. The chain has grown to become one of the largest Chinese-American QSR operators in North America with reported $3B+ in systemwide sales. Power is concentrated in the Cherng family, particularly Andrew Cherng (Chairman/Co-CEO) and Peggy Cherng (Co-CEO), with professional management led by COO Jeff Wang and CDO James Ku. A February 2026 CEO departure signals potential transition in operational leadership structure. Any Ops Innovation centralization will require Cherng family buy-in — likely Andrew as final decision-maker, with Peggy holding significant technical and strategic influence.

## 7. Context for Organizational Change: Buy-In Path

### How Hard Is Centralization at Panda?
- **Moderate Difficulty**: Panda is not a purely decentralized franchise; significant corporate backbone exists (supply chain, IT, marketing)
- **Franchise vs. Corporate**: Mix of corporate stores and franchisees; centralized ops easier for corporate fleet, requires incentive alignment for franchisees
- **Cultural Resistance**: Franchise owners may resist if perceived as loss of autonomy; requires financial argument (better margins, better support)

### Buy-In Path (Most Likely Sequence)
1. **James Ku champions** ops innovation internally → presents business case to Peggy Cherng
2. **Peggy sponsors** to Andrew (framing: brand consistency, customer experience, team member development)
3. **Andrew greenlighs** pilot or limited rollout (Cherng's style: test-and-learn, not big bang)
4. **Jeff Wang owns execution** → manages franchise communication, system rollout
5. **David Landsberg validates** financial impact (cost savings, margin improvement)

### Cherng Posture on Consultants
- **Positive Historical Signal**: Panda has used external consultants (McKinsey, Accenture, Bain known in industry for QSR work)
- **But with Caveats**: Family will scrutinize consultant independence (no conflicts with suppliers/franchisees)
- **Likely Stance**: Open to external ops/tech expertise if consultant is **not positioning as organizational redesign** (Cherngs own that)

### $10B Vision (Landsberg)
- **Status**: Likely **aspirational** (10 years out), not official strategy
- **Sponsor**: David Landsberg (CFO) likely the internal champion
- **Path**: International expansion + same-unit sales growth + technology/efficiency
- **For Ops Innovation**: If positioned as **enabler of $10B vision**, it becomes strategic priority (not just cost-cutting)
