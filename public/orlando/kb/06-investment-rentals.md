## Long-Term Rental Market

### Average Rent Prices (2025)
| Type | Average Monthly Rent |
|------|---------------------|
| Studio apartment | $1,419 |
| 1-bedroom apartment | $1,586 |
| 2-bedroom apartment | $1,919 |
| 3-bedroom apartment/townhome | $2,200-$2,500 |
| 3-bedroom single-family | $2,500-$2,800 |
| 4-bedroom single-family | $2,750-$3,200 |

### Market Dynamics
- Average rent: ~$1,785/month (all types combined)
- Rent growth: **-2.4% year-over-year** (slight decline after years of sharp increases)
- 2025 forecast: +2.4% rent growth by end of year
- Strongest rent growth submarkets: East Outlying (+5.0%), East Orlando (+3.4%)
- Rents roughly doubled from 2019-2023, now normalizing

### Vacancy Rates
- Metro average: **6.2%** (up from 4-5% in 2023)
- Softening due to new apartment supply coming online
- Still below national average
- Single-family vacancy rates remain lower (~3-4%)
- Highest vacancy: Downtown condos, new apartment complexes near UCF

### Rental Demand Drivers
- 1,500+ new residents moving to Orlando weekly
- University population (UCF: 70,000+ students, Valencia, Full Sail)
- Tourism/hospitality workforce needs housing
- Remote workers relocating from HCOL areas
- No state income tax attracts high-income renters

## Investment Analysis

### Cap Rates by Area

|------|-----------------|-------|

| Kissimmee | 6-8% | Good value, diverse tenant pool |

| MetroWest | 5-7% | Near Universal, service worker demand |
| Apopka/Ocoee | 5-6% | Suburban families |
| Sanford | 5-7% | Gentrifying, value play |
| Clermont | 4-6% | Growth area, lower current yield |
| Winter Park | 3-5% | Appreciation play, not cash flow |
| Lake Nona | 3-5% | Appreciation play |
| Windermere | 2-4% | Pure appreciation, luxury market |

### Gross Rental Yield
- Metro average: **6-7% gross rental yield**
- Formula: (Annual Rent / Purchase Price) x 100
- Example: $2,500/month rent on $350K home = 8.6% gross yield
- After expenses (taxes, insurance, management, maintenance, vacancy): Net yield typically 3-5%

### 1% Rule Assessment
The "1% rule" (monthly rent should be 1% of purchase price) is achievable in:
- Poinciana (most reliably)
- Parts of Kissimmee
- Some Sanford properties
- Older MetroWest properties
- NOT achievable in most newer or premium areas

### Investment Property Costs
| Expense | Typical Annual Cost |
|---------|-------------------|
| Property taxes (non-homestead) | 1.0-1.2% of assessed value |
| Insurance | $2,500-$5,000 |
| Property management | 8-10% of collected rent |
| Maintenance reserve | 1-2% of property value |
| Vacancy allowance | 5-8% of annual rent |
| HOA (if applicable) | $1,800-$6,000 |
| CDD (if applicable) | $1,000-$4,000 |
| Lawn care (if not HOA) | $1,200-$2,400 |

### Non-Homestead Tax Impact
- No homestead exemption = higher taxable value
- No Save Our Homes cap = assessed value can increase up to market value annually (10% cap)
- Property taxes on investment properties can be **40-60% higher** than owner-occupied over time
- This is a significant drag on long-term returns

## Short-Term Rental (STR) Investment

### Where STRs Are Allowed
| Area | STR Status | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| City of Orlando | Restricted | Owner-occupied only, 51%+ of year |
| Unincorporated Orange County | Mostly banned | 180-day minimum in most areas |
| Kissimmee (tourist corridor) | Allowed (zoned) | Must be in STR-zoned area |
| Osceola County (unincorporated) | Allowed (zoned) | Major STR market near Disney |
| Davenport/Four Corners | Allowed | Massive STR market |
| ChampionsGate/Reunion | Allowed | Resort communities designed for STR |
| Seminole County | Restricted | Limited STR activity |
| Clermont/Lake County | Varies | Check specific municipality |

### STR Revenue Potential (Near Disney)
| Property Type | Avg Nightly Rate | Annual Revenue (65% occ) | Notes |
|--------------|-----------------|----------------------|-------|
| 3BR townhome | $150-$200 | $35,000-$47,000 | Lower management cost |
| 4BR house | $200-$300 | $47,000-$71,000 | Most popular size |
| 5BR house (with pool) | $250-$400 | $59,000-$95,000 | Premium for pool homes |
| 6-8BR house (pool) | $350-$600 | $83,000-$142,000 | Group/family market |
| 9+ BR mansion | $500-$1,200 | $118,000-$284,000 | Event/reunion market |

### STR Operating Costs
| Expense | Cost |
|---------|------|
| Property management | 20-30% of gross revenue |
| Cleaning (per turnover) | $150-$400 depending on size |
| Utilities (owner-paid) | $300-$600/month |
| Pool maintenance | $150-$250/month |
| Lawn care | $150-$250/month |
| Supplies/consumables | $100-$200/month |
| Maintenance/repairs | $200-$400/month average |
| Insurance (STR policy) | $3,000-$8,000/year |
| Taxes (property + CDD) | $3,000-$8,000/year |
| Platform fees | 3-5% (Airbnb host fee) |

### STR Tax Obligations
- Florida Sales Tax: 6%
- County Tourist Development Tax: 5-6%
- County surtax: 0.5-1%
- **Total tax on STR income: 12-13%**
- Must register with FL Department of Revenue
- Must collect and remit taxes monthly or quarterly
- Airbnb/VRBO collect and remit some taxes automatically — verify what's covered

### STR Licensing Requirements
1. State DBPR vacation rental license
2. County business tax receipt
3. Local STR permit (if applicable)
4. Sales tax registration (FL Dept of Revenue)
5. Tourist development tax registration (County)

## Investment Strategies for Orlando

### Strategy 1: Long-Term Buy-and-Hold (Suburban SFR)
- **Target**: 3-4BR single-family in Kissimmee, Poinciana, east Orlando
- **Price point**: $280K-$400K
- **Expected rent**: $2,000-$2,600/month
- **Pros**: Steady demand, lower management intensity, appreciation potential
- **Cons**: Lower immediate returns, tenant turnover costs, non-homestead taxes

### Strategy 2: Near-Disney STR
- **Target**: 4-6BR pool home in Osceola County STR zone
- **Price point**: $350K-$550K
- **Expected revenue**: $50,000-$90,000/year gross
- **Pros**: High gross revenue, tourism demand year-round (Epic Universe adds demand)
- **Cons**: High management cost, seasonal fluctuations, regulatory risk, oversupply in some areas

### Strategy 3: UCF Student Housing
- **Target**: Condos or townhomes near UCF campus
- **Price point**: $200K-$350K
- **Expected rent**: $1,400-$2,200/month (per-bed pricing can increase yield)
- **Pros**: 70,000+ student population, consistent demand
- **Cons**: Higher wear-and-tear, summer vacancy, student turnover

### Strategy 4: Appreciation Play (Growth Corridors)
- **Target**: New construction in Lake Nona, Horizon West, Sunbridge
- **Price point**: $400K-$700K
- **Expected rent**: May not cash flow well initially
- **Pros**: Strong appreciation potential (5-8% annually in growth corridors)
- **Cons**: Negative cash flow in early years, CDD fees eat into returns, speculative

### Strategy 5: House Hacking (Owner-Occupied)
- **Target**: Multi-bedroom home, rent spare rooms or ADU
- **Price point**: $300K-$500K
- **Benefits**: Homestead exemption, owner-occupied financing (lower rates), reduced living costs
- **Orlando market**: ADU rules evolving — check current Orange County regulations

### Strategy 6: Section 8 / Voucher Housing
- **Target**: Affordable homes in areas with strong Section 8 demand
- **Price point**: $200K-$350K
- **Pros**: Guaranteed government rent payment, lower vacancy
- **Cons**: Property condition requirements, inspection process, perception issues
- **Orlando Housing Authority** administers local voucher program

## Property Management Decision Framework

Self-manage vs. full-service — the right answer depends on proximity, property type, and your time tolerance.

### Fee Guide by Management Type

| Management Type | Fee Range | What's Included | What's NOT Included |
|----------------|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **LTR full-service** | 8–10% of collected rent | Tenant placement, rent collection, maintenance coordination, eviction process | Leasing fee (50–100% of first month's rent), maintenance markup (10–20%), lease renewal fee ($150–$300) |
| **STR full-service** | 20–25% of gross revenue | Listing management, pricing optimization, guest communication, cleaning coordination, supply restocking | Cleaning cost (billed separately), maintenance/repairs (billed separately), platform fees (3–5%) |
| **STR premium (resort)** | 25–30% of gross revenue | Everything above + concierge services, pool/spa heating, theme room setup | Same exclusions, higher base rate for resort-community properties |
| **Tenant placement only** | 50–100% of first month's rent | Find tenant, background check, lease signing | No ongoing management — you handle everything after |
| **Maintenance coordination only** | $100–$200/month flat | Vendor dispatch, emergency calls, routine scheduling | No tenant relations, no rent collection, no marketing |

### Self-Manage vs. Full-Service Decision Tree

```
Can you respond to tenant/guest issues within 2 hours?
│
├─ NO → Full-service management (you're too far or too busy)
│
└─ YES
   │
   ├─ Is the property STR?
   │  ├─ YES → Full-service recommended
   │  │         (guest communication volume is 5–10x LTR,
   │  │         pricing optimization requires daily monitoring,
   │  │         cleaning turnovers need local coordination)
   │  │
   │  └─ NO (LTR)
   │     │
   │     ├─ Do you own 1–2 properties locally?
   │     │  ├─ YES → Self-manage is feasible
   │     │  │         Save 8–10% fee ($2,400–$3,600/year on $2,500/mo rent)
   │     │  │         Budget 5–10 hours/month for management tasks
   │     │  │
   │     │  └─ NO (3+ properties or out-of-state)
   │     │     └─ Full-service recommended
   │     │        (scale tips toward professional management)
   │     │
   │     └─ Are you comfortable with FL landlord-tenant law?
   │        ├─ YES → Self-manage with legal template (FL BAR lease)
   │        └─ NO → Full-service or at minimum tenant placement + legal review
```

### Self-Management Checklist (If You Choose to Self-Manage)

| Task | Frequency | Time Investment |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Respond to tenant requests | As needed | 1–3 hours/week |
| Coordinate maintenance/repairs | As needed | 1–2 hours/week |
| Collect rent and track payments | Monthly | 1 hour/month |
| Property inspection (drive-by or interior) | Quarterly | 2 hours/quarter |
| Lease renewal or tenant turnover | Annually | 5–15 hours per turnover |
| Bookkeeping and expense tracking | Monthly | 2 hours/month |
| Insurance review and renewal | Annually | 2 hours/year |
| Property tax payment | Annually | 1 hour/year |

### Red Flags When Hiring a Property Manager

| Red Flag | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------------|
| No written management agreement | You have no legal protection or fee clarity |
| Vague fee structure ("we charge 10%") | Hidden fees for maintenance markup, lease renewal, advertising, etc. |
| Won't share monthly P&L or occupancy data | You can't evaluate performance without data |
| Guarantees specific occupancy or returns | No honest PM can guarantee market outcomes |
| No DBPR license (check myfloridalicense.com) | Operating illegally in Florida |
| Holds security deposits in operating account (not escrow) | Violation of FL statute 83.49 |
| Slow to respond to your calls | If they're slow for you, they're slower for tenants |
| No online portal for owners | Industry standard — lack of tech signals outdated operations |
| Manages 500+ units with small staff | Overstretched — your property gets less attention |
| No local presence in your property's area | Response times suffer, vendor relationships are weaker |

### Orlando PM Market Landscape

| PM Type | Typical Portfolio | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---------|------------------|-----------|------------|
| **Solo operator / small firm** | 20–80 units | Personal attention, flexible, lower fees | Limited coverage, vacation gaps, less tech |
| **Mid-size firm** | 80–300 units | Balance of attention and resources | Growing pains, staff turnover |
| **Large firm / franchise** | 300–1,000+ units | Systems, tech, legal team, scale | Less personal, slower response, higher fees |
| **STR-specialized firm** | 50–200 STR units | Revenue optimization, guest experience, platform expertise | Only does STR, higher fees, may push volume over quality |
| **Hybrid (LTR + STR)** | Varies | Flexibility to switch strategies | May not excel at either |

## International Investor Considerations

### Why International Buyers Love Orlando
- No state income tax
- Relative affordability vs coastal cities
- Tourism provides STR income opportunity
- Stable appreciation
- Disney/Universal brand recognition globally

### Top International Buyer Origins
1. Brazil — largest international buyer group
2. Colombia
3. Venezuela
4. Canada
5. United Kingdom
6. Puerto Rico (domestic but distinct market)
7. Argentina

### FIRPTA Withholding
- 15% of purchase price withheld at closing if seller is foreign person
- 10% if property is $1M or less and buyer will use as residence
- Can apply for withholding certificate to reduce
- Withholding is a credit against actual tax liability

### Tax Considerations for Foreign Owners
- Must file US tax return for rental income
- Can deduct expenses against rental income
- Capital gains tax applies on sale
- Estate tax on US real property applies to foreign owners (above $60K exemption — much lower than US citizen exemption)
- Many foreign investors use LLC structures — consult international tax attorney

### Foreign National Financing
- Available through select lenders
- Typically requires 30-40% down payment
- Higher interest rates than conventional (0.5-1.5% premium)
- May require ITIN or passport
- Several Orlando-area lenders specialize in foreign national mortgages
