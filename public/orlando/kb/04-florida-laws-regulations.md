# Florida Real Estate Laws & Regulations

Florida-specific laws every buyer, seller, investor, and agent needs to know.

---

## Contract Types

### FAR/BAR Contracts
- Florida uses standardized contract forms created jointly by **Florida Realtors (FAR)** and **The Florida Bar (BAR)**
- Two main residential contracts:
  1. **FAR/BAR "As-Is" Contract** — Most commonly used in Orlando
  2. **FAR/BAR Standard Contract** — Includes repair/maintenance obligations

### As-Is Contract
- **Does NOT mean buyer can't inspect** — buyer retains right to inspect
- Seller is not obligated to make repairs
- Buyer can cancel during inspection period if they don't like findings
- Inspection period is negotiable (typically **10-15 days**)
- After inspection period, buyer cannot cancel for property condition reasons (without forfeiting deposit)
- This is the **dominant contract type in Orlando** — ~80%+ of transactions

### Standard Contract
- Seller has obligation to maintain property and make repairs
- Repair limit is negotiable (commonly $1,500-$3,000)
- If repairs exceed limit, either party can cancel
- Less common but used in some transactions

### Key Contract Provisions
- **Effective date**: When last party signs
- **Deposit (Earnest Money)**: Typically 1-3% of purchase price, held by escrow agent
- **Financing contingency**: Buyer must apply for mortgage within X days
- **Closing date**: Negotiable, typically 30-45 days
- **Title commitment**: Typically delivered within 5 days of effective date
- **Walk-through**: Buyer has right to final walk-through before closing

## Seller Disclosure Requirements

### What Sellers Must Disclose
- Florida follows **"caveat emptor" (buyer beware)** with important exceptions
- Sellers must disclose **known material defects** that are not readily observable
- No standardized disclosure form is required by state law (unlike many states)
- However, most agents use a **Seller's Property Disclosure** form
- Must disclose: known foundation issues, roof leaks, mold, sinkholes, flooding, environmental hazards, code violations

### What Sellers Do NOT Have to Disclose
- Florida statute specifically exempts disclosure of **deaths on property** (including murders, suicides)
- HIV/AIDS status of occupants
- Proximity to sex offenders (buyer must research independently)
- Stigmatized property events (hauntings, crimes)

### Johnson v. Davis Standard
- Landmark Florida case establishing seller's duty to disclose material defects
- Seller who knows of defects not readily observable must disclose them
- Failure to disclose = potential lawsuit for fraud/misrepresentation

## FIRPTA (Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act)

### Why It Matters in Orlando
- Orlando has a **massive international buyer market** (Brazilian, Colombian, Venezuelan, Puerto Rican, Canadian, British)
- FIRPTA requires the **buyer to withhold 15%** of the purchase price and remit to IRS when the seller is a foreign person/entity
- Withholding rate is **10%** if purchase price is $1M or less and buyer intends to use as residence
- Foreign sellers can apply for a **withholding certificate** to reduce the amount
- **Critical**: If buyer fails to withhold and seller is foreign, buyer is liable for the tax

### Exemptions
- Sale price under $300,000 and buyer intends to use as residence — no withholding required
- Seller provides IRS certification of non-foreign status (W-9)
- Seller obtains withholding certificate from IRS

## Landlord-Tenant Law (Chapter 83)

### Key Provisions
- **Security deposit**: Must be held in separate Florida bank account (interest-bearing or non-interest-bearing). Landlord must notify tenant within 30 days of receipt with bank name, address, whether interest-bearing.
- **Returning deposits**: Within **15 days** if no claim against deposit. If claiming deductions, must send notice by certified mail within **30 days** with itemized list. Tenant has **15 days** to object.
- **Eviction process**: 3-day notice for nonpayment of rent. 7-day notice for lease violations. Must go through court for formal eviction.
- **Rent increases**: No rent control in Florida (preempted by state law). Landlords can raise rent at lease renewal with proper notice.
- **Notice to terminate**: Month-to-month: 15 days before end of month. Annual lease: terminates at end of lease term.
- **Landlord's access**: 12 hours notice required for routine access. No notice needed for emergency.
- **Tenant's remedies**: Can withhold rent if landlord fails to maintain premises (after proper 7-day notice).

### Florida Specifics
- **No rent control**: State law preempts all local rent control ordinances
- **No "just cause" eviction**: Landlord can choose not to renew lease for any non-discriminatory reason
- **Mold**: No specific Florida statute on mold disclosure in rentals, but general habitability standards apply
- **HOA approval**: Many HOAs require tenant screening/approval — can take 2-4 weeks

## Short-Term Rental Regulations

### State Level
- All vacation rentals under 30 days must be licensed with **DBPR (Department of Business and Professional Regulation)**
- State license required regardless of local rules
- Florida Sales Tax: **6%** on rental income
- State preemption: Local governments cannot ban vacation rentals outright (2011 law), but can regulate them

### City of Orlando
- **Whole-home STRs are generally NOT allowed** in residential zones
- Allowed only if owner lives on-site **51%+ of the year**
- Definition: Renting for <30 days, more than 3x/year
- Registration required: **$275 first year, $125/year after**
- Limited to **1 booking at a time**, max **4 guests**
- Strictly enforced — City has active compliance team

### Orange County (Unincorporated)
- **Minimum rental term: 180 days** in most unincorporated areas
- Short-term rentals effectively banned in most residential areas
- Some resort-zoned areas (near Disney) are exceptions

### Osceola County
- More STR-friendly, especially in tourist corridor areas
- Many communities near Disney zoned for short-term rental
- Tourist Development Tax: **6%** on top of state sales tax
- Total tax burden on STR income in Osceola: **~12-13%**

### Seminole County
- Short-term rentals restricted in most residential areas
- Very limited STR activity compared to Osceola

### Lake County
- Regulations vary by municipality
- Clermont has some STR restrictions in residential areas

### Tax Obligations for STR Hosts
| Tax | Rate |
|-----|------|
| Florida Sales Tax | 6% |
| County Surtax (Orange) | 0.5% |
| Tourist Development Tax (Orange) | 6% |
| **Total (Orange County)** | **12.5%** |
| Tourist Development Tax (Osceola) | 6% |
| **Total (Osceola County)** | **12.5%** |

## Condo-Specific Laws

### Chapter 718 (Florida Condominium Act)
- Governs all condominiums in Florida
- Buyers have **15-day rescission period** for new condo purchases from developer
- Resale buyers have **3-day rescission period** after receiving condo documents
- Association must provide buyer with: declaration, bylaws, rules, financial statements, insurance info

### SB 4-D / Condo Safety (Post-Surfside Collapse)
- Passed after 2021 Champlain Towers collapse in Surfside
- **Structural inspections required** for buildings 3+ stories:
  - 30 years after construction (25 years if within 3 miles of coast)
  - Every 10 years thereafter
- **Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS)** required
  - Must fully fund reserves for: roof, structure, electrical, plumbing, waterproofing, windows, HVAC, etc.
  - **No more waiving reserves** for these items
- **Impact**: Driving massive special assessments and HOA fee increases at older condos
- Many older Orlando condos facing $10,000-$100,000+ per unit in special assessments

### Condo Termination
- Florida law allows condo termination (dissolution) with **80% owner vote**
- Used by developers to buy out condos for redevelopment
- Controversial — minority owners can be forced to sell
- Has affected some older Orlando condo buildings

## Construction & Building

### Florida Building Code
- One of the **strictest building codes in the US** (post-Hurricane Andrew reforms)
- All new construction must meet wind resistance standards
- Hurricane-rated windows/doors required in most areas
- Minimum roof standards for wind uplift
- Energy efficiency requirements (Florida Energy Code)

### Impact Fees
- Charged by counties/municipalities on new construction
- Cover schools, transportation, parks, fire/EMS, water/sewer
- Orange County impact fees: **$15,000-$25,000+** per new home
- Osceola County: Similar range
- These are typically paid by the builder but passed to buyer in home price

### Construction Defect Statutes
- **Statute of limitations**: 4 years for latent defects from date of discovery
- **Statute of repose**: 10 years from completion of construction
- Chapter 558: Pre-suit notice requirement before filing construction defect lawsuit
- Buyer should always get a **home inspection** even on new construction

### Common Construction in Orlando
- **Block construction (CBS)**: Concrete block/stucco — standard for most Orlando homes
- **Frame construction**: Some townhomes and second stories
- **Slab-on-grade foundation**: Standard for all Orlando (no basements — water table too high)
- **Stucco exterior**: Dominant, though hardy board/fiber cement becoming popular
- **Tile roofs**: Common in higher-end, very durable but expensive to replace
- **Shingle roofs**: Most common, 15-25 year lifespan in Florida
