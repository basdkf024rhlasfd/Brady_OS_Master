# Community Concierge

Practical first-90-days guidance for Orlando buyers who need help turning a closing into a working life on the ground. Use this file when the question moves beyond "Can I buy?" into "How do I actually get set up once I own the home?"

## When to load this file

Load this file for:

- Utility setup and address changes after closing
- School enrollment logistics, not just school-zone matching
- Healthcare onboarding and insurance basics
- Banking, ITIN, and credit-building questions
- Bilingual professional referrals and vendor handoff
- Portuguese-speaking or international buyers who need an arrival plan

For Brazilian or other foreign-national buyers, pair this file with:

- `kb/15-brazilian-buyer-guide.md`

## Client-Facing Checklist

Use this as the default handoff list for international and relocating buyers.

### Immediate: 0-72 hours after closing

1. Confirm power, water, internet, and trash service are active.
2. Save key local contacts in the phone: insurer, lender, title company, HOA, and primary utility providers.
3. Change delivery address with USPS, Amazon, banks, and employer.
4. Photograph the property condition, serial numbers, and utility account numbers.
5. Verify where shutoffs are located: electrical panel, main water, HVAC disconnect, and gas shutoff if applicable.

### First 30 days

1. Register the homestead filing deadline if the property will be a Florida primary residence.
2. Set up recurring payments for mortgage, HOA, insurance, and utilities.
3. Establish PCP, pediatrician, dentist, and urgent-care preferences.
4. Start school enrollment document collection if children are moving during the school year.
5. Open or stabilize US banking relationships and begin credit-building if needed.

### First 90 days

1. Review actual monthly cost vs. pre-close estimate.
2. Complete vendor introductions for lawn, pest, pool, HVAC, and handyman support.
3. Submit review request only after the buyer feels operationally settled.
4. Add the client to the nurture calendar with the right language and referral context.

## Utility Setup Quickstart

This is an agent-facing outline for post-close setup. Confirm the exact provider by municipality and subdivision before giving instructions.

| Category | Typical Orlando-area provider examples | What to prepare | Agent note |
|---------|------------------------------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Electric | OUC, Duke Energy | Closing date, service address, ID, payment method | Confirm whether service is city- or utility-territory specific |
| Water/Sewer | OUC, TOHO Water Authority, local city utility | Service address, start date, owner name | Many buyers confuse electric and water jurisdictions |
| Internet | Spectrum, AT&T, Quantum Fiber where available | Move-in date, router preference, installation window | For remote workers, confirm fiber availability before close |
| Trash/Recycling | City, county, or HOA-managed | Address, municipality, pickup day | HOA communities may hide this detail in welcome docs |
| Mail forwarding | USPS | Old and new address, start date | Useful for foreign buyers juggling multiple addresses |

### Utility call script

Use this structure when the client is nervous on the phone:

1. "I just closed on a home and need to start service."
2. Give service address and requested start date.
3. Confirm whether a deposit is required.
4. Ask whether a technician visit is needed.
5. Ask for the first bill date and autopay options.

## School Enrollment Navigator

`kb/09-schools-quality-of-life.md` helps with area selection. This section covers the friction after the home is already chosen.

### Core document packet

- Parent photo ID
- Proof of address: deed, lease, settlement statement, or utility bill
- Child birth certificate or passport
- Immunization records
- Prior transcripts or report cards
- Any ESOL, IEP, 504, or custody documentation

### Friction points to anticipate

- Brazilian records often need certified translation before district staff can process them cleanly.
- Families may have a zoned-school answer but still need district-office guidance for late moves, magnet questions, or mid-year placement.
- Vaccine schedules do not always map perfectly; advise families to verify with the district or pediatrician before the first day.
- If the family is moving mid-semester, ask whether they need a soft landing plan: temporary childcare, before/after care, or transportation clarity.

### Agent handoff

Do not try to practice law or school administration. Your role is:

1. Explain the likely document list.
2. Point the buyer to the right district and school contacts.
3. Flag translation or immunization issues early.
4. Stay involved until the family has a named next step.

## Healthcare Onboarding

This is usually the least planned part of a relocation and the fastest way anxiety spikes after closing.

### Minimum setup recommendation

1. Pick one primary care option.
2. Pick one urgent care close to home.
3. Pick one pediatrician if children are involved.
4. Confirm the nearest in-network hospital if insurance is already active.

### Common education points

- Explain the difference between PCP, urgent care, ER, specialist, and telehealth.
- For newly arrived buyers, explain that US care is network-driven and admin-heavy compared with walk-in expectations they may be used to.
- If the buyer is between plans, help them frame the question correctly for a licensed insurance professional instead of improvising medical-benefit advice.

## Banking & Credit Building

Use this section when the buyer has the property but not the financial infrastructure.

### Immediate banking priorities

1. Confirm where mortgage drafts, HOA drafts, and insurance drafts will come from.
2. Open a US checking account if one is still missing.
3. Set up a reserve account for taxes, insurance deductibles, and home repairs.

### Credit-building priorities

1. Start with one secured or entry-level card if the client has little or no US file.
2. Keep utilization low and autopay on.
3. Avoid stacking multiple new credit applications in the first 30 days.
4. If the buyer is a foreign national, separate "ability to buy property" from "ability to build standard US credit quickly."

### Referral categories to maintain

- Portuguese-speaking CPA or tax preparer
- Cross-border financial advisor
- Insurance broker who can explain US coverages clearly
- Immigration attorney when visa status affects planning

## Bilingual Professional Referral Map

The existing business directory is consumer-facing community context. This section is the agent-side referral overlay.

### Highest-priority categories

- CPA / tax preparer
- Insurance agent
- Real estate attorney
- Estate-planning attorney
- Pediatrician / family doctor / dentist
- Mortgage lender familiar with foreign-national scenarios
- Translator / interpreter / notary
- Handyman, HVAC, pest, lawn, pool, locksmith

### Referral standards

Every concierge referral should meet the same baseline:

1. Licensed where applicable
2. Adequately insured
3. Responsive within one business day
4. Clear on pricing and scope
5. No kickback expectations
6. Comfortable serving Portuguese-speaking clients when positioned that way

## Phone / WhatsApp Scripts

These are simple frameworks, not legal or medical advice.

### Utility setup intro

"Hi, I just bought a home in Orlando and need to start service at this address. Can you tell me what documents or deposit you need?"

### School enrollment intro

"We just moved into the school zone and want to confirm the enrollment steps, required documents, and whether any records need translation."

### New-patient healthcare intro

"We are new to the area and want to establish care. Are you accepting new patients, and do you have anyone who can help in Portuguese if needed?"

## 30-60-90 Day Community Onboarding Plan

### Day 30

- Utilities stable
- Internet working
- Core vendor list delivered
- School or childcare next step clear

### Day 60

- Healthcare relationships started
- Banking and autopay stable
- Community introductions made: church, sports, local businesses, or cultural organizations

### Day 90

- Cost-of-ownership reality check completed
- Review and referral ask becomes appropriate
- Nurture track personalized by lifestyle, not just transaction date

## Agent Handoff Workflow

Use this file in combination with `kb/21-nurture-system.md` and `kb/17-brazilian-business-directory.md`.

1. Pre-close: identify language, family, school, healthcare, and banking complexity.
2. Closing week: send the client-facing checklist plus only the referral categories they actually need.
3. Week 2: confirm utilities, locks, insurance, and urgent issues are resolved.
4. Day 30: check whether the buyer has a functioning local support stack.
5. Day 90: only then move from concierge mode into referral-generation mode.

## Limits and caution

- Provider names, hours, and language support can change quickly; verify before presenting specifics.
- Do not promise school placement, visa outcomes, tax treatment, or insurance coverage.
- Use this file to structure the handoff, not to replace licensed professionals.
