You're right, and it's a better story than I expected. Short version: yes, we're cousins through the old Eastern Shore Bull family. Here's what I found.

**Bull and Boole are the same family.** That's not me reaching — the Eastern Shore of Virginia Public Library catalogs the whole lineage under one file titled "Descendants of Tobias Bull I — also spelled Bool, Boole, Boll, Booll." Their compiled database (MilesFiles) files your Boole ancestors and my Bull ancestors under the same surname, in the same two counties. Your branch stayed in Northampton and the spelling drifted to Boole; mine moved up into Accomack on Pungoteague Creek and kept Bull.

**Your lighthouse keeper checks out, to the day.** Your line runs Nicholas Boole (died 1823) → David Nottingham Boole (1821–1896). The database has David marrying Sally Bell on 23 Nov 1840 and dying 1 Feb 1896 — which is the exact same man, same dates, as the Hog Island Lighthouse keeper records (Principal Keeper 1864–1872, buried at Red Bank Baptist in Marionville). The "Nottingham" middle name in your family comes from Nicholas's wife, Elizabeth Nottingham.

A few things that make this fun:

- **Hog Island shows up on both sides, a couple centuries apart.** Your David kept the light there in the 1860s. One of my Eastern Shore ancestors, John Floyd, patented Hog Island back in 1681 — he's literally "John Floyd of Hog Island" in the records. Same speck of barrier island, different centuries, both families on it.
- **Your David Bull had a backbone.** The Northampton court records have him getting indicted in 1861 for standing up in a Birdsnest tavern and arguing that slaveowners had "no right of property in their slaves." They jailed him three months for it. Union troops freed him and put him in charge of the Hog Island light. That's a good ancestor to have.
- **The deep roots are shared too.** The Bulls first turn up as "Bulluck" on the 1666 Northampton tithables, and the Accomack branch settled a stretch of Pungoteague Creek that locals still called "Bull Run." That's your deep history as much as mine.

**And here's the part that actually nails it.** A certified genealogist named Mary Frances Carey worked up the Accomack Bulls from the original county records, and she documents the fork. Tobias Bull I (died 1687) had, among his sons, two that matter here: **Tobias Bull II**, who carried the Bull name down to me, and **Richard Bull, born about 1686**, who moved to Northampton and died there in 1728 — and whose children are recorded under the spelling **Bool**. Carey's exact line: *"The Bulls in Northampton County continued to spell their name as Bool or Boole."* That Richard is the head of your branch. So the common ancestor isn't a guess — it's **Tobias Bull I**, and our two lines split at his sons: your Richard, my Tobias II.

**One honest caveat so I'm not overselling it.** What's airtight is the top (Tobias Bull I as the shared ancestor) and the bottom (your line down from Nicholas Boole, mine down from Daniel Bull). The one stretch I'm still filling in is the middle of your Boole line — exactly which of Richard's children carries down to your Nicholas. So the *connection* is documented; the *precise* cousin number (I'd call it 8th cousins, give or take one) wiggles only because of those middle generations, not because of any doubt that we're related.

Either way — hello, cousin. If you ever want the full write-up I've been building on the Bull family, I'm happy to send it. And if you've got anything from your side on the Booles between Richard (born ~1686) and your David's father Nicholas, that's the one stretch that would lock every generation into place.
