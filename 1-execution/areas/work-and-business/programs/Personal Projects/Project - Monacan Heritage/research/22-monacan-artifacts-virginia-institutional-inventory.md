# 22 — Monacan Artifacts: Virginia Institutional Inventory

**Compiled:** 2026-05-09
**Scope:** Every institution holding material identified as Monacan or removed from Monacan-occupation territory (Virginia Piedmont — Albemarle, Amherst, Buckingham, Bedford, Nelson, Powhatan, Goochland, Hanover, Henrico, Rockbridge, Orange, Fluvanna Counties, plus the Brunswick / Fort Christanna corridor and Cumberland / James-Rivanna confluence).
**Standard:** Public records only. Federal Register notices, ProPublica's NAGPRA Database, institutional websites, finding aids, peer-reviewed and press citations. Login walls flagged. Cred 5 = Federal Register or institutional primary source / 4 = mainstream press / 3 = secondary aggregation.
**Scope rule (Phase 2 v2):** Federal recognition (2018) flips Monacan Indian Nation from "state-recognized" to "federally-recognized Indian tribe" under 25 U.S.C. § 3001(2), unlocking full NAGPRA standing for every federally-funded institution. The repatriation legal posture **before** 2018 (when MIN had to use special petitions or work via DHR custodianship) and **after** 2018 (NAGPRA-direct) is a load-bearing distinction throughout this file.
**Feeds:** UVA Stewardship Essay (deliverable C5) + Recognition Push companion (deliverable C6) + Demise + Divergence chapter (B6 / file 23).

## Per-Institution Sections

### 1. Smithsonian Institution — NMNH and NMAI

**Standing:** *None* under NAGPRA. Smithsonian operates under the **National Museum of the American Indian Act (NMAIA, 1989; amended 1996)** — same spirit (inventory, consult, repatriate), but excluded from the NAGPRA Federal Register notice system. ProPublica's database explicitly states: *"the Smithsonian Institution is not listed in the database because its repatriation process falls under the National Museum of the American Indian Act."*

**Volume:** NMNH holds ~30,000 human remains across all cultures. System-wide Smithsonian has offered ~7,000 Native American individuals for repatriation since 1984; ~5,000 repatriated. NMNH specifically: 6,219 Indigenous remains repatriated or made available.

**Monacan-specific strands:**

1. **Bushnell Monasukapanough fieldwork** — formal excavation 1911 + 1931, Smithsonian-sponsored. NAA Manuscript 2255 ("The Indian Grave — A Monacan Site in Albemarle County, Virginia") is 8-page typescript. The 1930 SMC vol. 82 no. 12 *Five Monacan Towns* publication is foundational. Material removed in 1911/1931 seasons would have flowed into NMNH/NAA accessioning. **Public NMNH catalog search interface returned no Monacan-specific NIC results in this scan; full query requires browser-based interaction (login wall).**
2. **James Mooney Papers** — NAA Collection NAA.1992-34. Late-19th-century Virginia Siouan fieldwork; pre-Plecker. Documentary, not skeletal. (master sources C5)
3. **Frank G. Speck Papers** — NAA portion + APS portion. 1914–1942 photographs and field notes on Monacan families. (master sources C6)
4. **Rassawek documentation (1880s+).** Per *Save Rassawek* / National Trust: "Beginning in the 1880s, the Smithsonian documented the important archaeological resources and extensive human burials at Rassawek." **Specific Smithsonian-removed materials never publicly enumerated under NMAIA repatriation track.** This is the single largest known Smithsonian Monacan-specific holding still in legal limbo.

**MIN consultation:** No formal dated NMNH/NMAI–MIN consultation surfaced in public record. Smithsonian consultation cadence increased after the Jan 12, 2024 NAGPRA-revision spillover and AMNH-style 2024 closures, but Monacan-specific activity is not visible in this scan.

**FOIA posture:** Smithsonian partially FOIA-exempt as trust instrumentality. Repatriation routes through NMNH Repatriation Office (repatriation@si.edu) or NMAI Repatriation Office. **2018 federal recognition of MIN activates NMAIA's full government-to-government framework** — MIN qualifies as a federally-recognized Indian tribe under the same definition NMAIA uses.

**Keystone insight:** The Bushnell + Mooney + Speck Smithsonian Monacan fieldwork (1880s–1942) plus the 1911/1931 Monasukapanough excavations plus the 1880s+ Rassawek documentation almost certainly make Smithsonian **the single largest holder of Monacan-territory material on Earth**. The NMAIA-not-NAGPRA legal status means that holding has the lowest public visibility of any major institution. ProPublica's exclusion of the Smithsonian from its database masks the magnitude of the gap.

### 2. Virginia Department of Historic Resources (VDHR)

State agency (Virginia SHPO, Secretariat of Natural and Historic Resources). Subject to NAGPRA. Located 2801 Kensington Avenue, Richmond. Curates more than **6 million archaeological objects**; ProPublica reports **880 Native American remains, 169 available, 711 not available** — the largest in-state un-repatriated count. Email: NAGPRA@dhr.virginia.gov.

**Filing status:** DHR has filed inventories and is "currently engaged in a systematic review of its archaeological collections." A **temporary moratorium on access to all indigenous cultural material** is in place; access only "on a case-by-case basis as determined appropriate through consultation with Native American Tribes." Collections Room closes Oct 1, 2025 for construction.

**MIN consultation:** Deep, decades-long. The **1990–2000 Hayes Creek Mound repatriation** is the canonical pre-2018 Monacan precedent. When Mann S. Valentine II's 1901 excavation deposited remains of ~400 individuals at the Valentine Museum, those transferred to DHR. Per NAGPRA Review Committee recommendation + Secretary of the Interior's concurrence, in 2000 DHR repatriated **105 individuals from Hayes Creek to MIN** — at the time still state-recognized only. MIN successfully petitioned the federal government, refused intermediary tribe routing, and once a Federal Register notice with no competing claim was published, the remains were cleared for return. DHR was also custodian of the Rapidan Mound material before UVA's 1998 reburial.

**Counties:** DHR is the de facto state archaeology repository for every Piedmont/Blue Ridge county including Albemarle, Amherst, Buckingham, Bedford, Nelson, Powhatan, Goochland, Hanover, Henrico, Rockbridge, Orange, Fluvanna, Cumberland.

**Highest-leverage gap:** The 711 unrepatriated remains, most likely "culturally unidentifiable" pre-2024 NAGPRA — a category the Jan 2024 NAGPRA revision (43 CFR Part 10) was engineered to dissolve.

### 3. University of Virginia (UVA)

Subject to NAGPRA. ProPublica reports **5 remains, all 5 available** — **almost certainly understated** because it reflects only the Nov 14, 2024 NIC.

**Federal Register Nov 14, 2024 (2024-26454):** Site **44OR1 Rapidan Mound, Orange County**, excavated **August 2, 1988** by **Jeffrey Hantman**. Material: "teeth NO36 L1-alluv 8/2/88" + "23 contexts with bone that is potentially human (including charred material)." Cultural affiliation: "reasonable connection... and the **Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians** and the **Monacan Indian Nation**." Repatriation eligible **on or after Dec 16, 2024**. Hantman's research described as focused on Monacan lands in **Albemarle, Nelson, and Orange Counties**.

**Pre-existing repatriations:** October 1998 Rapidan Mound — Hantman's project repatriated remains of "hundreds of Monacans" to MIN for reburial at Bear Mountain cemetery. The 2024 NIC reflects only the *retained sample* the Tribal Council approved keeping for future research. Monasukapanough — UVA + Archaeological Conservancy + Monacan Nation 2001+ collaboration documented occupation 1300–1670; material retention path not transparent in public records.

**MIN consultation:** Substantive. Karenne Wood (Monacan, UVA PhD 2016, deceased 2019) was UVA's most embedded Monacan academic. Diane Shields (now Tribal Chief) was named in Hantman's 1990s Rapidan consultation per Archaeology Magazine 2000.

**Field-school / collection footprint:** UVA Anthropology runs the Interdisciplinary Archaeology Program. UVA-affiliated field schools at Monticello (private foundation) and Poplar Forest (Bedford, Monacan ancestral). UVA Piedmont projects at Rapidan, Wood Glen, Bremo Bluff, and Monasukapanough since 1985 likely have residual collection footprints beyond the 2024 NIC.

**Karenne Wood's UVA-specific papers — open question.** Her dissertation ("The Language Ghost," 2016) and Virginia Indian Programs work at Virginia Humanities generated documentary and field material. No dedicated processed paper collection located at Albert and Shirley Small Special Collections Library as of file 14's scan. The Discovery Virginia archive + Virginia Indian Archive (which Wood produced) are the main extant document corpus. **Status of her UVA-specific papers, manuscripts, recordings, and field notes undetermined.** Five-to-seven-year processing window is closing.

**Why UVA matters beyond its 5-remain count:** Land + scholarly debt + named-Monacan deficit. UVA sits on Monacan ancestral territory. Its anthropology department generated the modern Monacan archaeological record. Its first Monacan PhD is deceased. Its tribal liaison is non-Monacan. The ratio of moral standing to compliance volume is most out of balance here — and UVA is the institution most responsive to a stewardship-essay framing rather than a pure FOIA / NAGPRA push. **This is what the v2 UVA Stewardship Essay (deliverable C5) frames.**

### 4. William & Mary, Department of Anthropology

Subject to NAGPRA. **Fed Reg Oct 11, 2022 (2022-22038):** **158 individuals** of Native American human remains + associated funerary objects, removed from VA sites **1967–1978**. "A detailed assessment of the human remains was made by William and Mary's Anthropology Department professional staff in consultation with representatives of the **Chickahominy Indian Tribe; Monacan Indian Nation; Nansemond Indian Nation; Pamunkey Indian Tribe; Rappahannock Tribe, Inc.; Upper Mattaponi Tribe; and the Mattaponi Indian Tribe**." Notice references the **fall line** as historical boundary "separating the territory of the Indian Tribes that comprised the Monacan to the west, and the allied Tribes of the Powhatan Chiefdom to the east." ProPublica: 158 made available, 0 retained — 100% rate of currently inventoried.

**WMCAR (Center for Archaeological Research, est. May 1988):** CRM-services unit. Late Archaic Dismal Swamp, Middle Woodland James-York Peninsula, Late Woodland village studies. Piedmont/Monacan-territory work not centered in public-facing publications. **Unclear whether WMCAR holds Piedmont accessions outside the Department of Anthropology's NAGPRA inventory.** Worth a direct query. WMCAR Special Collections finding aid: scrcguides.libraries.wm.edu/repositories/2/resources/840.

**American Indian Resource Center (AIRC):** Dir. Dr. Danielle Moretti-Langholtz. Land-acknowledgment-level partnership (file 14). Not curatorial.

### 5. Valentine Museum (Richmond)

Private museum, federally-supported; subject to NAGPRA. ProPublica: 4 remains, 4 available, 0 retained. Apr 27, 2021 NIC + Aug 3, 2022 NITR.

**Hayes Creek Mound (canonical case):** Mann S. Valentine II excavated Hayes Creek Mound in Rockbridge County in **September 1901**, accumulating remains and material from "more than 400 individuals" displayed in the museum library. By 1990 transferred to DHR's care. **In 2000, DHR repatriated 105 individuals from Hayes Creek to MIN** — the canonical pre-2018 Monacan repatriation precedent. The Valentine's residual 4-remain inventory + 2022 cultural-items NITR are the post-Hayes-Creek tail.

### 8. Virginia Tech (VT)

**NAGPRA standing:** Federally-funded. Subject. **No NAGPRA Federal Register notice filed for Native American human remains has been located in this scan.**

**Possible holdings:** VT operates a Sociology + Anthropology program, an Indigenous Community Garden, and the AIICC (American Indian and Indigenous Community Center). ### 9. Bridgewater College (Special Collections)

Subject to NAGPRA. **~4,000 Native American artifacts in special collections; at least 6 individuals of human remains + funerary belongings**, mostly from **Rev. Reuel B. Pritchett's 1954 donation**. Items removed from gravesites in **Virginia, Tennessee, and Arkansas**, mostly early-to-mid 20th century (Cardinal News, May 4, 2023). Archivist: **Stephanie Gardner**.

**MIN consultation: yes — direct, named, dated. MIN representatives, including a spiritual leader, visited Bridgewater in January 2022 to view funerary belongings and ancestral remains.** Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians is second consulting tribe. Active repatriation paperwork in process as of May 2023; items under research moratorium since 2022. ProPublica's 6/1/5 split reflects "in process" coding. **Best-case-scenario benchmark of how a small institution navigates NAGPRA-MIN compliance.**

### 10. Colonial National Historical Park (Yorktown), NPS

Federal agency, subject to NAGPRA. **Fed Reg Nov 7, 2022 (2022-24220):** Col. Wirt Robinson collected remains + artifacts from VA counties **Amherst, Appomattox, Buckingham, Campbell, and Nelson** in late 19th–early 20th centuries. 1940: NPS purchased collection from Robinson's widow; placed at Colonial NHP / Jamestown museum. 2000: loaned to MIN for analysis by Jeffrey Hantman. 2008: **14 human bones + 1 human tooth (2 adults + 1 juvenile)** determined; artifacts (none determined to be associated funerary objects) **deaccessioned and transferred to MIN**. 2022 NIC formalizes the federal record. "All of the Virginia counties from which the human remains and artifacts were removed have been **identified as Monacan ancestral territory**." Best-case-scenario benchmark of multi-decade federal-agency / university / tribe collaboration.

### 20. Monacan Indian Nation Ancestral Museum (Bear Mountain)

Tribal-controlled museum on the 7.5 acres returned by the Episcopal Church in 1995 (Indian Mission Episcopal Church site, est. 1908; log-cabin school is a National Historic Landmark). Open Thursday–Saturday, 10–3, $5/person; groups by appt. Email enrollment@monacannation.gov for genealogy queries.

Holdings: pottery shards, pipes, beads, fishing lures, games (standing exhibit); **facial reconstructions of Hayes Creek ancestors** (sculptor Sharon Long, VFH grant — the Monacan Tribal Council's 1990s collaborative-archaeology signature decision); **158 artifacts** returned by Mary Fleming Trumbull (5th-generation Galt descendant, Fluvanna) on May 25, 2023 to Lou Branham (Director); items returned by Fluvanna Historical Society in Sept 2021; Wirt Robinson Collection artifacts deaccessioned and transferred from Colonial NHP in 2008; other family-collection donations not all individually publicized.

Funding via Virginia Foundation for the Humanities, Sweetgrass Foundation, Easley Foundation; operated by the Monacan Nation Cultural Foundation. Tribal sovereignty is the legal frame, not NAGPRA.
