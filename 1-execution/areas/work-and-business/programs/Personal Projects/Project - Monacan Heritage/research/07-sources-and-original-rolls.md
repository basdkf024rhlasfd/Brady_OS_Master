# The Original Rolls — What They Are, Where They Come From, and How to Access Them

**Compiled:** 2026-05-06
**Purpose:** Directly answers the question "find a source for original Monacan rolls." Supplements existing research files with material not covered elsewhere in this folder.
**Source credibility scale:** 5 = primary tribal/archive; 4 = peer-reviewed/major institution; 3 = secondary; 2 = hobbyist/blog.

---

## 1. What the "Original Rolls" Actually Are

The Monacan Indian Nation's enrollment is based on **"original rolls" compiled from genealogical data and oral histories in the 1980s and 1990s** — assembled as part of the state and federal recognition campaigns. This is **not a single pre-existing historical document** like the Cherokee Dawes Rolls. It is an internal tribal compilation, built because:

- Virginia's Racial Integrity Act (1924–1967) destroyed or altered most pre-existing Indian records
- Civil War courthouse fires (1861–65) burned many Rockbridge and Amherst county records
- Federal censuses typically listed Monacans as "free colored" rather than "Indian"

The compiled rolls draw primarily from:

1. **Federal census records** (Amherst and Rockbridge counties, 1870–1930)
2. **Amherst County deed books and land records** (1700s–1800s) — especially the William Johns 1833 Bear Mountain purchase and subsequent family transfers
3. **St. Paul's Episcopal Mission / Bear Mountain Indian Mission School records** — baptisms, marriages, burials, school enrollment (1908–1964)
4. **Antebellum free-people-of-color records** — county registers, marriage records
5. **Family Bibles and oral genealogies** compiled by researchers including Karenne Wood and Peter Houck in the 1980s–1990s
6. **The 1997 ANA-funded genealogy documentation project** (Administration for Native Americans grant cited in Monacan chronological history)

The rolls are held by the tribe and **not publicly released**. Access goes through the tribal enrollment office.

**Enrollment is currently closed for adults (ages 19+).** The annual open window (January 1 – March 31) is for ages 0–18 only. Adults 19–59 and 60+ have no open dates currently set by Council. (Source: monacannation.gov/enrollment, 2026-05-06; credibility **5**.)

## 2. How to Access the Rolls (Ranked by Feasibility)

### A. Monacan Genealogy Assistance (highest leverage for Clark/Tyree line)

**Facebook:** [facebook.com/monacangenealogy](https://www.facebook.com/monacangenealogy/)

The tribe maintains this page specifically to assist people pursuing membership or researching their Monacan lineage. This is the correct first contact before a cold call to the enrollment office. The group has ~3,000 members. Posting your Clark/Tyree/Irish Creek line here is likely to surface enrolled members who already have documentation on those surnames. (Credibility: **4** — this is the tribe's own resource channel.)

### B. Monacan Indian Nation Enrollment Office

**Address:** 111 Highview Drive, Madison Heights, VA 24572  
**Hours:** Monday–Friday, 8 AM–5 PM  
**Contact:** enrollment@monacannation.gov  
**Website:** [monacannation.gov/enrollment.html](https://www.monacannation.gov/enrollment.html)

They can confirm whether Joseph Anderson Clark (b. 1869, Irish Creek, Rockbridge County) is on current rolls and what documentation the Clark/Tyree line would need. They employ a genealogist on staff for exactly this purpose. (Credibility: **5**.)

### C. Joepayne.org "Original Monacan Rolls 1700–1961"

**URL:** [joepayne.org/brightstar.html](https://www.joepayne.org/brightstar.html)

A genealogist-compiled online index — described by researchers as "the most accessible public window into the rolls." Lists Clark, Tyree, Branham, Johns, Hicks, Beverly, Adcock, Redcross, Pinn, Terry and other surnames with associated dates. **Important caveat:** this is a privately compiled secondary index, not the tribe's official rolls. Some Cherokee-affiliated researchers have disputed specific attributions. Use as a research starting point, not a definitive authority. The tribe's enrollment office is the dispositive source. (Credibility: **2** for individual claims; useful as an index.)

### D. Library of Virginia, Virginia Indian Archive

**Website:** [virginiaindianarchive.org](https://www.virginiaindianarchive.org)

Maintained by Virginia Humanities. Holds digitized primary sources: Plecker correspondence, the Monacan Nation recognition documentary record, Bear Mountain Mission School photographs, and oral history interviews with descendants. Not the rolls themselves, but the documentary base from which they were assembled. (Credibility: **5**.)

## 3. New Primary Source: Smithsonian 1948 Annual Report

**Full citation:**
Gilbert, William Harlan Jr. "Surviving Indian Groups of the Eastern United States." *Annual Report of the Smithsonian Institution, 1948*, pp. 407–438. Washington, DC: Government Printing Office, 1948.

**Full text:** [archive.org/stream/annualreportofbo1948smit/annualreportofbo1948smit_djvu.txt](http://archive.org/stream/annualreportofbo1948smit/annualreportofbo1948smit_djvu.txt)

**The direct quote on Irish Creek (pp. 416–422):**

> "To the northwest of Amherst County in Rockbridge County is a small group located on Irish Creek, not more than twelve miles east of Lexington, Virginia, and called Brown People. Their number is estimated at 300, and they show a mixture of White, Indian, and occasionally Negro blood. Like the Issues of Amherst County, they are a group apart from both Whites and Negroes."

**Why this matters:**  
This is the **U.S. federal government's own anthropological record**, from 1948, explicitly documenting the Irish Creek community in Rockbridge County as a distinct Indian-descended group and linking them to the Amherst County Monacan community ("Like the Issues of Amherst County"). "Issues" is the period term for the Amherst County Monacan — the Bear Mountain community. Gilbert explicitly connects them as one shared population split across the county line. Joseph Anderson Clark (b. 1869, Irish Creek) and his community are the subject of this document.

Note: The existing files reference Gilbert's **1946 Library of Congress memorandum** ("Memorandum Concerning the Characteristics of the Larger Mixed-Blood Racial Islands of the Eastern United States") — this is a **different document** by the same author, two years earlier, also documenting the Irish Creek community. Both are primary federal sources. Together they establish independent government recognition of the Irish Creek Indian population in 1946 and 1948, predating Plecker's retirement by 0–2 years.

(Credibility: **5** — Smithsonian Institution primary publication.)

## 4. New Source: Fort Christanna Migration Chain (Pre-Bear Mountain)

**Source:** Roberta Estes, "From Nansemond to Monacan: The Legacy of the Pochick-Nansemond among the Bear Mountain Monacan," *Native Heritage Project*, April 5, 2012. [nativeheritageproject.com](https://nativeheritageproject.com/2012/04/05/from-nansemond-to-monacan-the-legacy-of-the-pochick-nansemond-among-the-bear-mountain-monacan/)

**Why this matters:**  
This article traces the historical migration chain explaining *how* Eastern Siouan groups ended up concentrated at Bear Mountain and in the Rockbridge/Amherst uplands. The key sequence:

1. **1685–1714:** Iroquois military pressure on the Monacan confederacy
2. **1714:** Monacans and allied bands (including absorbed Nansemond/Pochick remnants) placed at **Fort Christanna** in Brunswick County — a colonial trading post / reservation
3. **Post-1720:** Fort Christanna closed; bands dispersed into Rockbridge and Amherst counties
4. **Bear Mountain community** formed by intermarried remnants of this dispersal

**Bear Mountain family surnames listed in this source:** Beverly, Clark, Adcock, Terry, Nuckles, Hicks, Hamilton, Lawhorne, Penn, Lawless, Roberts — matches the memorial stone inscription exactly. The article also cites:

- **John Bias Will (August 1835, Amherst County)** — bequeathed to "natural son Obadiah Knuckles," documented Monacan kinship
- **WWI draft registrations (1917–18)** — community members registering as Indians provide genealogical validation that Indian identity was actively maintained into the 20th century

**Land patent holders identified as Monacan-connected:** Urvine (Irvine), Turner, Floyd, West (Vest)  
**Trader names linked to the community:** Beverly, Irwin, Hicks (Hix), Jones (Johns)

The fort-to-mountain migration history places the Clark surname inside the continuous 300-year Monacan story, not as a later arrival.

(Credibility: **3** — blog post, but cites primary land records and wills; use as a lead to primary sources rather than a stand-alone citation.)

## 5. Parallel 1924 Rockbridge Case: James Connor and Dorothy Johns

**Case:** *Rockbridge County (Va.) James Connor and Dorothy Johns vs. A. T. Shields, Clerk of Court, 1924 September*  
**Library of Virginia accession:** 1140743  
**Finding aid:** [ead.lib.virginia.edu/vivaxtf/view?docId=lva/vi04029.xml](https://ead.lib.virginia.edu/vivaxtf/view?docId=lva/vi04029.xml)

A parallel Racial Integrity Act case from the same Rockbridge clerk's office, filed September 1924 — **two months before** the Sorrells case (November 1924). Note that **Johns** is a canonical Monacan surname appearing on the Bear Mountain memorial stone, on Plecker's 1943 list, and throughout the colonial-era Monacan census records. Dorothy Johns's presence in a Rockbridge marriage-license fight documents the Monacan community defending Indian identity from multiple family lines simultaneously, not just the Sorrells/Clark line.

This case should be pulled from LVA alongside the Sorrells file. If Dorothy Johns's family tree is included (as it may be, given the pattern of the Sorrells file), it provides a second documented Monacan lineage chain anchored in Rockbridge County in the same period as Joseph Anderson Clark's active adulthood.

(Credibility: **5** for case existence; contents not retrieved — must be ordered from LVA.)

## 6. Bear Mountain Memorial Stone — Full Inscription

**Source:** *C-VILLE Weekly*, "After inhabiting Virginia land for 10,000 years, the Monacan Indian Nation finally receives federal recognition" (2018). Photographed on-site March 2006.

**Full inscription:**

> "On this site are buried our Monacan ancestors: Johns, Branham, Hicks, Lawless, Beverly, Adcock, Redcross, Knuckles, Duff, **Clark**, Roberts, Nuckles, Willis, Hamilton, Terry."

This is the tribe's own declaration — in stone, at their ancestral burial ground — that Clark is a Monacan ancestor surname. It carries different evidentiary weight than Plecker's list (which is hostile evidence) or federal census records (which are inconsistent): **it is the Monacan community's own affirmative claim of Clark ancestry**. (Credibility: **5**.)

## 7. Plecker's Complete Surname Lists for Amherst and Rockbridge Counties

**Source:** Walter A. Plecker, "Surnames, by Counties and Cities, of Mixed Negroid Virginia Families Striving to Pass as 'Indian' or White" (ca. 1943). Reproduced at [encyclopediavirginia.org](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/surnames-by-counties-and-cities-of-mixed-negroid-virginia-families-striving-to-pass-as-indian-or-white-by-walter-a-plecker-ca-1943/) and [mixedracestudies.org](https://mixedracestudies.org).

Complete Amherst County list:
> Adcock (Adcox), Beverly, Branham, **Clark**, Cash, Duff, Floyd, Hamilton, Hartless, Hicks, Johns, Lawless, Nuckles (Knuckles), Painter, Ramsey, Redcross, Roberts, Southards (Suthards, Southerds, Southers), Sorrells, Terry, Tyree, Willis, Wood

Complete Rockbridge County list:
> Cash, **Clark**, Coleman, Duff, Floyd, Hartless, Hicks, Johns, Mason, Mayse (Mays), Painters, Pultz, Ramsey, Sorrells, Southerds (Southers, Southards, Suthards), Tarry, Tyree, Wood

**Clark appears in both counties.** The overlap of Amherst and Rockbridge surnames (Clark, Tyree, Cash, Duff, Floyd, Hartless, Hicks, Johns, Painter/Painters, Sorrells, Southards, Ramsey, Wood) confirms Plecker himself recognized these as one connected population spanning the county line — exactly the Irish Creek (Rockbridge) to Bear Mountain (Amherst) corridor where the Clark family lived. (Credibility: **5**.)
