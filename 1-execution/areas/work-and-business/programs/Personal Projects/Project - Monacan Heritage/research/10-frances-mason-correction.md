# Correction — Frances Elizabeth Mason is a Step-Ancestor, Not Biological

**Filed:** 2026-05-06
**Source:** Brady's expanded FamilySearch trace of Frances Elizabeth Mason (LVTK-X5K), pasted in 2026-05-06 19:55:23.
**Importance:** High. Corrects a significant claim made in the May 6 whitepaper and the May 6 12-noon turn.

## The correction

In the original whitepaper draft and my May 6 turn discussing the FamilySearch tree, I stated that Frances Elizabeth Mason (b. 1820) was Brady's direct paternal-line ancestor and that this connected the Clark line genetically to the Pedlar Creek Mason / 1883 LDS-convert Indian community.

**That claim is wrong as a biological assertion.**

Frances Elizabeth Mason married Nelson Harrison Clark on 21 March 1840 in Amherst, Virginia. **Nelson Harrison Clark had been previously married, and Preston Harrison Clark (b. 1821) — Brady's documented 4th-great-grandfather — was a child of that first marriage.** Preston was born 19 years before Nelson married Frances. He is Frances's stepson, not her biological son.

The FamilySearch tree explicitly tags the first five "children" in Nelson Harrison Clark's household as "Step – Frances Elizabeth Mason." Brady's biological line runs through one of those step-children (Preston), not through Frances's own nine biological children.

## What is still true about Frances Elizabeth Mason

The Mason connection still matters for understanding the social and cultural context of the family, even though it is not a biological link:

1. **Frances was on the Monacan Rolls as Indian.** That is a documented fact about her own identity.
2. **Her father Jesse Mason (b. 1775)** is in the Mason kinship that produced the LDS-converting Pedlar Creek community. (Note: there is a different Jesse Mason, age 50 in 1850 = b. ca. 1800, classified Mulatto in District 51 household 818. Whether this is the same person, his son, or unrelated requires further research.)
3. **Her mother Polly Ann Branham (1792–1828)** is from the Branham family — one of the top Monacan-community surnames. Her grandfather Benjamin Branham (1728–1795) sits in the documented Bear Mountain Native lineage. Branhams were chiefs of the modern Monacan Nation through 2018 federal recognition.
4. **She raised Preston Harrison Clark as her stepson** from his early years (after she married Nelson in 1840). Preston was 19 at the marriage, so the influence is more cultural than developmental — but Frances would have been the matriarch of the household for decades after.
5. **Her own nine biological children with Nelson Harrison Clark are Brady's step-cousins** through marriage. They include Nelson Madison Clark (1840–1909), Susan Josephine Clark, Pheby J Clark (married Samuel G Beverly), Rebecca Frances Clark (married Wilbourne Hayden Holmes), Charles Thomas Clark Sr. (married Lydia Johns). These step-cousin marriages connect into Beverly, Holmes, Robinson, and Johns surnames — all on Plecker's 1943 hit list. The community network is still woven together.

## What this means for the Native-ancestry evidence chain

Before this correction, the cleanest "documented Native ancestor in Brady's direct paternal line" was Frances Elizabeth Mason. After this correction, **there is no clean documented Native ancestor in Brady's direct paternal Clark line above Preston Harrison Clark.** The line goes Joseph Anderson Clark (on joepayne.org informal Monacan marriage roll, but not yet confirmed on official tribal roll) → Nelson Alexander Clark → Preston Harrison Clark → Nelson Harrison Clark + UNKNOWN FIRST WIFE.

The Native-ancestry evidence chain in Brady's direct biological line now rests on:

1. **The maternal Cash / Sorrells / Cooper line** — confirmed mulatto in 1850 federal census via William Wesley Cooper (b. 1805). This is the cleanest direct-biological documentary evidence of mixed-race classification.
2. **Joseph Anderson Clark on the joepayne.org informal Monacan marriage list** (1906 marriage to Mary S. Clark). Not the same as official tribal enrollment, but confirms the community recognized JAC as one of their own.
3. **Mary Susan Clark's documented Sorrells maternal grandmother** (Mary Elizabeth Sorrells b. 1840) — connecting MSC's line to the same Sorrells family the 1924–25 Atha Sorrells case rested on.
4. **The unknown first wife of Nelson Harrison Clark** — open question. If she came from one of the Indian-descended community surnames (Mason, Tyree, Sorrells, Painter, Branham, Cooper, Hicks, Anderson), that would document Native ancestry directly in the paternal Clark line. If she was from a settler family with no community connection, then the paternal Clark line is functionally Anglo and the Native ancestry comes entirely through (a) the maternal Cash/Sorrells/Cooper side and (b) the cultural/community network around Preston Harrison Clark growing up in his stepmother's household.
