## Headline finding

**Peter Curry was a free Black taxpayer in Rockbridge County in 1849 and 1850.** Documented in two independent BHR datasets:
- *Free Black People in Rockbridge 1778–1865*, row 399: `Curry, Peter | PP 1849-1850` (PP = Personal Property tax records)
- *Taxpayers 1782–1850*, row 94: `Curry, Peter | First Year 1849 | Last Year 1850`

This is almost certainly the same Peter Curry referenced in the *Sorrells v. Shields* (1924–25) case file family tree as "purchased at auction" by Joseph Anderson Clark Sr. (b. 1797). The Native Heritage Project's 2012 article on Atha Sorrells repeats this. The new finding establishes that Peter Curry **survived enslavement, was manumitted or otherwise freed, and was a free Black property-paying resident of Rockbridge County by the late 1840s.**

The earlier research conclusion (in the agent report on Curry/Steph genealogy) that "no Curry surname appears in the Rockbridge Black record" was wrong. It was based on the Spurgeon/Stanton 2022 USCT paper, which only covers Civil War soldiers and would not catch a man already in his 50s+ in 1849.

This is the lineage backbone. **A free Black Curry family was living in rural Rockbridge County (Walker's Creek, Hays Creek) through the 1870s.** Samuel Curry (b. ~1824) was a free Black blacksmith violently killed in 1873; his parents are named **John and Lucy Curry**, his wife was **Rachel Curry**, and his son **John Curry** was old enough to be informant on the death record. Four years later, **John S. Curry and Elizabeth Curry** lost an infant son named Marshall Bell Curry on Hays Creek. John S. is plausibly Samuel's son John, now an adult head of household.

1. **Peter Curry** — purchased at auction by Joe Clark Sr. (Brady's ancestor), eventually freed, on Rockbridge tax rolls 1849–1850.
2. **John and Lucy Curry of Walker's Creek** — the documented parents of Samuel Curry (b. ~1824). Plausibly Peter Curry's son or close kin who took his surname into freedom.
3. **Samuel Curry** (b. ~1824, killed 1873) — free Black blacksmith, Walker's Creek.
4. **John Curry / John S. Curry** — Samuel's son, alive and married to Elizabeth by 1877, residing on Hays Creek.

## Implication for the Steph Curry connection

**Mack Curry** — Steph Curry's earliest documented Curry ancestor — was born in Virginia in **1824**, exact location previously undocumented. The Augusta County Curry line's earliest figure shares the *exact birth year* of **Samuel Curry** of Rockbridge County (b. ~1824, parents John and Lucy Curry).

This does **not** prove Mack Curry and Samuel Curry are the same person — Samuel was clearly still in Rockbridge through 1873 while Mack was in Augusta County. But it raises the live possibility that Mack Curry was a brother (also born to John and Lucy Curry of Walker's Creek) who relocated north up the Shenandoah Valley to Augusta County — a common antebellum/post-emancipation Black migration pattern.

If that's true:
- Mack Curry's parents would be **John and Lucy Curry of Walker's Creek, Rockbridge County**.
- Mack and Samuel would be **brothers**.
- Their grandfather (or great-uncle) could be **Peter Curry**, the man Joe Clark Sr. owned.
- Brady's family and Steph Curry's family would have a **documented historical entanglement** through the slaveholder/enslaved relationship between Joe Clark Sr. and Peter Curry — not a blood connection, but a moral and historical one worth acknowledging.

Confidence: **plausible but unproven.** The single missing data point is Mack Curry's parentage. Augusta County Freedmen's Bureau records (1865–1866 cohabitation registers) and the 1870 census for Augusta County are the next places to look.

**Notable absence:** Peter Curry does NOT appear in the *Emancipations* dataset or the *Register of Free Negroes*. He paid personal property tax in 1849–1850 but the manumission event itself is not indexed. Either it predates 1803 (unlikely given Joe Clark Sr.'s 1797 birth year), it happened informally, or it was recorded under a different name/document type.

**Also notable absence:** No Currys in the 1867 Qualified Colored Electors list. Samuel Curry was alive then (he died 1873 at age 49, so age 43 in 1867) — he should have been eligible to vote. His omission may reflect a registration gap, residency outside the township covered, or the standard 1867 under-registration pattern. Worth flagging.
