Questions to be asked are: 1) Are these your Cherokee family names? 2) Can you show any proof that they are Cherokee? 3) Do you want the Monacan to claim your familys heritage, if they are Cherokee and not Monacan? 4) What are you willing to do about it? 5) And most important: Are others so much in need of tribal status or a tribal card, that they believe you would allow them to use your family names, without proper and accurate geneology?"

This framing matters: it tells us that the names on this list are names Bright Star saw *somewhere* identified as Monacan and was actively contesting. He had an incentive to *over-include* names he wanted to argue against, not under-include — meaning the roster is more likely complete-ish than under-counted, but individual identifications carry his anti-Monacan filter.

### B.3 Earliest-by-date analysis

**Of the dated entries, "Joseph Clark — 1906 — Mary S. Clark" is the earliest Clark surname on the joepayne roll.**

This is significant in three ways:

1. **The earliest dated Clark on this roll is the JAC candidate himself.** There is no pre-1906 dated Clark entry. Bright Star's compilation jumps from Wyatt Ramsey (1876, no Clark) past 1881 (Calvin Nuckles + Luthena Tyree, no Clark) to 1885 (Anderson Adcock + Anelina Willis, no Clark), then through 1905 (James Beverly + Leanna Roberts Hix, no Clark) before the first Clark — Joseph Clark 1906 — appears. There are zero Clarks in the dated roll between 1700 and 1905.

2. **Six of the eleven Clark men whose marriage years are unrecorded sit on page 3** — meaning Bright Star or his source pulled them from a corpus where the dates were stripped. The simplest explanation for an undated marriage in a Virginia roll is that the source was a parish, school, or community register that did not keep dates — not a courthouse marriage license, which always carries a date. This means the page-3 Clarks are quite plausibly *older* than the page-1/2 Clarks (community-register Clarks pre-dating the Virginia courthouse marriage-license Clarks). If true, the roll's apparent "first Clark" of 1906 is an artifact of how the rolls were compiled, not a real first-Clark date.

3. **Two of the page-3 Clarks marry Tyrees** (John C. + Margaret Tyree; H.A. + Cora A. Tyree; James C. + Flora Tyree → three of six). One marries a Redcross (William Clark + Bessie Redcross). One is endogamous Clark/Clark (W.H. + Nancy Clark; Nelson H. + Franky Clark). **The Clark-Tyree intermarriage rate on the undated page is 50% — by far the most concentrated surname-pair coupling in the entire roll.** This is the single strongest signal in the data.

### B.4 The Nelson H. Clark + Franky Clark line

The page-3 entry "Nelson H. Clark — m — Franky Clark" is independently corroborated in v1's GEDCOM analysis (file 11) as Brady's biological 6×-great-grandfather Nelson Harrison Clark (1793–1871). Bright Star's roll documents Nelson's third (or pre-Edith) endogamous marriage to a "Franky Clark" who does not appear in v1's GEDCOM — making this one of two important leads:

- **Lead 1:** "Franky Clark" is a previously-undocumented Nelson marriage that the GEDCOM missed. Worth a Rockbridge marriage-records pull for "Frances Clark" or "Franky Clark" 1815–1870.
- **Lead 2:** "Franky Clark" is Bright Star's name-collapse of Nelson's 1840 third wife Frances Elizabeth Mason (file 10), erroneously substituting "Clark" for her natal "Mason." If so, Bright Star is conflating spouses by post-marriage surname only — useful as a cross-check warning for all other roll entries.

The fact that Nelson H. Clark is on this list at all is itself the most important new finding from this pull. **Nelson H. Clark — born 1793, died 1871 — is the documented "MULATTO" Clark in the 1860 federal census (file 12, source D39).** If Bright Star's Page 3 Clarks are pre-1906 community-register Clarks rather than post-1906 courthouse-license Clarks, then Nelson H. Clark on this roll is the strongest signal we have of a Clark surname presence in the Monacan community in the early-to-mid 19th century, even if the precise marriage date was not preserved.

### C.3 Earliest-by-date Tyree analysis

**The earliest dated Tyree on the roll is Samuel Tyree, 1875, married to Mary Roberts** — 31 years before the earliest dated Clark on the same roll. This is the single most important finding in the Tyree-side pull.

**Implications:**

1. **The Tyree surname is on the rolls a generation before the Clark surname on a like-for-like basis.** If we trust the Bright Star ordering as roughly chronological by marriage license date (1875 Tyree → 1906 Clark = 31-year gap), then the *Tyree* line entered the documented Monacan rolls a full generation before the Clark line did. The Clark surname's "appearance" on the roll in 1906 is plausibly a continuation of an earlier Clark-Tyree intermarriage pattern (page 3 — undated) where Clark men married Tyree women.

2. **The 1906 Joseph Clark + Mary S. Clark entry is structurally consistent with JAC having married into a Tyree-descended Clark line** — that is, "Mary S. Clark" may herself be the daughter or granddaughter of a page-3 Tyree-Clark marriage (Margaret Tyree, Cora A. Tyree, or Flora Tyree). The endogamous Clark-Clark marriage of 1906 thus reads as: an Irish Creek Clark man marrying a woman who was born into the Tyree-descended Clark community. That makes the 1906 entry not an isolated Clark event but the closing of a 31-year intermarriage cycle that started with Samuel Tyree's 1875 marriage and continued through three Tyree-women-into-Clark-men page-3 marriages.

3. **Tyree was the entry surname; Clark was the assimilated surname.** This matches the MIN narrative pattern — Tyree appears on Plecker's Rockbridge AND Amherst lists; Tyree appears on the Bear Mountain memorial stone (cross-check: the v1 extract of the C-VILLE 2018 inscription does NOT list Tyree on the stone, but Plecker's lists do. **Update needed in v1's reading of the stone — Tyree is not on the stone but is on Plecker's list both counties**). Clark is on both Plecker's list and on the stone.

## E. Intermarriage analysis

### E.1 The Clark-Tyree axis is the dominant axis

Six of fourteen total Clark entries (43%) and three of five total Tyree entries (60%) join the *other* of those two surnames. Both Clark and Tyree are on Plecker's lists for both counties. Tyree is missing from the Bear Mountain Memorial Stone but Clark is on it. The asymmetry suggests:

- **Clark** = the "stone" surname, the surname the tribe today claims as ancestral.
- **Tyree** = the "list" surname, on the hostile state apparatus's classification but not on the affirmative tribal monument.

The Clark-Tyree marriage cycle thus reads as one community connecting a stone-surname to a list-surname through repeated intermarriage. v1's reading was that the 1906 Joseph Clark + Mary S. Clark entry was the keystone Clark event; the data says the keystone is broader — it's the **Tyree → Clark intergenerational cycle** that ran from at least 1875 through 1906 and beyond.

### E.2 Page-3 Clark men are likely the older cohort

Of the six page-3 Clark men, three (John C., H.A., James C.) marry Tyree women. The Tyree dated entries begin in 1875. If the page-3 Clarks are older than the page-1/2 Clarks (which the date-stripping pattern implies), then those three Clark-Tyree marriages plausibly occurred in the **1860s–1880s window**. None can be dated more precisely from this source alone.

### E.3 The earliest Clark candidate by inference

Bright Star's roll cannot of itself name "the first Clark on the Monacan rolls" because:
- **(a)** the dated section starts the Clark line at 1906, and
- **(b)** the undated page-3 section contains six earlier Clarks but with no dates.

However, **inference from the structure of the v1 corpus + the joepayne list** points to four candidate "first Clarks" with descending confidence:

| Rank | Candidate | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Nelson H. Clark** (1793–1871, Brady's biological 6×g-grandfather, page 3 of joepayne roll, "MULATTO" 1860 federal census per file 12, married Edith Wane 1819 + Frances Mason 1840 + page-3 "Franky Clark") | Documented on 1860 census as MULATTO; documented on joepayne page-3 roll; documented Rockbridge marriage 1819; lifespan covers exactly the period the roll claims. | **3 of 5** |
| 2 | **Joseph Carter Clark Sr.** (1797–1878, Atha Sorrells case file family-tree root per file 02 and D27) | Named in the Sorrells v. Shields 1924 family-tree exhibit as the Clark progenitor; Clarks Mountain Cemetery patriarch; Nelson's half-brother (file 12). NOT on the joepayne roll directly — but his descendants (James Clark 1876, Joe Clark Jr., Atha Sorrells) all are connected to the line. | **3 of 5** (cross-source convergence) |
| 3 | **James Clarke** (~1755, father of both Nelson and Joseph Carter Sr., file 12) | Named in WikiTree D39 as common father; not on joepayne roll; pre-dates the roll's earliest plausible community date. Generation older than #1 and #2. | **2 of 5** (one-source) |
| 4 | An unnamed Clark man married to a pre-1819 Tyree, Mason, or Wane woman in Rockbridge — i.e. the Clark whose Y-DNA Brady carries, who entered the community before Nelson's 1819 marriage | No documentary trace; B5 Big-Y test (FTDNA) is the only path to test this. | **1 of 5** (speculative until DNA) |

**My current best guess** as of this pull: **Nelson H. Clark (1793–1871) is the earliest documented Clark on the Monacan rolls** — he sits on the joepayne page-3 roll *and* on the 1860 MULATTO census *and* is a confirmed Brady direct ancestor. He's the best-evidenced answer with three independent corroborations. **The "first Clark" under the strict definition of the SKILL prompt is more likely his father James Clarke (~1755) or an earlier Clark a generation above — but James does not appear on any roll, only in genealogical reconstruction.**

The honest answer is that **the joepayne roll cannot, on its own, name the first Clark.** It can only name candidates and rank them by other-source corroboration, which is what the table above does.
