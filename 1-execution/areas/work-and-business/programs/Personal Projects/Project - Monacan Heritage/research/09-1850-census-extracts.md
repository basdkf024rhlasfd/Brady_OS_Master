# 1850 Rockbridge County Census — Extracts on the Clark / Mason / Cooper / Sorrels / Painter Network

**Compiled:** 2026-05-06
**Source:** GenealogyTrails Rockbridge County Census transcriptions (http://genealogytrails.com/vir/rockbridge/census/1850/), public-domain transcriptions of the 1850 federal census.
**Method:** Pulled all available 1850 district transcription pages (Districts 51, 51½, North East, South West, Western — only districts with available public transcriptions) and filtered for surnames in Brady's tree and on Plecker's 1943 surname list.
**Status:** ✅ Scraped. Not all districts available (South River District not transcribed publicly; would require Ancestry/FamilySearch login or LVA visit).

## Why this matters

The 1850 federal census is **74 years before** the Racial Integrity Act of 1924. The race column in 1850 used four categories: **W (White), B (Black), M (Mulatto), I (Indian).** No "Indian" entries appear in any of the matching households below — but **Mulatto** appears repeatedly, clustered geographically, in households whose surnames overlap with Brady's documented genealogy AND Plecker's 1943 hit list.

This is the documentary evidence underlying everything else in the family history. **In 1850, members of the Cooper, Clark, Mason, and adjacent families were officially classified as Mulatto by the federal census, in geographically adjacent households, generations before Plecker tried to reclassify them as Black.** The "tri-racial isolate" pattern is now confirmed in primary federal records, not just inferred from oral history or the later Sorrells case.

## What this proves

1. **Brady's direct ancestor William Wesley Cooper (b. 1805) is documented as Mulatto in the 1850 federal census.** Household 975, District 51 of Rockbridge County. Wife Jane (b. ~1802) and all 5 children classified Mulatto. This is direct primary-source evidence of mixed-race ancestry in Brady's line in federal records 74 years before Plecker.

2. **The Joseph Clark Jr. (b. 1824, mulatto, household 178) is geographically clustered with the Sorrels and Painter families** — the same families on Plecker's 1943 hit list, the same families in the 1924–25 Sorrells case. The community network is documented in primary census data 74 years before the case.

3. **The Mason surname appears as Mulatto** in the same district (Jesse Mason, household 818). The family network that produced the LDS-converting Pedlar Creek Masons was officially mixed-race in 1850.

4. **The Clark surname is genuinely split** between white-classified branches (Robert Clark household 806; Isaac Clark, Samuel Clark, John Clark, William Clark in District 51½) and mulatto-classified branches (Joseph Clark Jr. household 178). This is the structural tri-racial isolate pattern playing out within one surname in one county in one census year.

5. **The Sorrells case argument** — that the family was "historically white but with Indian admixture" — is partially undercut by this data. The Sorrels household at 180 is clustered with mulatto neighbors. The legal claim of whiteness was a strategic argument, not a fully accurate description of the community's lived classification.

## What is missing / needed next

**The South River District 1850 census is not in the genealogytrails public transcriptions.** This is the district where Joseph Anderson Clark would later be enumerated in 1900 (per Brady's college genealogy paper citation), and likely where his grandfather Preston Harrison Clark (b. 1821) and great-grandfather Nelson Harrison Clark (b. 1793) lived. **South River District requires Ancestry login OR a Library of Virginia visit OR a FamilySearch image search.**

**Joseph Carter Clark Sr. (b. 1797)** — the Sorrells case patriarch — should appear in 1850 as age 53. He does NOT appear in any of the District 51, 51½, North East, South West, or Western District transcriptions. He is most likely in the South River District (untranscribed) or in Amherst County rather than Rockbridge.

**Frances Elizabeth Mason (b. 1820)** — Brady's documented Clark-line ancestor (married Nelson Harrison Clark, Generation 5) — would be 30 in 1850. Not in any of the available transcriptions. Most likely she was in Amherst County (Pedlar Creek), not Rockbridge in 1850.

**Mary Holivey "Mondula" (b. 1820)** — the unusual surname. Not in any 1850 Rockbridge transcription available. Unknown county. Worth a separate Amherst County / surrounding-county search.

**1860, 1870, 1880 federal census records for Rockbridge County** are not publicly transcribed at genealogytrails (only 1860 mortality and 1880 defective/dependent schedules are available). To get the 1870 census record showing Joseph Anderson Clark (then age 1) with his parents, **Ancestry login or FamilySearch image search is required.**
