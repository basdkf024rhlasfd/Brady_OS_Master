# Master Sources List — Monacan Heritage Research

**Compiled:** 2026-05-06
**Purpose:** Every source referenced across the full whitepaper project, organized by how to get it and whether we've already extracted the key content or you'd need to do manual steps.

**Status key:**
- ✅ **Scraped** — Key content extracted and built into the research files. No action needed unless you want the original.
- ⚠️ **Partially scraped** — We got summaries, abstracts, or secondary citations. Main arguments are known. Direct quotes and full detail require acquisition.
- ❌ **Not acquired** — Confirmed to exist, described in the research, but zero actual content retrieved. You must acquire this yourself.

**Credibility scale:** 5 = primary/peer-reviewed | 4 = major institution | 3 = regional press/university | 2 = general reference | 1 = uncorroborated

## SECTION A — BOOKS & MONOGRAPHS

### A1. Peter W. Houck, *Indian Island in Amherst County*
**Citation:** Lynchburg Historical Research Co., 1984. 167 pp. Expanded 2nd ed. 1993 with Mintcy D. Maxham.
**Credibility:** 4
**Status:** ⚠️ Partially scraped — key arguments summarized from secondary citations. Core claim (the Amherst County Indian community is Monacan) and its role enabling the recognition movement are well-documented in our research. Specific genealogical details on individual families (Beverly, Branham, Johns, Pinn, Terry) referenced but not read directly.
**Why it matters:** The book that changed everything. Before Houck (1984), most Bear Mountain families called themselves Cherokee. After Houck, the Monacan label took hold and the recognition campaign organized. Directly cites the same family surnames (Clark included) as Monacan-descended.
**How to get it:**
- Not digitized; limited print availability
- Search WorldCat (worldcat.org) for nearest library holding
- Try: Lynchburg Public Library, University of Lynchburg, Leyburn Library at Washington & Lee University (Lexington, VA)
- Interlibrary loan from your local library is the most practical path
- Occasionally appears on AbeBooks or eBay as used copies ($25–$60 range)

### A2. Jeffrey Hantman, *Monacan Millennium: A Collaborative Archaeology and History of a Virginia Indian People*
**Citation:** University of Virginia Press, Charlottesville, 2018. 217 pp.
**Credibility:** 5
**Status:** ⚠️ Partially scraped — key findings extracted from published academic reviews and secondary citations. We know: 13 burial mounds analyzed, AD 1000–2000 coverage, maize-diet confirmation, Monasukapanough 2001 excavation, collaborative tribal authorship, and its role in the federal recognition case. Direct quotes and chapter-by-chapter details not in hand.
**Why it matters:** The definitive modern synthesis. 30 years of Hantman's archaeology in one place. This is the book that proves the Monacan are who they say they are. Should be read alongside the whitepaper.
**How to get it:**
- Buy new: UVA Press (upress.virginia.edu) or Amazon ($30–$45)
- University library systems should all carry it; interlibrary loan easy
- Google Books has a partial preview

### C1. *Atha Sorrells v. A.T. Shields* — Full Case File
**Location:** Library of Virginia, 800 East Broad Street, Richmond, VA 23219
**Accession numbers:** 1140744 and 1164679
**Credibility:** 5
**Status:** ⚠️ Partially scraped — Judge Holt's full memorandum opinion and the petition text are now fully scraped (see D7). The depositions, witness lists, and the physical Clark family tree exhibit have NOT been retrieved — these exist only as paper in the LVA box. Plecker's testimony content is not in the research files. John Powell also testified for the defense (confirmed via Encyclopedia Virginia, D26); what he said is unknown.
**Why it matters:** Joseph Anderson Clark may appear by name in the depositions. The Clark family tree exhibit (described in the LVA finding aid as "a large family tree of the Clark family from whom Altha Sorrells descended") is the oldest documented lineage chart for your direct family. The depositions cover church and school attendance, neighborhood opinion of the families' color, and family relationships across the entire Irish Creek community — a goldmine of community documentation. This is the #1 priority archive pull.
**How to get it:**
- Call LVA Reading Room in advance: (804) 692-3888
- Request specific accessions; they can scan and email PDFs of case files for a fee
- Physical visit takes half a day; Richmond is the most practical option
- Note: the LVA finding aid spells her first name "Altha" — use that variant when making the request

### C9. *James Connor and Dorothy Johns v. A.T. Shields, Clerk of Court* (September 1924)
**Location:** Library of Virginia, 800 East Broad Street, Richmond, VA 23219
**Accession number:** 1140743
**LVA finding aid:** ead.lib.virginia.edu/vivaxtf/view?docId=lva/vi04029.xml
**Credibility:** 5
**Status:** ❌ Not acquired — confirmed to exist via LVA finding aid. Case contents not retrieved.
**Why it matters:** Filed September 1924 — two months before the Sorrells case. **Johns is a canonical Monacan surname** appearing on the Bear Mountain Memorial Stone, Plecker's 1943 lists for both Amherst AND Rockbridge, and throughout the Fort Christanna migration chain (Tartleton Johns, Susan Johns). This is a parallel Rockbridge County marriage-license fight from the same clerk's office (A.T. Shields), proving the Monacan community was defending Indian identity from multiple family lines simultaneously — not just the Clark/Sorrells line. If a Johns family tree is in the case file (following the Sorrells pattern), it provides a second independent Monacan lineage chain anchored in Rockbridge County during JAC's active adulthood. Key context documented in `07-sources-and-original-rolls.md` and `08-primary-source-extracts.md`.
**How to get it:**
- Same LVA request process as C1 — call (804) 692-3888; request accession 1140743
- Pull on the same trip as C1 (Sorrells), C2 (James Clark 1876), C3 (Plecker-Shields) — all at LVA Richmond

### G1. "Old Joe Clark" folk song — origin claim OVERSTATED in whitepaper v1

**Claim as originally written:** "The famous American folk song 'Old Joe Clark' originated at Joseph Carter Clark Sr.'s Irish Creek sawmill operation." Attributed to the 1956 Penick letter (FamilySearch memory, credibility 3/5). Treated in whitepaper v1 as an established connection.

**Correction:** The Irish Creek origin is one of at least two documented competing claims and is NOT the strongest one.

**The Kentucky claim (stronger):** Joseph Clark of Clay County, Kentucky (born 18 September 1839; shot and killed 1886). Kentucky State Historical Marker #1382 at Sexton's Creek commemorates him specifically as the song's subject. The earliest printed reference to the song in any scholarly source is *Journal of American Folklore*, Vol. 25, p. 152 (1905) — "sung by East Tennessee whites" — geographically consistent with the Kentucky/Tennessee corridor, not Rockbridge County, Virginia.

**The Virginia / Irish Creek claim (weaker):** 1956 Penick letter (97 years after Joe Clark Sr.'s death; local newspaper letter, not scholarly documentation) + 1983 Mike Seeger oral tradition documentation. Neither source engages the Kentucky competing claim. The Virginia tradition is genuine community memory, not fabrication — but it is not primary musicological evidence.

**Musicological consensus:** None. Library of Congress musicologist Alan Jabbour: no 19th-century printed examples found; song "likely no older than late 19th or early 20th century." Folklorist Samuel Bayard (1981): melody possibly derived from minstrel tune "Lucy Long"; lyrics assembled from floating verses (*Shady Grove*, *Cindy*, *Ida Red*). First commercial recording: Fiddlin' John Carson, OKeh 40038, 1923.

**Corrected claim for all future drafts:** The Irish Creek Clark community maintained a tradition that the song referenced their patriarch, documented in community memory by Penick (1956) and Seeger (1983). This is a genuine part of Irish Creek community identity. The folk song's ultimate origin is disputed and unresolved; the Kentucky claim has stronger external documentation. Do NOT state that Joseph Carter Clark Sr. is the definitive subject of the song.

**Credibility of Irish Creek origin claim:** 1/5 as fact; 3/5 as documented community tradition.

### G2. Clarksville, Virginia — not related to Irish Creek Clarks

**Claim:** Brady asked whether Clarksville, Virginia might be named after his Clark family.

**Correction:** Clarksville is in Mecklenburg County, in south-central Virginia, on the Roanoke River near the North Carolina border — approximately 150 miles southeast of Rockbridge County. It is a tobacco river town with no documented connection to the Irish Creek / Monacan Clark family. The Clark surname is one of the most common in Virginia and America. The Irish Creek Clark community was geographically centered in Rockbridge County (Irish Creek, South River corridor, Glasgow area) and Amherst County (Bear Mountain, Pedlar Creek). Mecklenburg County is outside the documented territory of this family network.

**Status:** False lead. No research required.
