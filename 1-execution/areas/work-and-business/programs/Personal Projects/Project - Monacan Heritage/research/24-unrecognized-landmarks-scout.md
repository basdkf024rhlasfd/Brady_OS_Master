## 1. Federal-Recognition Leverage Toolkit (post-PL 115-121)

Federal recognition on January 29, 2018 (Public Law 115-121, Thomasina E. Jordan Indian Tribes of Virginia Federal Recognition Act) flipped the Monacan Indian Nation's posture on every landmark from "advisory consultee at SHPO discretion" to "statutory consultee under federal law." Before mapping landmarks, the toolkit:

| Tool | Statute / Authority | Trigger | Gives MIN |
|------|---------------------|---------|-----------|
| **NHPA Section 106 consultation** | 54 U.S.C. § 306108; 36 C.F.R. Part 800 | Any federal undertaking (license, permit, funding) that *may* affect historic properties of religious or cultural significance | Mandatory consultation rights, ability to challenge findings of "no adverse effect," ability to demand a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) before work proceeds. Per 1992 NHPA amendments, applies *off* tribal lands when ancestral homeland is at issue. (https://broadbandusa.ntia.gov/sites/default/files/2024-04/NHPA_Section_106_Consultation_Process_Fact_Sheet_04_2024.pdf; https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/tribal/topics/historic/tcqa.htm) |
| **NAGPRA** | 25 U.S.C. §§ 3001–3013 (1990) | Federally-funded or federal-land institution holding Native human remains, funerary objects, sacred objects, or objects of cultural patrimony | Standing to demand inventory, request repatriation, sue for non-compliance. Federal recognition is the gate — pre-2018 MIN had no statutory standing here. |
| **Section 4(f) of the DOT Act** | 49 U.S.C. § 303 | Any DOT-funded transportation project (highway, transit, rail, FAA airport) using historic property | Requires "all possible planning to minimize harm" and consultation; high bar to overcome. Useful for VDOT US-29, Route 130, I-64 corridor projects in MIN homeland. |
| **Sacred Sites E.O. 13007** | Executive Order 13007 (1996) | Federal land manager planning action affecting a tribal sacred site | Requires accommodation of access and reasonable physical integrity protections. Triggered when federal agency owns or manages the parcel. |
| **DOI gov-to-gov consultation** | E.O. 13175 (2000); DOI Policy 512 DM 4 | Any DOI agency (NPS, BLM, BIA, USGS, USFWS) action with substantial direct effect on tribe | Tribe-to-agency-head consultation, distinct from project-level Section 106. |
| **Tribal Historic Preservation Officer (THPO)** | Section 101(d)(2) NHPA (54 U.S.C. § 302702) | Tribe formally assumes SHPO functions on tribal lands by NPS-approved program | Substitutes for SHPO on tribal lands; receives federal HPF funding stream. **Open question for MIN:** as of public-record search 2026-05-09, MIN's THPO designation status is not visible in the NPS THPO directory or in MIN public-facing materials. This is a **Recognition Push** action item — file 24 cannot confirm THPO active. |
| **NRHP listing** | 54 U.S.C. § 302101; 36 C.F.R. Part 60 | State Historic Preservation Officer (Va. DHR) nominates; National Park Service decides | Federal recognition of significance; mandatory Section 106 review for federal undertakings affecting the site. Doesn't itself bar private development. |
| **NHL designation** | 54 U.S.C. § 320101 | Secretary of the Interior designation, higher tier than NRHP | Higher protection threshold; greater visibility; MIN-relevant sites are NHL-eligible by criteria, not yet pursued. |
| **State historic marker** | Va. Code § 10.1-2204; DHR program | DHR-approved nomination, sponsor pays for marker | Public visibility; doesn't trigger preservation duty. Cheap symbolic win, useful for places with no buried artifact protection lever. |
| **Va. burial law** | Va. Code §§ 10.1-2305 to 10.1-2316 (Virginia Antiquities Act / Human Remains statute) | Disturbance of any unmarked human burial | Permit required from DHR; criminal penalty for unauthorized disturbance. Applies on private land. State-level analog to NAGPRA. **High-leverage tool for unrecorded farmland burials.** |

**Yellowstone-style tactic mapping:** Section 106 + Va. burial law + sacred-site assertion + NAGPRA + coordinated public-pressure (National Trust 11 Most Endangered listing, op-ed campaigns, social-media coalition) is the playbook that won Rassawek 2018–2022. Scaled to multiple sites simultaneously is the v2 strategy.

## 3. Per-Landmark Detail

### 3.1 Rassawek (Rasauwec)

**Source trail:** `research/04-leadership-oppo.md` § 4 already documents the 2018–2022 fight in detail; this section adds the designation status.

**Documentary record:** Captain John Smith's 1612 *Map of Virginia* names Rassawek as the "chiefest town" of the Monacan. Hantman's 1990 *American Anthropologist* paper dates mound activity AD 1070–1440 (https://www.jstor.org/stable/680342, paywalled abstract free). Continuous occupation across ~200 generations / ~4,730 years per archaeological record (https://savingplaces.org/places/rassawek; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rassawek).

**Current designation:**
- **State historic marker** O-49 "Rassawek" erected 1997 by Va. DHR at Columbia, Fluvanna County (https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=31603).
- **NRHP not listed** as a discrete property. The 2018–2022 fight ran on Section 106 procedural rights, NHPA "historic property" status determination, and the National Trust 11 Most Endangered listing (2020) — *not* on a perfected NRHP nomination. This is a critical strategic gap: the win locked out one project but did not lock in the site's status against future projects.

**Section 106 outcome:** James River Water Authority hired the tribe's preferred cultural-resources consultant (Cultural Heritage Partners-coordinated). Investigation of the alternative Forsyth site in 2021 showed low burial risk. JRWA voted unanimously March 15–16, 2022 to abandon the Rassawek site, transferred the parcel it had acquired to MIN, and pursued the Forsyth alternative (https://www.washingtonpost.com/history/2022/03/16/monacan-rassawek-resolution/; https://virginiamercury.com/briefs/water-authority-abandons-plans-to-site-pump-station-at-rassawek/; https://www.selc.org/news/rassawek-saved/).

**Recommended pathway:**
1. **NRHP nomination** as a discrete property (Criterion A: events significant in the broad pattern of Eastern Siouan history; Criterion D: archaeological information potential). Va. DHR sponsors the nomination; NPS lists. Estimated 12–24 month process.
2. **NHL designation** as a follow-up after NRHP listing (eligibility under NHL Criterion 4: "high archaeological significance to American history").
3. **Permanent conservation easement** on the parcel transferred from JRWA to MIN — locks the protective overlay even if MIN governance changes hands.
4. **National Monument feasibility study** as a long-horizon option (requires Presidential proclamation under the Antiquities Act of 1906, or Congressional designation). Long shot but not implausible given recent Western precedent (Bears Ears, Avi Kwa Ame).

**Yellowstone-tactic angle:** Burial-ground assertions are already established. The Va. burial law (§§ 10.1-2305 to 10.1-2316) overlay should be made explicit — any soil disturbance at Rassawek without a permit is a criminal matter. This is the formal-legal pillar paired with the public-pressure overlay (National Trust, SELC, Cultural Heritage Partners) that won the 2022 outcome.

**MIN posture:** Owns the land. Owns the win. Has not yet locked it in via NRHP/NHL. **This is a Recognition Push lead case study.**

### 3.2 Monasukapanough (UVA-area, Rivanna River)

**Documentary record:** Captain John Smith's 1612 map names Monasukapanough as one of five Monacan towns. Located on both sides of the South Fork Rivanna at a crossing point near modern Carrsbrook off Route 29, ~5 miles north of downtown Charlottesville (https://www.cvillepedia.org/Monasukapanough; https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/monacan-indian-nation/). Bushnell's *The Five Monacan Towns in Virginia, 1607* (1930) confirms the geography. Smithsonian sponsored excavations 1911 and 1931. Hantman + UVA students returned to the site late 20th century. Karenne Wood's tribal history project drew heavily on Monasukapanough.

**Current designation:**
- **State historic marker** G-29 "Monacan Indian Village" erected 2005 by Va. DHR (https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=106829).
- **NRHP not listed** as a discrete archaeological district despite eligibility being well-documented in scholarship.
- **Archaeological Conservancy preserve** covers a portion of the site (since ~2001, per cvillepedia).

**Recommended pathway:**
1. **NRHP nomination** as the "Monasukapanough Archaeological District" — should include Monasukapanough village proper, Jefferson's Mound, and the Indian Grave site as contributing properties (Criteria A and D).
2. **NHL designation** as a follow-up — Monasukapanough is mentioned by Jefferson in *Notes on the State of Virginia*; the historiographic significance plus archaeological significance creates a strong NHL case.
3. **Cultural Landscape designation** under NPS guidelines for the broader Rivanna corridor.
4. **Section 106 trigger** — UVA expansion, VDOT US-29 corridor projects, Albemarle County development should all run Section 106 with MIN as consulting party.
5. **NAGPRA inventory request** to UVA for Monasukapanough material — UVA ran the 1990s excavations; federal recognition unlocks NAGPRA standing post-2018.

**Yellowstone-tactic angle:** Section 106 is already legally available; UVA + VDOT + Albemarle County are recurring federal-undertaking nexuses. The aggressive move is *systematic* MIN intervention as consulting party on every federal undertaking in the Albemarle corridor — not waiting to be invited. Pair with NAGPRA repatriation pressure on UVA's holdings.

**MIN posture:** High strategic interest; tied to UVA Stewardship Essay (`deliverables/uva-stewardship-essay.md`). Karenne Wood's research site. **This is the second Recognition Push lead case study.**

### 3.3 Jefferson's Mound (Rivanna Mound)

**Documentary record:** Within the Monasukapanough cluster. Excavated by Thomas Jefferson in 1784 — described in *Notes on the State of Virginia* — disclosing layered burial of "near a thousand skeletons" (https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/jeffersons-mound-archaeological-site/; https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/jeffersons-excavation-native-american-burial-mound). Construction begun pre-1400 CE per archaeological dating. Hantman 1990 paper places the mound within the Monacan polity.

**Current designation:** DHR archaeological site only (number restricted under Va. Code § 10.1-2202). No state marker as a discrete site (but covered by the Monasukapanough G-29 marker). **Not NRHP-listed as a discrete property.**

**Recommended pathway:** Roll into the Monasukapanough Archaeological District nomination as a contributing property OR list separately as a discrete NRHP property (Criterion A for association with Jefferson's documented excavation as a foundational moment in American archaeology + Criterion D for archaeological information potential). The Jefferson connection makes this NRHP-eligible at the highest national-significance tier.

**Yellowstone-tactic angle:** Va. burial law on any soil disturbance; NAGPRA on any post-1990 federally-funded excavation material. The MIN reburial of remains here in the 1990s is a precedent — MIN should formalize into a permanent NAGPRA Plan of Action with UVA.

### 3.4 Rapidan Mound

**Documentary record:** South bank of the Rapidan River near Stegara village in western Orange County, east of Scuffletown (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapidan_Mound). UVA excavated 1988; in 1998 ~9,000 bones representing 1,000–2,000 individuals were reburied — the largest Native American burial site in Virginia. Manahoac confederation (Monacan ally). The Boyd & Boyd 2003 *American Antiquity* paper "Collective Burial in Late Prehistoric Virginia: Excavation and Analysis of the Rapidan Mound" is the academic foundation (Cambridge Core, paywalled — https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-antiquity/article/abs/collective-burial-in-late-prehistoric-virginia-excavation-and-analysis-of-the-rapidan-mound/2036B7E5E97FF4AB56BFCE139168090D).

**Current designation:** DHR archaeological site only. **No state marker. Not NRHP-listed.** This is striking given the site's significance — the 1998 reburial was a national-press-covered NAGPRA precedent.

**Recommended pathway:**
1. **NRHP nomination** under Criterion A (NAGPRA reburial precedent) + Criterion D (archaeological information potential). The 1998 reburial event itself is historically significant.
2. **NAGPRA inventory audit** at UVA for any remaining Rapidan Mound material.
3. **Va. burial-law overlay** asserted on any future disturbance.
4. **Cultural-affiliation determination** for MIN — Manahoac was a Monacan ally; whether MIN asserts direct cultural affiliation or speaks-for-Manahoac is a tribal-government decision.

**Yellowstone-tactic angle:** This site is the perfect "we already proved the model" exhibit. NAGPRA worked here — and yet the site itself remains NRHP-undesignated 28+ years after the reburial began. The optics of MIN driving its NRHP nomination is strong.

### 3.5 Mowhemencho / Mohemenco / Manakin Town

**Documentary record:** South bank James River, eastern Powhatan County, between Bernards Creek (east) and Jones Creek (west). Visited by Newport's party 1607–1608 after kidnapping a Monacan leader as guide. Visited regularly by Monacans into 1702 when site was called Manakin Town by the Huguenots who had settled there in 1699. Late-survival Monacan site. (https://www.virginiaplaces.org/nativeamerican/monacantribe.html; https://encyclopediavirginia.org/744hpr-24d5af2b9837969/.)

**Current designation:**
- **DHR marker O-3 "Mowhemcho-Manakin Town"** erected 2008 by Va. DHR (https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=21142).
- **DHR marker M-53565 "Mohemenco (A Monacan Village) and Drake House"** erected 2007 by Michaux-St. James Foundation (https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=53565).
- **DHR marker "Huguenot Settlement"** also at site (https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=21141).
- **NRHP:** the Manakin Huguenot layer has been variously documented; the Monacan layer specifically does not appear to be NRHP-listed.

**Recommended pathway:** NRHP nomination as a multi-component archaeological district (Monacan village + Huguenot settlement overlay). Politically tractable because Michaux-St. James Foundation is already engaged as a sponsor — this is collaboration not contestation.

**Yellowstone-tactic angle:** Late-survival site means the 1607 → 1702 occupation arc spans the polity collapse. Strong NRHP case under Criterion A for "the broad pattern of Eastern Siouan survival under colonial pressure."

### 3.6 Massinacack and 3.7 Monahassanugh

**Documentary record:** Two of the five Smith-mapped Monacan villages. Massinacack on right bank James near Mohawk Creek mouth, ~1 mile south of Goochland (some scholars equate to Mowhemencho — disputed). Monahassanugh estimated 25 miles distant from a fixed point, possibly at Wingina (Nelson County) or on the northern branch of Staunton River (Bedford County).

**Current designation:** Neither is NRHP-listed; neither has a state historic marker. DHR archaeological-site footprint only.

**Recommended pathway:** Geographic confirmation is the prerequisite. Once confirmed:
1. State historic markers (cheap symbolic wins).
2. NRHP nomination once boundaries are firm.
3. Section 106 trigger for any federal undertaking in Goochland, Nelson, or Bedford counties.

**Yellowstone-tactic angle:** Low-leverage by themselves but high-leverage as a packaged "all five Smith-mapped Monacan villages" thematic NRHP MPS (Multiple Property Submission). MIN-coordinated MPS would put NRHP listings on Rassawek + Monasukapanough + Mowhemencho + Massinacack + Monahassanugh in a single submission. This is the high-impact bureaucratic move.

### 3.9 Fort Christanna

**Documentary record:** Built 1714 by Gov. Spotswood; closed 1718 by House of Burgesses. Six-mile-square (36 sq. mi.) reservation. Saponi/Tutelo + remnants of as many as 15 other tribes settled at Junkatapurse (Tutelo: *chunketa pasui*, "horse's head"). ~600 Saponi ancestors moved 1712–1714. Some moved south to Catawba ~1728; others north to Shamokin PA 1740, formally adopted by Cayuga in NY 1753 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Christanna; https://saponitown.com/fort-christanna/).

**Current designation:** **NRHP-listed 1980 as a historic district.** Now Brunswick County park.

**Recommended pathway:**
1. **NHL nomination** — NRHP-listed since 1980, eligibility under NHL Criterion 4 is strong.
2. **MIN-led interpretive partnership** — MIN's claim is via Saponi/Tutelo cultural affiliation as Eastern Siouan kin.
3. **Coordination with Occoneechee-Saponi-Totaro Tribe of the Saponi Reservation** — they assert lineal-descendant status on this specific 6-mile-square reservation (https://occoneechee.org/pages/the-occoneechee-saponi-totaro-indian-tribe-of-virginia-and-the-saponi-reservation-in-brunswick-county-va).
4. **Section 106 lever** for any federal undertaking in the historical 36-sq-mile reservation footprint.

**Yellowstone-tactic angle:** Cultural-affiliation overlap is a feature not a bug — multiple tribes asserting overlapping claims raises the political profile. Coordination with Occoneechee-Saponi-Totaro is the play; not exclusive claim.

### 3.10 Bear Mountain Indian Mission School

**Documentary record:** Constructed ~1870 as log church/schoolhouse. Episcopal mission established 1908 by Rev. Arthur P. Gray Jr. 1930 fire destroyed most structures, schoolhouse survived. Operated until 1964 (when high school first became available to Monacan students). Now MIN-owned.

**Current designation:**
- **NRHP-listed February 21, 1997** (ref 97000152, DHR file 005-0230).
- VLR-listed September 18, 1996.
- DHR preservation easement.
- Marker erected 2007 (https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=104369).
- Source: https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/historic-registers/005-0230/.

**Recommended pathway:**
1. **District expansion** to include adjacent Daisy Adcock parcel + 339-acre original settlement + Bear Mountain itself (the geographic feature).
2. **THPO formalization** — MIN should formally take SHPO functions on MIN-owned land per § 101(d)(2) NHPA. Currently unconfirmed in public record.
3. **Maintain easement.**

### 3.11 Laurel Cliff Farm (Powwow Grounds)

**Documentary record:** 225 Laurel Cliff Rd, Monroe, Amherst County. 1,292.3 acres acquired September 1, 2021 for $5,500,000 from Realty Acquisitions LLC. Brick manor house, two tenant houses, multiple barns, two ponds. Powwow grounds since 2022. Source: `research/12-property-and-procurement.md` § 2.

**Current designation:** **None.** No marker, no NRHP, no Cultural Landscape designation.

**Recommended pathway:**
1. **Cultural Landscape designation** (NPS).
2. **NRHP listing as a Traditional Cultural Property** per NPS Bulletin 38 — requires demonstration of cultural significance to a living community, which the powwow attendance establishes.
3. **MIN-internal archaeological survey** before any further development; any undocumented burials trigger Va. burial-law protection automatically.
4. **Conservation easement** to lock in the protective overlay against future governance changes.

**Yellowstone-tactic angle:** TCP designation is the move. Bulletin 38 was designed for exactly this kind of property — a place whose significance lies in living cultural practice rather than archaeological-record-only.

## 5. Burial Grounds — Undocumented but Likely

Va. burial law (§§ 10.1-2305 to 10.1-2316) protects all unmarked human burials regardless of designation. Documented but un-designated cases:

1. **Clarks Mountain Cemetery** — Brady's family (§ 3.14).
2. **Amherst County Monacan Burial Ground** — referenced in DHR's 2010 Amherst Cost-Share Survey (https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/pdf_files/SpecialCollections/Amherst_CostShareSurvey_FINAL.pdf — partial pull, full survey requires DHR direct request). Specific GPS not in indexed public record under Va. Code § 10.1-2202 confidentiality.
3. **Bear Mountain ancestral burials** — internal to MIN, intentionally not publicly mapped.
4. **Laurel Cliff Farm** — 1,292 acres, no archaeological survey yet conducted publicly.
5. **Rassawek — non-mound burials** — Hantman + Cultural Heritage Partners-coordinated archaeological work documents adjacent burials beyond the AD 1070–1440 mound activity.
6. **Rapidan Mound + Stegara** — documented extensively by UVA 1988–1998.
7. **Rockbridge / Pedlar Creek "Brown People" community burials** — likely on private farmland; mapped only by family memory.

**Pathway (all undocumented cases):**
- Va. burial-law overlay automatic.
- DHR confidentiality protections under § 10.1-2202 — locations are not public.
- NAGPRA standing for any federally-funded development.
- MIN-internal archaeological survey of own holdings (Laurel Cliff, Bear Mountain expansion, Daisy Adcock, 339-acre).
- Family-driven Va. burial-law assertion for sites on private farmland (Brady can assert for Clarks Mountain Cemetery as descendant).

## 7. Highest-Leverage Next Steps — 2–3 Lead Case Studies for Recognition Push

Brady asked: which 2–3 landmarks should the Recognition Push companion deliverable lead with as case studies? The criteria: high public visibility + actionable legal lever + MIN governance already committed in some form + symbolic weight. Ranked:

### Lead Case Study #1 — **Rassawek (NRHP + NHL lock-in)**

**Why lead with this:** It's the win the Recognition Push exists to lock in. MIN owns the land. The 2018–2022 fight produced a unanimous JRWA reversal, parcel transfer, and national press coverage. Yet five years later the site is **still NRHP-undesignated as a discrete property.** That is documentation drift. Closing it is the most credible "we know how to use these tools" demonstration MIN can run. It signals to every future applicant for federal funding in the James-Rivanna corridor that Section 106 is not a procedural box — it's a substantive determination.

**Concrete asks:** (1) Va. DHR sponsors NRHP nomination Q3 2026; (2) MIN files NHL nomination Q1 2027 on the back of NRHP listing; (3) permanent conservation easement on tribally-held parcel by year-end 2026; (4) NPS feasibility study for National Monument designation as a long-horizon stretch.

**Pressure overlay:** National Trust + SELC + Cultural Heritage Partners coalition is intact. Reactivate.

### Lead Case Study #2 — **Monasukapanough Archaeological District + UVA NAGPRA audit (combined ask)**

**Why lead with this:** It pairs the most archaeologically-significant Monacan village outside Rassawek with the UVA Stewardship Essay deliverable. Karenne Wood's research site. UVA expansion + VDOT US-29 corridor + Albemarle development are recurring federal-undertaking nexuses where Section 106 should fire automatically. The combined ask of (a) NRHP listing for the district + (b) NAGPRA inventory audit at UVA + (c) institutional consultation MOU is the entire UVA stewardship play in one package. The Karenne Wood biographical marker is the human-interest hook.

**Concrete asks:** (1) NRHP Archaeological District nomination including Monasukapanough proper + Jefferson's Mound + Indian Grave site as contributing properties; (2) NAGPRA inventory request to UVA; (3) institutional MOU for systematic Section 106 consultation on all UVA federal undertakings in MIN homeland; (4) Karenne Wood biographical marker.

**Pressure overlay:** UVA institutional politics post-2017, Karenne Wood's standing in the Charlottesville cultural community, and MIN's federal-recognition standing make this a strong combined ask.

### Lead Case Study #3 — **"Brown People of Irish Creek" historic marker + Clarks Mountain Cemetery NRHP eligibility (Brady-family load-bearing)**

**Why lead with this:** It's the family-circulation case study. Plecker's 1943 list named Rockbridge County explicitly. Eighty years later there is no marker, no NRHP property, no formal community recognition in the Pedlar Creek / Irish Creek corridor. This is the recognition deficit at the diaspora level — and the one Brady can drive personally as a Clark/Tyree descendant. The marker push is cheap (~$2,500). The Clarks Mountain Cemetery NRHP eligibility evaluation is the structural follow-up. Together they put the Rockbridge-side Monacan diaspora on the public map for the first time.

**Concrete asks:** (1) Va. DHR marker nomination "Brown People of Irish Creek" (or similar) — sponsor: Brady + Rockbridge Historical Society + MIN; (2) NRHP eligibility evaluation for Clarks Mountain Cemetery; (3) Va. burial-law assertion overlay on the cemetery; (4) MPS feasibility study for "Rockbridge / Amherst Free People of Color / Indian-identifying Communities, pre-1924."

**Pressure overlay:** Family-historian coalition (Brady, extended descendants), Rockbridge Historical Society, MIN tribal government, and academic backing (Vest 2003, Estes 2012, Houck 1984, Smith 1992) are all in place. The 2024 *Sorrells v. Shields* archive material at LVA is the documentary foundation. Brady is already on the path to LVA Richmond per Tier A4 of V2-QUEUE.
