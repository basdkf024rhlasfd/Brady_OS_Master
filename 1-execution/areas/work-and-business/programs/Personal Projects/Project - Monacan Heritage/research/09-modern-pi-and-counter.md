### 8. Possible Irregularities / Open Questions (Honest List)

1. **Surname / family concentration in tribal government + Foundation board.** Branham, Shields, Bryant, Thompson, Latimer dominate both rosters. Normal for a 2,700-member tribe with documented Bear Mountain lineage; *not* normal in a corporate-governance sense. Public record does not contain any conflict-of-interest disclosure regime. **Open Q: does MIN have a written conflict policy?**
2. **Edith "Lou" Parrish drawing $19,360 from MNCF (FY2024) while serving as Assistant Chief.** Legal under tribal/IRS rules; legal under Virginia nonprofit law. Public-disclosure transparency is the question. **Open Q: how is MNCF compensation set and minuted?**
3. **CHP / Greg Werkheiser as both general counsel to the tribe and personal-capacity defense counsel for accused officials.** Normal arrangement; potentially conflicting if a sitting official is accused by a faction *of the tribe itself*. Brooks v. Branham was resolved on sovereign-immunity grounds before this conflict had to be litigated.
4. **No publicly-disclosed CHP fee schedule.** General-counsel fees from public-sector / tribal clients are typically $200K–$600K/yr at firms of CHP's size. **Public record contains zero data on what MIN pays CHP annually.** Fact gap, not allegation.
5. **No BIA self-governance compact or 477 plan publicly indexed.** Either MIN runs its programs through ~per-grant 638 contracts (typical for newly-recognized small tribes) or has an arrangement not surfaced in BIA's public materials. **Open Q: 638-contract list?**
6. **"Three fully independent audits, all came back perfectly clean"** — defense counsel's statement during *Brooks v. Branham*. **Audits not publicly published.** Single Audit Act requires federal-funding recipients above $750K/yr to file audited financials with the Federal Audit Clearinghouse (FAC). **Open Q: is MIN listed in FAC?** (https://facweb.census.gov/ — direct query needed.)
7. **Laurel Cliff Farm financing.** $5.5M cash purchase by a then-newly-recognized tribe with then-modest Foundation revenue ($254K in 2020) is a striking transaction. Source of funds likely CARES Act fiscal-recovery + donor + commercial loan, but **the breakdown is not in the public record.**
8. **CARES-money-as-per-capita policy.** The $91,600 / 9 plaintiffs = $10K each disputed in *Brooks v. Branham* implies MIN distributed CARES money on a per-capita basis to enrolled members. The ages 0–18 enrollment policy currently in force (per prior research file) means **adult members born after the policy locked are systematically excluded from per-capita streams.** This is a structural governance question, not corruption — but it is the engine behind the *Brooks v. Branham* case and any future faction litigation.
9. **No publicly-disclosed land-deal-with-tribal-officials.** No public record connects sitting council members or chiefs to land sold to or bought from the tribe. (Searched but no positive result.) That is a *negative* finding worth stating: the public record is clean on this question.
10. **Buffalo Ridge Cherokee identity dispute** (covered in prior doc) — no 2024–2026 development surfaced.

## PART III — The Other Side of the Ledger (Counter-Narrative)

### 1. Health & Social Service Outcomes

- **Madison Heights health clinic** — first Native-American-owned-and-operated full-service tribal clinic in Virginia, $10M, **tribe-self-funded** (not federal), 13,000 sq ft, drive-through pharmacy, dental, vision, hearing, behavioral health. Free care to any enrolled member of any federally-recognized tribe in the country. **More than 20 jobs created in Amherst County.** **[5]** **This is a substantial, clean, locally-rooted accomplishment.**
- **Mid-Atlantic Tribal Health Center (Charles City VA)** — $18M IHS facility serving MIN + 6 sister VA tribes (Pamunkey, Chickahominy, Chickahominy Eastern, Upper Mattaponi, Rappahannock, Nansemond). 27,000 sq ft. **[5]**
- **IHS PRCDA expansion (late 2023):** **+1,400 PRC-eligible members** added across the 7 mid-Atlantic tribes — measurable healthcare-access expansion attributable directly to federal recognition. **[4]**
- **Special Diabetes Program for Indians (SDPI):** ~$50K–$100K/yr recurring, treating a documented community-health priority. **[5]**
- **OAA Title VI elder programs:** $175K+/yr for elder nutrition + supportive services. **[5]**
- **LIHEAP energy assistance:** ~$22K/yr, low-income households.
- **CCDF child care:** $300K+/yr program.

### 2. Cultural Preservation

- **Karenne Wood (1960–2019)** — UVA PhD, directed Virginia Indian Programs at Virginia Humanities, Repatriation Commission member, Library of Virginia "Virginia Women in History" 2015. Co-author with Diane Shields of the Smithsonian-archived *The Monacan Indians: Our Story.* UVA later commissioned a memorial portrait. **[5]**
- **Rassawek victory (March 2022)** — defended what may be the long-lost capital from the James River Water Authority pumping station. CHP pro bono. National Trust for Historic Preservation listed Rassawek as one of America's 11 Most Endangered Historic Places. JRWA agreed to deed parcel to MIN. **A genuinely important repatriation precedent for ALL federally-recognized tribes east of the Mississippi.** **[5]**
- **Monacan Powwow** — annual third-weekend-of-May gathering at Laurel Cliff Farm (225 Laurel Cliff Rd). Open to public. Listed by Amherst County Tourism. **[4]**
- **Monacan Ancestral Museum / Culture Center** — adjacent to Bear Mountain on land now expanded by the Daisy Adcock Property. **[4]**
- **Yesáh Community House** — referenced in More Mountain Stories' "Monuments Across Appalachian Virginia" project. **[3]**
