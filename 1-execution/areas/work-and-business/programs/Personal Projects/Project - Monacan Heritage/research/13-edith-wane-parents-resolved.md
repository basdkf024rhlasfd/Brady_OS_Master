## Implication for the Native-ancestry question

The Sally Wane → English origin finding means **the Native ancestry in Brady's paternal Clark line does NOT trace cleanly through Edith Wane's mother's side.** That entire line is documented English, with primary sources from English parish registers.

The remaining candidate entry points for Native ancestry in the paternal Clark line, in order of likelihood:

1. **Harrison Wane (1773)** — Edith's father. **Most likely candidate** because of the absence of any documentation. The pattern fits Virginia tri-racial isolate families: free people of color and Native-descended families typically have minimal records in early-19th-century Virginia. A Harrison Wane who was a "marginal" family member would not appear in tax rolls, militia records, deeds, or census records. The fact that FamilySearch has only a name and a year for him is itself suggestive.
2. **Anne Moseley (~1755–1804)** — Nelson Harrison Clark's mother. Need to verify whether she had any documented Native ancestry or community ties in the Henrico / Rockbridge area.
3. **Earlier admixture in the deeper paternal Clarke line** — James Clarke (~1755) and his ancestors. Multiple Clarke lines in early Virginia, some with mixed-race branches.
4. **Direct admixture in Nelson Harrison Clark's own life** — the 1860 Mulatto classification could reflect his community membership and physical appearance (which is what census enumerators worked from), without requiring cleanly documented Native parentage. The community pattern was that Mulatto classification often followed who you married, where you lived, and how you were socially perceived — not strictly biological lineage.

Brady's Native ancestry is documented and real (the 1860 Mulatto classification for Nelson Harrison Clark is primary federal record evidence). But the *exact* path of how it entered the paternal Clark line is not yet pinned down to a specific named ancestor.
