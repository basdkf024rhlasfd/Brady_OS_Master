### 1.1 — Rockbridge personal property tax extractions (Heinegg, 1782–1821)

The South River location code (S.R.) is the same district where, three decades later, Brady's GEDCOM places Joseph Carter Clark Jr. (b. 1825) and his descendants down to Joseph Anderson Clark (b. 1869). **This is the first online primary-source evidence putting Brady's Clark line in the South River free-Negro community before 1820.**

## Part 3 — Curry surname, above Ransom Curry (Tier B3-online)

### 3.1 — The 1810 Mary Curry Amherst lead

Mary Curry, head of an "Other Free" household with 6 dependents in Amherst County in the 1810 federal census (`https://freeafricanamericans.com/1810VAa.htm`), is the earliest free-non-white Curry-surnamed Amherst entry in any digitized index Brady has access to. She **pre-dates Peter Curry's 1849–50 Rockbridge tax appearance by 39 years.** Two interpretations:

1. **Mary Curry of 1810 Amherst is Peter Curry's grandmother (or great-aunt).** She's old enough — to be heading a 7-person household in 1810 she's likely 30–60 years old, born 1750–1780. If she's even 35 in 1810, she could plausibly be the matriarch of the Curry line that produced Peter Curry (acquired by Joseph Carter Clark Sr. at auction in the 1820s–40s, freed before 1849, on Rockbridge tax rolls 1849–50, and likely the same Curry line that produced John and Lucy Curry of Walker's Creek per BHR file 16).
2. **Mary Curry of 1810 Amherst is unrelated to the Peter Curry / John & Lucy Curry / Samuel Curry chain.** Possible — the Curry surname is not unique. But a free-non-white Curry household in Amherst County 1810, 39 years before Peter Curry surfaces in Rockbridge tax records, in a county adjacent to Rockbridge and with active free-non-white community continuity, is a much stronger candidate than coincidence allows.

If interpretation #1 holds, **the Curry line above Ransom Curry runs back through at least the 1780s into Amherst County**, paralleling the Clark line's own Amherst-Rockbridge corridor. That puts Brady's Curry connection in the same Monacan-county system as his Clark connection — and probably for the same reason.

### 3.2 — Curry not in Heinegg's Amherst PPT 1782–1822 individual extracts

Notable absence: when Heinegg's `https://freeafricanamericans.com/Amherst.htm` is grep-pulled for Curry, **no Curry tax-list entry appears 1782–1822**. Two possibilities:

1. Heinegg's Amherst extract is selective; Curry tax entries exist but weren't pulled into his free-Negro-focused index. (The index name is *Free African Americans*, not "every free non-white person ever recorded.") The Mary Curry 1810 census hit suggests she'd have appeared on Amherst tax rolls — possibly under a husband's name or as a non-tithable widow.
2. Mary Curry of 1810 was a recent migrant into Amherst from another Virginia county. Worth checking adjacent counties (Bedford, Buckingham, Nelson) for 1800–1810 free-non-white Curry households.

### 3.4 — Curry surname in Will Books, BHR Rockbridge dataset

## Part 5 — Gaps named explicitly

1. **Joseph Anderson Clark's parents — still a paper-trail gap.** v1 GEDCOM (master sources D37, file 11) places JAC b. 1869 with parents Nelson Alexander Clark (1857–1902) + Mary Martha Robertson (1860–1898). FamilySearch ID for JAC needs to be captured during the Brady-on-Chrome walk (V2-QUEUE Tier A1). No online primary source pulled in this sweep names JAC by name.
2. **Edith Wane's parents — still unknown.** File 13 closed the question of Sally Wane (English-born) but Harrison Wane (b. 1773) appears in FamilySearch with zero sources. Online sweep produced no new lead.
3. **Joseph Carter Clark Sr.'s 1816 "F.N." Rockbridge tax entry (Heinegg) is not yet linked to him by primary source.** The 1816 Heinegg extract reads "Joseph Clark F.N. 1 tithe 1 horse." Joseph Carter Clark Sr. (b. 1797) would be 19 in 1816 — the right age for first tithable enrollment. But the surname Joseph Clark was common; this could be a different Joseph. Linking requires a continuous run of "Joseph Clark F.N." entries 1816–1820s onward, until the Joseph Carter Clark Sr. household composition (children's birth years) matches the tax-list household composition. **That's an LVA microfilm reading-room job (or full Ancestry sub) — none of it is online free.**
4. **The first Peter Curry Rockbridge tax appearance year between 1822 and 1849.** Curry surname enters Rockbridge somewhere in this 27-year window. Heinegg cuts off 1821; BHR has 1849. The 28-year gap is the manumission-or-arrival window for Peter Curry. Online free? No. Ancestry sub or LVA microfilm reading room.
5. **Mary Curry (Amherst, 1810) parentage and descent.** No online source yet links her to Peter Curry, John & Lucy Curry, or any later Rockbridge Curry. McLeRoy's *Strangers in Their Midst* is the highest-leverage single lead.
6. **The "William Clark, Sr., M. Couler" of 1815 Amherst is not yet linked to any branch of Brady's Clark tree.** He's clearly in the same micro-community as the Branhams and Coopers. He's likely related to the white William Clark of 1782 Amherst (10056 livestock + slaves) only through the surname; the Heinegg classification "M. Couler" 33 years later means a distinct individual, not the same man. Whether he descends from one of the 1782 Amherst Clark slaveholders' enslaved or near-relations is unknowable from online sources alone.
7. **No online-free primary source places a Curry in the Monacan rolls (joepayne.org) for the pre-1869 era.** Curry is not a canonical Monacan surname per the v1 master sources. If the Curry-Clark connection exists at all, it runs through a slaveholding relationship (Joseph Carter Clark Sr. → Peter Curry → free), not through tribal-marriage rolls.
8. **Tyree surname Rockbridge pre-1850 — no online free hits.** Tyree is in Amherst from 1782 (Jacob Tyree as substantial slaveholder; Nathan Tyree more modest). The Clark/Tyree Monacan-rolls 1906 marriage entry on joepayne.org joins two Amherst-anchored surnames; but pre-1869 Tyree presence in Rockbridge County (which v2 B2 wants) is not documented in the online sources pulled today.
