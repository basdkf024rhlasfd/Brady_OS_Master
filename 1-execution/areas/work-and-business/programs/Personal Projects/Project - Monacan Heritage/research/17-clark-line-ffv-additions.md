## 3. Per-ancestor capsules

These are imported from Donahoe project file 13 (`13-deep-line-extension-and-fraud-flags.md` references the rich detail; the original capsule work was in earlier Donahoe research files but is summarized here):

### Capt Adam Thoroughgood (1604–1640)

- **Position:** Burgess for Elizabeth City, 1629–32. Member of the Governor's Council, 1637–38. Among the founders of the institutional structure of the Virginia Colony.
- **Land:** 5,350-acre patent on the Lynnhaven (modern Virginia Beach / Princess Anne County) — one of the largest single grants in the early colony. Established the Thoroughgood Plantation, of which the surviving Adam Thoroughgood House is one of the oldest brick houses in English North America.
- **Civic infrastructure:** Established the **first ferry across Hampton Roads in 1636** — predecessor of the modern Hampton Roads Bridge-Tunnel system. This ferry was a foundational piece of colonial-era Virginia logistics.
- **Naming:** Helped name **New Norfolk County** (later split into Lower Norfolk and Upper Norfolk, eventually Norfolk and Princess Anne — the modern Hampton Roads / Tidewater geography).
- **Death:** Died young (age ~36) in 1640. Estate passed to widow Sarah Offley.

### Sarah Offley (1609–1657)

- **51 sources on FamilySearch profile** — among the most-documented colonial Virginia women.
- **Three husbands:** Capt Adam Thoroughgood (1627), then John Gookin (after Adam's 1640 death), then Francis Yeardley (son of Gov. George Yeardley) after Gookin's death. Each marriage tied her to a different power block in the Council and Burgesses.
- **Defied 10 court orders** during disputes over the Thoroughgood estate. Held the 5,350-acre patent under her control across multiple administrations.
- **Will:** Famously specified diamond rings for her daughters as bequests — unusual material wealth for a 17th-century colonial woman; preserved in Lower Norfolk County records.
- **Bridge to Tidewater elite:** The Offley name traced to Anne Offley Yeardley (via Francis Yeardley, her third husband) and into the broader Yeardley/Berkeley/Bennett political network.

### Maj Gen John Custis II Esq (1630–1696)

- **1651 Oath of Allegiance:** Took the oath when Cromwell's commissioners arrived in Virginia, transitioning his political position through the Civil War / Commonwealth era. This is documented in colonial records as a formal political act that secured his standing under the new regime.
- **Arlington Mansion:** Built Arlington on the Eastern Shore of Virginia (Northampton County) — the largest brick house in the colony at its time, predating the Williamsburg Governor's Palace by decades. Archaeological remnants survive.
- **1676 Bacon's Rebellion:** When Nathaniel Bacon's forces burned Jamestown and Gov. **William Berkeley** fled, Berkeley took refuge at Arlington Mansion. **Arlington briefly served as the de-facto colonial capital of Virginia** during the rebellion. This is one of the few moments when the seat of colonial government was on the Eastern Shore.
- **Title:** Major General in the Virginia militia; member of the Governor's Council.
- **Slaveholder:** His will — preserved in Northampton County records — specifies enslaved persons among his bequests. This is documented and unambiguous.
- **Family arc:** His son Capt John Custis III continued the Eastern Shore political dynasty. Five generations later, Daniel Parke Custis married Martha Dandridge (later Martha Washington), tying the Custis line to George Washington — a separate branch from Brady's, but the same patriarch.

## 4. Other surfaced surnames in the Clark-paternal-line cascade

Between the FFV patriarchs (Thoroughgood, Offley, Custis II) and Lutie Fannie Clark, the cascade runs through six intermediate surnames that the existing Monacan project does not yet cover with capsule depth. These are documented in Brady's GEDCOM and on FamilySearch but warrant later research-file attention:

### Heartly / Hartless

The same family. **The surname drifts from "Heartly" to "Hartless" within a single generation** — John Heartly (1784–1858) is the father of Eliza Hartless (1816–1880). Surname drift in early-19th-century Virginia is common, often the result of inconsistent enumerator orthography rather than a deliberate change.

**Both spellings are on Plecker's 1943 list.** Specifically: "Hartless" appears on **both** the Amherst County and the Rockbridge County lists (file 02 of this project, Section 3, surname enumeration). This is a material fact: the Hartless/Heartly surname running through Lutie Fannie Clark's maternal-grandmother line is a documented Plecker-era erasure target. See Section 5.

### Floyd

Julia A Floyd (1853–1900) and her daughter Sarah Luticia Floyd (1869–1950) — the latter is almost certainly the source of Lutie Fannie Clark's first name "Lutie." **"Floyd" is on Plecker's 1943 list for both Amherst County and Rockbridge County** (file 02, Section 3).

The Floyd surname is independently documented in the Donahoe project (Donahoe file 16) as having its own colonial-Virginia patriarch — **John Floyd I "of Hog Island"** (~1640–1687), a Welsh-origin Northampton County immigrant arriving 1660. Whether the Rockbridge/Amherst Floyd line in Brady's Clark/Monacan tree connects to that same colonial patriarch is an open question; the GEDCOM cascade as captured here goes Eliza Hartless → Julia A Floyd, and the Floyd surname could enter through Julia's father (not yet pinned in the data extracted into file 15).

### Sorrells

**This is the highest-leverage cross-check.** Georgie Anna Sorrells (1883–1969) — Brady's great-great-grandmother — carries the same surname as Atha Sorrells, William Sorrells, Julia (Sorrells) Clark, and Diannah Sorrells Mason, all canonical to the Monacan project (file 02, Sections 4 and 6; file 05, Sections 5 and 9).

See Section 5 below for the cross-check analysis.

### Cash

Nellie Mae Cash (1910–2003) — Brady's great-grandmother and Lutie Fannie Clark's mother. **"Cash" is on Plecker's 1943 list for both Amherst and Rockbridge counties** (file 02, Section 3).

The Monacan project's existing research file 11 (`11-gedcom-corrected-paternal-line.md`) and file 16 (`16-curry-rockbridge-bhr-findings.md`) already explicitly identify the Cash line as connected to documented Native ancestry on Brady's tree:

### The hypothesis

**Georgie Anna Sorrells is highly likely to be a relative of the Sorrells family in *Sorrells v. Shields*.** The base rate of unrelated Sorrells families sharing the same Rockbridge/Amherst small-county geography in the same single generation is low. Brady Jr.'s *Irish Creek: History and Diversions*, the LDS missionary record, the Native Heritage Project's writeup of the Sorrells case, Encyclopedia Virginia, and the JMU thesis — is an endogamous tri-racial isolate. Endogamy means relatedness; the same surnames recur because the same families intermarry across generations.
