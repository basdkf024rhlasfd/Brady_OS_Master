**Prepared for:** Brady Smallwood
**Date:** 2026-05-06
**Methodology:** Public records only. Web searches, named-entity sweeps, ProPublica Form 990 lookup, federal court archive. No PACER pull, no paid people-search aggregators, no VSB lookup (vsb.org returned 403 on direct fetch).

## 1. Adrian Compton — Tribal Administrator

**Email of record:** TribalAdmin@MonacanNation.gov (per VA Secretary of the Commonwealth, state-recognized-tribes page; cred 5).
**Named defendant** in *Brooks v. Branham* (W.D. Va., 2022).

**What the public record says:**
- Tribal Administrator of the Monacan Indian Nation; primary public-facing operations spokesperson on land acquisitions and the Mid-Atlantic Tribal Health Center groundbreaking. (Source: News & Advance "groundbreaking" coverage Oct 2021; WDBJ7; cred 5.)
- **Age 68** as quoted in News & Advance reporting (so birth ~1957, ±1). (Source: News & Advance, "Amid pandemic challenges, new Monacan tribal office offers support"; cred 4.)
- **Grew up in Amherst County.** Direct quote attributed to Compton: getting into Amherst County public schools "was a fight" when he was a child. This places him in the desegregation cohort — Amherst County's *Sorrells v. Shields* desegregation order came down in 1963; Compton would have been roughly 6 years old at that time. He is therefore **plausibly Plecker-era affected**, which is consistent with — though not proof of — Monacan tribal membership. (Source: News & Advance; cred 4 for the quote, cred 3 for the desegregation-cohort inference.)
- Quoted in 2020-2021 saying the Nation purchased its 111 Highview Drive headquarters property "in the beginning of October" (2020) and that Sens. Kaine and Warner were instrumental in the IHS-funded clinic. He speaks for the Nation on operational matters; Branham/Shields on political and ceremonial matters. (Source: WDBJ7; News & Advance; cred 5.)

**What is NOT in indexed public record:**
- **No LinkedIn profile** that can be matched to the Monacan Adrian Compton. The four Adrian Comptons surfaced by direct LinkedIn search — a Texas pastor, a masonry apprentice, a Snellville GA football recruit, and a deactivated profile — are demonstrably not him. (Source: LinkedIn People Directory; cred 4 — absence of evidence.)
- **No prior employer named.** No Amherst County government role, no nonprofit board seat, no military service surfaced.
- **No salary disclosure on the Monacan Nation Cultural Foundation Form 990 FY2024.** The MNCF 990 lists Sally Latimer ($44,505), Jessica Maupin ($23,232), Edith Parrish ($19,360), Jennifer Austin ($1,380), and three uncompensated officers (Lindsey Taylor, Matt Latimer, Dean Branham). **Compton is not on the MNCF 990 at all** — meaning his Tribal Administrator salary is paid by the **tribal government entity, not the cultural foundation**, and the tribal government does not file a public 990 (federally recognized tribes are sovereigns and not required to). His salary is therefore not publicly knowable. (Source: ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer EIN 54-1656446; cred 5.)
- **No Virginia state court records** could be confirmed (vsb.org and the VA Courts CIS portal were not accessible to this sweep; would require manual pull).
- **No Compton surname in the canonical Bear Mountain ancestral list.** The publicly cited Bear Mountain ancestral surnames are Johns, Branham, Hicks, Lawless, Beverly, Adcock, Redcross, Knuckles, Duff, Clark, Roberts, Nuckles, Willis, Hamilton, Terry. **Compton does not appear.** (Source: Native Heritage Project; Monacan Nation history page; cred 4.) This does **not** rule out tribal enrollment — the lineal-descent rule allows enrollment via maternal line, and Compton may be enrolled through a mother whose surname is on the list — but the patrilineal Compton thread is not historically dominant in Bear Mountain.

**Flags for a PI:**
- **Salary opacity is structural, not suspicious.** Tribal government employees of federally recognized tribes are inside sovereign immunity and outside public-disclosure regimes. Anyone wanting to know what Compton earns would need a tribal-budget leak, a federal grant audit (HHS/IHS/HUD), or BIA OIG involvement.
- **The patrilineal-surname gap is the most actionable PI thread.** A genealogist could quickly resolve whether Compton is enrolled via a documented Bear Mountain maternal line, hired-from-outside as a non-enrolled administrator, or something else. The Monacan enrollment packet is publicly published; an enrolled Adrian Compton would be findable in the rolls if those rolls were ever public (they aren't).

## 2. Bobby Thompson Jr. — Lead Plaintiff, *Brooks v. Branham*

**What the public record says:**
- Lead public spokesperson for the nine-plaintiff group that sued Chief Kenneth Branham, Tribal Administrator Adrian Compton, and Secretary Amber Fink in **June 2022**, alleging wrongful denial of CARES Act / health / housing / per-capita federal distributions. (Source: WSET ABC13 Investigates; News & Advance; cred 4.)
- Plaintiffs' attorney was **Joe Sanzone** (Lynchburg-area criminal-and-civil practitioner). (Source: WSET; cred 4.)
- Thompson's specific public claim: leadership "disenrolled" members and used suspension to halt benefits; over 100 tribal citizens attended a November 2021 meeting that voted to remove Branham's council and elect a replacement; the replacement faction asserted bylaw and federal-law authority for the action. (Source: WSET; cred 3-4 — paraphrased local broadcast.)
- The federal court (W.D. Va. Lynchburg) dismissed in **June 2023** for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction and on tribal sovereign immunity grounds. (Source: News & Advance June 2023; NARF *Brooks v. Branham* PDF; cred 5.)

**What is NOT in indexed public record (post-June 2023):**
- **No appeal to the Fourth Circuit could be identified.** Searches for "Brooks v. Branham" on the Fourth Circuit opinion archive return unrelated cases. Findlaw indexes the W.D. Va. Lynchburg dismissal but not an appellate disposition. (Cred 4 — strong signal that no appeal was filed or the appeal was administratively closed without a published opinion.)
- **No 2024-2025-2026 public statements by Bobby Thompson Jr. about Monacan governance** could be located via standard search. The "Bobby Thompson" name is heavily polluted by a British touring-car driver, a strongman, a college football player, a California personal-injury attorney, and the Navy charity scammer — none of whom are the Monacan plaintiff.
- **No formal organized faction** ("Monacan Concerned Members" or similar) surfaces in 2024-2026 press, Facebook, or petition platforms. The breakaway slate's public footprint appears to have collapsed after the dismissal.
- **No subsequent state-court litigation, BIA Office of Federal Acknowledgement complaint, or Inspector General audit petition** could be located in indexed public sources.

**Structural read:** the dismissal removed the federal lever; tribal sovereign immunity blocks the state-court lever; BIA-OFA only audits *recognition* status, not internal disputes; HHS-OIG and BIA-OIG would only investigate on a referral. The plaintiffs have **no obvious remaining legal channel**, which is consistent with the silence.

**Flags for a PI:**
- The most informative artifact would be the **November 2021 breakaway-meeting resolutions and attendance list** — those documents would name every dissenting tribal citizen. They were not filed in *Brooks v. Branham* in a way that became public.
- Joe Sanzone's case file is the highest-yield single source. He's a private attorney, so the file is not public, but a journalist with a relationship could plausibly reach it.

## 3. Buffalo Ridge Cherokee / United Cherokee Indian Tribe of Virginia — 2024-2026

**Organization:** United Cherokee Indian Tribe of Virginia, Incorporated (UCITOVA), commonly the **Buffalo Ridge Band of Cherokee**, ~650 members, headquartered in Madison Heights, Amherst County — **same county as the Monacan Indian Nation**. 501(c)(3). Public-facing email ucitova@aol.com. (Source: ucitova.org; virginiaplaces.org; cred 4.)

**State-recognition history:**
- **2013:** Sen. Steve Newman (R-Lynchburg) introduced recognition bills for both UCITOVA and the Appalachian Cherokee Nation. Both failed; the federally recognized Cherokee tribes (Eastern Band, Cherokee Nation, United Keetoowah Band) opposed. (Source: virginiaplaces.org; Cherokee Phoenix; SJ300 text; cred 4.)
- **2024-2025:** UCITOVA itself does **not** appear to have a fresh state-recognition bill in motion. Recognition activity in this window has shifted to a **different** Cherokee-claim group — the **Wolf Creek Cherokee Tribe of Virginia** (HJ14, prefiled January 2024; reintroduced as HJR14 in 2025). Wolf Creek's headquarters is in Henrico County (Richmond suburbs), not Amherst, but its existence further fragments the "Virginia Cherokee" identity claim. (Source: Richmond Sunlight HJ14 2024; LIS HJ14 2025; cred 5.)
- **HB 2134 (2025):** Updates Virginia Code to formally name the federally recognized tribes. **Passed the legislature; vetoed by Gov. Youngkin.** This is a setback for the federally recognized tribes (Monacan included) and an indirect win for unrecognized claimants. (Source: web search; cred 4 — confirms what 04-leadership-oppo.md flagged as a gap.)

**Current Buffalo Ridge leadership:** ucitova.org's "Tribal Officers" page was not accessible in this sweep; no current chief or council names could be confirmed. **Not in indexed public record at this depth.**

**Direct conflicts with MIN:** No public statement by either group naming the other in 2024-2026. The geographic overlap (both in Madison Heights / Amherst) is **the** structural conflict — UCITOVA descends in part from the same Bear Mountain free-people-of-color community that Peter Houck's 1984 *Indian Island in Amherst County* re-identified as Monacan. Each enrollment for one group is an implicit claim against the other's identity narrative. The Monacan have not publicly engaged.

**Flags for a PI:**
- The **Youngkin veto of HB 2134** is the under-covered story. A Republican governor blocking recognition language for federally recognized tribes is a political signal worth following.
- Wolf Creek's HJ14 2024-2025 push deserves its own thread — the geography is wrong (Henrico, not historically Cherokee territory), the linguistic claim is contested, and yet it has a sponsor and momentum.

## 4. Other Named MIN Officials — Quick Sweeps

### Sally Latimer — MNCF Director ($44,505 FY24)
- **Co-Director of the Monacan Indian Nation Food Bank** since 2018. Joined to address food insecurity in the Madison Heights / Amherst community; the food bank serves all food-insecure Amherst County residents, not Monacan-only.
- Featured in Southern Foodways Alliance oral history interview (Aug 2020) alongside Jennifer Austin. Profiled by Feeding America and Blue Ridge Area Food Bank.
- **Day job:** the public record describes her food-bank work as her primary identifiable role; no parallel professional employment surfaces. (Source: SFA interview PDF; sharegreaterlynchburg.org; cred 4.)
- **Family:** **Matt Latimer** is listed as MNCF Secretary on the FY24 990 (uncompensated). Strong inference: husband or close family. (Source: ProPublica EIN 54-1656446; cred 4.)

### Jessica Maupin — MNCF Chairman ($23,232 FY24)
- Chairman of the MNCF board. Compensation suggests roughly half-time engagement at a modest stipend.
- **Day job / background: not in indexed public record.** No press hits, no LinkedIn match confirmed, no biographical material on minculturalfoundation.org or monacannation.gov beyond the title.

### Edith "Lou" Branham Parrish — Assistant Chief / MNCF Director ($19,360 FY24)
- **Eldest daughter of Ronnie and Eleanor Branham**, both Monacan. (Source: monacannation.gov bio; cred 5.)
- **Dual role:** Assistant Chief of the Monacan Indian Nation **AND** Director of the Monacan Indian Nation Ancestral Museum (which sits inside MNCF). The museum directorship explains the MNCF 990 line.
- The combined Asst. Chief + Museum Director role makes her one of the **most operationally embedded** members of leadership — political, cultural, and financial visibility all converge on one person. (Source: moremountainstories.org "Yesáh Community House"; randolphcollege.edu land-acknowledgment coverage; cred 4.)

### Diane Shields — Chief
- Born in Virginia; family relocated to Ohio at age 5; returned to Amherst County as adult.
- **Career outside the tribe:** office and records management at the **Palm Bay Police Department (Florida)**, **Amherst County Schools**, and **Central Virginia Community College**. (Source: CHP-prepared bio at monacannation.gov; cred 5.)
- Began working with state officials in **1994** to correct birth records of hundreds of tribal members — directly remediating Plecker-era documentary erasure. This is an exceptional, documented Plecker-redress contribution.
- Pivotal role in compiling genealogical and historical evidence for the BIA federal-recognition petition.
- After 2018 recognition, served as **Director of the Monacan Nation Cultural Foundation** before being elected Chief on **June 17, 2023**.
- **Note for PI follow-up:** the Palm Bay PD records-management role is the unusual line on her resume — Florida law-enforcement records-clerk experience, in a town that has no obvious Monacan connection. Worth a single follow-up call to confirm dates.

### Daniel B. Branham vs Dean Branham
- Both sit on the current Tribal Council per monacannation.gov. The relationship between them is **not stated in indexed public record**. Branham is the most heavily concentrated surname in Monacan governance — Kenneth Branham (former Chief), Dean Branham (former Chief and current Council), Daniel B. Branham (current Council), Edith Lou Branham Parrish (Asst. Chief, *née* Branham), Mary Frances Branham Bryant (mother of Sharon Bryant), Harry Loving Branham (Sharon Bryant's grandfather, former Chief). The surname dominance is a real-world feature of the Bear Mountain Branham core line, not a research artifact.
- **Dean Branham** specifically: prior Assistant Chief, then Chief for three years, then Council member. (Source: News & Advance; cred 4.) Long-tenure operator inside the same family network that produced Kenneth Branham.

### Active business ownership in Virginia (VA SCC search)

## 6. Faction-Tracking — Organized Opposition?

**Direct finding:** **No organized faction is publicly visible in 2024-2026.**

Specifically:
- **No Facebook page** named "Monacan Concerned Members," "Monacan for Reform," "Save the Monacan Nation," or close variants surfaces on standard search.
- **No blog or Substack** by any of the nine *Brooks v. Branham* plaintiffs surfaces.
- **No petition** on Change.org, BIA Office of Federal Acknowledgement, or comparable platforms petitioning for audit, decertification, or election supervision of MIN.
- **No 2024-2025-2026 news article** in News & Advance, WSET, Virginia Mercury, Roanoke Times, or Washington Post returns hits on a Monacan factional dispute beyond the 2022-2023 *Brooks v. Branham* coverage already captured in `04-leadership-oppo.md`.
- **One named *Brooks v. Branham* plaintiff (Amber Rasnick Fink) is now a sitting Tribal Council member.** This is the single largest signal that the leadership and the dissident faction either reconciled or absorbed each other — the dispute is no longer publicly active.

**Historical disenrollment cases beyond George Whitecalf 1996:** None surface in indexed public record. *Brooks v. Branham* alleged disenrollment as a *mechanism* but did not name disenrolled individuals in public-facing court coverage. Wilkins & Wilkins (*Dismembered*, 2017) covers Whitecalf only.

**Significance:** The absence of an active dissident infrastructure two years after dismissal is consistent with three readings, in descending probability:
1. **Reconciliation** — Fink's seat suggests structural re-absorption of the breakaway.
2. **Demoralization** — federal sovereign immunity and the absence of a remaining legal channel killed the organizing.
3. **Suppression** — anything organized is operating below the public-search waterline (private Facebook groups, family conversations).

The public record cannot distinguish (1) from (2) from (3). What it **can** say is that no faction is currently making public claims.

## Executive Summary (5 bullets)

- **Adrian Compton is opaque by design, not by suspicion.** As a tribal-government employee of a federally recognized sovereign, his salary, prior employers, and personnel record are outside the public-disclosure regime — he is **not** on the MNCF Form 990, and the tribal government doesn't file one. The Compton surname is **not** in the canonical Bear Mountain ancestral surname list, which makes his lineage path (likely maternal) the highest-leverage genealogist question. Quoted age 68, grew up in Amherst, plausibly Plecker-cohort affected.
- ***Brooks v. Branham* is dead and has no apparent successor.** No Fourth Circuit appeal is in indexed public record; no state-court refiling; no BIA-OFA petition; no organized "Monacan Concerned Members" faction in 2024-2026. Lead plaintiff Bobby Thompson Jr. has been silent in indexed press since 2022. Critically, **one named defendant (Amber Fink) now sits on Tribal Council** — strong signal of structural reconciliation or absorption, not ongoing fracture.
- **Cultural Heritage Partners is small, married-founder, and a party — not a neutral.** 10 people total (6 attorneys), Richmond-headquartered, founded 2010. Greg Werkheiser also runs ArtGlass (AR cultural-tourism). They were defense counsel in *Brooks v. Branham*, so any CHP-published narrative about Monacan governance is advocacy. **VSB disciplinary lookup was not completable** — recommend manual pull.
- **Buffalo Ridge / UCITOVA recognition is dormant; the action has shifted to Wolf Creek Cherokee** (HJ14 2024 / HJR14 2025) in Henrico County. **Gov. Youngkin vetoed HB 2134 (2025)**, the bill that would have updated Virginia Code to formally name federally recognized tribes — **a setback for the Monacan and a structural opening for unrecognized claimants** that is under-covered in regional press.
- **MNCF FY24 financials and salaries are clean and auditable.** Revenue $940K, expenses $531K, net $409K, total assets $1.24M. Compensation: Sally Latimer $44.5K, Jessica Maupin $23.2K, Edith Parrish $19.4K, Jennifer Austin $1.4K, three uncompensated officers (including Dean Branham). No outsized executive salaries, no related-party flags surfaced in this pass. **The financial story is not where the governance story sits** — the governance story is in the tribal-government entity, which doesn't file a 990, and that's the fundamental limit on this kind of public-records sweep.
