# 12 — Property and Procurement: MIN Real Estate, Construction Contracts, and Land Acquisitions

**Research date:** 2026-05-06
**Scope:** Monacan Health Center (Madison Heights), Mid-Atlantic Tribal Health Center (Charles City), Laurel Cliff Farm (Monroe), Bear Mountain expansion parcels (Daisy Adcock + 339-acre original settlement), powwow zoning.
**Standard:** Public records / indexed reporting only. Credibility 5 = court/government primary, 4 = trade-publication or contractor disclosure, 3 = secondary reporting.

## 1. Monacan Health Center — Madison Heights (111 Highview Dr)

### Procurement (Credibility 4)
- **General Contractor:** Tazewell Contracting (Norfolk, VA). SBA 8(a)-certified. Founder/President: Heather Robinson.
  - Source: https://www.tazewellcontracting.com/projects/project-two-llrgk-m6sjs
  - Source: https://www.wdbj7.com/2021/10/20/monacan-tribe-breaks-ground-first-native-american-owned-operated-full-service-healthcare-facility-virginia/
- **Architect / Design Firm:** DSGW Architects (Duluth, MN; offices also in Virginia and Twin Cities). DSGW has a portfolio specialty in Native American / IHS clinics (e.g., Flandreau Santee Sioux, Lac Vieux Desert).
  - Source: https://www.tazewellcontracting.com/projects/project-two-llrgk-m6sjs
  - Source: https://dsgw.com/category/projects/native-american/
- **Civil Engineer:** Not in indexed public record. Tazewell project page does not name civil sub.
- **Permitting (Amherst County):** Not in indexed public record online. Amherst County Building Permits division at https://www.countyofamherst.com/department/division.php?structureid=106 — direct request to Building Permits office would be required.

### Construction Specs (Credibility 4)
- **Square footage:** 14,200 SF (per Tazewell project sheet; original 2021 reporting cited 13,000 SF — final-built grew ~9%).
- **Final cost:** $10.7M — vs. $10M groundbreaking estimate. Contractor explicitly states "Completed on time. In budget. Safely." despite +$700K (7%) variance from original public number.
- **Funding:** Tribally self-funded for construction; IHS-funded for staffing/operations.
- **Completion:** August 2024 (per Tazewell).
- Source: https://www.tazewellcontracting.com/projects/project-two-llrgk-m6sjs

### Operating Status (Credibility 5 — IHS official)
- **Status:** Active.
- **Address (current):** 108 Dixie Airport Rd, Madison Heights, VA 24572 (note: IHS lists this address; original groundbreaking referenced 111 Highview Dr — same six-acre parcel, two frontages at the U.S. 29 Bus / Dixie Airport Rd intersection).
- **Phone / Fax:** (434) 895-6672 / (615) 467-1582.
- **Hours:** Monday–Friday, 8:00 AM – 4:30 PM.
- **Currently delivered services:** Purchased/Referred Care (PRC) for medical, dental, behavioral health; behavioral health assessments + therapy (mental health and substance use); Consolidated Mail Order Pharmacy program for chronic medications; patient education, cognitive assessments, diabetic education.
- **Planned (not yet live):** Primary Care, Nutrition, In-House Pharmacy, Dental.
- **Providers named publicly:** Not specified on IHS facility page.
- Source: https://www.ihs.gov/Nashville/healthcarefacilities/monacan-health-center/

### Procurement still in flight (Credibility 5)
- **VOIP Phone System contract** posted 2025-04-15: solicitation **75H71525P00020**, NAICS 334210, place of performance Madison Heights, VA 24572, contracting agency Nashville Area IHS. Indicates Madison Heights clinic was still mid-commissioning in spring 2025.
- Source: https://www.federalcompass.com/rfp-opportunity-detail/75H71525P00020

### Flags
- $700K cost variance vs. $10M public groundbreaking number. Reasonable for SF growth (13K → 14.2K SF). Not necessarily an overrun, but worth noting.
- Tazewell Contracting won both the Madison Heights ($10.7M) and Charles City ($21M) IHS clinics. Same architect (DSGW) on both. **Not a no-bid pattern per se** — both are SBA 8(a) set-aside competitive — but the same GC/architect pair winning back-to-back IHS work in Virginia is a procurement concentration worth noting.
- Original public cost was $10M tribally self-funded; final was $10.7M. No record of who absorbed the $700K delta.

Ambiguous whether the deed is held by the federally recognized tribal government or by **Monacan Nation Cultural Foundation** (501(c)(3); minculturalfoundation.org), which manages the powwow. Powwow page on the foundation's site indicates active foundation involvement at the property.
- **Deed book / page number:** Not in indexed public record. Requires Amherst Circuit Court clerk land records query.

- **Easements / restrictions:** Not in indexed public record.
- **Current parcel-tax assessment:** Not in indexed public record (Amherst GIS Timmons portal at https://www.amherstgis.timmons.com/ refused connection during this research; would normally show by parcel ID).
- **Prior owner before Realty Acquisitions LLC:** Not in indexed public record. Realty Acquisitions LLC may be a holding entity from a prior estate sale; deed history would need to be walked back via Amherst Circuit Court land records.
- Sources:
  - https://www.howardhanna.com/Property/Detail/225-Laurel-Cliff-Rd-Rd-Monroe-VA-24521/BrightMLSSold/610127

### Improvements at acquisition
- Brick manor house, two tenant houses, multiple barns, two ponds (one 8-acre), open farmland and hardwood forest.

### Planned uses (per Chief Branham, Oct 2021 reporting)
- New powwow grounds (live since 2022).
- Retirement homes.
- Rehabilitation/treatment center.
- **Explicitly disclaimed:** No casino.

### Flags
- Public deed details (grantor/grantee on the deed itself, deed book + page, mortgage/lien, prior chain) are not online — requires Amherst Circuit Court walk-in or paid land records portal. This is the single biggest gap.
- Realty Acquisitions LLC structure and beneficial owner are not yet researched. **If** Realty Acquisitions LLC was tribally-affiliated, member-affiliated, or controlled by anyone in tribal leadership/council, that would be a related-party flag. **Currently no evidence either way** — this is an unanswered question, not a finding.

- **Acquiring entity:** Monacan Indian Nation.
- **Location detail:** Adjacent to the Monacan Museum and Culture Center on Bear Mountain in Amherst County. Contains "Outstanding Forest Conservation Value" hardwood forest.
- **Parcel ID / tax map number:** Not in indexed public record (DCR list-level only; Amherst GIS query needed).
- **Seller:** Not disclosed in DCR list. Project name "Daisy Adcock" suggests the parcel is named after a prior owner (Daisy Adcock is a recurring Monacan-area surname); whether the seller in 2024–25 was Daisy Adcock herself, her estate, or an heir is not in indexed public record.
- **Conservation easement holder:** Not specified in DCR list. VLCF grants typically require co-holder by a qualified easement holder (Virginia Outdoors Foundation, a land trust, etc.) — but not stated here.

- **Project name:** "Preserving Bear Mountain."
- **Description:** "The site of the original Bear Mountain Settlement in the 1880s that forms the core of the Monacan Indian Nation community today."
- **Parcel ID / seller / easement holder:** Not in indexed public record.
- Source: https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/land-conservation/vlcf-project-list-fy26

### Flags
- **Self-dealing pattern check is not possible from indexed records alone.** Whether any of the FY25/FY26 acquired parcels was previously owned by current or former tribal officials (council members, chief, foundation board) cannot be answered without Amherst Circuit Court deed walk-back. **Open question, not a finding.**
- "Daisy Adcock" — surname matches Monacan-area genealogical record. Worth checking whether she is/was a tribal member, and whether the property transferred from her or her heirs to the nation at fair market value.

- **Outcome of vote / conditions / approval status:** Not extracted from indexed public record (CitizenPortal.ai article since removed). Powwow held May 31 – June 1, 2025 as the 32nd annual event, suggesting either approval or pre-existing legal use as of the May date.
- Sources:

## Exec Summary (5 bullets)

1. **Both IHS clinics built by Tazewell Contracting (Norfolk, VA, SBA 8(a)) with DSGW Architects (Duluth, MN).** Madison Heights = 14,200 SF / $10.7M / completed Aug 2024 / now operating active per IHS (Mon-Fri 8-4:30, behavioral health + PRC + mail-order pharmacy live; primary care + dental + in-house pharmacy still pending). Charles City Mid-Atlantic = 28,000 SF / $21M / completed Dec 2024 / opened to patients June 9, 2025. Same vendor pair winning consecutive IHS-VA tribal clinics is a procurement concentration to flag, though both were SBA 8(a) competitive set-asides.

2. **Cost overruns are present but small.** Madison Heights: +$700K (7%) over $10M groundbreaking estimate, partially explained by SF growth (13K → 14.2K). Charles City: +$3M (17%) over $18M groundbreaking estimate. Mid-Atlantic also ran ~12 months past the public "summer 2024" opening target.

3. **Laurel Cliff Farm seller = Realty Acquisitions LLC** (per deed records cited by WSET and Chief Branham). Sale closed Sept 1, 2021 for $5.5M, ~1,292 acres. **Realty Acquisitions LLC's beneficial owner / prior chain of title / mortgage / easements are NOT in indexed public record** — this is the single largest open gap and requires Amherst Circuit Court walk-in deed search. Whether the buyer is the tribal government or the Monacan Nation Cultural Foundation 501(c)(3) is also ambiguous from indexed sources.

4. **Bear Mountain VLCF acquisitions are documented at the grant-list level only.** FY25 Daisy Adcock = 100.4 acres / $285,367. FY26 Bear Mountain Settlement = 339 acres / $1.21M. Sellers, parcel IDs, easement holders, and self-dealing checks (was any parcel previously held by tribal officials individually?) **cannot be resolved from indexed records** — same Amherst Circuit Court land records walk-back is needed. "Daisy Adcock" may be a Monacan family surname; relationship of seller to current tribal leadership is an open question.

5. **Powwow special exception filed late.** A1 special-exception application for farm fairs/festivals at 225 Laurel Cliff Rd went to Amherst Board of Supervisors April 16, 2025 — 3+ years AFTER the property was acquired and powwows had already been held on-site. Powwow proceeded May 31 – June 1, 2025, suggesting approval, but indexed record does not confirm vote outcome or conditions. Worth checking whether prior years' events operated under A1 compliance or as an unpermitted use.
