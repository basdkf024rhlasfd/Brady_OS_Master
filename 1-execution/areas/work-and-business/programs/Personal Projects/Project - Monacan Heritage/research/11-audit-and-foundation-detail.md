### CHP fees through MIN's federal CRF — Treasury OIG confirmed
**This is the only public, specific dollar/material flow from MIN to CHP I could verify.** Treasury OIG Semiannual Report to Congress, **September 2024 (page 29)**, in the section "Allegations of CARES ACT Fraud by Monacan Indian Nation — Unsubstantiated":

> "On July 26, 2024, Treasury OIG completed its investigation into a complaint alleging that the Tribal leadership of Monacan Indian Nation was misspending the funding on property, new vehicles, and repaired existing buildings for unknown purposes. **Treasury OIG's investigation also revealed the Tribe's law firm, Cultural Heritage Partners, received a significant amount of funds from the receipt of CRF.** Treasury OIG, in coordination with the Office of Audit, conducted an eligibility meeting and concluded the purchase of the properties, the renovations to the properties, the purchase of a vehicle, **and the law firm's funds was considered an eligible use of CRF assistance.** The allegations were unsubstantiated."

Source: oig.treasury.gov/system/files/2024-12/September-2024-SAR-for-508-complaint-locked.pdf (cred 5, primary).

**Plain reading:**
1. CHP was paid out of CARES Act Coronavirus Relief Fund (CRF) money — i.e., federal dollars MIN received and re-disbursed to its outside counsel.
2. Treasury OIG declined to call this fraud — it ruled the payments were an "eligible use" of CRF.
3. The "significant amount" is not quantified in the public OIG document. MIN received $1,491,889 in CRF (per FAC FY2022 SEFA, source above). The fraction of that going to CHP is not separately disclosed in any public record I could index.

## 6. Bear Mountain Indian Mission Inc

**Not registered as a 501(c)(3) under any current ProPublica/IRS-EOS search.** The Bear Mountain Indian Mission (Saint Paul's Mission) was an Episcopal Diocese of Southern Virginia property dating to ~1908; the school operated 1908–1964. The Episcopal Diocese **deeded the land back to the Monacan Indian Nation in 1995** (per Encyclopedia Virginia and Wikipedia). Since 1995, the site has functioned as the Monacan museum and cultural center, owned by the tribe.

There is **no separate 501(c)(3) called "Bear Mountain Indian Mission Inc"** in modern IRS records. The mission's institutional successor is the Monacan Nation Cultural Foundation Inc (which uses the museum on the deeded land). No Form 990 has ever been filed under the Bear Mountain name.

Source: projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/search?q=Bear+Mountain+Indian+Mission (returned 0 nonprofits, cred 5).
