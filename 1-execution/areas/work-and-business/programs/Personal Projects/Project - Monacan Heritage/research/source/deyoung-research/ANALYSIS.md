## How this memo is organized

Marcene's research is a serious piece of work. She produced a deep FamilySearch-anchored pedigree, transcribed census records back to 1900, and assembled the European-origin lineages out to medieval England, Scotland, Germany, and Switzerland. To respect that effort and stay factual, I've classified what she produced into three buckets:

1. **HELPFUL** — things she got right that we should keep, and a few things she added that we did not have

## 2. INACCURATE — where her file conflicts with primary records

### 2.1 Race column missing from her pedigree — but every primary record has one

Her spreadsheet has columns for First Name, Last Name, ID No, Family, Generation, Chart Position, Year Born, Place Born, Year Died, Place Died. **There is no Race column.** This is the single most consequential omission for the heritage question. The federal census records from 1850, 1860, 1870, 1900, 1910, 1920, 1930, and 1940 all carry race fields for every household member, and FamilySearch indexes them. By not surfacing this column, the spreadsheet implies a uniformity that the underlying source records do not show.

Her writeup transcribes some of these race entries selectively: Mary S. Clark is shown as "Race: White" in the 1920, 1930, and 1940 censuses. That is what the enumerators wrote in those specific years for that specific household. **It is not what was recorded for everyone in the line in every year.**

### 2.2 Nelson Harrison Clark (Brady's 6x-great-grandfather) is shown without race — primary record says Mulatto

Marcene's sheet has Nelson Harrison Clark (1793–1871) at the 8th-generation slot. **The 1860 federal census recorded him as "Mulatto"** in Amherst County, Virginia. Source: "United States, Census, 1860," FamilySearch ARK 1:1:M4B1-4DF (corroborated via WikiTree profile Clarke-11533 and pulled via Bright Data 2026-05-06; full citation in `12-nelson-harrison-clark-mulatto-1860.md`).

This is not a fringe finding. It is the direct census entry for the named individual in her own pedigree, four generations down from Joseph Carter Clark Sr. and three generations up from Joseph Anderson Clark. Both of those bracketing ancestors are in her spreadsheet by name.

The full 1860 household enumeration shows Nelson H. Clarke (66, Mulatto), wife Frances E. Clarke (40), eight children, and Frances's father **Jesse Mason (80)** living in the household. The Mason name and the Pedlar Creek / Bear Mountain LDS-convert kinship is therefore in Brady's direct paternal great-grandfather's household by 1860 even though it is not in his direct biological line.

### 2.3 Joseph Carter Clark Sr. (1797) is correctly placed but the Sorrells case is silent

Her spreadsheet has Joseph Carter Clark Sr. (b. 1797, Rockbridge) at the 4x-great-grandparent slot. **What the spreadsheet does not record is that this man is the genealogical anchor of the family tree submitted as evidence in *Sorrells v. Shields* (1924–25), Library of Virginia accession 1140744 / 1164679.** The case file states that Joe Clark Sr. owned an enslaved man named Peter Curry, purchased at auction. The same case file is the documentary anchor for the entire Irish Creek Clark line. Source: Native Heritage Project (Roberta Estes, 2012) and Library of Virginia finding aid.

This is not a contradiction with her data. It is a contextual fact about the same person she has correctly identified.

### 2.4 The "Lee Harrison Clark (1837–1870)" entry at 3x-great-grandparent does not match the GEDCOM

Marcene has Lee Harrison Clark (1837–1870) as the 3x-great-grandfather married to Mary Elizabeth Sorrells (1840–1902). The GEDCOM-corrected paternal line from Brady's RootsMagic 6 export (May 2026, 4,287 individuals) has the chain running:

Joseph Anderson Clark (1869) → **Nelson Alexander Clark (1857–1902)** → Preston Harrison Clark (1821–1903) → Nelson Harrison Clark (1793–1871) + Edith Wane (1798–1835).

Marcene's "Lee Harrison Clark" is not the documented father of Joseph Anderson Clark in the GEDCOM. Cross-reference needed: this may be a parallel Clark cousin in the same community, or a transcription drift. Worth resolving before publishing either tree as final.

### 2.5 Mary Holivey "Mondula" / Robertson — different attribution

Her sheet has Mary Martha Robertson (1860–1898) as Joseph Anderson Clark's mother. This matches the GEDCOM. The unusual surname "Mondula" (Mary Martha Robertson's mother) is on Marcene's sheet as **Mary McCarter** (line 2823: ` | McCarter | KH13-SLJ | Clark | 8 | 32.03`). The GEDCOM has Mary Martha Robertson's mother as Mary Holivey "Mondula" (b. 6 FEB 1820, Scotland — surname being a transcription drift from "Menduel"). The discrepancy is worth resolving — possibly two separate maternal grandmothers depending on which Robertson line is correct.

### 2.6 No mention of the LDS conversion of Joseph Anderson Clark in 1911

Marcene's writeup contains a personal devotion section about her own Bible reading and her 1972 inclusion of the Book of Mormon. **It does not contain the documented 1911 LDS baptism of Joseph Anderson Clark by missionaries on Irish Creek**, nor the 1951 priesthood-ordination case in which Eugene Larsen (Branch President, Buena Vista LDS Branch) wrote to LDS Church headquarters and Elder William S. Tanner of the Church Genealogical Society dispatched two Utah genealogical missionaries to assemble a counter-classification brief. Joseph Anderson Clark was 82 years old during the 1951 episode. The case is documented in Ted Olson's *Crossroads* chapter and corroborated in the Buena Vista LDS Branch History (Shaybo).

This is the single most consequential gap. The 1911 baptism and the 1951 priesthood case are the Mormon thread of the family story.

## 3. LACKING — what is documented and absent from her file

Her file is silent on every one of the following primary-source findings. None of these are speculative; all are anchored in Library of Virginia case files, federal census records, FamilySearch primary documents, or peer-reviewed scholarship.

### 3.1 James Clark v. J. P. Moore, 1876 — mandamus case in Rockbridge County

In December 1876, **James Clark** (a Rockbridge Clark, in the same family tree) applied to County Clerk J. P. Moore for a marriage license to marry **Cerinda Robison**, "a white woman." Moore denied the license on the grounds Clark was "a black man." Clark filed a petition for writ of mandamus in Rockbridge County Circuit Court. The court ruled in his favor and ordered the license issued.

Source: County Court Order Book, 1876, pages 137 and 174. Case file Library of Virginia accession **1140710**. This is the earliest documented legal challenge by a member of this Clark line to Virginia's racial-classification regime, 48 years before Atha Sorrells.

### 3.2 Atha Sorrells v. A. T. Shields, 1924–25 — Rockbridge County Circuit Court

In November 1924, **Atha Sorrells (1904–1979)**, age 20, pregnant, applied for a marriage license to marry Robert Painter at the office of County Clerk A. T. Shields. Shields refused, citing the Racial Integrity Act of 1924. Atha filed a petition for writ of mandamus. **Walter Plecker himself testified for the defense.** Judge Henry W. Holt ruled for Sorrells in January 1925. The marriage license was ordered issued. Holt's opinion held that the evidence covering 130 years showed Sorrells had no non-white blood "except Indian, and there is not enough of that to come within the statute."

Atha's maternal great-grandparents were **Joe Clark Jr. and Julia (Sorrells) Clark**. Joe Clark Jr. was a son of Joseph Carter Clark Sr. (1797) — **the same patriarch in Marcene's spreadsheet.**

Source: Case file Library of Virginia accession **1140744 / 1164679**. Plecker letter to Shields dated 9 May 1925 advising against appeal: accession **1160754** (Plecker-Shields correspondence). Plecker explicitly wrote that if the Virginia Supreme Court upheld the ruling, it would "embolden the Rockbridge tribe."

This case is the single most consequential individual documentary fact in the entire family record. It is named for Atha Sorrells and brought on the genealogical evidence of the Clark family tree that Marcene's pedigree replicates.

### 3.3 Joseph Anderson Clark's 1906 marriage entered as "colored" in Amherst County

The 1906 marriage record of Joseph Anderson Clark to Mary Susan Clark is entered in the Amherst County marriage register as "colored." This was discovered in 1951 by Elder William S. Tanner of the LDS Church Genealogical Society and reported to Branch President Eugene Larsen in a letter dated **1 May 1951**. The discovery triggered the genealogical research that produced the 1951 priesthood-ordination case. Source: Olson, *Crossroads* (2004), citing Buena Vista LDS Branch History.

**Marcene's writeup transcribes the 1920, 1930, and 1940 census entries for Mary Susan Clark as "White." It does not transcribe the 1906 marriage record. The 1906 record is the earlier of the two and is the federal-tier classification.**

### 3.4 The 1943 Plecker "hit list" — Clark surname on Rockbridge County

Walter Plecker's "List of Mixed Negroid Virginia Families Striving to Pass as 'Indian' or White" (1943, on Bureau of Vital Statistics letterhead, distributed to all Virginia county clerks) named families by surname and county. The Rockbridge County entry includes **Clark** as one of the surnames marked for reclassification of all birth, marriage, and death records. The list is held at the Library of Virginia. This is primary federal record evidence that the Clark surname in Rockbridge was specifically targeted for state-sponsored racial reclassification.

### 3.5 William Wesley Cooper household — Mulatto in 1850 census, Brady's maternal Cash line

Through Brady's maternal Cash line (Nellie Mae Cash → Toliver Edward Cash → Elihu Cooper + Susanna A. Sorrells), the 1850 federal census records **William Wesley Cooper (b. 1805) and his entire household as Mulatto** in Rockbridge County, District 51, household 975. This is direct biological descent through Brady's maternal grandmother Lutie Fannie Clark → Nellie Mae Cash → Cash → Cooper. Source: 1850 U.S. Census, Rockbridge County, Virginia.

Marcene's sheet has the Cash and Sorrells and Cooper surnames present in the right slots but does not surface the 1850 Mulatto classification.

### 3.6 Peter Mason of Pedlar Creek — first LDS convert in the community, 1883

Peter Mason (b. ~1814, Pedlar Creek, Amherst County) and wife Diannah Sorrells Mason — same Sorrells family network — were baptized into the LDS Church in 1883 by missionaries J. Golden Kimball and Charles Welch. Kimball described Mason in his field journal as "of Indian descent, his skin being almost as dark as an Indian's. His hair was long and black." Source: J. Golden Kimball journal, cited in Olson *Crossroads* (2004).

This is the door that opened the LDS missionary circuit into the Pedlar Creek / Irish Creek community. Without Peter Mason's 1883 baptism there is no 1911 baptism of Joseph Anderson Clark on Irish Creek.

### 3.7 Brady's AncestryDNA file

Brady's raw AncestryDNA file (V2.0, GRCh37, 668,912 SNPs) is in the research/source/attachments/dna-data/ folder. **It is not "zero" on any indigenous-ancestry signal.** The full ethnicity estimate is in `06-dna-analysis.md`. The AncestryDNA ethnicity result is one data point; mtDNA testing (FamilyTreeDNA mtFull Sequence, $159) is queued as a next action to definitively pin maternal haplogroup. Until that test runs, the operational summary is: AncestryDNA shows European-dominant ancestry with measurable non-European signal consistent with the documentary record of Mulatto classification four generations up.

## 4. The "zero Native American heritage" claim, stated factually

The claim that the Smallwoods (or this family network) have "zero Native American heritage" is contradicted by multiple independent primary records. To stay strictly factual:

1. **1860 federal census, Amherst County, Virginia** records Brady's direct 6x-great-grandfather Nelson Harrison Clark as Mulatto. ARK 1:1:M4B1-4DF.

2. **1850 federal census, Rockbridge County, Virginia, District 51, household 975** records Brady's direct 5x-great-grandfather William Wesley Cooper and his entire household as Mulatto.

3. **1876 Rockbridge County Circuit Court** ordered a marriage license issued to James Clark on the grounds that he was "of mixed blood, not Black, and less than 1/4 Indian or Negro." This is the same Clark family network — the Clark family tree from this case overlaps with the Sorrells case tree and with Marcene's pedigree.

4. **1924–25 Rockbridge County Circuit Court** ruled in *Sorrells v. Shields* that Atha Sorrells's documented Clark/Sorrells lineage covered "130 years" of evidence showing no non-white blood "except Indian." The judge — Henry W. Holt — issued a written opinion. Walter Plecker himself testified for the defense and lost. This case was decided on the Clark family-tree exhibit that anchors at Joseph Carter Clark Sr. (1797) — the same individual at slot 4.08 in Marcene's sheet.

5. **1906 Amherst County marriage register** classified Joseph Anderson Clark and Mary Susan Clark as "colored" at marriage. The same census enumerators recorded them as "White" in 1920, 1930, and 1940. **The federal record on the same household is inconsistent across years.** This is the textbook documentary pattern of a Virginia tri-racial isolate community.

6. **1911 LDS baptism** of Joseph Anderson Clark on Irish Creek as part of a missionary circuit that began with Peter Mason's 1883 conversion. Mason was described by missionary J. Golden Kimball as visibly Native.

7. **1943 Plecker reclassification list** named Clark in Rockbridge County among "Mixed Negroid Virginia Families Striving to Pass as 'Indian' or White." This is a state document distributed to county clerks. The Clark surname was specifically targeted.

8. **1951 LDS priesthood-ordination case** for Claude Clark, grandson of Joseph Anderson Clark, was decided in favor of ordination on the genealogical evidence that the family was Native (Lamanite, in the doctrinal framing of the time) — not Negro. The case overturned a Branch President's initial denial. Source: Buena Vista LDS Branch History.

9. **2018 federal recognition** of the Monacan Indian Nation (P.L. 115-121, Thomasina E. Jordan Indian Tribes of Virginia Federal Recognition Act). The Monacans are the Native nation whose Irish Creek / Pedlar Creek / Bear Mountain settlement community is the documented community Brady's Clark and Cash lines belong to.

None of those nine facts requires re-interpretation, second-hand inference, or family folklore. They are anchored in court files, census records, marriage registers, missionary journals, state documents, and federal law.

**What is true is that the *documentary* record on this family is inconsistent.** That inconsistency is itself the historical fact: an Indian-descended Virginia community was repeatedly reclassified by state enumerators across the Plecker era. Marcene's pedigree captures the European side of that record well. The Native side is also primary-source documented and is missing from her file.
