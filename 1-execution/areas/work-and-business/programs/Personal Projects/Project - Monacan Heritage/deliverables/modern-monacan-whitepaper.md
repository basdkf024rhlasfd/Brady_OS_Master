## Money, Governance, Universities — A Heritage Investigator's Whitepaper

**Prepared for:** Brady Smallwood
**Date:** 2026-05-06
**Posture:** Public-records-only investigation, paired with the counter-narrative the same evidence supports
**Reading time:** ~25 minutes
**Underlying research files:** `research/09–14` in this project folder

## Executive Summary

Eight years after federal recognition under the Thomasina E. Jordan Act (PL 115-121, signed January 29, 2018), the Monacan Indian Nation has moved from a sleepy state-recognized tribe of roughly 2,700 enrolled members to a small federal sovereign with **$16.72 million in audited federal expenditures across FY 2022–2024**, a $940K nonprofit Foundation, ~2,150 acres reconsolidated around Bear Mountain, two clinics carrying its name, and one general-counsel firm — Cultural Heritage Partners — woven through nearly every line item that matters.

The current modern record contains both a clean current audit picture *and* the residue of older problems. Three Single Audits filed at the Federal Audit Clearinghouse (FY22, FY23, FY24) all came back unmodified with zero findings and zero material weaknesses. The FY 2022 audit *also* lists prior, since-resolved findings at six federal agencies — EPA, Treasury, IMLS, HUD, DOI, and HHS. Both things are true. The narrative often shorthand'd as "perfectly clean" is more accurately "clean now, after working through prior findings at every agency the tribe has ever drawn money from."

The most consequential person in the tribe's modern footprint who is *not* enrolled Monacan is **Marion F. Werkheiser** of Cultural Heritage Partners, a 10-person Richmond firm founded in 2010 that has positioned itself as the legal infrastructure for newly-recognized eastern tribes. Greg Werkheiser is MIN's General Counsel; Marion was its registered Virginia state lobbyist from June 2024 — a registration that **lapsed and was not renewed for 2025–2026**, even as her firm continued lobbying registrations for Nansemond and Rappahannock. Treasury's Office of Inspector General Semiannual Report (September 2024, page 29) confirms in writing that CHP "received a significant amount of funds from the receipt of CRF" — meaning CHP was paid out of MIN's $1.49M CARES Coronavirus Relief Fund draw. Treasury OIG ruled the use eligible and the underlying allegations unsubstantiated. The exact dollar figure was not made public.

The tribe's land base has roughly tripled. Laurel Cliff Farm — 1,292 acres at 225 Laurel Cliff Road, Monroe — was acquired for **$5.5 million from a Delaware-style holding entity called Realty Acquisitions LLC** in September 2021. Bear Mountain reconsolidation added 100.4 acres (Daisy Adcock property, FY25 VLCF $285,367), 339 acres (original 1880s settlement, FY26 VLCF $1.21M), and a 421-acre cultural-access easement co-held with The Conservation Fund, Virginia Outdoors Foundation, and Sweet Briar College — the **first cultural access easement in the Commonwealth.**

The Indian Health Service footprint also tripled. The Monacan Health Center at 111 Highview Drive, Madison Heights — 14,200 SF, ~$10.7 million, **tribe-self-funded** — opened in August 2024 with limited services live. The shared Mid-Atlantic Tribal Health Center in Charles City — 28,000 SF, ~$21 million, IHS-built, serving all seven federally-recognized Virginia tribes — opened to patients on **June 9, 2025**. Both buildings were built by the same general contractor (Tazewell Contracting, an SBA 8(a) firm in Norfolk) and the same architect (DSGW Architects, Duluth, MN). One vendor stack across two consecutive eight-figure IHS-Virginia clinics is a procurement concentration worth seeing.

The thinnest part of the picture is not money or land — it is the human pipeline into Virginia's universities. Across eighteen Virginia higher-ed institutions, only **five named Monacan individuals** have direct, ongoing university-side connections: the late **Karenne Wood** at UVA (the deepest legacy by far), **Victoria Ferguson** at Virginia Tech (the only paid Monacan-by-name operational role in the state), and ceremonial signings by **Kenneth Branham**, **Diane Shields**, and **Edith "Lou" Branham Parrish** at Lynchburg, Sweet Briar, and Randolph. UVA's tribal liaison is Pueblo / Eastern Cherokee, not Monacan. William & Mary's American Indian Resource Center director is non-Native. The pipeline is thinner than the ceremonial activity suggests, and that gap is itself part of the modern story.

The investigator's verdict and the counter-narrative both come from the same evidence: a small, kin-dense tribal government has executed a ten-year scaling sprint with one outside law firm, a Michigan auditor, and an Indian Health Service partner — built two clinics, won one Supreme-Court-of-historic-preservation fight at Rassawek, lost one major USDA food program, recombined an ancestral land base, and earned three consecutive clean federal audits — while also concentrating procurement, leaving its 2022 per-capita-distribution lawsuit unresolved by the merits, and operating powwows on a property whose zoning special-exception was not formally filed until 2025. There is no public-record evidence of corruption. There is ample public-record evidence of a small tribe with limited internal capacity leaning hard on a small set of outside professionals, with all the conflicts that implies.

What follows is the long form. Every claim cites a public source. Every gap is named.

## Part I — The Money Trail

### 1. The Confirmed Federal Total: $16.72 Million Across Three Years

The Monacan Indian Nation files its federal expenditures audit at the Federal Audit Clearinghouse under EIN 82-4783213 / UEI P5ESN1NG3RC5. The clearinghouse data is the cleanest single window into MIN's federal money:

| Fiscal Year | Federal Expenditures | Auditor | Findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9/30/2022 | **$8,241,449** | Midwest Professionals PLLC (Gaylord, MI) | 0 |
| 9/30/2023 | **$4,514,374** | Midwest Professionals PLLC | 0 |
| 9/30/2024 | **$3,962,616** | Melissa B. Petersen CPA LLC (Grand Rapids, MI) | 0 |
| **3-year total** | **$16,718,439** | | |

All three audits are unmodified, zero current findings, zero material weaknesses, no going-concern flags. The FY22 audit explicitly lists *prior* unresolved findings from six federal agencies (EPA, Treasury, IMLS, HUD, DOI, HHS) that were closed out by the FY22 audit cycle — meaning the historical record was not as "perfectly clean" as defense counsel framed it during the 2022 *Brooks v. Branham* litigation, but the *current* audit posture is genuinely clean.

A small structural flag: the auditor changed from Midwest Professionals PLLC to a **sole-practitioner CPA** (Melissa B. Petersen, single-member LLC) for the FY24 audit, despite MIN's August 2023 RFP for a three-year audit contract suggesting continuity. Both auditors are Michigan-based; the tribe is in Virginia. The change is unexplained in the public record.

### 2. The Pandemic Money Was the Big Wave

MIN's FY 2022 federal expenditures of $8.24M are dominated by two pandemic relief streams:

- **ARPA State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds (CFDA 21.027):** **$4,557,299** in FY22, plus $2,312,140 in FY23, plus $1,253,477 in FY24 — over **$8.1 million total** drawn from a single relief program.
- **CARES Coronavirus Relief Fund (CFDA 21.019):** **$1,491,889** in FY22.

Treasury OIG quietly confirmed in its September 2024 Semiannual Report (page 29) that **Cultural Heritage Partners "received a significant amount of funds from the receipt of CRF"** — meaning CHP was paid from MIN's $1.49M CARES draw. Treasury OIG examined the use, ruled it eligible, and ruled the underlying allegations unsubstantiated. The exact CHP-paid figure was not disclosed.

The disputed funds in *Brooks v. Branham* — **$91,600 collectively across 9 plaintiffs, or roughly $10,000 per member** — almost certainly came out of this CARES draw. Distributed across MIN's roughly 2,700 enrolled members, a $10K-per-member CARES per-capita would have totaled around **$27 million** if everyone had received it. They didn't. The case was dismissed June 2023 on tribal sovereign immunity, before the merits were reached.

### 3. The Recurring Federal Backbone: HUD IHBG

The most predictable, repeating money flowing to MIN is the **HUD Indian Housing Block Grant (CFDA 14.867)**: a formula allocation every federally-recognized tribe receives annually based on population and need.

- FY 2020 IHBG formula allocation: **$329,194** (total available with carry-over: $1,439,194; FY20 spend: $190K — a COVID-year under-execution)
- FY 2022 IHBG draw: $1,241,912 (combined formula + COVID supplemental)
- FY 2023 IHBG draw: $883,104
- FY 2024 IHBG draw: $369,764 (returning to baseline formula range)

Reclassification note: the HUD project number **55-IT-51-10470** referenced in MIN's "EPIC" document on the tribe's own website — and previously labeled as ICDBG in earlier research — is in fact an **IHBG award**, not a competitive ICDBG. This matters because IHBG is the steady backbone; ICDBG is project-based and competitive.

### 4. The HHS Operating Stream: ~$2.36M Over Three Years

HHS's Tracking Accountability in Government Grants Spending (TAGGS) database confirms operating-grant flows totaling **$2,356,005 across FY 2024–FY 2026**, dominated by:

- ACF Child Care and Development Fund: ~$300K/yr
- ACL Older Americans Act Title VI elder programs: ~$140–175K/yr
- IHS Special Diabetes Program for Indians: ~$50–100K/yr
- LIHEAP energy assistance: ~$22K/yr

These are program-formula flows, not capital. They do not include the clinic construction.

### 5. The USDA Loss: A $783K Program Was Cancelled

The **Walut Pí:se ("Food is Good")** Local Food Purchase Assistance program — USDA cooperative agreement AM23LFPA0000C005, **$783,063 total**, ran November 2022 through October 2024, served 425 families and ~1,300 meals/month via 19 local food partners — was MIN's largest single competitive federal win.

**USDA cancelled the LFPA Plus renewal in early 2025**, leaving a $19K unreimbursed bill and an estimated $30K near-term gap. LFPA had been covering 60% of program food purchases. This is the largest documented program-funding loss MIN has taken since federal recognition, and it has not been publicly replaced.

### 6. The EPA Door That Closed

MIN was one of the tribes selected for **EPA Climate Pollution Reduction Grants Phase 1 (planning)** under a per-tribe ceiling of $500,000. MIN produced its full Priority Climate Action Plan in April 2024 (a 2.9 MB document on file at epa.gov).

MIN was **not selected** for the September 5, 2024 announcement of **34 tribes / territories receiving $300 million in CPRG Phase 2 implementation grants** (averaging $8.8 million each, ranging $1M–$24M). The implementation-money door closed without a Monacan award.

### 7. The Treasury Pandemic Total — Estimate, Not Citation

Treasury's per-tribe allocation tables for CARES CRF and ARPA SLFRF were never made fully searchable; the tribe-by-tribe lines exist in PDFs that no third party has indexed. The **PRAC pandemic-oversight dashboard confirms MIN's SLFRF was below $30M** (consistent with a 2,000-citizen tribe). A methodology-derived **floor estimate**, combining the standard CARES Title I tribal-formula minimum with ARPA's equal-allocation tier and employment minimum, yields approximately **$4.5 million** in total pandemic relief — but no official source publishes this figure for MIN specifically. Treat it as estimate, not citation.

### 8. The State of Virginia Pipeline Is Cleanest

Virginia state money — primarily through the Virginia Land Conservation Foundation — is the most transparent stream:

- **FY 2025 VLCF: $285,367** for the Daisy Adcock Property (100.4 acres, Bear Mountain)
- **FY 2026 VLCF: $1,210,000** for the Original Bear Mountain Settlement (339 acres, 1880s site)
- **FY 2026 VLCF: $287,500** to The Conservation Fund as fiscal partner on a 421.3-acre Bear/Cedar Mountain cultural-access easement co-held with VOF, Sweet Briar, and MIN

**Three-grant state total: ~$1.78M.** Every dollar publicly itemized on the Virginia Department of Conservation and Recreation website. This is the single cleanest pipeline of any source flowing to MIN.

### Money Roll-Up

## Part II — Governance and Power Dynamics

### 1. The Elected Government

Chief **Diane Shields** (Linda Diane Johns Shields), elected June 17, 2023; second woman to hold the office after Sharon Bryant. Shields was born in Virginia, raised in Ohio from age 5, returned to direct the Monacan Nation Cultural Foundation post-recognition, served as Tribal Treasurer, Assistant Chief, and Tribal Secretary at various points before running for chief, and worked at **Central Virginia Community College** in records — the only Monacan chief with documented community-college employment history.

Assistant Chief **Edith "Lou" Branham Parrish** — eldest daughter of Ronnie and Eleanor Branham (both enrolled Monacan).

Tribal Council (per the official monacannation.gov government page):
- Dean Branham (former Chief, now Director of Monacan Nation Cultural Foundation)
- Daniel B. Branham
- Mark Bryant
- Bertha Riley
- Amber Rasnick Fink
- Isaiah Moss
- Rhonda Matthews

Other officers: Tribal Secretary Teresa Pollak; Tribal Treasurer Pamela Thompson.

The surnames repeat — Branham, Bryant, Shields, Thompson, Latimer, Parrish — because the documented Bear Mountain core families have been intermarrying since the 1800s. This is genealogy, not nepotism. It is also a real-world feature of small-tribe governance: the same families sit on the elected council, the Foundation board, and the federal-funding sign-off chain.

### 2. The Defendant-to-Council-Member Trajectory

The 2022–2023 *Brooks v. Branham* federal lawsuit (W.D. Va., Lynchburg Division) was filed by nine enrolled members led by Bobby Thompson Jr., alleging that then-Chief Kenneth Branham, Tribal Administrator Adrian Compton, and Tribal Secretary Amber Rasnick Fink had improperly withheld approximately $91,600 in collective per-capita CARES distributions. The case was **dismissed in June 2023** on tribal sovereign immunity / lack of subject-matter jurisdiction grounds. The merits were never reached.

**Amber Rasnick Fink — a named defendant — now sits on the elected Tribal Council.** Defendant to councillor in roughly 18 months. The case dismissal cleared her path to office. There is no public-record evidence of a continuing organized opposition slate.

**Bobby Thompson Jr.** has been silent in indexed press since 2022. No Fourth Circuit appeal was filed. No state-court refiling. No BIA Office of Federal Acknowledgment petition surfaced. No organized faction is visible 2024–2026. The dispute appears structurally absorbed — dissolved, not resolved.

### 3. Adrian Compton, the Operating Chief

Tribal Administrator **Adrian Compton** is the day-to-day operations lead — the person whose signature actually moves federal money. He has been the public-facing voice on the health center groundbreaking, the Laurel Cliff acquisition, and the Bear Mountain expansion. He gave the framing "*you can't do anything in a tribe without land. Land is the basis for everything.*"

The public-record bio on Compton is unusually thin. The **Compton surname is not in the canonical Bear Mountain ancestral list** — meaning his lineage path into the tribe is most likely maternal, or he was hired from outside. As a tribal-government employee of a federally-recognized sovereign, his salary, prior employers, and personnel file sit outside public-disclosure regimes; he does not appear on the MNCF Form 990 because he works for the tribal government, not the Foundation. He is **the most opaque sitting official in MIN's modern administration**, and the genealogist question of how the Compton line connects to Bear Mountain is the highest-leverage unanswered question for understanding the tribe's modern operational leadership.

### 4. The Vetoed Sovereignty Bill

In the 2025 Virginia General Assembly session, **HB 2134 / SB 949** would have updated the Virginia Code to formally name the Commonwealth's federally-recognized tribes and codify their inherent sovereignty. Patrons: Del. Paul Krizek (D-Fairfax) and Sen. Ryan McDougle (R-Hanover) — bipartisan, and both publicly received Tribal Champion awards from tribal leaders.

**Governor Glenn Youngkin vetoed the bill.** This is a setback for MIN's state-level standing and a structural opening for unrecognized claimant groups (Buffalo Ridge Cherokee, Wolf Creek Cherokee) to argue the Commonwealth has not formally distinguished between them and the federally-recognized tribes. The veto has been under-covered in the regional press and corrects earlier reporting that suggested the bill was merely in flight.

### 5. The Shifted Cherokee Pressure

Buffalo Ridge / United Cherokee Indian Tribe of Virginia state-recognition pressure has gone dormant. The action has shifted to **Wolf Creek Cherokee** (HJ14 2024 / HJR14 2025) seeking state recognition through the Henrico County legislative path. MIN has not publicly weighed in. The implicit boundary dispute — who gets to claim Native identity in central Virginia — remains live but quieter.

## Part III — The Real Estate Story

The modern Monacan Indian Nation is, as Adrian Compton said, a real-estate story.

### 1. Laurel Cliff Farm — $5.5M, Realty Acquisitions LLC, 2021

In September 2021, MIN closed on **1,292 acres at 225 Laurel Cliff Road, Monroe, VA** for **$5.5 million**. Brick manor house, two tenant houses, multiple barns, hardwood + farm + two ponds. Chief Branham announced plans for new powwow grounds, retirement homes, and a rehabilitation center, explicitly stating "*the plans do not include a casino*" — a notable disclaimer given that the federal recognition act expressly prohibits gaming under IGRA for the six 2018-recognized tribes.

The seller of record is **Realty Acquisitions LLC**. The beneficial owner of Realty Acquisitions LLC, the deed book and page number, the financing instrument, the mortgage holder (if any), and whether the grantee was the tribal government directly or the Monacan Nation Cultural Foundation 501(c)(3) — **none of these are in the indexed public record**. They are sitting in the Amherst Circuit Court Clerk's deed records, retrievable in a single visit.

This is the single biggest unknown in MIN's modern transaction history. A $5.5M cash purchase by a then-newly-recognized tribe with then-modest Foundation revenue ($254K in 2020) is a remarkable transaction that the public record has not explained. Source of funds is most likely some combination of CARES Act fiscal recovery, donor contribution, and commercial loan, but the breakdown is not in any public document.

### 2. Bear Mountain Reconsolidation

Three additional tracts have been added since 2021, all state-funded:

- **Daisy Adcock Property** — 100.4 acres adjacent to Monacan Museum and Culture Center — FY 2025 VLCF grant of **$285,367**. Whether Daisy Adcock was the actual seller or the parcel is just named after her is not in indexed press.
- **Original Bear Mountain Settlement** — 339 acres, the 1880s settlement site itself — FY 2026 VLCF grant of **$1,210,000**.
- **Bear/Cedar Mountain cultural-access easement** — 421.3 acres held jointly by The Conservation Fund, Virginia Outdoors Foundation, Sweet Briar College, and MIN — described by VLCF as "**one of the first cultural access easements in the Commonwealth.**" FY 2026 VLCF $287,500 partner award to TCF.

Aggregate land base under MIN control or co-protection: approximately **2,150+ acres** in Amherst County. The cultural access easement is a new legal instrument in Virginia that other Eastern tribes can model.

### 3. The Powwow Zoning Late Filing

Amherst County zoning records show that the **A-1 special-use exception** authorizing public-event use of Laurel Cliff Farm for the Monacan Powwow was filed with the Board of Supervisors in **April 2025** — three-plus years after the property was acquired and after multiple powwows had already been held on the site. The May 2025 powwow proceeded, suggesting the variance was approved, but the formal vote outcome is not in the indexed record. The implication is that the prior years of powwows operated outside formal zoning compliance — a low-grade issue, but a real one for a sovereign that emphasizes its lawful posture.

## Part IV — Cultural Heritage Partners: The Vendor Layer

### 1. The Firm

Cultural Heritage Partners PLLC is a 10-person, 6-attorney boutique law firm headquartered in Richmond, founded 2010 by **Marion Forsyth Werkheiser** and **Greg Werkheiser** — partners both married and professional. The firm has positioned itself as the legal infrastructure for Eastern federally-recognized tribes, serving as **General Counsel to all six 2018-recognized Virginia tribes** as well as the Pamunkey (federally recognized 2015 via BIA administrative process).

Greg Werkheiser also operates **ArtGlass**, an augmented-reality cultural-tourism technology company. Whether ArtGlass has any commercial relationship with the Monacan Ancestral Museum or the planned Bear Mountain cultural infrastructure is **not in the public record**. The cross-ownership creates a question that should be asked.

### 2. The Roles

**Greg Werkheiser** is publicly designated **General Counsel to the Monacan Indian Nation**. He was the named defense attorney in *Brooks v. Branham*, defending Branham, Compton, and Fink in their official capacities. This means CHP simultaneously held (a) the tribal government as a general-counsel client and (b) sitting tribal officials as personal-capacity defense clients in a case where the plaintiffs were *also* enrolled tribal members. The structural conflict was resolved in favor of the officials by the sovereign-immunity dismissal — before the conflict had to be litigated.

**Marion F. Werkheiser** registered as Monacan Indian Nation's Virginia state lobbyist on **June 27, 2024** (VPAP record 164018). The registration **was not renewed for the 2025–2026 cycle**. CHP did renew its 2025–2026 lobbying registrations for Nansemond and Rappahannock. The non-renewal of MIN's registration is not explained in any public source. It may indicate a quiet shift in the relationship, a temporary lapse, or a deliberate decision to operate as counsel rather than registered lobbyist. It is worth seeing.

### 3. The CHP-CARES Connection

**Treasury Office of Inspector General Semiannual Report, September 2024, page 29** confirms in writing:

> Cultural Heritage Partners received a significant amount of funds from the receipt of CRF.

CRF = Coronavirus Relief Fund, MIN's $1.49M CARES Act draw. Treasury OIG examined the use, found it eligible, and ruled the underlying complaint allegations unsubstantiated. The exact dollar figure CHP received from MIN's CARES money was not made public.

This is the closest the public record gets to a documented CHP fee. It is from the federal regulator, not from CHP or MIN. It is dispositive: CHP was paid out of MIN's pandemic relief money, and the regulator confirmed it was a legal use. What it does not tell us is the magnitude.

### 4. The Federal Lobbying Picture

CHP has **never reported MIN as a federal Lobbying Disclosure Act client.** This is consistent with a general-counsel arrangement where advocacy flows through attorney-client privilege rather than registered lobbying — common, legal, and structurally less transparent than a registered LDA arrangement would be. There is no Hobbs Straus or Akin Gump retainer wrapped around MIN's federal-money work. The advocacy infrastructure runs through CHP.

## Part V — The Audit Picture, Honestly

The phrase "three perfectly clean independent audits" was attributed to defense counsel during the 2022 *Brooks v. Branham* litigation. The phrase deserves a careful read.

The verifiable picture, drawn from the Federal Audit Clearinghouse:

- **As of the time the public claim was made (mid-2022), zero MIN Single Audits had been filed at FAC under the modern UEI regime.** The FY22 audit was not accepted by FAC until well after the litigation.
- All three modern Single Audits (FY22, FY23, FY24) **are technically clean** — unmodified opinion, zero findings, zero material weaknesses, no going-concern flags.
- The FY22 audit explicitly lists **prior unresolved findings from six federal agencies** — EPA (66), Treasury (21), IMLS (45), HUD (14), DOI (15), HHS (93). These were closed out by the FY22 audit cycle.
- The auditor changed between FY23 and FY24 — from a 4-person firm (Midwest Professionals PLLC) to a **single-member firm** (Melissa B. Petersen CPA LLC) — despite a 3-year audit RFP issued in August 2023.

The honest readback: the *current* audit posture is clean. The *historical* posture had findings at every agency MIN had drawn money from, all subsequently resolved. The "perfectly clean from day one" framing is inaccurate. The "we are in good standing now" framing is supportable.

## Part VI — The University Footprint, Honestly

Brady's specific ask was for direct named-Monacan connections to Virginia universities — not generic Native programs, not non-Monacan academics who study the tribe.

The honest finding across eighteen Virginia higher-ed institutions: **five named Monacan individuals hold meaningful direct university connections.** The pipeline is thinner than the ceremonial activity suggests.

### 1. Karenne Wood (1960–2019) — UVA, the deepest legacy

- **PhD in Anthropology, UVA, 2016** — first Monacan ever to earn a doctorate at UVA. Dissertation: *"The Language Ghost: Linguistic Heritage and Collective Identity Among the Monacan Indians of Central Virginia."* Advisors included Eve Danziger, Jeffrey Hantman, Lise Dobrin, Christian McMillen.
- Taught in UVA Department of Anthropology; directed Virginia Indian Programs at Virginia Humanities for 11 years.
- **Memorial portrait** by Esther Candari (the artist who also created the Monacan mural in Amherst) hangs in **Shannon Library** as part of UVA's Fine and Decorative Arts Collection. Depicts Wood in regalia in front of Bear Mountain. Unveiled by President Jim Ryan.
- **Memorial bench outside New Cabell Hall.**
- Two poetry collections (*Markings on Earth*, 2001; *Weaving the Boundary*, 2016).

This is the single deepest Monacan-by-name legacy at any Virginia university. UVA has invested in named Monacan honorific infrastructure no other VA institution has matched.

### 2. Victoria Ferguson — Virginia Tech, the only paid embedded role

- **Enrolled member, Monacan Indian Nation.** Marshall University graduate. Self-trained historian on Yesáh (Eastern Siouan) lifeways with 25+ years of seed-keeping research.
- Current VT roles:
  - **Program Director of Solitude** (the oldest building on Virginia Tech's campus)
  - **Presidential Ambassador to Native Nations at Virginia Tech**
  - Founding elder of the **VT Indigenous Community Garden** (established 2014)
- Public-facing: "Who's Hollerin'?" Speaker Series (April 2025), Taubman Museum Member Hour, VPM/PBS feature on heirloom seed-keeping.

Ferguson is the **single most operationally embedded Monacan-by-name role at any Virginia university** — paid, recurring, three named institutional positions. Not a one-off ceremony, not a posthumous portrait, but an ongoing operating relationship.

### 3. Kenneth Branham — the signing chief

Then-Chief Kenneth Branham personally signed two of the three most consequential financial partnerships:

- **University of Lynchburg Monacan Indian Nation Scholarship**, signed February 21, 2022 with President Alison Morrison-Shetlar. Renewable four years; among the university's highest monetary awards. First two recipients were Amherst County High School graduates (the high school primarily serving the Bear Mountain Monacan community).
- **Virginia Tech Tribal Match Scholarship** — originated from a direct conversation between Branham and VT President Tim Sands. Provides up to $2,500/yr matching tribal scholarship funding 1:1.

One man, two of the three most consequential university financial partnerships.

### 4. Diane Shields and Edith "Lou" Branham Parrish — Sweet Briar (September 26, 2025)

Current Chief and Assistant Chief co-fronted the most recent partnership: Sweet Briar's land acknowledgment plaque dedication and Founders' Day Convocation on September 26, 2025. The first Monacan scholarship recipient enrolled in 2024. Sweet Briar is the geographically closest VA institution — same county as Bear Mountain.

### 5. Randolph College — Lynchburg

Randolph hosted the first VA college Monacan land-acknowledgment ceremony. Tribal partnership active.

### 6. The Negative Finding That Matters

UVA's Tribal Liaison **Kody Grant** is Pueblo of Isleta and Eastern Band of Cherokee — not Monacan. William & Mary's American Indian Resource Center director **Danielle Moretti-Langholtz** is non-Native. Virginia Tech's AIICC director **Melissa Faircloth** is Coharie / Eastern Cherokee — not Monacan.

Liberty, GMU, ODU, CNU, Hollins, Washington & Lee, Roanoke, Radford, Mary Baldwin, JMU, and VCU show **zero direct named-Monacan connections** in the public record. The 2,700-member Monacan tribe has, as best the public record can tell, **one** paid Monacan-by-name operational role across all of Virginia higher education — Victoria Ferguson at Virginia Tech.

The pipeline thinness is itself the story. A federally-recognized tribe with a half-billion-dollar effective economic footprint flowing into its home county is producing ceremony at universities faster than it is producing operating roles. That is not a scandal. It is a generational gap that the next wave of Monacan undergraduates — the kids on the Lynchburg and Sweet Briar scholarships — would have to close.

## Part VIII — The Investigator's Verdict

If a private investigator briefed Brady on what the public record actually shows, the dossier would read like this:

**On corruption: nothing.** No OIG findings against MIN. No GAO reports. No BIA enforcement action. No Justice Department matter. Three consecutive clean Single Audits at the Federal Audit Clearinghouse. The 2022 *Brooks v. Branham* per-capita-distribution dispute was dismissed on jurisdictional grounds before reaching the merits, and the named defendants either remain in office or have been absorbed back into the elected leadership. There is no public-record predicate for an allegation of tribal-government corruption.

**On concentration: real.** Cultural Heritage Partners is the single most consequential outside actor. CHP holds the general-counsel seat at all six federally-recognized Virginia tribes. CHP defended MIN officials in *Brooks v. Branham*. CHP was paid out of MIN's $1.49M CARES money (Treasury OIG confirms; ruled eligible; figure undisclosed). One general contractor and one architect built both IHS-Virginia clinics. Two out-of-state Michigan accounting firms have audited the federal money. The advocacy footprint is small, kin-dense on the tribal side, and sole-source-feeling on the vendor side.

**On opacity: meaningful.** Adrian Compton's lineage path into the tribe is undocumented in the public record. The Laurel Cliff Farm beneficial seller (Realty Acquisitions LLC), deed details, and financing structure are sitting in the Amherst Circuit Court Clerk's office. The non-renewal of Marion Werkheiser's 2025–2026 Virginia lobbying registration is unexplained. The exact dollar amount paid to CHP from CARES is unredacted only in a Treasury OIG closing memo that has not been FOIA'd. The Schedule L (related-party transactions) section of MNCF's Form 990 was filed FY15–18 then disappears FY19–24 — meaning whatever related-party transactions existed pre-2018 are no longer being publicly reported, but the kin-density of the Foundation board has only increased.

**On scaling: aggressive.** MIN went from a sleepy ~$50K/yr Foundation in 2017 to drawing $16.7M in federal expenditures across FY22–24, building two clinics, reconsolidating ~2,150 acres, and standing up a Priority Climate Action Plan, all in less than seven years. That kind of scaling sprint at a 2,700-member tribe is structurally impossible without leaning hard on outside professionals — CHP, IHS, Tazewell, DSGW, two Michigan auditors. The dependence is the trade-off the recognition era required.

**On governance health: fragile.** The 2022 *Brooks v. Branham* dispute is dead on the merits but not resolved. The Youngkin veto of HB 2134 closed a state-recognition codification door. The USDA Walut Pí:se cancellation removed the largest competitive food program. The EPA CPRG Phase 2 implementation door closed. The current administration is governing well, but the macro-environment for newly-recognized eastern tribes — funding, state recognition reinforcement, federal-program durability — is weakening, not strengthening.

The investigator's conclusion: there is no fire here. There is enough smoke from procurement concentration, vendor cross-roles, opaque transactions, and kin-density of governance that the next decade of MIN's history will be either (a) an exemplary example of how a small recognized tribe matures its institutions or (b) the period in which one or more of those concentrations becomes a problem the public record actually documents. Both outcomes are still on the table.

## Part IX — The Counter-Narrative

The same evidence reads differently if the question is "is this tribe building well?"

**Two clinics, one new health authority, fifteen hundred patients of additional capacity.** The Madison Heights tribe-self-funded clinic was the first Native-American-owned-and-operated full-service tribal clinic in Virginia. Free care to any enrolled member of any federally-recognized tribe in the country. The Mid-Atlantic Tribal Health Center in Charles City added 1,400 PRC-eligible members across seven mid-Atlantic tribes. Both buildings opened on schedule for buildings of their size and complexity. That is a real, locally-rooted public-health expansion.

**Rassawek.** The James River Water Authority pumping-station fight ended in March 2022 with JRWA voting unanimously to abandon the Rassawek site and deed the parcel back to MIN. This is the **most consequential eastern-tribe sacred-site repatriation precedent of the post-recognition era** — and it succeeded under the National Historic Preservation Act, NEPA, and Clean Water Act 404 permitting levers that federal recognition unlocked. CHP's role on Rassawek was characterized by the firm as four years of pro bono effort. Whatever later concentration the relationship produced, the Rassawek win is not in dispute.

**The first cultural-access easement in the Commonwealth.** The 421.3-acre Bear/Cedar Mountain easement co-held by The Conservation Fund, Virginia Outdoors Foundation, Sweet Briar College, and MIN is a *new legal instrument* in Virginia — model-able by other Eastern tribes, transferable in form, scalable in concept. That is not just a land deal. It is institutional invention.

**The 2,150-acre reconsolidation around Bear Mountain.** From a tribe that had effectively no land base in 2018 to a co-protected ~2,150-acre landscape including the original 1880s settlement site by 2026. The land base is the foundation for everything else: the powwow, the museum expansion, the housing, the cultural infrastructure, the ability to actually be a sovereign rather than just a recognized one.

**The audit posture is genuinely clean now.** Three consecutive unmodified opinions, zero findings, zero material weaknesses, zero going-concern flags. The prior findings at six agencies were resolved. The current posture is what an auditor would want to see.

**Karenne Wood's legacy is irreplaceable.** First Monacan UVA PhD. UVA portrait. Memorial bench. Two published poetry collections. Eleven years directing Virginia Indian Programs. The Monacan story has a permanent academic anchor in the state's flagship university because of one woman's career arc. That is a generational asset.

**Victoria Ferguson is showing what's possible.** One paid, embedded, three-named-roles position at Virginia Tech, founded around seed-keeping and indigenous food sovereignty, recurring across years. The model can be replicated. The first Monacan to earn a UVA PhD in 2016 was Wood; the first Monacan to hold an endowed seat in Virginia higher ed remains an open opportunity.

**The kin-density of governance is also continuity.** The same families have been at Bear Mountain since the 1800s. The same families fought through the Plecker era. The same families navigated the 1985 state recognition. The same families navigated the 2018 federal recognition. Calling that nepotism is reading a corporate-governance frame onto a tribe whose continuity *is* its kinship structure. A council without Branhams, Bryants, Shields, Thompsons, Latimers, and Parrishes would not be a Monacan council.

**The vendor concentration is also institutional capacity-building.** CHP exists in part because no other firm built the legal infrastructure for newly-recognized eastern tribes. Tazewell and DSGW build IHS clinics because IHS's procurement universe is small. Michigan auditors audit small tribal governments because Big Four firms don't compete for tribal Single Audits at this scale. The "concentration" is also "the limited supply of professionals who actually do this work."

**There is no publicly-documented predicate of malfeasance.** Not one OIG finding. Not one state audit finding. Not one criminal charge. Not one civil judgment against the tribe or its officials. The Brooks v. Branham dispute, the only formal challenge to current leadership, was dismissed without reaching the merits and the parties are now back at the same table. In a federal-recognition era that has produced a steady stream of intra-tribal disputes at other newly-recognized tribes, MIN's record is calmer than the baseline.
