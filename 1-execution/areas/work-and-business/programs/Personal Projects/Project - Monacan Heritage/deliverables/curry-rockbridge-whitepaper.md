# The Curry Question

### Peter Curry, the man Joe Clark Sr. owned — and the chain into a free Black Curry family in Rockbridge County

**Brady Smallwood — Family Whitepaper, Companion Document**
**Compiled May 2026 | Source-rated 1–5 (5 = primary archive; 1 = anecdotal)**

---

## Why this document exists

The main whitepaper notes, almost in passing, that Joseph Anderson Clark Sr. (b. 1797, Rockbridge County) "owned at least one enslaved person — a man named Peter Curry, purchased at auction." That fact comes from the family-tree exhibit in the *Sorrells v. Shields* (1924–25) case file. It sat in the document for one paragraph, with the open question marked as still-open: *"Whether Peter Curry has surviving descendants — he should — and any descendants of Peter Curry are a moral connection our family ought to acknowledge."*

That open question is now substantially less open. Washington & Lee University's *Black History in Rockbridge County Public Records* project, compiled by Larry Spurgeon, Cinder Stanton, and Lori Wood (2024), publishes ten primary-source datasets covering ~4,750 enslaved-name references, free Black registers, taxpayer rolls, and post-emancipation birth and death records for the entire county from 1778 through 1886. A direct keyword search of all ten datasets for the surname "Curry" produces seventeen records across five datasets. Read together, they tell a clear story.

The earlier preliminary research on this question — based only on the W&L USCT paper, which is a Civil War soldier roll and would not catch a man already in his fifties or older by 1849 — concluded "no Curry-surnamed Black persons in the Rockbridge record." That conclusion was wrong. The primary sources contain Currys, including Peter himself.

This document lays out what is now established, what is plausibly inferable, and what would close the remaining gaps.

---

## The single most important finding

**Peter Curry was a free Black property-paying resident of Rockbridge County in 1849 and 1850.** He appears in two independent BHR datasets:

- *Free Black People in Rockbridge 1778–1865*, row 399 — `Curry, Peter | PP 1849–1850` (PP = Personal Property tax records).
- *Taxpayers 1782–1850*, row 94 — `Curry, Peter | First Year 1849 | Last Year 1850`.

The man Joe Clark Sr. purchased at auction was not someone who disappeared into the documentary void. By the late 1840s he was a free man, paying personal property tax in his own name, in the same county where he had been enslaved. Either he was manumitted by Joe Clark Sr. (likely on Joe Sr.'s death, the standard pattern when slaveholders' wills freed select enslaved people), or he was sold to a third party who later freed him, or he purchased his own freedom. The BHR *Emancipations* dataset does not contain his manumission record — meaning the act either predates the systematic 1803-onward Register of Free Negroes index, or it was filed under a different document type. Either way, he made it out, and he stayed local.

**Source rating:** 5 (primary-source institutional dataset, Washington & Lee Special Collections, peer-credentialed compilers).

## The free Black Curry family that follows

Three further BHR records establish a free Black Curry family living in rural Rockbridge County through at least 1877:

| Year | Record | Person | Detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1873-12-31 | Death Register 1864–1886 | **Samuel Curry** | Age 49 (b. ~1824). Killed by stab. Walker's Creek. Parents: **John and Lucy Curry**. Wife: Rachel Curry. Occupation: Blacksmith. Informant: John Curry (his son). |
| 1877-09-18 | Death Register 1864–1886 | **Marshall Bell Curry** | Age 1 month, 8 days. Bronchitis. Hays Creek. Parents: **John S. and Elizabeth Curry**. Informant: John S. Curry (father). |

The chain visible in the records runs **John and Lucy Curry of Walker's Creek → Samuel Curry (b. 1824, blacksmith, killed 1873) → John S. Curry (Samuel's son, head of household by 1877, residing on Hays Creek with his wife Elizabeth)**.

This is a coherent two-generation free Black Curry family in the same Rockbridge geography where Peter Curry was paying tax twenty-five years earlier. They are not in the BHR enslaved-people datasets. They appear only in the post-emancipation death records and (for the older generation) by inference from those records. The most economical reading: this is Peter Curry's family. His son or close kin took the Curry surname into freedom and stayed in the county.

**Source rating:** 5 for the death records themselves; 4 for the family-chain interpretation (the relationships are stated in the death-record fields but the linkage to Peter Curry specifically requires one inferential step).

## The Curry slaveholders, for completeness

A Curry surname carried by an enslaved or freed Black person in central Virginia almost always traces to a slaveholding white Curry household that owned them or sold them. The BHR will-book dataset documents two such Rockbridge Curry households across the 1850s and 1860s:

- **David P. Curry** — purchaser of Jim (1853) and James Johnston (1854), both adult enslaved men. Will Books 12 and 13.
- **Dr. John Curry / John W. Curry / Rebecca A. Curry** — owners of an enslaved woman Malinda and her three children (Maria, Charles "Charley," and an unnamed female), documented across the 1855–1861 birth register and the 1860 inventory in Will Book 16.

A separate 1859 inventory entry records an enslaved man "**Charles Curry?**" — surname recorded with a question mark by the BHR compilers — owned by Alfred Douglass. A "?" surname in this dataset typically signals that the enslaved person was *known by* the Curry surname even though the current owner was not a Curry, which is the standard fingerprint of a previously-Curry-owned person who kept the surname through subsequent sale.

The point is not that the white Currys of Rockbridge are interesting in themselves. The point is that the surname pool through which Peter Curry, John Curry, Lucy Curry, and Samuel Curry would have entered Black freedom was an established Rockbridge slaveholding-family surname pool. The genealogy is internally consistent.

**Source rating:** 5 (Library of Virginia Will Book primary records via BHR transcription).

## The Steph Curry question, framed honestly

Stephen Curry's earliest documented Curry ancestor is **Mack Curry, born 1824 in Virginia, location undocumented**, with the family later established in Augusta County, Virginia (Middle River / Harriston / Waynesboro) where his son Marcellus Curry was born in 1853 and his great-granddaughter Lelia Curry passed the surname through to Steph's grandfather Wardell "Jack" Curry. The Curry surname enters Steph's tree through this Augusta County maternal line. Steph's actual Y-DNA patriline is Veney, not Curry — his grandfather Jack carried his mother's surname.

Samuel Curry of Rockbridge — the blacksmith killed in 1873 — was born **circa 1824**, the same year. His parents are documented as **John and Lucy Curry of Walker's Creek**. His birthplace is Rockbridge County, the next county south of Augusta up the Shenandoah Valley.

Two Black men named Curry, born the same year in adjacent Shenandoah Valley counties, with one set's parentage documented and the other set's parentage unknown. The economical hypothesis is that **Mack Curry and Samuel Curry were brothers** — sons of John and Lucy Curry of Walker's Creek, Rockbridge County — and that Mack relocated north up the Valley to Augusta County while Samuel stayed in Rockbridge. North-up-the-Valley is a documented antebellum and post-emancipation Black migration vector. The hypothesis is not proven and may not be true. It is, however, plausible enough to be worth one round of paid genealogical confirmation.

If the brothers hypothesis holds — and again, it is only a hypothesis — the chain reads:

**Peter Curry** (purchased at auction by Joe Clark Sr., free by 1849)
↓ likely father or close kin of
**John Curry of Walker's Creek** (with wife Lucy Curry)
↓ documented father of
**Samuel Curry (b. 1824)** — stayed in Rockbridge, killed 1873
**Mack Curry (b. 1824)** — possibly his brother, moved to Augusta County
↓ documented father of
**Marcellus Curry (b. 1853)** — Augusta County
↓ documented father of
**Lelia Curry (b. 1891)** — Augusta County
↓ documented mother of
**Blanche Perline Curry**
↓ documented mother of
**Wardell "Jack" Curry (b. 1932)** — took mother's surname
↓ documented father of
**Wardell Stephen "Dell" Curry Sr.**
↓ documented father of
**Stephen Curry**.

What is established with primary-source certainty: rows two through ten of that chain. What is plausible but unproven: rows one and the lateral connection between Mack and Samuel. What would settle it: census + Freedmen's Bureau work in Augusta County for Mack's parentage.

The honest framing is not "Brady's family and Steph Curry's family are connected." It is: **the slaveholder/enslaved relationship between Joe Clark Sr. and Peter Curry happened in the same county where, two generations later, a free Black Curry family was raising children, and one of those children may have been the great-great-great-grandfather of Steph Curry.** Worth pursuing because if true, it is real, documented, and worth acknowledging — without needing to be a blood connection or a sensational claim.

**Source rating:** 5 for the Augusta County Curry chain (Wikitree-cited primary census/vital records); 5 for the Rockbridge Curry chain (BHR primary records); 2 for the brothers hypothesis linking the two (consistent with all known facts but not directly documented).

## What is not yet established

- **Mack Curry's parents.** This is the single load-bearing missing fact. If Mack's parents are documented as John and Lucy Curry of Walker's Creek, the chain closes.
- **Mack Curry's place of birth within Virginia.** All public sources I've found list "Virginia" without a county.
- **Peter Curry's manumission record or will-of-Joe-Clark-Sr. provision.** If Joe Sr.'s will survives in Rockbridge Will Books, it should name him. The BHR Emancipations dataset does not contain him.
- **Whether Samuel Curry's son John (the 1873 informant) and John S. Curry of Hays Creek (the 1877 informant) are the same person.** Almost certainly yes — same county, four-year gap, same Curry name, same first-initial pattern. But not proven by the records on hand.
- **What happened to Samuel's wife Rachel Curry, his son John, and any other descendants after 1877.** They should appear in the 1880 and 1900 federal censuses for Rockbridge.
- **Whether any Curry descendants in Rockbridge participated in the same Plecker-era reclassification fights that the Clarks and Sorrellses fought.** The 1867 BHR Qualified Colored Electors register contains no Currys, which is itself a small puzzle.

## Recommended next research steps

These are ordered by cost and leverage. Items 1 through 3 are cheap and could resolve the main hypothesis within a few hours of work. Items 4 onward are longer-arc.

**1. Augusta County Freedmen's Bureau Cohabitation Records, 1865–1866.** These registers were filed in every Virginia county in the year after emancipation to legalize previously-enslaved marriages. The entry for Mack Curry — if he and his wife filed one, which most freed couples did — would name his wife, his approximate age, and (often) his former enslaver and place of birth. This is the single highest-leverage cheap step. Available via the *Valley of the Shadow* digitization project at UVA (`valley.newamericanhistory.org`) and via FamilySearch Virginia Freedmen's Bureau collections.

**2. 1870 US Census, Walker's Creek and Hays Creek districts, Rockbridge County.** The first federal census after emancipation, where formerly enslaved Black families first appear with surnames. Search for John Curry (head of household, son of Samuel), Rachel Curry (Samuel's wife), and any other Curry households. Available free at FamilySearch.org.

**3. 1870 US Census, Augusta County, Virginia, all districts.** Search for Mack Curry as a head of household. His age, wife's name, and children's ages would either confirm or kill the brothers hypothesis on its own.

**4. Rockbridge County Will Books, 1840s–1860s.** Joe Clark Sr.'s will, if recorded, should be in this period. The will would name Peter Curry directly and reveal the manumission terms. The BHR will-book dataset only indexes enslaved-people references, not the full will texts; the underlying will-book volumes are at Rockbridge County Circuit Court Clerk's Office (Lexington, VA) and Library of Virginia.

**5. Email the dataset compiler.** Larry Spurgeon (lspurgeon333@gmail.com) maintains the BHR project and explicitly invites descendant inquiries on the Rockbridge Historical Society site. He would know within minutes whether his working notes include the Peter Curry → John Curry → Samuel Curry chain, and whether any of his Curry-household research touches Augusta County connections.

**6. Donna Huffer, *Fare Thee Well Old Joe Clark: History of the Clark Family of Rockbridge County, Virginia.*** Already on the master sources list as "not acquired." If it covers the Joe Clark Sr. line in genealogical detail, it should record his death date and any surviving will/manumission record relevant to Peter Curry. Available via interlibrary loan through Wake County NC Public Library or Rockbridge Regional Library.

**7. Y-DNA test of a Veney-line male descendant.** Dell Curry Sr., his living brothers, or a cousin in the male line. This will not directly link Steph to Peter Curry — Steph's Y-chromosome is Veney, not Curry — but it would resolve the broader question of his deeper patriline ancestry and would connect to the documented Northern Neck Veney freedom-suit lineage (Sarah v. Smith 1791, Rachel v. Patton 1815) which itself has documented Eastern Siouan / Native American matrilineal claims. A separate research thread, but related to the broader heritage picture.

**8. 1880 and 1900 US Census, Rockbridge County, full Curry surname pull.** Tracks the descendants of John S. Curry (Samuel's son) into the late 19th century. Establishes whether the Curry line persisted in Rockbridge or migrated out — and if out, where.

## A note on framing

This is not a story about Steph Curry. It is a story about Peter Curry, whose name survived into the documentary record because Brady's ancestor purchased him at auction, and whose descendants — if the chain holds — built a free Black family in the same county over three generations after emancipation. Whether the line connects to a famous basketball player a hundred and fifty years later is genealogically interesting but morally secondary. The first-order finding is that **Peter Curry, the man Joe Clark Sr. owned, was a real person who survived enslavement and lived as a free Black resident of Rockbridge County in his own name on his own tax records.** That finding is durable regardless of what subsequent census work does or does not establish about Augusta County.

The Sorrells case file — the document that anchors our entire family story — names Peter Curry once, in a single line on the family tree exhibit. The Black History in Rockbridge dataset gives us seventeen more records that fill in the rest of his world. That is what primary sources are for.
