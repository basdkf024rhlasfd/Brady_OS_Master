## The queue

### 1. The Oral History Capture — "While They Remember" `[FIELD]` ⚠️ EXPIRING
The only item on this list that gets harder every year. Gene's half-siblings (Simmons, Weyerman, Pugh, Lee lines) and surviving cousins hold living memory of Lutie Fannie Clark, Charles Richard Smallwood, and secondhand Nelson Roudnal. Product: recorded interviews → transcripts in `research/oral-history/` → a "Voices" whitepaper quoting them directly. Both new Smallwood-side papers list this as research item #1. **Recommend this is the next thing that actually happens.** Needs: names/contacts from Brady, a recording plan (Otter), consent notes.

### 2. The War Between the Dates — The Civil War Generation `[RESEARCH+WRITE]`
Nobody has asked what this family did 1861–1865, and the tree is full of men the war had to have touched: Henry J. Smallwood (b. 1831, prime conscription age), William Wiseman (d. 1862 at 29 — cause unknown), James Smallwood (d. ~1865), Lee Harrison Clark (1837–1870), Nelson Alexander Clark's childhood in wartime Amherst, and the unexamined question of what the war meant for a Mulatto-classified family (Nelson Harrison Clark, 1860) living in Confederate Virginia. Sources needed: NPS Soldiers & Sailors + Fold3 service-record sweeps (one evening per surname), 1860 slave schedules check, county court minute books. The Mulatto-classification angle makes this potentially the most original paper in the series — the war from inside a tri-racial isolate community.

### 3. One Family, Two Virginias — The Convergence Synthesis `[WRITE, blocked]`
The big-frame paper: 350 years of the Eastern Shore English line (Bulls of Pungoteague, in Virginia by the 1650s) and the Blue Ridge Monacan-descended line running in parallel — settlers and displaced, tidewater and mountain, never touching until Gene Smallwood married Susan Dean in the 20th century. Two opposite experiences of the same colony, one family. **Blocked on:** recovering the *Virginia Deans and Donahoes v2* whitepaper into the repo (it lives in Notion / `~/brady-os-local` — flagged in `Project - Deans and Donahoes/PROJECT.md`). Recover it first; this paper then writes itself from the two corpora.

### 4. The Deep Lines Audit — Generations 12–19 `[AUDIT]`
The DeYoung workbook extends to generation 19 — medieval England, Scotland, Germany, Switzerland — and the master whitepaper already references a Moseley → Heigham → Waldegrave → Cotton chain. Deep FamilySearch consensus trees are where fabrication and wishful merging concentrate; **do not narrate these lines until they're audited.** Product: a verification memo (not a whitepaper) grading each pre-1700 line: verified / plausible / broken. Whatever survives the audit becomes the honest raw material for a future "Deep Time" paper. This is the anti-fabrication gate for the whole medieval layer.

### 5. The Kids' Edition `[WRITE]`
The master whitepaper's stated purpose was always "for Brady's kids" — but 35,000 words is for adult kids. Product: an age-banded retelling (the kids-test-results skill already has per-kid color identities and reading bands to calibrate against): the story of the brown people of Irish Creek, the woman who beat the bad clerk in court, the family that translated its own name into English, the grandmother who kept the name. One printed keepsake per kid. Pairs naturally with `kids-test-results-onepager` production patterns (HTML → print). Real outputs land in `~/brady-os-local/family/kids/` per repo privacy rules if kid-specific.

### 6. Speaking Monacan — The Language Keepsake `[WRITE]`

Pairs with mception-design-system for a printable artifact. The only paper in the series a 7-year-old could hold.

### 9. Harrison Wane — The Highest-Leverage Unknown `[RESEARCH+WRITE]`
Files 13 and the new matriarchs paper converge on one man: Harrison Wane (b. 1773), zero sources, no parents, no birthplace — and the most likely paternal entry point for Native ancestry. Research: Rockbridge tax rolls, marriage bonds, and militia lists 1790s–1810s; the Wane/Vane/Wayne spelling cloud; Sally Wane's Aylesford registers to fix the family's arithmetic. If he surfaces in any record, that's a paper. If he surfaces in none, *that pattern is itself the paper* — absence as evidence, the way the community's families actually appear in this era.

### 10. The mtDNA Verdict `[FIELD → WRITE]`
The FamilyTreeDNA mtFull Sequence ($159) has been queued since May (ANALYSIS §5.4). When it runs, the result — European, Native, or African deep-maternal haplogroup — gets its own short paper interpreting what it does and doesn't prove, alongside the existing `06-dna-analysis.md`. Cheap, definitive on one specific question, and the family's first new *biological* evidence since the AncestryDNA pull.

### 11. Ship Lists and Signatures — The Palatine Verification `[RESEARCH+WRITE]`
Follow-through on the Palatine Current paper: Strassburger & Hinke's *Pennsylvania German Pioneers* sweep for Knisely/Zumbro/Geldmacher/Weismann/Gabbert/Baumgartner arrivals — with the realistic prize of an ancestor's own signature on a Philadelphia oath of allegiance. Plus Valley church-book identification. Upgrades that paper's (2)-rated European claims toward (4–5), and may produce a facsimile-signature artifact for the wall.

### 12. The Nelson Roudnal File `[RESEARCH+WRITE]`

Short paper or an update woven into "The Name Itself." Cheap, high-certainty, emotionally central.
