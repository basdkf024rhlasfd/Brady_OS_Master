## Headline going-in finding

The GEDCOM already extends the Smallwood patriline back to **James Smallwood (b. ABT 1755 Stafford, VA, d. 15 JAN 1792 King George, VA)**. That dramatically reshapes the migration question: Brady's Smallwoods did NOT come into Augusta VA via the Frederick County VA / Loudoun County corridor (the route Jean Carter Smallwood's book documents). They came in via the **Northern Neck of Virginia** — Stafford and King George counties sit directly across the Potomac River from Charles County, Maryland, where Col. James Smallwood (1639) anchored his line. The Charles Co. MD → Northern Neck VA migration is a documented 1740s–1770s corridor.

This James Smallwood (b. 1755 Stafford) is **not in Jean Carter Smallwood's 2002 book**. He's either:
1. A previously undocumented branch of the Charles Co. MD Smallwoods (most plausible)
2. A separately-arrived Smallwood immigrant line (one of Randall 1620, Matthew 1632, John 1637, Matthew 1652 per the book)
3. A line that came into VA from a different colony entirely

This is the central question Phase 2 has to resolve.

**Adjacent finding:** James Smallwood (1755) married **Catherine Clark** (b. 1756 Stafford VA, d. 1820 Spotsylvania VA). The Stafford/King George Clark surname in this generation is associated with the Carter family of Corotoman (Robert "King" Carter's descendants) through marriage. **If** Catherine Clark traces to that gentry network, the Carter connection in Brady's tree runs through this Catherine Clark — *not* through Jean Carter Smallwood's Carters.

(The Catherine Clark above is a different Clark line from Brady's paternal grandmother Lutie Fannie Clark of Rockbridge County / Monacan project. The two Clark surnames are 100+ miles apart geographically and likely unrelated, but worth a Phase 2 footnote because the engine demands surface checks like that.)

## Religious / cultural overlay (going-in)

**Confirmed: German Baptist Brethren / Church of the Brethren.** Three independent cemeteries on the maternal-side Knicely/Johnson/Wiseman ancestors:
- Pleasant Valley Brethren Church, Weyers Cave (Ida Florence Johnson)
- Middle River Church of the Brethren, New Hope (Elijah Gold Knicely + family)
- Summit Church of the Brethren, Centerville (George Robert Knicely + Sarah Amanda Wiseman)

Brethren are one of the three German "peace churches" with Mennonites and Quakers — pacifist, plain-dress historically, German-language services into the 20th century. Heavy concentration in Rockingham/Augusta counties from 1740s onward. **The Brethren community kept exceptionally good records** (membership lists, council minutes, cemetery records). This is a Phase 2 / Phase 3 archive opportunity.

The Smallwood patrilineal side (Henry James Smallwood, James Smallwood 1755) does not show Brethren cemetery affiliation in the current GEDCOM — likely Methodist or Anglican / Episcopal given the Northern Neck VA origin. To be confirmed in Phase 2.

## Public-policy overlay

- Augusta and Rockingham counties were Confederate during the Civil War, but the Brethren community was a documented pacifist holdout — many refused conscription. Anything in the family's relationship to that war is a Phase 2 question.
- **Plecker era (Racial Integrity Act 1924–1967):** Augusta and Rockingham counties were within Plecker's enforcement zone. Same overlay as the Clark/Monacan project (paternal grandmother's line). Phase 2 erasure-specialist agent will check.
