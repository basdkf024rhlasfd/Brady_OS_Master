### 1.4 The smoking gun for H1 (and the cliff for the bridge generation)

**The single most important fact in this entire audit:**

> **Kit 75030 — documented descendant of Col. James Smallwood (d. 1714 Charles Co. MD) — carries Y-haplogroup R-FTC1754.**

That's the Charles Co MD modal haplotype. It is in Branch 2 (admin estimate: common ancestor 1600–1700), which is consistent with Col. James (b. 1639) as the project anchor.

**Kit 175123** — Robert Smallwood, 1733–1775, "Unknown Origin" — also carries **R-FTC1754** but is in Branch 1 (common ancestor born after 1700). This is a meaningful pattern: the admin grouped Robert separately because of insufficient paper trail, but the SHARED terminal SNP (R-FTC1754) is the diagnostic — Robert is almost certainly a Col. James descendant whose paper trail just hasn't been worked back yet.

**No other testers in the project carry R-FTC1754.** Every other Smallwood Y-tester is either:
- R-M269 unrefined (most common — Y-37 only, no terminal SNP yet because they haven't upgraded to Big-Y)
- A different terminal branch (R-A415, R-BY117817, R-Y18211, R-M173, R-L151)
- E-M2 (sub-Saharan African — Kit 53637, William Smallwood b. c1821 VA, surname-only NPE somewhere upstream — see §1.6)

**What this means for Brady's $119 Y-37 bet (recasting `06-dna-analysis.md` §3 with this evidence):**

- A Y-37 alone WILL NOT detect R-FTC1754. Y-37 returns the predicted haplogroup at intermediate resolution (typically R-M269 unrefined). To resolve to R-FTC1754, Brady needs Big-Y 700 ($349 sale / $449 list) OR a single-SNP test for FTC1754 at YSEQ (~$18) once Y-37 confirms the broad R-M269 backbone.
- BUT — Y-37's STR-match list will tell us almost as much almost as fast. If Brady's Y-37 STR profile lands at genetic distance (GD) 0–2 from Kit 75030 (Col. James) or Kit 175123 (Robert 1733), he is near-certainly R-FTC1754 and H1 is essentially confirmed even before the SNP test. If Brady's Y-37 lands GD ≥5 from both, H1 is essentially killed.
- **The recommended path is now sharper than file 06 stated:** Y-37 first, and the moment results come back, run a YSEQ FTC1754 single-SNP test ($18) if STR distance is 0–4 from Kit 75030/175123. Total cost path: $119 + $18 = $137 to a definitive answer. If Brady wants the publish-grade terminal-SNP placement for the family record, upgrade to Big-Y after; otherwise the FTC1754 yes/no resolves the project hypothesis.
- **If Brady's Y-37 places him in an R-M269 cluster with NO clear matches to 75030/175123, but with strong matches to other R-M269 Smallwoods in Branches 1, 3, 3a, or 4** — that would be diagnostic for H2 (a separately-arrived Smallwood immigrant line). Branch 1's "born after 1700" estimate fits a Northern Neck VA migrant family well.

**Project administrator outreach is the second-highest-leverage move.** Charles Smallwood (chas7557@gmail.com) maintains the project and almost certainly has paper trail mappings he hasn't published. A direct email asking "do you have any tester whose paper trail goes back to a James Smallwood b. ~1755 in Stafford or King George VA, and what cluster do they fall into?" costs nothing and may pre-empt the entire $137 testing path. Recommend this be written into the action surface.

### 2.2 The parent attribution on WikiTree Smallwood-1620

WikiTree links James (Smallwood-1620) to:
- **Father:** William H. Smallwood (Smallwood-168, b. 1720 Charles Co MD, d. Mar 1782)
- **Mother:** Unknown

Pulling Smallwood-168 (https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Smallwood-168, accessed 2026-05-09):
- **William H. Smallwood's parents:** Pryor Smallwood (Smallwood-788, b. ~1680 d. 23 Feb 1734 Charles Co MD) + Elizabeth Stone (Stone-11643, b. 1682)
- **Pryor's parents (per WikiTree Smallwood-788):** James Smallwood (Smallwood-27, b. 1639 d. 1714 — i.e., **Col. James Smallwood**) + Hester Evans

**This is the exact H1 chain — and WikiTree has it mapped end-to-end:**

> Col. James Smallwood (1639–1714) → Pryor Smallwood (~1680–1734) → William H. Smallwood (1720–1782) → James Smallwood (1755–1792, Stafford/King George) → James Smallwood (1792–~1865, Spotsylvania→Rockingham) → Henry J Smallwood (1831–1904, Rockingham) → Hamilton Osborn Smallwood (1864–1938, Rockingham/Augusta) → … → Brady

**But — and this is the load-bearing caveat — the WikiTree chain is sourced almost entirely from compiled material.** Specifically:

- Smallwood-1620 (James 1755) cites only FamilySearch MQRJ-VCB as source. No primary record.
- Smallwood-168 (William H. 1720) cites Find-A-Grave Memorial #158170127, FamilySearch.org, and Ancestry Family Trees. No deed, no will, no probate. The profile itself acknowledges "birth information varies from 1720 to 1725 to 1730." Death listed as "March 1782, age 61–62, North Carolina or Maryland" — meaning the profile cannot even agree on which colony he died in. **This is a low-confidence link.**
- Smallwood-168 also flags a spouse discrepancy: "Find-A-Grave lists a spouse as Margaret Travis Smallwood, but the WikiTree profile lists Priscilla (Heabard) Smallwood." So the mother of James (1755) is contested in the published trees.
- Smallwood-788 (Pryor 1680–1734) is better sourced — cites Maryland Wills and Probate Records 1635–1777 and Early Colonial Settlers of Southern Maryland and Virginia's Northern Neck Counties. The Pryor → Col. James link is the Carter 2002 / Keith 1926 chain and is reasonably solid.
- Smallwood-27 (Col. James 1639–1714) is well sourced — Charles Co Circuit Court records, MD Land Warrants and Rent Rolls 1642–1753, MD Archives, Testamentary Proceedings, even Stafford Co VA Deed & Will Books. This is the anchor.

**Net assessment of the WikiTree chain:** the top end (Col. James → Pryor) is reasonably defensible. The bottom end (William H. → James 1755 → James 1792) is **compiled-tree-only and unconfirmed**. The pivot point is Smallwood-168 (William H. 1720) — and that profile is exactly where the documentation thins out. WikiTree has assembled the H1 narrative but has not proven it.

### 2.3 Spouse / will / executor evidence — the strongest primary-source signal

Multiple non-WikiTree sources converge on a will record that the WikiTree profile fails to cite:

> **James Smallwood of King George County died January 15, 1792. His will dated January 15, 1792 was probated July 2, 1793 in King George County. The Executors Bond was dated July 2, 1793. Executors: Charles Clark, Thomas Clark, and Catherine Smallwood (his widow). Legacies: wife Catherine, sons Charles Smallwood and William Smallwood, with the remainder of his property equally divided among his other children.**

Sources for this (multiple convergent compilations, accessed 2026-05-09):
- "Early Colonial Settlers of Southern Maryland and Virginia's Northern Neck Counties" admin tree (Mike Marshall) — colonial-settlers-md-va.us, person ID I24779 (page returned 403 to scraping but search snippets confirm content)
- Find a Grave Memorial #158168907 (page returned 403; search snippets confirm)
- ourfamtree.org / Catherine Clark page p21007 (gated by CAPTCHA)
- WikiTree search hit indirectly confirmed via Google search snippets

**This is meaningful evidence even before LVA verification:**

1. Two **Clark** executors (Charles + Thomas) plus the widow Catherine → strongly supports the GEDCOM's "Catherine Clark" wife claim. Two Clarks acting as estate executors are almost certainly Catherine's brothers or her father — placing the Clark family squarely in King George County in 1793.
2. Sons named **Charles** and **William** are the two named legatees. Charles is plausibly named for his Clark uncle (the executor); William is plausibly named for his Smallwood paternal grandfather (the William H. Smallwood of WikiTree's claim). **The name "William" here is mildly supportive of the William → James (1755) link** but not conclusive — naming after a colonial-era grandfather was conventional and also could honor a different William.
3. The will exists and is in King George County Will Book — meaning a Phase 3 LVA pull WILL find an image. This is the single highest-leverage primary-source action remaining for the bridge generation.
4. The will does NOT name a specific son James born 1792 — meaning either (a) James (1792 Spotsylvania, the next-generation son in the GEDCOM) was born AFTER the will date but before probate (a 6-month window) and therefore covered under the "remainder equally divided among other children" clause, or (b) the WikiTree-claimed son James (Smallwood-1619) is a misattribution. Option (a) is plausible — colonial wills routinely listed only the named heirs and lumped the rest. Option (b) needs the will image to settle.

**Catherine Clark's parents (per multiple search hit summaries):** William Clark + Sarah Sanders. Not yet verified against any primary source. This would put Catherine in a Stafford/King George Clark family connectable to the Charles Clark + Thomas Clark executors. **Identity reconciler (file 05) should follow up.**

### 2.4 The Margaret Travis vs Priscilla Heabard collision

This is a real fork in the WikiTree data, and it matters:

- Search hits returning "James Smallwood was the son of William Smallwood and Margaret Travis" come from the colonial-settlers-md-va.us tree and from ourfamtree.org. This is the **Margaret Travis attribution.**
- WikiTree profile Smallwood-168 (William H. 1720) lists his spouse as **Priscilla Heabard** (b. 1715 Stafford Co VA, d. 14 Oct 1784).
- WikiTree profile Smallwood-553 lists Major General William Smallwood (1732–1792), Revolutionary War general — son of Bayne Smallwood + Priscilla Heabard. This General William Smallwood **never married and had no children** (per his WikiTree profile).
- The Priscilla Heabard attribution to Smallwood-168 (William H., b. 1720) appears to be a conflation: Priscilla Heabard was the wife of **Bayne Smallwood (b. 1701)**, NOT of William H. Smallwood (b. 1720). Bayne and William H. were brothers (both sons of Pryor + Elizabeth Stone). Priscilla Heabard had only the documented children of Bayne (including General William Smallwood and Anne Middleton Smallwood Travis b. 25 Jun 1755).
- So the "James Smallwood (1755) → William H. Smallwood (1720) father → Priscilla Heabard mother" link in WikiTree is **almost certainly wrong on the mother attribution**. The mother is more likely Margaret Travis (per the colonial-settlers tree) or someone else entirely.
- This conflation is consequential: Anne Middleton Smallwood Travis (Smallwood-166, b. 25 Jun 1755 Stafford VA) is documented as Bayne + Priscilla's daughter. James (1755 Stafford) and Anne (25 Jun 1755 Stafford) are nearly the same age. The compiled trees may have invented William H. as the father of James (1755) by analogy to Bayne being the father of Anne (1755), with the Priscilla Heabard mother attribution dragged across from Anne's profile.

**Implication for H1:** the chain Col. James → Pryor → William H. → James (1755) is plausibly correct, but the supporting evidence on WikiTree is contaminated with conflation between William H. Smallwood (1720) and his brother Bayne Smallwood (1701). **The link between William H. (1720) and James (1755) needs primary-source proof — likely a Charles Co MD orphan-court record, a Stafford VA tax list pairing them, or the King George 1792 will if it names a paternal kinsman as overseer.**

### 3.2 The mother attribution — Elizabeth Jackson (GEDCOM) vs Elizabeth Gear (WikiTree)

**This is the second most important data conflict the audit surfaced.**

**Most plausible reading:** James Smallwood (1792, Smallwood-1619) had two wives. Elizabeth Jackson was the first wife (married 1816, mother of older children including the daughter Elizabeth Margaret b. 1824). **Alternative reading:** WikiTree Smallwood-1220 is wrong about "Elizabeth Gear" — it's a transcription error or another tree's contamination, and the mother was Elizabeth Jackson all along. The Ancestry.com marriage record (1816 Spotsylvania) is the strongest primary-source anchor; it favors the Jackson attribution.

**Action:** Primary-source extractor (file 08) should pull the 1850 Rockingham VA census image — Henry J Smallwood would be 18–19, living in the household of his father James and his mother. The mother's name and age in that census record is the diagnostic.

**Provisional update for Brady's GEDCOM (FAMC F155):** Do not change "Elizabeth Jackson" yet — but flag both possibilities (Jackson 1795 first wife, Gear ~1800 second wife) and note that the 1850 census will resolve.

## 4. What this changed

### H1 ("James Smallwood 1755 Stafford descends from Col. James Smallwood 1639 of Charles Co MD")

**Materially upgraded — but only conditionally.**

- The FTDNA Smallwood Project has a documented **Y-haplogroup R-FTC1754** for Col. James Smallwood (Kit 75030). H1 is now **testable in the strict scientific sense**: a single Big-Y on Brady (or a Y-37 + YSEQ FTC1754 single-SNP for $137 total) gives a definitive yes/no.
- WikiTree has the H1 chain mapped end-to-end (Col. James → Pryor → William H. → James 1755 → James 1792 → Henry J → Hamilton Osborn → … → Brady), but the **William H. (1720) → James (1755) link is the load-bearing weakness** and is currently supported only by compiled trees, not by primary sources. The Carter 2002 book did not record this chain at all (per file 02 §2.1), so the WikiTree chain represents either (a) a finer-grained later finding the book missed, or (b) compiled-tree creep. Provisional weight: 60-40 in favor of the chain being substantively correct, conditioned on the William H. → James (1755) generational link being verified by a Charles Co MD orphan-court record or equivalent primary source.
- The King George 1792 will (executors Charles Clark + Thomas Clark + widow Catherine; sons Charles + William) is now confirmed to exist in compiled-tree consensus and is the **single most important Phase 3 LVA pull remaining** for the bridge generation. The will image will resolve (a) the spouse identity (Catherine Clark — almost certainly), (b) the immediate sibling/in-law network (Clark brothers as executors), and (c) potentially the paternal kin if any Smallwood is named as an overseer or witness.

### H2 ("Brady's Smallwoods are a separately-arrived immigrant line, not Col. James descendants")

**Materially weakened but not killed.**

- No tester in the FTDNA project carries an obviously separate-immigrant Smallwood Y-cluster from Stafford/King George/Spotsylvania VA. The Branch 1 group ("common ancestor born after 1700") includes some R-M269 and R-A415 testers but none with documented Northern Neck VA paper trail.
- If Brady's Y-37 STR profile lands far from R-FTC1754 (the Col.
