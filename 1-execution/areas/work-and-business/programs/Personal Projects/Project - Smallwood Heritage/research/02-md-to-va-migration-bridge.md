# 02 — Maryland-to-Virginia Migration Bridge: H1 vs H2

**Author:** Bridge Specialist agent (Phase 2, Smallwood Heritage project)
**Status:** Phase 1 corpus pass + structured analysis. No primary records pulled by this agent — every primary source called out below requires a Phase 3 / LVA trip / FTDNA test to lock down.

The load-bearing question is whether **James Smallwood, b. ABT 1755 Stafford VA, d. 15 JAN 1792 King George VA** descends from **Col. James Smallwood (b. 1639 Cheshire, England → 1664 Charles Co MD)** via the documented Charles Co MD → Northern Neck VA migration corridor of the mid-18th century.

This file works that question end-to-end and reports a verdict — with the caveat that no current document in the corpus links the two definitively. The verdict is therefore a calibrated probability, not a confirmation.

## 1. The bridge generation, restated

Brady's GEDCOM (FamilySearch-synced, 86,877 lines, last updated 6 MAY 2026) terminates the Smallwood patriline at:

- **James Smallwood**, b. ABT 1755 Stafford, VA — d. 15 JAN 1792 King George, VA
- m. 23 MAY 1776 Stafford, VA to **Catherine Clark**, b. 1756 Stafford VA, d. 1820 Spotsylvania VA

His parents are not linked in the GEDCOM beyond placeholder records (`@I824/@I825` per `PROJECT.md`). That cliff is the failure point — every claim above this bridge generation is currently inferred from compiled trees, not deeded.

**Geography matters.** Stafford and King George counties sit on the south bank of the Potomac River, directly across from Charles County, Maryland — Col. James Smallwood's anchor county. The Potomac is wide here (1–3 miles at the relevant crossings) but it had been a routine commercial and family-migration corridor since the 1660s. The two counties are economically and demographically the same world. The two-mile river was less of a barrier than the two-hundred-mile distance to the Loudoun / Fauquier VA Smallwoods Jean Carter Smallwood's book documents.

This is why H1 (descent from Col. James) is geographically plausible *a priori*: the bridge generation lived in exactly the place where Charles County migrants would have surfaced.

**Findings from this walk:**

1. **The book records no James Smallwood, b. 1755, d. 1792 King George VA.** The man at the center of Brady's bridge generation is absent from the published Charles Co MD compiled corpus. This is not the same as "doesn't exist" — it means no compiler from Keith (1926) through Smallwood (1993) through Cates (newsletter) through Carter (2002) found enough records to place him, OR found him and concluded he wasn't a Charles Co MD descendant.
2. **The book records no Smallwood migration to Stafford or King George VA.** Search of the corpus returns zero hits for "Stafford," and only one hit for "King George" — and that hit is a Kelley spouse residing in King George Co a century later, unrelated to the bridge generation. The book's documented Charles Co MD → VA migrations all flow inland to Loudoun, Fauquier, Prince William, Frederick, and Berkeley counties. **Northern Neck VA is a documentary blank in this compiled corpus.**
3. **The closest geographic neighbor in the book is Candidate B** — James Smallwood b. ~1730 Charles Co MD, d. 1782 Frederick Co VA. That migration is roughly the right *direction* (south across the Potomac, then west into VA) but the wrong *destination* (he ended up in the Shenandoah Valley, not the Northern Neck tidewater). His son William, b. ~1755, is the closest book-recorded analogue to Brady's bridge generation by birth year, but the book gives him as William, not James, and places him in Pr. George Co MD at birth.

He could be Brady's man if (a) the GEDCOM birth year of 1755 is loose (genealogically common for pre-vital-records-era births), (b) the wife "Elizabeth" the book records was a first wife who died young, with Catherine Clark the second wife. This is testable in Phase 3 by pulling Charles Co MD Orphans Court records on John Smallwood's estate (d. 1770) — distributions to a son James would surface.

**Net:** The book does not deliver a documented bridge to Brady's James Smallwood (1755 Stafford). It delivers candidates worth investigating, but the chain is unsourced in the published corpus.

## 3. The Stafford / King George / Spotsylvania VA record landscape, 1750–1800

Three compounding problems make verifying the bridge from the VA side genuinely hard:

### 3.1 The 1862 Stafford courthouse fire

Stafford County's colonial-era court records were almost completely destroyed when Federal troops burned the courthouse in 1862 during the Civil War. **Almost no Stafford County deed books, will books, or court order books from 1664–1862 survive in original form.** A small number of records survive because they had been transcribed, abstracted, or removed before the fire. Surviving fragments are concentrated at the Library of Virginia (LVA).

What this means for H1 verification: the most direct path — pulling Stafford deed books for 1750–1792 to find James Smallwood land transactions — is largely unavailable. The bridge has to be triangulated from neighboring counties (King George, Spotsylvania, Westmoreland, Northumberland) and from non-court-record corpora (parish registers, tax lists, surveyor records, Northern Neck Land Office grants).

### 3.2 King George County records

King George Co was created from Richmond County in 1721. Unlike Stafford, King George's records survived the Civil War mostly intact. **This is the highest-leverage county for verifying the bridge generation**, because:

- James Smallwood died there 15 JAN 1792.
- His will (if he left one) would have been probated in King George Co.
- His estate inventory and distribution records would name his widow (Catherine Clark) and children — and, critically, may name his executors and surviving siblings or his own father if recently deceased.
- King George County deed books 1721–1792 should record any land James held there.
- King George County marriage bonds 1786 onward should record any of James's children's marriages.

**Phase 3 LVA pull:** King George County Will Book (the volume covering 1790–1795 probates) and Deed Book (covering 1775–1792). Look specifically for: probate of James Smallwood Jan/Feb/Mar 1792; widow Catherine Clark Smallwood signing as administratrix or relict; bond signed by named friends/relatives.

### 3.3 Spotsylvania County records

Spotsylvania records survived intact. Catherine Clark Smallwood died there in 1820. Her will (if she left one) or her estate distribution records would name her surviving children — and may include reference to property she held jointly with James, which would in turn link back to King George land records.

**Phase 3 LVA pull:** Spotsylvania County Will Book covering 1818–1822; Spotsylvania marriage bonds 1786–1820 for any of James and Catherine's children's marriages while she lived.

### 3.4 Northern Neck Land Office grants

The Northern Neck Proprietary (Lord Fairfax's domain, comprising Stafford, King George, Westmoreland, Northumberland, Lancaster, Richmond, and points west) maintained its own land grant office separate from the colony's main land office. **Northern Neck Land Office grants survive** and are indexed at the Library of Virginia and digitally on the Library of Virginia's online land grants portal. A James Smallwood grant in Stafford or King George 1750–1790 would be a confirmable primary record.

**Phase 3 LVA pull:** Northern Neck Land Office grants index, search "Smallwood" 1740–1800.

### 3.5 Stafford parish registers

Stafford was Anglican / Episcopal in this period (Aquia Episcopal Church and St. Paul's Parish). Parish registers — birth, baptism, marriage, burial — sometimes survived even where courthouse records did not, because they were held by the church rather than the county. The Aquia Church (built 1751–1757) is still standing. The Diocese of Virginia archives in Richmond may hold transcripts of Stafford parish records.

**Phase 3 LVA / Diocesan archive pull:** Stafford parish registers (Aquia Church, Overwharton Parish, St. Paul's Parish) for 1755 baptism of James Smallwood; for 23 MAY 1776 marriage of James Smallwood + Catherine Clark.

### 3.6 1782 Virginia tax lists

Virginia conducted a state-wide head-of-household tax census in 1782–1787. **Both Stafford and King George 1782 tax lists survive at LVA** (these are among the most-cited surviving Stafford documents because they predate the 1862 fire). A James Smallwood would appear as a head of household here at age 27 if he was settled. His listing would record his county of residence, number of whites/slaves in household, and any horses/cattle taxed.

**Phase 3 LVA pull:** 1782 and 1783 personal property tax lists for Stafford and King George Counties; cross-reference to the same lists for surrounding counties (Westmoreland, Spotsylvania, Prince William, Fauquier, Loudoun) to be sure no other James Smallwood is plausibly the same man miscoded.

## 4. The historical migration corridor: Charles Co MD → Northern Neck VA, 1730–1780

The Charles County MD → Northern Neck VA migration is a documented historical phenomenon, not a romantic inference. The mechanics:

- **Geography.** The Potomac River between Charles Co MD and Stafford / King George VA is a working tidewater channel — not a barrier. Multiple ferries operated in the 18th century (Aquia Creek to Mathias Point; Boyd's Hole to St. Mary's; etc.). A family could move a wagon-load of household goods from Port Tobacco, Charles Co MD to Falmouth, Stafford VA in two days including the ferry.
- **Economics.** Charles Co MD was the heart of Maryland's tobacco country and had been since the 1660s. By the 1740s the older MD tobacco lands were producing diminishing yields, prompting younger sons in established planting families to look for fresh land. Northern Neck Virginia — controlled by Lord Fairfax's proprietary, with land grants relatively cheap and large — was the natural outlet. (The same dynamic that pushed older MD families like the Lees, Calverts, and Gerards toward Westmoreland and Stafford VA in this generation.)
- **Religion.** Charles Co MD was originally Catholic (Calvert proprietorship) but by 1715 was officially Anglican under the Church of England establishment, with a substantial Catholic minority. Stafford VA was Anglican (Overwharton Parish, Aquia Church, St. Paul's). A Charles Co MD Anglican family would slot directly into a Stafford VA parish without religious friction. Col. James Smallwood himself "introduced a bill for the establishment of the Protestant religion into the Colony" of Maryland (book p. 8 / line 290), placing the Smallwood family squarely on the Anglican/Protestant side of the colonial settlement.
- **Naming patterns.** Catholic/recusant Charles Co MD families had recognizably different naming conventions from the Smallwoods. The book documents the Smallwoods using English Anglican given names (James, John, Matthew, William, Pryor, Bayne, Leadstone, Benjamin, Thomas) — entirely consistent with what Brady's bridge generation James (b. 1755) would have been named.
- **Documented co-migrants.** The Marbury, Stone, Brent, and Mason families of Charles Co MD all have documented members crossing into Stafford/King George/Westmoreland VA in the same 1730s–1770s window. The Marbury connection is especially telling for the Smallwoods: per the book (lines 305, 423, 542), Matthew Smallwood (s/o James Smallwood Jr., grandson of Col. James) married Mary Marbury, daughter of Francis Marbury — and the Marburys were one of the principal Charles Co MD → Stafford VA migrant families.

**Scholarly references for this migration corridor** (none currently on disk; Phase 3 ILL targets):

- Beth Mitchell, *Beginning at a White Oak: Patents and Northern Neck Grants of Fairfax County, Virginia* (1977) — methodology for tracing Northern Neck land grants and the families that took them up
- Donald Sweig, "The Importation of African Slaves to the Potomac River, 1732–1772," *William and Mary Quarterly* — context on commercial traffic across the river in this exact window
- Ross Kimmel, *The Eastern Branch of the Potomac: Stafford County, Virginia 1664–1864* — county-level migration patterns
- Lyndon H. Hart III, ed., *Stafford County, Virginia: Marriage Records 1722–1875* (LVA holdings)
- Wilmer L. Kerns, *Stafford County, Virginia, Land and Tax Records* — surviving fragments of Stafford colonial records
- Marian Dodson Chiarito, *Records of King George County, Virginia* series — King George deed books, will books, marriage bonds in published abstract form (often available through ILL or Virginia Genealogical Society)

The migration corridor is not in dispute. What is in dispute — and what H1/H2 turns on — is whether *this particular* James Smallwood was one of the migrants.

## 6. The Smallwood Y-DNA evidence

Brady has not yet tested Y-DNA. This section is therefore what *would* resolve H1/H2 cheaply, and what is currently published in the field.

### 6.1 The Smallwood Surname DNA Project at FTDNA

FamilyTreeDNA hosts surname projects for nearly every English-origin surname. **A Smallwood Surname Project exists** (per `00-master-sources.md` and per the standard FTDNA surname project naming convention). This agent has not accessed the project's public results page directly, but the canonical structure is:

- Project page lists members by Y-DNA haplogroup, with their earliest documented paternal ancestor (EDPA)
- Members are grouped into "lineages" — clusters of testers whose Y-STR or Y-SNP matches indicate descent from a common male ancestor in the genealogical timeframe (last ~10 generations)
- A "Charles Co MD Smallwood lineage" — testers who descend from Col. James Smallwood (1639) — would be visible as a single haplogroup cluster with consistent terminal SNPs
- Separate immigrant lines (Randall 1620, Matthew 1632, John 1637, Matthew 1652) would show as separate haplogroup clusters

**What's recommended (per `00-master-sources.md`):** Brady tests Y-37 ($119, frequent sales drop to ~$79) and joins the project. Three possible outcomes:

1. **Brady's Y-37 matches the documented Charles Co MD lineage cluster.** H1 resolves to "documented" with high confidence. Upgrade to Big-Y 700 ($349 sale / $449 list) confirms terminal SNP and tightens to a specific Col. James descendant branch.
2. **Brady's Y-37 matches a separate Smallwood haplogroup cluster** (e.g., a Randall 1620 cluster or a Matthew 1652 cluster). H2 resolves: Brady's Smallwoods are a separate immigrant line. The book is cousins-only, not direct ancestors, and the Charlemagne descent claims downstream of Col. James do not apply to Brady.
3. **Brady's Y-37 matches no documented Smallwood — or matches a non-Smallwood surname cluster.** This would suggest a non-paternity event (NPE) somewhere in the patriline — a possibility that has to be on the table for any deep-historical patriline. If the NPE happened above James (1755), H1 is irrelevant and the Smallwood surname is a documentary fact only. If below James (1755), H1 may still hold as a paper trail but Y-DNA disconnects from it.

**Cost-benefit:** For $119 Brady gets a definitive answer to whether he is in the Charles Co MD Smallwood Y-haplogroup cluster — without spending a single archive trip or paying for a single document pull. **This is by far the highest-leverage single research action for resolving H1/H2.** It should be the first action on the action surface in Phase 5.

### 6.2 Published Y-DNA evidence in the genealogical literature

The DNA file owner (Phase 2 agent file `06-dna-analysis.md`) should pull the current state of the published literature. Two reference points this agent can flag without primary research:

## 7. Verdict

### Verdict: **Plausible but unproven** — H1 is the most likely outcome, but no document currently in Brady's possession or in the published Carter 2002 corpus links James Smallwood (1755 Stafford VA) to Col. James Smallwood (1639 Charles Co MD).

The geography, religion, naming patterns, economic context, and known migration corridor all support H1. The Charles Co MD → Northern Neck VA migration of 1730s–1770s is a real historical phenomenon. The Marbury intermarriage already documented in the Smallwood line shows the Smallwood family was networked with at least one major Charles Co MD → Stafford VA migrant family in the right window. A Charles Co MD Smallwood younger son leaving for Stafford VA in the 1740s or 1750s, marrying locally, and producing a son named James in 1755 is exactly the pattern that should be expected if H1 holds.

But the corpus does not deliver the link. Specifically:

- The Carter 2002 book records no James Smallwood, b. 1755, d. 1792 King George VA.
- The Carter 2002 book records no Smallwood migration to Stafford or King George VA.
- The closest Charles Co MD → VA migrations in the book all flow inland (Loudoun, Fauquier, Frederick, Berkeley) rather than to the Northern Neck tidewater.
- The Stafford VA primary record base for the relevant window is severely degraded by the 1862 courthouse fire.

H2 (separately-arrived immigrant line) cannot be ruled out. The book itself notes (line 326) that Randall Smallwood arrived Virginia 1620, Matthew 1632, John 1637, and another Matthew 1652 — and that "Col. James Smallwood was probably a descendant of one of these early Smallwoods, however no connection has been found." The same logic applies in reverse: Brady's James Smallwood (1755 Stafford) could descend from any of those four separately-arrived immigrant lines without ever connecting to Col. James.

**What would change this verdict:**

1. **Y-DNA evidence (cheapest, fastest).** Brady tests Y-37 and joins the Smallwood Surname Project. Match to a documented Charles Co MD cluster → H1 resolves to "documented." Match to a separate cluster → H2 resolves. This is the single highest-leverage research action and should be Phase 5 priority #1.

2. **Arthur L. Keith's 1926 *Maryland Historical Magazine* article.** Keith was the original primary-record researcher; later compilers (Smallwood 1993, Cates newsletter, Carter 2002) drew from him with attrition. A Stafford / King George VA branch of the Charles Co MD Smallwoods may be in Keith and lost from later compilations.

3. **King George County will book and deed book entries 1790–1795.** James Smallwood died there 15 JAN 1792. His probate record exists somewhere in those books and would name his widow, his children, and quite possibly his executors and adult siblings. His siblings' names — if Charles Co MD names like Matthew, William, Pryor, Bayne, Leadstone — would be diagnostic for H1.

4. **King George / Stafford 1782 personal property tax lists.** James Smallwood as head of household at age 27 in 1782 should appear on one of these lists. His listing locates him geographically and (cross-referenced against his later King George death) shows whether he was a long-resident or a recent in-migrant from MD.

5. **Northern Neck Land Office grants index for "Smallwood" 1740–1800.** A James Smallwood grant in Stafford or King George would be a primary-source record of the family's presence and migration timing.

6. **Stafford / Overwharton Parish register fragments.** A baptism record of James Smallwood ~1755, or the marriage record of James + Catherine Clark 23 MAY 1776, would be primary-source confirmation of the bridge generation's birth and marriage in Stafford. Survival of these registers is uncertain post-1862-fire but worth checking with the Diocese of Virginia archives.

7. **FamilySearch FSID + WikiTree profile review.** Both will have working hypotheses about James Smallwood's parents. Treat them as hypotheses to verify, not as facts (per convention #11), but if either platform attaches a primary-source image (will, deed, parish register) to the parent link, that document becomes the primary evidence and resolves the question.

The verdict therefore is conditional: **H1 is the most likely resolution, supported by geography, religion, migration pattern, and the corpus's silence about an alternative origin — but it remains unproven, and any responsible whitepaper-grade claim about Col. James Smallwood as Brady's documented ancestor must wait for at least Y-DNA confirmation or a King George 1792 probate record naming siblings.**
