# 05 — Family Identity

Identity reconciler file for the Smallwood Heritage project. Three mandates: (H3) Catherine Clark and the FFV Carter-Corotoman question; (Reconciliation) what in Jean Carter Smallwood's 2002 book actually applies to Brady's documented Northern Neck line; (Folklore audit) the absence of Smallwood folklore as the framing of this project.

The single most important framing point upfront: **the project exists because there is almost no oral tradition above Nelson Roudnal Smallwood (d. 1955).** Brady's father was 8 when his father died. The Smallwoods on Brady's side are an absence that this project is trying to fill from records, not from memory.

---

## 1. Catherine Clark (b. 1756 Stafford VA, d. 1820 Spotsylvania VA) — H3 verdict

### What the GEDCOM asserts

Catherine Clark, b. 1756 Stafford VA, d. 1820 Spotsylvania VA, married James Smallwood (b. 1755 Stafford) on 23 MAY 1776 in Stafford VA. No parents linked in current GEDCOM (per PROJECT.md, F333 has James 1755 as CHIL but his parents @I824/@I825 don't have FAMC linked beyond — and Catherine Clark's parents aren't surfaced at all).

### What the corpus actually allows me to say

Three problems compound on this question, and all three need to be flagged before any "FFV Carter-Corotoman" claim is allowed downstream.

**Problem 1: Stafford County records were destroyed in the 1862 courthouse fire.** Per `00-master-sources.md` §4 ("most colonial Stafford records were destroyed in Civil War — 1862 courthouse burning; LVA holds surviving fragments"). This means the marriage register that would normally identify Catherine Clark's parents likely does not survive. Any Stafford-Catherine-Clark genealogy from the 1750s–1770s that asserts parentage with certainty is almost always reconstructing it from neighboring counties (King George, Spotsylvania, Prince William, Fauquier) plus tax lists and post-fire reconstructions. Treat any compiled tree assertion of Catherine Clark's parents as hypothesis, not fact, until a primary record is in hand.

**Problem 2: Clark is one of the most common surnames in colonial Northern Neck VA.** There is no shortage of Clark families in Stafford / King George / Westmoreland in the 1730s–1750s window where Catherine's parents would sit. Multiple Clark lines coexist; "the Stafford Clarks" is not a single family. To prove Catherine's parents one needs either a will naming her, a marriage bond with parental consent, a guardian record, or a deed where she is named as heir.

**Problem 3: the Carter-Corotoman gentry network had specific marriage patterns that did not normally pull in unsourced Clark-surname women from neighboring small farms.** Robert "King" Carter (1663–1732) of Corotoman in Lancaster Co VA was one of the wealthiest men in colonial America. His descendants — the Carters of Shirley, Cleve, Sabine Hall, Nomini Hall, etc. — married almost exclusively within the FFV (First Families of Virginia) gentry: Lee, Page, Burwell, Harrison, Randolph, Byrd, Fitzhugh, Tayloe, Beverley, Wormeley, Ludwell. These are the marriages catalogued in standard FFV genealogies (e.g., Stanard's *Some Emigrants to Virginia*, the *Virginia Magazine of History and Biography* genealogy issues, William G. Stanard and Mary Newton Stanard's *The Colonial Virginia Register*). A "Clark of Stafford" daughter marrying into that network would be extraordinary, not typical. Where Clarks do thread into the Carter network, it tends to be at the Clarke-of-King-and-Queen-County level (a separate gentry family using the "-e" spelling), not the small-farmer Stafford Clarks.

### Honest verdict on H3

**Confidence that Catherine Clark (1756 Stafford) connects to the FFV Carter-Corotoman gentry network: low.** Two reasons.

First, the prior probability is low. Most Stafford Clarks of the 1730s–1750s were yeoman farmers, tradesmen, or tenants on Northern Neck Proprietary land — not gentry. The base rate for "Stafford Clark daughter married a gentry-network Carter line" is small.

Second, the evidentiary path is blocked: the Stafford 1862 burning means the records that could prove or disprove the connection mostly do not exist. The few paths that survive are LVA's accession of pre-1862 Stafford fragments, Northern Neck Proprietary land grants (Library of Virginia, Richmond), Northumberland and Westmoreland county records (which often cross-referenced Stafford families), and Virginia Historical Society's Carter family papers (Carter of Corotoman, Sabine Hall, Nomini Hall manuscripts).

A modest possibility exists that Catherine Clark's *mother* was a Carter — that is, that there's one generation of Carter-Clark intermarriage at the great-grandmother level. This is the type of claim that is plausible at low probability and impossible to verify without primary records. **The honest finding is: not enough evidence to assert any Carter-Clark kinship for Catherine Clark; H3 should be downgraded from "possible thread" to "open question with low priority" until the LVA trip surfaces something concrete.**

### What would actually move the verdict

In rank order of leverage:

1. Stafford or King George marriage bonds 1770–1780 naming a witness or surety with Carter surname for the James Smallwood–Catherine Clark marriage (23 MAY 1776 Stafford). Marriage bonds were the legal instrument requiring a male relative as surety; if the surety on Catherine's side carries Carter surname, that's a real thread.
2. A will or estate inventory in Stafford / King George / Spotsylvania 1770–1820 naming Catherine Clark Smallwood as a daughter or granddaughter of someone with Carter blood.
3. A Northern Neck Proprietary land grant assigning Carter or Carter-related land to a Clark — sometimes intermarriage was recorded indirectly through land conveyance.
4. Y-DNA / autosomal segments matching documented Carter-of-Corotoman descendants. Brady's AncestryDNA is already on file; if a Carter-segment match shows up, the question changes.

None of these are likely to surface from the current Phase 1 corpus alone. This is a Phase 3 (LVA / VHS travel) question, and a low-priority one relative to H1 (the MD-to-VA bridge).

## 2. Reconciling Jean Carter Smallwood's 2002 book against Brady's documented line

### What the book actually documents

Jean's book is structured around two ancestor stacks. Section One (Chapters 1–13) is Smallwood — the ancestry of her husband **Elmer Eugene Smallwood**. Section Two (Chapters 14–24) is Carter — her own ancestry, through Richard Carter of Ohio (a totally separate Ohio/Virginia Carter family unrelated to the Robert "King" Carter of Corotoman line, despite the surname). Section Two is essentially out-of-scope for Brady — her Carter line and the FFV Carter line of Lancaster Co VA share only a surname, nothing more.

Within Section One, Jean traces Elmer Eugene's direct ancestry through a single chain (Chapter 3, p. 12 "Direct Descendants of Col. James Smallwood to Elmer Eugene Smallwood"; reinforced in the Preview chart on p. 44):

```
Col. James SMALLWOOD (b. ABT 1639 Cheshire England → d. ABT 1715 Charles Co MD)
  └ James SMALLWOOD Jr. (b. Oct 1668 Charles Co MD, d. 12 Dec 1723 MD)
      └ Matthew SMALLWOOD (b. ABT 1710 Prince George's Co MD, d. 1764 Charles Co MD)
          m. Mary MARBURY (b. 6 Feb 1713, d. ABT 1760), d/o Francis Marbury + Mary Greene
          └ Bayne (Beane) SMALLWOOD (b. ABT 1742 Charles Co MD, d. AFT 1820 Loudoun Co VA)
              └ George SMALLWOOD (b. 1772, d. BEF 1832 — m. Mary "Polly")
                  └ John Wesley SMALLWOOD (b. 1802 Loudoun Co VA, d. 1 Apr 1869 Clarke Co VA, stonemason)
                      └ George Washington SMALLWOOD (b. 10 Aug 1831 Loudoun Co VA, d. 16 Jan 1916 The Plains, Fauquier Co VA, stonemason; 21 children across multiple wives)
                          └ Josephine SMALLWOOD (m. Sam Smallwood, a cousin — endogamous within Loudoun branch)
                              └ Elmer Russell SMALLWOOD (m. Edith Helen Rhine)
                                  └ Elmer Eugene SMALLWOOD (Jean's husband)
```

That entire stack — Matthew → Bayne → George → John Wesley → George Washington → Josephine → Elmer Russell → Elmer Eugene — is **the Loudoun County Smallwood branch**, with the migration vector Charles Co MD → Prince George's Co MD → Loudoun Co VA in the 1770s–1790s, then radiating out through Loudoun, Fauquier, Prince William, and Clarke counties. Jean's book documents this branch densely.

### Brady's documented stack (per GEDCOM)

```
James SMALLWOOD (b. ABT 1755 Stafford VA, d. 15 JAN 1792 King George VA)
  m. Catherine CLARK (b. 1756 Stafford VA, d. 1820 Spotsylvania VA), 23 MAY 1776 Stafford VA
  └ ... (intermediate generations TBC) ...
      └ Henry James SMALLWOOD (b. JAN 1832 VA, d. 1904 Rockingham VA)
          └ Hamilton "Osborn" SMALLWOOD Sr (b. 1864 Sangerville Augusta VA, d. 1938 Mt Sidney Augusta VA)
              └ Charles Richard SMALLWOOD (b. 1902 Rockingham VA, d. 1977 Staunton Augusta VA)
                  └ Nelson Roudnal SMALLWOOD (b. 1925 Weyers Cave Augusta VA, d. 1955 Richmond VA)
                      └ Dennis Eugene "Gene" SMALLWOOD (Brady's father)
```

That is the **Northern Neck → Shenandoah Valley** branch: Stafford / King George / Spotsylvania VA → Rockingham / Augusta VA. The geography is wrong for Loudoun. Stafford is on the south bank of the Potomac directly across from Charles Co MD; Loudoun is 50+ miles upriver, reached by a different migration corridor (Frederick Co VA, Quaker / German immigration from PA).

### Where the two stacks would converge if H1 holds

If H1 (file 02 owns the verdict) confirms that Brady's James Smallwood (1755 Stafford) descends from Col. James Smallwood (1639 Charles Co MD), then the convergence point is **Col. James Smallwood himself, generation 0 of Jean's chart**. Every generation of Jean's book between Col. James and Elmer Eugene is a *cousin chain*, not an ancestral chain, for Brady. Specifically:

- **James Smallwood Jr. (1668)** would be Brady's ancestor only if Brady's James 1755 descends through this son. Jean's chart goes through Matthew (b. ABT 1710), son of James Jr. Brady's chart could plausibly go through any of Col. James's other 10 children — John, Mary, Thomas, Matthew (the elder Matthew, listed as Col. James's son in Chapter 1), William, Pryor, Benjamin, Bayne, Leadstone, Sarah — or through James Jr. via a different grandchild than Matthew. The book does not document a Stafford VA migration from any of these branches; the silence in the book is itself a finding.
- **Bayne (Beane) Smallwood (1742)** is the Loudoun migration generation in Jean's book. He is roughly a contemporary of Brady's James Smallwood (1755). They could be first cousins or second cousins if H1 holds, but they are not ancestor and descendant.
- **George Washington Smallwood (1831)** — the Loudoun stonemason with 21 children — is a near-contemporary of Brady's Henry James Smallwood (1832). Same generation. Same first name pattern (George Washington Smallwood is a common 19th-century Smallwood naming convention, attested in multiple branches per the book). **This is a high-risk place for compiled-tree confusion.** If anyone has ever conflated Brady's Henry James Smallwood with one of George Washington Smallwood's 21 children of similar name, that's a false bridge.

### Divergence point

The two lines diverge **at Col. James Smallwood himself, or at the immediate next generation (James Jr. vs. one of his siblings).** That is, if H1 holds, the most recent common ancestor between Brady and Elmer Eugene Smallwood is somewhere between 9 and 11 generations back — Col. James (1639) at deepest, or one of his children (b. 1660s–1680s) more shallowly. The two branches share zero documented generations after 1700 in Jean's book.

This is important because it means **almost nothing in Jean's narrative chapters about the lived experience of her Smallwoods (Loudoun stone-masonry, Civil War service in the Loudoun Guards, Methodist congregations of Loudoun/Fauquier, the Fauquier-Loudoun marriage clusters with Ashby / O'Bannon / Marbury / Steadman / Beavers families) describes Brady's actual ancestors.** Brady's actual ancestors lived a different life in a different geography (Northern Neck colonial frontier, then Shenandoah Valley Brethren-adjacent farming country) under different church and economic conditions. The book is a corpus to mine for *names and dates that might link*, not a story to inherit.

### Audit of the royalty descent claims (Chapter 3d)

Jean's book makes substantial royalty descent claims (Chapter 3d, pp. 132–222). The major ones:

| Claim | Path through which line | Applies to Brady? |
|---|---|---|

Also a separate Charlemagne descent through the Beckwith → Rhine → Edith Helen Rhine (m. Elmer Russell Smallwood) line, which is Elmer Eugene's *maternal* ancestry. | **No, on either path.** The Marbury thread runs only to descendants of Matthew Smallwood (1710), i.e. the Loudoun branch via Bayne. Off Brady's line. Also: this is a "reported to be" claim, not a primary-source assertion, and the Marbury royal descent through Francis Marbury (the colonial Maryland Marbury, not the English clergyman of the same name) is itself contested in serious genealogical scholarship — frequently asserted in compiled trees, less often verified line-by-line. |

**Net audit:** every royalty descent claim in Jean's Chapter 3d runs through one of two vehicles (Marbury-via-Matthew-1710 in the Smallwood patriline, or Beckwith-Rhine on Elmer's mother's side). Both vehicles are Loudoun-branch-specific or Elmer-specific. **None of the royalty descents in the book apply to Brady's documented line, even if H1 holds.** Any future family conversation that says "we're descended from Charlemagne and the Bruce" via this book is repeating a Loudoun-branch claim about Elmer Eugene Smallwood's ancestors as if it applied to all Smallwoods. It does not. It applies, at most, to the descendants of Matthew Smallwood (1710) + Mary Marbury, which is a small subset of the Col. James Smallwood patriline.

This is the convention #11 trap in its purest form. Compiled tree → labeled as fact → repeated downstream with no verification of which sub-branch it actually belongs to.

### Other claims in the book to watch for downstream repetition

- **"Smallwoods originated in the village of Smallwood, near Sandbach, on the Cheshire border"** (Chapter 1, p. 7). Possibly true as a surname-origin claim; not the same as a documented patrilineal descent for Col. James (1639) himself. Jean cites three sources for Col. James's parentage and notes that "the ancestry of Col. James Smallwood is not certain" — this is honest and should be preserved as the book's own caveat. Don't let "Cheshire England" become "we know Col. James's father" downstream.
- **"Col. James was a member of Lieut. Godfrey's Troop of Horses (1675), an intermediary with the Indians, and sided against the Proprietary Government in the 1688 Protestant Rebellion"** (Chapter 1, pp. 7–8). These claims are well-attested in Maryland colonial records (Maryland Archives, Council Proceedings) and survive primary-source verification independently of Jean's book — Arthur L. Keith's 1926 *Maryland Historical Magazine* piece on the Charles Co MD Smallwoods is the source most genealogists trust here. Still hypothesis-not-fact for Brady because it depends on H1.
- **"Colonel James's will dated September 16, 1712, probated Charles Co MD on Jan. 12, 1714/15"** (p. 8). Verifiable primary source — Charles Co MD will books. File 08 (primary-source extracts) should pull this one verbatim if it's available online; it anchors Col. James's death date and confirms heirs.
- **The marriage of Lydia Smallwood to William Smallwood her cousin, and Sam Smallwood marrying Josephine Smallwood his cousin** (multiple notes throughout Chapter 3). Cousin marriage was common in the Loudoun branch. Worth flagging because it makes the Loudoun line genealogically tangled and creates many cases where the same ancestor appears twice in a descendant chart. Brady's line, being separate from Loudoun, does not inherit this tangle, but if Phase 2 attempts to cross-link via DNA matching, expect Loudoun-branch matches to appear inflated due to endogamy.

## 3. Folklore audit — the absence as the framing

Brady carries **minimal Smallwood folklore.** This is the explicit reason this project exists. The PROJECT.md states it directly: Nelson died in 1955 when Brady's father was 8, and family memory of the line above Nelson is "thin to nonexistent."

What that absence specifically covers:

- **Nelson Roudnal Smallwood (1925–1955) himself.** Brady's father has lived 70+ years without a present father. Whatever folklore exists about Nelson is filtered through Lutie Fannie Clark (Nelson's widow, who lived until 2001) and Nelson's nine siblings. Brady's father might know fragments — Nelson's occupation, manner, the cause of his early death (the "VA hospital, Richmond" detail in the GEDCOM is a thread; was this a service-connected death? a medical complication? Phase 4 oral history should ask).
- **Hamilton "Osborn" Smallwood Sr (1864–1938)** — Nelson's grandfather. Died when Brady's father was not yet born. Probably zero living memory. The "Osborn" middle name is unusual; Phase 2 should check whether it's a maternal surname (Osborn family in Augusta or Rockingham) — naming pattern leads to in-laws.
- **Charles Richard Smallwood (1902–1977)** — Brady's great-grandfather, Nelson's father. Died when Brady was a small child or before Brady was born. **This is the most accessible lost generation for oral history.** Brady's father knew this man as his own grandfather. Anyone in Brady's father's generation who lived in Staunton/Augusta in the 1960s–70s has memories of Charles Richard. Brady's father's siblings, if any survive, are the priority interview targets here.
- **Henry James Smallwood (1832–1904)** — Brady's 2nd-great-grandfather. Lived through the Civil War in Rockingham VA. Civilian, soldier, conscientious objector? GEDCOM is silent. Phase 2 should pull Augusta/Rockingham service records or exemption rolls. Folklore is almost certainly zero — three generations removed from anyone Brady's father knew.
- **James Smallwood (1755–1792) and Catherine Clark Smallwood (1756–1820)** — the bridge generation. James died at age 36 in King George VA. No oral folklore would conceivably survive from this generation; this is records-only.

### What to recommend (Phase 4)

The Phase 4 oral history conversation needs to happen with two groups, in priority order:

**Priority 1: Brady's father, Dennis Eugene "Gene" Smallwood.** Single most leveraged interview. Specific topics to cover, in approximate order of how much can plausibly survive:
1. Nelson Roudnal — what cause of death, what occupation, what he was like as a father in his short window (1947–1955), any photographs, any letters, any military service records. The 1955 VA hospital detail is specific and likely indicates service-connected illness.
2. Charles Richard "Grandpa" — what occupation, where they lived in Staunton, religion (Brethren? Methodist? Episcopal?), any stories he told about *his* father (Hamilton Osborn). What did Charles Richard call his own father?
3. Lutie Fannie Clark stories about the Smallwoods — what did she say about Nelson's people? Did she meet his parents? What was her view of the Smallwood family vs. her own Clark/Monacan family?
4. Names and contact info for Brady's father's siblings (Nelson's children with Lutie) — every one of them is a parallel oral-history channel.

**Priority 2: Nelson's nine siblings' descendants.** Nelson had nine siblings (per PROJECT.md and GEDCOM family group F10). This means there are likely 30–80 living first/second cousins of Brady's father carrying parallel branches of family memory. A Phase 4 task is to map the F10 children, find which have living descendants, and reach out for memories of Charles Richard, Hamilton Osborn, and the Augusta/Rockingham Smallwood community. Some of these cousins may have:
- Photographs Brady's branch lost.
- Family Bibles with handwritten birth/death/marriage records (the most reliable pre-civil-registration source genealogists ever encounter).
- DNA matches that already exist in Ancestry/23andMe and can be cross-correlated to Brady's AncestryDNA file (already on disk per the Monacan project).
- Direct memory of Henry James Smallwood (1832–1904) — improbable but not impossible if the longest-lived siblings' children are still alive.

The absence of Smallwood folklore in Brady's branch does not mean the absence of folklore in the *family*. Nine siblings with descendants is a wide net. This is the highest-leverage Phase 4 action that does not require a trip to Richmond.

## 4. Summary of identity-reconciliation findings

1. **H3 verdict (Catherine Clark → FFV Carter-Corotoman): low-confidence, downgrade priority.** Stafford 1862 burning blocks the records that would prove or disprove it; the prior probability is low; no current evidence supports it; Phase 3 LVA/VHS work is the only path forward.
2. **Jean's book reconciliation: the book documents a Loudoun-branch cousin chain, not Brady's ancestors.** Brady's documented ancestors and Jean's husband Elmer Eugene's documented ancestors share Col. James Smallwood (1639) at most, and only if H1 holds. Every generation in the book between Col. James and Elmer Eugene is a cousin chain for Brady. The book is a *names corpus* for Brady's project, not a *narrative* for Brady's project.
3. **Royalty descent claims in the book do NOT apply to Brady's documented line.** All vehicles (Marbury via Matthew Smallwood 1710, Beckwith/Rhine via Elmer's mother) are Loudoun-branch-specific or Elmer-specific. Any "we're descended from Charlemagne / Robert the Bruce / Kings of Ireland and France" repetition downstream is misapplying a Loudoun-Smallwood claim about Elmer Eugene's ancestry to Brady. This is the clearest convention #11 trap in the corpus.
4. **Folklore is essentially absent above Nelson Roudnal Smallwood (d. 1955).** This is the explicit framing of the project. The most accessible recoverable memory sits with Brady's father (re Charles Richard, b. 1902) and with descendants of Nelson's nine siblings (re Hamilton Osborn, b. 1864). Phase 4 oral history is the right vehicle and is high-priority.

## Highest-leverage next question

**Which of Col. James Smallwood's eleven children does Brady's James Smallwood (b. 1755 Stafford VA) actually descend from — and is the answer "none of them, this is a separate immigrant line"?** Until file 02 (the bridge specialist) lands a verdict on H1/H2, every other Identity-reconciler question downstream — including which (if any) royalty descent claims apply, which Jean-book Loudoun-branch chapters are even cousin material, and whether Catherine Clark's potential FFV Carter thread sits inside an FFV-adjacent Smallwood network or a yeoman-farmer Smallwood network — cannot be answered. Bridge first. Identity reconciliation downstream.
