## How to read this file

Each ancestor section follows the same structure:

1. **Identity block** — name, dates, FSID, ARK URL pattern.
2. **Find a Grave** — verbatim headstone transcription if locatable; memorial number; URL.
3. **FamilySearch profile** — ARK; what attached sources purport to be; flagged if compiled-tree-only.
4. **Census** — line-by-line transcription with year, ED, page, household number.
5. **Vital records** — births, marriages, deaths.
6. **Military / draft** — registration cards, service files, pension files.
7. **Probate / land / court** — wills, inventories, deed books.
8. **Gaps** — what could not be retrieved online for free; pointer to where it lives (LVA accession, county courthouse, NARA RG#).

**Conventions:**
- Direct quotes use blockquote (`>`).
- Bracketed `[illegible]`, `[torn]`, `[crossed out]` follow standard transcription practice.
- Where the actual record image was not directly viewed in this pass, the section is marked `[NOT YET VIEWED — pointer only]` with the exact retrieval path, so a follow-up pass can fill verbatim text without re-research.
- FamilySearch ARK pattern: `https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XXXX-XXX` (record) or `/ark:/61903/4:1:/XXXX-XXX` (image), and `https://www.familysearch.org/tree/person/details/{FSID}` for person profiles.

### WWII Fourth Registration "Old Man's Draft" — Apr 1942

This is the highest-leverage primary source for Charles. The Fourth Registration captured every American male age 45–64 (born 28 APR 1877 to 16 FEB 1897) initially, then expanded. Charles, born NOV 1902, was age 39 in Apr 1942 — **outside the Fourth Registration window**. Correction: Charles was eligible only for the Third Registration (Feb 1942, men 45–64 born after 28 Feb 1897 to 31 Dec 1921... actually no — the Third Registration captured men aged 20–44 born 1897–1922).

Re-check the registration windows:
- 1st Reg (Oct 1940): men 21–35 (born 1905–1919) — Charles age 37, miss
- 2nd Reg (Jul 1941): men who turned 21 since 1st reg
- 3rd Reg (Feb 1942): men 20–44 (born 17 Feb 1897 – 31 Dec 1921) — Charles age 39, **YES, this is his draft**
- 4th Reg "Old Man's Draft" (Apr 1942): men 45–64 (born 28 Apr 1877 – 16 Feb 1897) — Charles too young
- 5th, 6th, 7th: subsequent age-up cohorts

So Charles Richard Smallwood would have registered in the **Third Registration, Feb 1942**, in his Augusta County draft board.

### Probate / will / inventory — King George County 1792

This is **SPECIAL FOCUS extract #3** from the agent mandate.

**What would be in the King George County clerk's office:**
- Will Book (if James left a will): would name wife Catherine Clark, name children, name executors, devise land and personal property
- Inventory Book / Appraisal Book: estate appraisal filed by executors, listing every item of personal property (furniture, livestock, tools, enslaved persons if any, books, clothing) with assigned value in £/shillings/pence (Virginia still used £ accounting through 1790s alongside dollars)
- Order Book: court orders related to probate (admission to probate, appointment of guardians for minor children, division of estate)
- Bond Book: executor / administrator bonds with sureties named — **the sureties are the key social-network data point**, often brothers, brothers-in-law, neighbors

- FamilySearch catalog search: `https://www.familysearch.org/search/catalog/results?q.placeNameOrId=King+George+County` — look for "Wills, 1721–1921" or similar. Microfilm reels for 1786–1799 will books are the relevant volumes.
