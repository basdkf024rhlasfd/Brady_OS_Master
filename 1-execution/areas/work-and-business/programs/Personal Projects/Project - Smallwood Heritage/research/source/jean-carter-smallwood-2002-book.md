**PREFACE (why this book was written)** 

What is in a name? Some may even remark, "Who cares?" One name is probably not much better than another. However, if we search extensively enough within the history of our family names, we will likely find ancestors distinguished in every aspect of life. There were possibly doctors, lawyers, and yes, even an occasional Indian chief, as well as presidents, kings and war heroes. We owe it to our descendants to collect and publish such information to encourage pride in family history and appreciation for the roads prepared by our ancestors so that our travels may be more enriched. 

The surname Smallwood stands out in history to such an extent that the public schools of Maryland, required all 5th graders to study the life of General William Smallwood, who was from Maryland and fought admirably with General George Washington in the Revolutionary War. When each of my two sons reached the 5th grade, and asked me if they were related to this General Smallwood (through my husband, Elmer Eugene Smallwood), my response was an indifferent, "I don't know." But by the time my daughter, the youngest, made the same inquiry, I relented to finding an answer. My children continued to finish their schooling but it took me many more years of research before I could say, "Yes, children, you are related\!" Gen. William Smallwood was the grandson of Pryor (Prior) Smallwood, who was the brother of James Smallwood Jr., born 1668 who was your 7th great-grandfather." 

Located in Maryland is a most interesting historical site known as SMALLWOOD RETREAT where many documents about General Smallwood are preserved. A large picture of General Smallwood with George Washington can be seen in the rotunda ofthe CAPITAL BUILDING in Washington D.C. 

*This book has linked together numerous Smallwoods and related families who are descended from royalty. See Descendant Charts in Chapter 3d for Emperor Charlemagne and King Robert I of Scotland. The history of King Robert I (Bruce) was told in the popular movie Braveheart. These descendants tie into other kings and queens from many countries. Chapter 3d also contains the oldest dated chart in this book, titled Descendants of Antenor, who is reported to have died 443 B.C. Robert E. Lee is recorded in Chapter 3d. Chapter 5 has connections to Abraham Lincoln (see Lincoln Connection). Other famous persons are listed in Chapter 8\. See the story of Robert "King" Carter, with connections to President Benjamin Harrison, King Edward I of England and Kings of Ireland and France. The many possible Smallwood connections to Lord Baltimore include Winifred Calvert Seyborn, reported to be a daughter of the first Lord Baltimore. Mention of other historical persons can be found as follows: President Andrew Jackson (see Chapter 17 for Margaret Jackson), and President Pierce (Chapter 18c).*

**ON THE LIGHTER SIDE** 

*Much humor can await genealogists in research. Anl870 census in the Archives, Washington D.C., shows a Cuckoo Township and in another census, not related to this book, an occupation was listed as prostitute. There was an elderly woman who stated her occupation was knitting. The following story from the Shelbourne Parish records, Loudoun Co., VA shows how even the clergy will remark about human nature and the twists it provides in their respective lives. This tale was written in 1899 by a clergy about a certain couple he married who were not a part of the church. He said, " The most shocking and Lugubrious affair, Mrs.—, an old toothless, wrinkled woman, more like an Egyptian mummy than a bride, arrayed in a borrowed white cashmere, in which same gown I had already beheld 3 mountain lassies, standing before me for the marriage vow, took into meek captivity a youth not far past his maturity. Beware of bidders." Readers can only guess what he meant by bidders", but his description of the bride is very detailed and sprinkled with a touch of humor. Then there was an unusual name concerning the record of a father whose surname was Snow. He named his son Ice with middle name of And which completes a name of Ice And Snow.* 

Mention must be made regarding the tale of a most unusual family (author unknown). It seems a young fellow came mshing into the genealogist's research offices requesting to see the chief genealogist right away. "What's the problem?" inquired the researcher. "I'm working on our family charts," he pleaded, "and it's driving me half crazy. Nobody was ever so mixed up. I need help and need it desperately." The sympathetic genealogist responded "Let's see what you have written there." This is what he read: "I met a young widow with a grown-up stepdaughter and I married the widow. Then my father married our stepdaughter, and that made my wife the mother-in-law of her father-in-law and made my stepdaughter my stepmother and my father became my stepson. Then my stepmother, the stepdaughter of my wife, had a son. That child was my brother, as he was the son of my father, but he was also the son of my wife's stepdaughter, and therefore her grandson. Then my wife had a son, my nephew and my father's brother-in-law. The stepsister of my son is also his grandmother, because he is her stepson's child. My father is the brother-in-law of my child because the stepsister is my father's wife. I am the brother of my own son, who is also the child of my grandmother. So I am my mother's son-in- law, my wife's child's uncle; my son is my father's brother, and I am my own grandfather. Now, lets see you put that down on a family group sheet\!" 

*On a more serious side, one may ask ," Where did they get the word pedigree\!" Five hundred years ago people were as interested in tracing their roots as they are today. Monks spent endless hours researching records and writing genealogical charts and histories. As the monks would note the descent from one generation to the next, they would make a little three-line mark- a long line with two little ones angling out at the bottom. This mark looked to them like the footprint of a crane, known in the French language as "pied de grue." It is this foot print that we now refer to when we speak of our pedigree."*

MISSING PERSONS in the SOURCE CHART 

Some individuals are not listed separately in the source chart and readers should look for the name of that person's ancestor or descendants where sources may be listed. In cases such as in books, census, etc., a single ancestor may be the only one credited with that source, and in reality many family members may be found in the actual record. It should be noted that all types of sources, including secondary sources (such as letters from individuals) are included. While primary sources (vital records, etc.) were sought out as much as possible, it would be wise for serious genealogists to confirm secondary sources. 

If a reader does not agree with the data of a person listed in a Descendant Chart, they should consult the Source Chart and read what or who furnished the source for that data. A careful attempt was made to copy the data as it was furnished unless further research made a correction necessary.

Evidently young Smallwood lived more or less among the native Indians, trading with them and acquiring their language and confidence. Soon he became an intermediary between them and the Proprietary Government. Summonses to attend meetings of the Council were frequent in those days of Indian massacres, and shortly Smallwood and Capt. Brant were ordered to meet with the Indians and reduce them to submission. To the end of a long life Smallwood retained the confidence of the natives, and on many occasions was called by them to act as their intermediary with the white man. 

Despite the confidence placed in him by the Baltimoreans, it is noted in the rebellion of 1688 (Protestant Rebellion), that Smallwood sided against the Proprietary Government, and joined with its leaders, Coode, Blackison and Beale, aiding them with his presence and experience in warfare. In 1688-1689 James was involved in the Orange Rebellion. 

*On April 8, 1692 "Major James Smallwood " as he was called then, was appointed to deal with the Indians and was authorized to raise his company on the east side of Port Tobacco Creek and on the north side of the Potomac River. In the same year he contributed 800 pounds of tobacco in support of a free school. From 1692 until his death, James represented Charles County in the Maryland Assembly. In 1694 he was appointed high sheriff of Charles County. He became an assemblyman of Charles County and introduced a bill for the establishment of the Protestant religion into the Colony. In the same year he was appointed Major of Foot of the militia and Commissioner of the Peace of Charles Co. Later, June 9, 1700 he was called Lt. Col. James Smallwood and after that date to his death his name is regularly prefixed with the title, Colonel.* 

The land transactions of James Smallwood were numerous. **GENERAL TURNER ASHBY** 

General Turner Ashbys gr.-gr.-grandfather was Captain Thomas Ashby from which the Smallwood genealogy descends. Captain Thomas Ashby served in the Colonial services fighting Indians. He became one of the most prominent landowners in the area of Fauquier Co., VA, for whom the Ashby Gap was named. Thomas had 10 children. The Ashby family came to America (Virginia) as early as 1635\. See Descendant Chart. 

GENERAL TURNER ASHBY and OTHER DESCENDANTS OF ROBERT ASHBY 

The story of General Turner Ashby (bom in 1828\) is well known. General Turner Ashby descended from Robert, son of Capt. Thomas Ashby. His ancestors were of English nationality and traced their origin to Richard de Ashby, who in 1296 was lord ofthe manors of Quenby and South Croxton in Leicestershire. Turner was made brigadier general, May 23, 1862\. Serving at Harpers Ferry, he became known for his daring escapades, including his undercover intelligence gathering trip to Chambersburg, Pa During the 1st Manassas Campaign he teamed up with Jeb Stuart to deceive Union General Patterson into believing that Johnston's army was still in Shenandoah Valley. He joined Gen Stonewall Jackson in the fall of 1861 when that officer took over command of the Valley district. Problems arose however between the two men and when Ashby was promoted to brigadier general by the War Dept., it was without Jackson's recommendation. Ashby gained control of the full cavalry force of the enlarged Valley army and four days later the newly-named general was killed, in a minor action at Harrisonburg, Virginia (1862). Robert Ashby was also an ancestor in the Smallwood line. Robert had 7 children. 

*Descendants of Ashby can be found in The Ashby Book by Reese (1976 and 1982). At the request of Ms. Reese in 1972, the author of this book submitted a chapter on the Smallwoods which was later included in her book. The Smallwood link to the Ashby family occurred when Nimrod Ashby, (son of Robert, d.1868), married Mary Agnes Smallwood, great- grandaunt of Elmer Eugene Smallwood. Mary Agnes was daughter of John Wesley Smallwood, (b. 1802), a descendant of James Smallwood Jr.. Nimrod and Mary had 7 children. See James Smallwood Jr. Descendant Chart.* 

CAPTAIN JOHN ASHBY 

Capt. John Ashby, another son of Captain Thomas Ashby, was a bold adventurer and set out in 1774 to explore westward lands. Forming the acquaintance of Daniel Boone, he accompanied that famous frontiersman on some of his expeditions. John had a grant of 2000 acres in the bluegrass region of Kentucky. He fought in the French and Indian War between Great Britian and France and served as an advance guard to Gen. Braddock's expedition against Fort Duquesne in 15 where he met Daniel Morgan who became a famous General in the Revolutionary War.

**BRYAN O'BANNON and PRESLEY NEVILLE O'BANNON** 

*Bryan O'Bannon was the great-grandfather of Presley Neville O'Bannon, a famous war hero. The book Valiant Virginian by Sundberg and Gott, contains a thorough study of the O'Bannons. The O'Bannon Chart in this book lists only a few descendants, and connects to Presley Neville O'Bannon, and the Smallwood family through son John O'Bannon (d. 1774). From the Valiant Virginian we are told:77ie family begins with Bryan O'Bannon, an Irishman who came to Virginia about 1702\. Descendants tell us Bryan claimed descent from Brian Boru, King of Munster in the tenth century and there is a pile of stone on the River Shannon said to be Brougal Castle built in the 2nd century (?) and occupied by the O'Bannons until the 17th century. Another castle, near the River Shannon in the County Offaly, known as Leap Castle is said to be connected to the O'Bannon family. Bryan first settled in Westmoreland Co., VA, where he was referred to as Bryant Obaning. He was in Fauquier Co., VA before 1741\. Bryan bad six children. Details can be found in the Valiant Virginian.* 

PRESLEY NEVILLE O'BANNON 

John, (d.1774), a son of Byran, had 12 children. His son, William, was the father of Presley Neville O'Bannon. Presley was bom 1776 in Fauquier Co., VA. and later became a Deputy Sheriff of that county. He served as a Marine Lieutenant and in 1805 fought in the Capture of Deme, Tripoli, a Barbary pirate harbor fort. He planted the American flag for the first time over the fortress of the Old World. Presley later served in the Kentucky legislature. He died in 1850\. 

SARAH JANE SMALLWOOD 

Samuel, another son of John (d. 1774), inherited about 165 acres from his father. Samuel had a son, Enoch, bom 1780\. Enoch had a son, Enoch Washington O'Bannon, who married Sarah Jane Smallwood. Their son Corbin O'Bannon married his cousin, Georgiana Smallwood, daughter of George Washington Smallwood who was the great-grandfather of Elmer Eugene Smallwood. 

ISHAM O'BANNON 

John O'Bannon (d. 1774\) had another son, John who had a son, Isham who married Polly (Mary) Winn. Her family resided in Rock Hill, near The Plains, VA, which was also the home- place of many Smallwoods.
