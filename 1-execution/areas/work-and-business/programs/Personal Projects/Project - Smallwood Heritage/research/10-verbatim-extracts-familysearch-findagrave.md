### Engine-convention #11 caveat for this memorial

The memorial's biographical text is a verbatim transcription of an indexed/abstracted Spotsylvania probate volume (the "Spotsylvania County, Virginia Records 1721-1800" — almost certainly William Armstrong Crozier's published abstract, *Spotsylvania County Records 1721-1800*, NY 1905, or a parallel compilation). **This is one step removed from the primary will image** — but the actual will-book image *is* attached as a Find a Grave photo (Book E pg 1274) and is independently retrievable. **The probate transcription is therefore primary-tier evidence** that James Smallwood of King George Co. died 15 Jan 1792, leaving wife Catherine and naming sons Charles and William.

**What the probate does NOT directly establish:**
- That this James's father was William H Smallwood of Charles Co MD (the Find-a-Grave parental link is a tree-attachment by Bev Williams; no primary citation)
- That this Catherine's maiden name was Clark (the probate names her only as "Catherine Smallwood, wife"; the "Clark" identification is **inferred from the witness Charles Clark + the executor Thomas Clark + Phebe Clark, who are presumed to be her father and brother + sister-in-law**)

The Catherine→Clark identification is reinforced (but still circumstantial) by the deed photo (Image 3 above), which states that the land James owned at his death **was purchased from Charles Clark in Spotsylvania Bk N pg 358-359**, and that after his death **Catherine Smallwood (the widow) sold 75 acres of it back to herself and Charles Clark sold her another tract** (per the cross-deed below).

### Hypothesis-relevant findings (H3, H1)

The deed transcription **"Charles Clark and Phebe, his wife, of Caroline Co., to Catherine Smallwood, widow of James Smallwood, Decd., of Spots. Co. 60 curr. 75 acres in Spots. Co. 2 Jul 1793"** is a verbatim abstract of a real Spotsylvania County deed dated 2 July 1793 (the same day the executors' bond was filed). Charles Clark & wife Phebe are conveying land to Catherine. **This near-simultaneous conveyance from Charles Clark (Caroline Co.) to widow Catherine Smallwood (Spotsylvania) on the date of probate is the strongest circumstantial evidence that Charles Clark is Catherine's father, supporting H3 partially** — but H3 specifies the **FFV Carter-Corotoman** Clarks; the deed shows Caroline County Clarks, which is a different (Northern Neck-adjacent but distinct) gentry network. **The Clark line is real; the Carter connection is unsupported by these documents.**

**Catherine's second marriage 29 Dec 1799 to Joseph Milstead** is new information (PROJECT.md and 08-primary-source-extracts.md did not have this). This means by 1820 her widow's name was actually Catherine Smallwood Milstead (or the marriage didn't last; she's catalogued as "Catherine Clark Smallwood" on Find a Grave and the 1820 death year matches).

### CRITICAL implication for Brady's line

**Charles A. Smallwood (1783-1860) migrated to MUSKINGUM CO OHIO in 1836** and died there 1860. **He is NOT Brady's ancestor** — Brady's documented Henry J. Smallwood was born JAN 1831 in VIRGINIA (per WikiTree Smallwood-1220 + FamilySearch GQ97-TPK) and died 1904 in Rockingham Co VA. **Henry J. cannot descend from Charles A. via Charles A.'s Ohio family** because Henry was already born in Virginia in 1831 before Charles migrated west in 1836.

**However**: WikiTree (Smallwood-1619) **claims** Henry J's father was a James Smallwood b. 1792 Spotsylvania Co., d. ~1865 Rockingham Co., m. Elizabeth Jackson 6 Feb 1816 in Spotsylvania (later married Elizabeth Gear). **This James-1792-Spotsylvania could plausibly be a younger son of James-1755 / Catherine Clark** (a posthumous son, or one not named in the will because still a minor). The 1792 will explicitly says "the remainder of my property to be equally divided among my children" — meaning there were unnamed children beyond Charles and William. Catherine was widowed January 1792; a son born late 1792 (named after his father) is biologically possible.

**This is the missing-link bridge candidate.** Need primary documentation of (a) the 1816 Spotsylvania marriage of James Smallwood + Elizabeth Jackson, (b) that this James was Catherine's son.
