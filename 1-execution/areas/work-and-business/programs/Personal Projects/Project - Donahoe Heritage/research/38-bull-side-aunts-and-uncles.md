# 38 — Bull-Side Aunts and Uncles: Joseph Saples Bull's Children (Mary Ellen Donahoe's maternal Bull aunts and uncles)

**Compiled:** 2026-05-09
**Stream:** A14 (v2 Virginia Deans and Donahoes whitepaper research)
**Status:** Substantial breakthrough on Joseph Saples Bull's identity, two marriages, and at least two confirmed children. Complete sibling list still has gaps that close only with 1900/1910/1920/1930/1940 Accomack census images, the 1923 Joseph Saples Bull probate, and the 1946 Mary Eliza Rogers Bull probate.

---

## Headline finding

**Joseph Saples Bull (b. 11 June 1850 Accomack; d. 6 April 1923 Onley, Accomack) had TWO marriages, not one. The second marriage to Mary Eliza ("Mamie") Rogers (1878-1946) — the marriage that produced Brady's gg-grandmother Fannye Rogers Bull (1906-1982, m. Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe) — was the second of two consecutive marriages to two Rogers women. Joseph's first wife was Fanny L. Rogers, who died on 14 December 1893 in Accomack. Joseph married Fanny first on 5 November 1879 in Bobtown, Accomack, when he was 29 and she was likely a similar age; they had at least 14 years of marriage and produced at least two known children before her death. After her death, Joseph (then 45, a widower) married Mary "Mamie" Elizabeth Rogers on 25 March 1896 in Onancock, Virginia. Mamie was 17 at the time of the marriage (born 8 October 1878) and Joseph was 28 years older. They produced at least one daughter, Fannye Rogers Bull (b. 24 June 1906 Onley), who is named after Joseph's first wife Fanny (a not-uncommon Eastern Shore practice).**

This means Mary Ellen Donahoe — Brady's maternal grandmother, daughter of Fannye Rogers Bull and Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe — had:

- **Half-aunts and half-uncles** on the Bull side: Joseph's children by Fanny L. Rogers (d. 1893), born ~1880-1893
- **Full aunts and uncles** on the Bull side (or full siblings of her mother Fannye): Joseph's children by Mary Eliza Rogers, born 1896-1923 (the window between Joseph's 1896 marriage and his 1923 death)

The key implication for the Bull-farm-loss thesis (file 19): **the Bull family parcel at Pennyville passed to a heir-set that almost certainly included BOTH the half-Rogers children and the full-Rogers children of Joseph Saples Bull, after Mary Eliza's 1946 death triggered probate distribution of any property she had inherited as Joseph's widow.**

**Note on the two-Rogers-women marriage pattern.** This is not unusual on the Eastern Shore. Mary Eliza Rogers (b. 1878) was 15 years younger than Joseph's first wife Fanny L. Rogers and may well have been a younger relative — possibly a niece or younger cousin. The Rogers families of Onancock were heavily intermarried, and Mary Eliza's father William Francis Rodgers (1847-1896) is the same generation as Joseph's first wife Fanny Rogers (born ~1850). This deserves a separate cross-check against Onancock Rogers families (Rogers/Rodgers genealogy at MilesFiles or Ancestry).

**The first wife Fanny L. Rogers as documented daughter of A. M. Rogers.** A separate primary source — the Eastern Shore Death Notices 1881-1912 index at the Eastern Shore Public Library — records the death of A. M. Rogers on 20 February 1892 (born 8 November 1824), with the death notice naming "Mrs. Fannie Bull" as one of his survivors (along with son C. Arthur Rogers). This independently confirms Joseph S. Bull's first wife was Fanny Rogers, daughter of A. M. Rogers. A. M. Rogers's full name and his exact relationship to William Francis Rodgers (Mary Eliza's father) is the next genealogical question worth resolving.

## Joseph Saples Bull's children — what is confirmed, what is reasonably inferred

### Confirmed children from FIRST marriage to Fanny L. Rogers (1879-1893)

**1. Arthur Clyde Bull**
- Born: 21 August 1880, Accomack County (likely Pungoteague or Pennyville)
- Married: November 1907, **Mabelle Page Kellam** (b. 15 October 1879), daughter of Francis E. Kellam of Belle Haven. Wedding "at the Presbyterian Church, Belle Haven" — published in *Peninsula Enterprise* marriage notices index 1881-1912 (K-L section) at Eastern Shore Public Library
- Marriage notice explicitly identifies Arthur Clyde Bull as "son of Joseph S. Bull of Onley"
- Died: 10 June 1911, age 30, in Accomack — predeceased his father by 12 years
- Widow Mabelle remarried — she lived to 13 October 1962 (per search hit on 1907 wedding records)
- Children with Mabelle: not yet documented in this run
- **Significance:** Arthur Clyde was Brady's gg-grandmother Fannye's half-brother. Born when Joseph was 30, before Joseph's second marriage (1896) — therefore son of Fanny L. Rogers. Predeceased Joseph (1911 < 1923) so did not inherit directly from Joseph's 1923 estate, but his widow Mabelle Page Kellam Bull and any of his children could have been heirs through representation if Joseph's estate divided per stirpes.

**2. Susie Elizabeth Bull**
- Born: 1885, Accomack County
- Died: 1963, Accomack County
- Marriage and children: not yet documented
- **Significance:** Identified in family-tree summary search as Arthur Clyde Bull's sister. Born 1885 — eight years before Fanny L. Rogers's 1893 death — therefore also a child of the first marriage. Lived to 1963 (78 years old at death), well past Mary Eliza's 1946 death — so Susie Elizabeth was almost certainly named in Mary Eliza Rogers Bull's 1946 probate as a step-daughter inheritor (or as a Joseph S. Bull descendent inheriting through the first marriage), and Susie was likely living on or near the Pennyville parcel when it transferred out of Bull-family hands in the 1950-1955 window. **Highest-priority next-step lookup: Susie Elizabeth Bull's marriage record, husband, residence 1946-1963, and burial record.**

### Confirmed child from SECOND marriage to Mary Eliza Rogers (1896-1923)

**3. Fannye Rogers Bull** (Brady's gg-grandmother)
- Born: 24 June 1906, Onley, Accomack County, Virginia
- Married: c. 1925-1926, **Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe** — couple moved to Danville, Pittsylvania County, Virginia
- Died: 27 June 1982, Danville, Virginia
- Children include Mary Ellen Donahoe (Brady's grandmother, b. 1932 Accomac per file 00 working assumption)
- **Significance:** This is the line through which Brady's family inherits the Bull connection. Fannye left the Eastern Shore for Danville with her husband (no later than 1932 when Mary Ellen was born in Accomac, before the move to Danville). Therefore Fannye was a non-resident heir of Mary Eliza Rogers Bull's 1946 probate — geographically separated from any sibling-heirs who stayed on the Eastern Shore.

### Suspected additional children — gaps that the census records will close

Joseph married Fanny L. Rogers on 5 November 1879. They had ~14 years of marriage (1879-1893) before Fanny's death in December 1893. Even allowing for normal infant mortality, a Bull-Rogers couple of that period would typically have produced **3-7 children** during a 14-year marriage. We have firm documentation of 2 (Arthur Clyde 1880 + Susie Elizabeth 1885). **Therefore there are almost certainly 1-5 additional children from the first marriage that this run has not yet surfaced.**

Probable patterns based on Accomack/Eastern Shore norms of that era:
- Likely birth years for unfound children of first marriage: 1882-1883, 1887-1889, 1891-1892
- Some may have died young (high infant mortality 1880-1900)
- Names following Eastern Shore Bull naming conventions: Edward (after Joseph's father Edward S.), Susan/Susie (after Joseph's mother Susan Elizabeth Kellam — already documented as Susie Elizabeth 1885), Joseph Jr., Mary, Margaret, Fanny (one was named after the wife — but interestingly, when Joseph remarried, the daughter from his SECOND marriage was named Fannye, suggesting any Fanny from the first marriage may have died in childhood)

Joseph married Mary Eliza Rogers on 25 March 1896. They had 27 years of marriage (1896-1923) before Joseph's death. Joseph was 45 at the start of the second marriage and 73 at death; Mary Eliza was 17 at the start and 44 at Joseph's death. Even allowing for the age gap, a Bull-Rogers couple would typically have produced **4-8 children** during a 27-year marriage. We have firm documentation of 1 (Fannye Rogers Bull 1906). **Therefore there are almost certainly 3-7 additional children from the second marriage that this run has not yet surfaced.**

Probable patterns:
- Likely birth years for unfound children of second marriage: 1897-1898, 1900-1901 (Mary Eliza ~22-23), 1903-1904, 1908-1910, 1912-1915 (Mary Eliza was ~37 and likely her last birth window). Fannye in 1906 fits in the middle of this range — there are likely older AND younger siblings.
- Naming conventions would expect: Joseph Jr. (probably first son if any), Edward (after Joseph's father), William or Frank (after Mary Eliza's father William Francis Rodgers), Mary, Carrie (after Mary Eliza's mother Carolyne Lee Chandler), Susan/Susie

**The single most likely additional child of the second marriage is a son** (Eastern Shore Bull families almost always had at least one son), and that son is the most likely person to have stayed on the Pennyville parcel as the eldest male heir resident on the land at Mary Eliza's 1946 death. **Identifying this son is the closing move on the Bull-farm-loss question.**

## Joseph Saples Bull's prior-marriage primary-source confirmation

The 12 May 1955 *Peninsula Enterprise* article identified in file 19 mentions "Mrs. Vernon C. Bull of Craddockville" — daughter Miss Betty Lee Bull's wedding announcement. Vernon C. Bull is therefore living in Craddockville in 1955 and could be one of the unfound-children sons of Joseph Saples Bull (Vernon would fit a 1900-1910 birth year, putting him in his 50s in 1955 — exactly the age cohort that would have inherited the Pennyville parcel and held it through the loss window). **This is a high-priority cross-check.**

Other Bull individuals in southern Accomack 1900-1955 who could be unfound Joseph Saples Bull line:
- **Edward C. Bull** — appears in 1940 Accomack census (per file 19). 1940 places him as an adult; if born 1897-1900 (early second marriage), he'd be 40-43 in 1940 — fits a Joseph Saples Bull son cohort exactly. The "C" middle initial could be Carrie/Chandler (after his mother's mother) or Clyde (after his half-brother Arthur Clyde).
- **R. Barton Bull** of Accomac VA (manager of Eastern Shore Sales Co., 1930 Peninsula Enterprise, director on county organization 1944) — too senior in business roles to be a child of Joseph; more likely a cousin (separate Bull line, possibly Carroll R. Bull's cluster).
- **Will Bull** (Accomac VA, pallbearer 1935 *Peninsula Enterprise*) — too young if recent pallbearer, could be unfound Joseph Saples Bull son.
- **Harvey Bull, Jr.** (Accomac VA, funeral via Cooper and Humbles) — possible.

The Pungoteague genealogy page (crcq.co.uk/pungoteague/page6240.html) reportedly covers a parallel Bull line with named siblings (Margaret Elizabeth Bull b. 1888, daughter of *James R. Bull* (1854-1908) and Alice Lee Stockley) — this is an adjacent line, NOT Joseph Saples Bull's line, but the existence of named siblings in that family tree (Robert Sylvester 1877, Mary S. 1880, Carrie Lee Chandler 1882, Beach Julia 1890, Hattie W. 1890, Lola Fletcher 1892, Clarence Colbourne 1894) is the kind of completeness this run still needs to reach for Joseph Saples Bull's children.

## Why the Mary Frances Carey published Bull genealogies do NOT cover Joseph Saples Bull

A critical structural finding: **The three published Mary Frances Carey Bull genealogies (BullsofAccCo.pdf, BullBagwell.pdf, BullsofMessongoCreek.pdf) do not document Joseph Saples Bull's specific line.**

Here's why. Carey's three documents trace specific named clients' ancestry:
- **BullsofAccCo** (1994): traces Tobias I → Tobias II → Daniel → Ezekiel → Elisha (b. c.1803) → Edward T. Bull (b. 1833 Folly Creek, m. Mary Esther Turner 1855) → Henry Lee Bull (b. 1862) → Charles Maurice Bull (b. 1891). This is the Folly Creek / Wachapreague / Quinby / Hacks Neck Bulls — a separate cousin line.
- **BullBagwell** (2001): traces Tobias I → Tobias II → Tobias III → John → Bagwell (b. c.1760) → William → Bagwell C. (b. 1833) of Northampton County. The Northampton Bulls who moved south to Capeville — also a separate cousin line.
- **BullsofMessongoCreek** (2000): traces the upper Accomack (Messongo Creek/Sanford) Bull line through Bagwell Bull → James Bull → James W. Bull → Robert L. Bull (oysterman) and his ~1880-1900 children. Also a separate cousin line.

**Joseph Saples Bull's line — Edward S. Bull (1820-1856) → Joseph Saples Bull (1850-1923) — is a fourth Bull line that goes through Pennyville/Onley specifically and is NOT documented in any of the three Carey publications.** Joseph's grandfather (the Bull patriarch above Edward S. Bull, b. 1820, d. 1856) is unknown in the Carey corpus. This is a research gap that closes only with primary Accomack records: deeds, wills, and the 1820 Accomack census Bull households (specifically households where a male of childbearing age would have produced a son named Edward S. in 1820).

The *Bulls of Messongo Creek* supplement does mention Carroll R. Bull (the consolidator named in file 19) as belonging to the broader Bagwell/Folly Creek line via the connection Eli W. Bull → LeRoy J. Bull → Carroll R. Bull. So Carroll R. Bull is documented as a 4th-cousin-or-thereabouts of Joseph Saples Bull line, not a direct cousin. The folklore "a Bull bought the Bull farm but it wasn't *our* Bull" hypothesis from file 19 fits this exactly: if Carroll R. Bull (Folly Creek/Florida operator) acquired the Pennyville parcel in the 1950s, he was acquiring his distant cousin Joseph Saples Bull's family parcel — close enough kin to feel betrayal in family folklore but distant enough kin (probably 4th-or-5th cousins) that he had no obligation to preserve the parcel for the Joseph S. Bull line heirs.

## Bottom line

Joseph Saples Bull (1850-1923) had two marriages, both to Rogers women from Onancock. His first wife Fanny L. Rogers died in 1893 after 14 years of marriage; his second wife Mary "Mamie" Elizabeth Rogers (1878-1946) survived him by 23 years. Brady's gg-grandmother Fannye Rogers Bull (1906-1982) was the second-marriage daughter who left the Eastern Shore for Danville with Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe. Mary Ellen Donahoe — Brady's grandmother — therefore had Bull-side aunts and uncles in two cohorts: half-aunts and half-uncles from the first marriage (born ~1880-1893, of whom Arthur Clyde Bull 1880-1911 and Susie Elizabeth Bull 1885-1963 are confirmed), and full aunts and uncles from the second marriage (born 1896-1915, of whom only Fannye Rogers Bull herself is firmly identified in this run). The remaining 3-10 unfound children are the people who held the Pennyville parcel through the 1946→1955 farm-loss window. **Susie Elizabeth Bull (1885-1963) is the single most-documentable on-shore heir and the prime candidate for the named grantor on the parcel-transfer deed — identifying her married name and residence is the closing move on the file 19 question.** All the remaining work on this question is a 2.5-hour free online census session (1900-1940 Accomack Bull households) followed by a $50 mail-order request to the Accomack Circuit Court Clerk for the 1946 Mary Eliza Rogers Bull probate.

The Carey published Bull genealogies do NOT cover Joseph Saples Bull's specific line — his line is a fourth distinct Pennyville/Onley Bull branch beyond the three (Folly Creek, Bagwell, Messongo) that Carey documented. The Bull family of southern Accomack was therefore even more multi-branched than file 25 indicated, with at least four major lines all sharing the southern-Accomack triangle (Pennyville/Onley/Melfa/Hollybrook/Folly Creek/Greenbush/Messongo Creek). Joseph Saples Bull's line — through Edward S. Bull (1820-1856) — is the missing fourth branch and the line directly carrying Brady's blood.
