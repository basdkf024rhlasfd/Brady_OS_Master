Primary copy in research folder at `research/source/susan-elzey-story-of-my-life-2024.pdf`; full text extracted to `research/source/susan-elzey-story-of-my-life-text.txt`.

This file captures the **first-person family memory** Susan recorded about her mother (Mary Ellen Donahoe Dean), her grandparents (Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe and Fannye Bull Donahoe; Roy Franklin Dean and Editha Read Dean), and any earlier ancestors Mary Ellen passed down to her. The autobiography is a primary-source-grade family record that materially extends what the FamilySearch tree and GEDCOM provide.

**Note on terminology:** When Susan writes "Mother" or "Mama" in the autobiography, she means **Mary Ellen Donahoe Dean** (her mother). When she writes "Mama and Dada" together, she means her grandparents Fannye Bull Donahoe and Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe. "Pop" or "Daddy" = Delbert "Del" Allen Dean (her father).

## 1. New / corrected facts about the immediate-family generation (closes prior open questions in v1)

### Susan and Paulette's birth circumstances

- **Susan Dean** born 18 September 1953 at the **Naval Air Base at Chincoteague, Virginia** (autobiography p. 2). Mother Mary Ellen was sent to her parents' home (then on the Eastern Shore) for the birth because Del was on Army recruiting duty in Michigan with no nearby Army hospital.
- **Paulette Dean** born 21 months later (so ~June 1955), also at Chincoteague under similar circumstances.
- Susan's first six weeks of life were on the Eastern Shore at her grandparents' home; then back to **Jackson City, Michigan** (Del's recruiting station).
- "That is how we wound up being Southerners, not Yankees" (p. 2) — Susan's framing of why she identifies as Southern.

**Confirms:** Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe and Fannye Bull Donahoe were still living on the Eastern Shore in 1953–1955.

### How Mary Ellen and Del met

Brady's prior knowledge: they met during military service. Mary Ellen was a recruiter, Del was Army.

The autobiography doesn't elaborate the meeting story directly in the early pages, but confirms (p. 2) Del was on Army recruiting duty in Michigan when Susan was born, suggesting Mary Ellen had transitioned out of recruiting service by 1953 and was already a military wife by then.

### Mary Ellen's military service window

Open question for v2: dates of Mary Ellen's WAC / WAVES / military service. Susan's autobiography may name the dates in a section not yet pulled (the autobiography is 189 pages). Worth a focused re-read.

### Del's Korean War service

Brady's prior knowledge: Del Dean served in the Korean War. The autobiography (p. 12) confirms Del was a "first sergeant so he had a degree of clout in his company" during their Ft. Riley, Kansas station years (Susan was 3, so ~1956). His Korean War service would have been earlier (1950–1953). Open question for v2: NPRC pull on Del's service record could fill in the dates and unit.

## 2. **MAJOR CORRECTION — Stephen Alexander Donahoe was the Methodist minister**

V1 left this open and I had briefly speculated it might be Roy Dean. The autobiography settles it definitively.

**Page 52, autobiography (in the dedicated grandparents section):**

> "Even though his father was a Methodist minister, Dada had trouble behaving in church even as an adult and would entertain Paulette and me by folding handkerchiefs into bunny rabbits and feeding us candy."

"Dada" = Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe. Paul's father = **Rev. Stephen Alexander Donahoe** (b. 1866, m. Leona Ellen Rollyson 30 June 1891 at Gauley Bridge, Fayette Co., West Virginia; FSID LC7S-HZZ in the FamilySearch tree where he is listed with the prefix "Rev.").

This is the explanation for Mary Ellen's lifelong Methodism (Onley UMC growing up; Mt. Vernon Methodist Danville). Susan's page 22 confirms:

> "Mama would never be anything but a Methodist since her father-in-law had been a Methodist minister."

"Mama" = Fannye Bull Donahoe. Fannye's father-in-law = Paul's father = Rev. Stephen Alexander Donahoe.

**This unlocks a new v2 research thread:** *Methodist Episcopal Church, South* ministerial records — ordination, charge appointments, conference assignments — for **Rev. Stephen Alexander Donahoe** in West Virginia (and possibly Augusta County VA earlier) between his ordination and his death (probable date 1935 per the FamilySearch profile listing him as 1866–1935). The MEC South kept careful conference journals; many are digitized through the General Commission on Archives and History of The United Methodist Church (gcah.org). Stephen's ordination, churches he served, and the dates would substantially deepen the Donahoe-line story.

Methodist ministers in late-19th-century WV mountain counties were the textbook Scots-Irish-Presbyterian-converted-to-Methodist demographic that Susan's father's family fits. The conversion likely traces back through Hugh Casslin Donaghe's Augusta County Presbyterian network of the 1740s.

## 3. Paul "Dada" Riley Kidd Donahoe — civil servant, organist, named-by-committee

The autobiography (pp. 51–54) gives a full portrait Brady should reference for v2's Paul-Donahoe section.

- **Civil Service career at Cherry Point, North Carolina** before retirement (p. 51). This is **new — places Paul in eastern North Carolina, not just Eastern Shore VA, before his retirement to Danville VA**. Marine Corps Air Station Cherry Point was the Civil Service employer; specific role not stated.
- "He had been named after everyone who was in the room when he was born, and he always said he was glad that there weren't fifty people in there" (p. 51). "Paul Riley Kidd" was a four-name committee tribute. Riley and Kidd are likely surname middle-names from his family or his mother's family.
- **Piano lessons given to Shirley Temple Black's daughter** "for a time on the Eastern Shore" (p. 52). Shirley Temple Black's daughter Linda Susan Agar was born 1948, so this would have been late 1940s / early 1950s on the Eastern Shore — the same window when the Donahoes were still in Onley/Pennyville.
- Beautiful cursive script. Wrote letters in a "long, flowing script, the one men of that generation wrote in." Suggests Paul kept active correspondence — letters from Paul to Susan and Mary Ellen may survive in family papers (worth Brady asking Susan).
- **Organist at First Christian Church, Danville** while attending Mt. Vernon Methodist with Fannye. First Christian Church = Disciples of Christ tradition.
- Death: "**hardening of the arteries**" (atherosclerosis). "Spent a few weeks in Roman Eagle Nursing Home before he died" (p. 54). GEDCOM date: 5 December 1979. Buried Danville National Cemetery. **Veteran-status check at NCA gravelocator.cem.va.gov is the close-out for v2.**
- Last words to Susan in nursing home: "Thank you, Suki" (p. 20). "Suki" was Paul's special nickname for Susan.

## 4. Fannye "Fannie" Bull Donahoe — disciplined caregiver, Bible-reader, stern

- Read her Bible every night (autobiography p. 21). Inspired Susan to start Bible-reading.
- "I'm proud to weigh 225 pounds, and I wished I weighed more" (p. 21).
- Took total care of Uncle Billy (Down Syndrome). Her devotion to Billy "may have made her relationship with my grandfather suffer" (p. 53).
- Member of **Mt. Vernon Methodist Church, Danville**.
- After Paul's December 1979 death, "Mama died a couple of years later after arthritis attacked her, and she basically decided to starve herself to death. A stubborn woman to the end" (p. 54). GEDCOM: 27 June 1982.
- Buried Danville National Cemetery alongside Paul (per GEDCOM).

## 5. Uncle Billy Donahoe — substantial new biographical material

- Born 22 September 1935 Onley, Accomack VA (per GEDCOM @I17@; full name William "Billy" Rollyson Donahoe).
- Down Syndrome.
- **Attended Virginia School for the Deaf and Blind for two years when he was four** (~1939–1941). Learned to read and write there. "It totally enriched his life" (p. 54).
- Lived with parents Paul and Fannye in Danville for decades. After Fannye's June 1982 death, **lived with Mary Ellen and Del Dean for another 13 years**.
- Died 5 October 1995 Danville (per GEDCOM). Cause: Alzheimer's. "He basically starved to death too. But he lived a lot longer and better than the 16 years he was predicted to live" (p. 55).
- Daily routines documented vividly: 12 noon lunch (turkey noodle soup Tuesdays/Thursdays + exactly 11 crackers); 4:30 supper; 6–7 PM bathroom shower; copied cast lists from TV Guides year after year; kept 3x5 cards on professional wrestling.
- Susan and Paul shared Saturday morning wrestling on TV.

## 7. Roy Franklin Dean and Editha "Addie" Read Dean — Del's parents

The autobiography mentions Susan's paternal grandparents far less than the Donahoe side (the bulk of the grandparent material is about the Eastern Shore Donahoes who lived nearby in Danville). What's confirmed:

- The Dean family was Michigan-based (Susan and Paulette were born at Chincoteague but the Deans were Michigan, not Eastern Shore).
- Roy Franklin Dean (b. 1886, d. 1964) and Editha Addie Read (b. 1894, d. 1980).
- Editha lived to see Susan grow up — she was alive until 5 October 1980.

The autobiography may have specific Roy/Editha biographical material in a section not yet pulled. **The Dean-side family memory is genuinely thinner than the Donahoe-side because the Deans were geographically distant from Susan's childhood.**
