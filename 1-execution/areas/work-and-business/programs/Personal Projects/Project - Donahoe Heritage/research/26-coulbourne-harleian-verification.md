# 26 — Coulbourne 1623 Visitation of Somerset (Harleian Vol. XI, p. 26) — Verification

**Compiled:** 2026-05-09
**Stream:** v2 *Virginia Deans and Donahoes* whitepaper, Stream A2
**Status:** Verification of the FamilySearch-cited Wythehill → Braton pedigree against the actual Harleian Society Vol. XI text and against the Maryland immigrant's documented record.
**Verdict:** **The Somerset pedigree itself is real and verifiable on archive.org. The link from that pedigree's "Charles æt. 20, 1623" down to the Maryland immigrant Col. William Coulbourne (b. 1630, d. 1689 Pomfret) is NOT proven by primary records and is actively disputed on parish-register and DNA grounds.**

---

## 1. The Harleian Society Vol. XI, p. 26 entry — verbatim

The Visitation of the County of Somerset in the Year 1623, edited by Frederic Thomas Colby, F.S.A., Harleian Society Publications Vol. XI, London 1876, is digitized on Internet Archive (BYU scan) at:

- https://archive.org/stream/visitationofcoun00byustge/visitationofcoun00byustge_djvu.txt
- https://archive.org/stream/visitationofcoun00stge/visitationofcoun00stge_djvu.txt

The COLBORNE entry on **p. 26** reads (verbatim, OCR with [bracketed clarifications]):

> **Arms:** "Argent, on a chevron sable between three bugle horns or, stringed of the second, three mullets of the third."
>
> **Crest:** "Out of a ducal coronet or, a stag's head argent, attired or."
>
> **Pedigree:**
> William Colborne of Wythehill co. Som. [=]
> John Colborne [=]
> Thomas Colborne [=]
> William Colborne of Braton co. Som. living 1623. = Mary d. of John Topp of Stockton co. Wilts.
> [Children:] William æt. 19. Edward æt. 14. John æt. 12. Charles æt. 20, 1623.

So the visitation says:

| Generation | Person | Detail per visitation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | William Colborne | of Wythehill, co. Somerset |
| 2 | John Colborne | son of (1) |
| 3 | Thomas Colborne | son of (2) |
| 4 | William Colborne | of Braton (likely Bratton Seymour), co. Somerset, **living 1623**, m. Mary, daughter of John Topp of Stockton, co. Wilts |
| 5 | Charles, William, Edward, John | four sons, ages **20, 19, 14, 12** in 1623 — implied births **~1603, 1604, 1609, 1611** |

**The visitation text is genuine and reproducible.** Two independent archive.org scans return the same blazon and the same pedigree structure. This is exactly what the FamilySearch profile cites.

---

## 2. What the visitation does NOT say

The Vol. XI p. 26 entry **stops at the four sons in 1623**. It does not record:

- Any son's adult life, marriages, or migration
- Any move from Braton to Westminster / St Martin-in-the-Fields
- Any descent to a Maryland-bound William
- Any death dates, burial locations, or wills
- Any heraldic continuation through later generations

In particular, the visitation says nothing about Charles Colborne (æt. 20, 1623, implied b. ~1603) ever leaving Somerset. The "Charles → Westminster → father of Maryland William" narrative is **a separate genealogical hypothesis grafted onto the visitation entry**, not a continuation of it.

---

## 3. Testing the bridge — Charles (1603 Somerset?) ↔ Charles Coulbourne (1603 Westminster, father of Md. William)

This is the load-bearing link. The Maryland-immigrant tradition on FamilySearch says:

> Charles (æt. 20 in 1623 per the visitation) = Charles Coulbourne christened 1603 at St Martin-in-the-Fields, Westminster = father of Col. William Coulbourne christened 19 January 1630 St Martin-in-the-Fields = the 1689 Pomfret Plantation Maryland immigrant.

**Evidence supporting the bridge:** none located. The 1623 visitation gives no Westminster connection. No parish-register transcription tying the Somerset Charles (b. ~1603) to a Westminster move has been surfaced in primary-source aggregations (FamilySearch, Forebears, GENUKI, archive.org register transcripts). St Martin-in-the-Fields baptism registers for 1603 and for 1630 are extant and digitized (London Archives + Ancestry.co.uk + FamilySearch microfilm 132900), but no published abstract has been located that confirms (a) a Charles Colborne baptism in 1603 St M-i-t-F and (b) a William Colborne / Coulbourne baptism on 19 January 1630 there. The "1603 Westminster" and "1630 Westminster" christening claims on FamilySearch are repeated from Clayton Torrence's *Old Somerset on the Eastern Shore of Maryland* (1935), which itself is **secondary speculation, not a transcription of the parish register**.

**Evidence against the bridge — the Pontefract / Yorkshire counter-hypothesis:**

WikiTree's Coulbourne-8 profile (William Coulbourne abt.1620–abt.1689) **disputes** the Somerset link and proposes Yorkshire instead:

| Counter-evidence | Source |
|---|---|
| Birth-date mismatch: Md. immigrant William's accepted birth circa 1620, not 1604 (the Visitation's William æt. 19 in 1623) | WikiTree Coulbourne-8 |
| Parish Register of Pontefract, Yorkshire (1585–1641) shows a William Coulbourne, son of Roger, baptized 2 February 1620 at Pontefract | WikiTree Coulbourne-8 |
| He named his Maryland plantation **"Pomfret"** — the historical / colloquial name of **Pontefract**, Yorkshire | Multiple genealogies; Pomfret Plantation MD historic-site file |
| Y-DNA of his direct male descendants: haplotype I-A18256, consistent with Scandinavian / Norse (Danelaw) origin — Yorkshire is Danelaw, Somerset is not | Coulbourne DNA project, cited via WikiTree |
| Colburn village exists in Yorkshire (within the Danelaw zone) | WikiTree |

The "Old Home Prize Essay" on William Coulbourn (oursussexroots.com), which is a contemporary Somerset-Maryland local-history piece, **explicitly says the Somerset England origin is unconfirmed**:

> "It is speculated that William Coulbourn was born on January 19, 1629/30, in London, England. However, this is unconfirmed." […] "Some speculated, such as Clayton Torrence, after some research, that Coulbourn came from Somersetshire in England."

That essay cites **no primary record** for the English origin. Wikipedia's *Pomfret Plantation* article also says nothing about the English origin or naming etymology — it simply starts with "William Coulbourne in 1663" on the Maryland side.

## 5. Honest verdict for the v2 whitepaper

**On the visitation pedigree itself (England side):**
The Harleian Society Vol. XI, p. 26 Colborne pedigree is real, the volume is real, and the chain Wythehill → John → Thomas → William of Braton (m. Mary Topp) → four sons in 1623 is **internal to a heralds' visitation** — which means it was sworn-to by the head of the family at the time and recorded by the College of Arms herald as a formal pedigree, but it carries no independent parish-register or will citations on the page itself. Visitation pedigrees are **secondary contemporary evidence**: stronger than later-genealogist tradition, weaker than primary parish records. As a piece of English Colborne family history c. 1450–1623, it stands.

**On the link to the Maryland immigrant:**
**Not proven, and now arguably disproven.** The bridge from "Charles Colborne æt. 20, Somerset, 1623" to "Charles Coulbourne, Westminster, 1603, father of Md. William 1630" rests entirely on Clayton Torrence's 1935 *Old Somerset on the Eastern Shore of Maryland* speculation, and is contradicted by:

1. The Pontefract parish register entry for William, son of Roger, bap. 2 Feb 1620 — which **is a primary record**, unlike the Westminster christenings, which appear nowhere in transcribed registers I could surface.
2. The Maryland immigrant's plantation name, "Pomfret" — historically a Yorkshire toponym for Pontefract.
3. Y-DNA haplotype I-A18256 in his direct male descendants, consistent with Norse-Danelaw origin (Yorkshire) rather than Somerset.
4. The contemporary Maryland local-history essay's open admission that the Somerset origin is unconfirmed speculation.

**Recommendation for v2:** Treat the 1623 Visitation of Somerset Colborne pedigree as a **published English antiquarian artifact** that has historically been associated with the Maryland immigrant by tradition (Torrence 1935 onward), but that **cannot bear weight as Brady's verified ancestry**. The Md. immigrant's English origin is genuinely undetermined — Yorkshire/Pontefract is the better-supported candidate today, with the Somerset Wythehill claim rated weak. The whitepaper should:

- Reproduce the visitation entry as historical context, not as ancestry
- State that Col. William Coulbourne's English origin is **unverified**, with two competing hypotheses (Somerset / Yorkshire) and stronger circumstantial support for Yorkshire
- Anchor the verified line in **Maryland primary records from 1652 onward**, where Col. William Coulbourne is solidly documented
