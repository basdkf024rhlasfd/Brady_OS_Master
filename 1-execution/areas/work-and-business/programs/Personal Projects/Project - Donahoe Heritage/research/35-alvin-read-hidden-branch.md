# 35 — Alvin Read: The Hidden Branch (Stream A11)

**Compiled:** 2026-05-09
**Stream:** A11 — v2 *Virginia Deans and Donahoes* whitepaper research
**Question:** Who was Alvin? — Editha Read's son born out of wedlock before she married Roy Franklin Dean, kept secret from himself by his own family until after Editha's death in 1980.
**Source seed:** Mary Ellen Donahoe Dean oral history, 3 May 2020 (`research/24-mary-ellen-dean-oral-history-2020-05-03.md`)
**Sensitivity:** This is a private family thread. Alvin's existence was deliberately concealed. Brady is researching it for honest family history. Treat with the discretion the family extended for most of a century.

---

## 1. The premise, restated cleanly

Per Mary Ellen's oral history:

- **Editha Read** (b. 1894, d. 1980) had a son named **Alvin** out of wedlock, before her marriage to **Roy Franklin Dean**.
- As a result, Editha's parents — **Albert Read** and **Addie Read** — excluded Editha from their inheritance entirely.
- Editha later married Roy Dean and had at least one son with him: **Delbert Allen Dean** ("Pop") (1930–1996), Brady's maternal grandfather.
- **Alvin did not learn of his illegitimacy or the inheritance exclusion until after Editha's death in 1980.** He was, in Mary Ellen's words, "very upset."
- Alvin therefore grew up not knowing what every other adult in the room appears to have known. The family kept his origin from him as a sustained, multi-decade discretion.

This makes Alvin biologically the half-brother of Brady's grandfather Del Dean, and therefore Brady's half-great-uncle on the maternal Dean side.

## 2. What this stream confirmed about the family around Alvin

Even though Alvin himself does not yet appear in the public web record, Stream A11 closed several pieces of the family scaffolding around him. These confirmations matter because they fix the real-world geography and surnames in which Alvin would or would not appear — and they validate the oral history at a level of detail it had not yet been validated at.

### 2.1 The Read farm is a real, locatable, named property

The "Read farm on Rushton Road" of Mary Ellen's oral history is **10084 Rushton Rd, Rushton, Michigan** (Salem Township, on the Washtenaw / Oakland / Livingston tri-county boundary; mailing address South Lyon, MI 48178). It is on the **National Register of Historic Places** (listed 1972) under the name **Alonzo W. Olds House**, and is also referred to in local sources as the **Albert Read House**.

The chain of title given by the Wikipedia entry on the property:

| Owner | Years | Note |
|-------|-------|------|
| Alonzo W. Olds | 1835–1851 | Built the Greek Revival main house 1848–49 |
| Warren Clark | 1851–1863 | Farmed |
| **William Read** | 1863–1888 | First Read owner; died 1888 |
| **Mary Read** | 1888–1900 | William's widow; died 1900 |
| **Albert Read** | 1900–c.1940 | William and Mary's son; **this is Editha's father** |
| **Albert Read Jr. ("Allie")** | c.1940–1975 | **Editha's brother** in the oral history; died 1975 |
| **Harriet (Driver) Read** | 1975–1992 | Allie's widow; lived on the property until her death |

The match to Mary Ellen's oral history is exact at every layer: a "Read farm on Rushton Rd"; an Albert Read who is the patriarch; a brother "Allie" who inherited and continued operating it as a dairy. The 1972 National Register listing also attests to the family's continuous tenure on the land.

This is the first time in v1 or v2 that the Dean / Read side has had a named, locatable, registered property anchor. The Donahoe side has the lost Bull farm; the Dean side now has the still-standing Read farm. They are different in tone — the Bull farm is a loss story, the Read farm is a continuity story interrupted by an inheritance fracture — but each is now physically locatable.

### 2.2 Editha's brother and his three children are real and confirmed

Mary Ellen named Allie's children as **Loren, Lois, and Leslie Read** — Pop's first cousins. All three are now confirmed via funeral home obituaries and matching parents:

- **Loren Albert Read** — b. 2 August 1928, d. 3 February 2009. Phillips Funeral Home, South Lyon, MI.
- **Lois A. Smith** (née Read) — b. 28 February 1932, d. 30 March 2024. Phillips Funeral Home, South Lyon, MI.
- **Leslie M. Read** — b. 13 May 1934 in South Lyon, MI; d. 27 August 2021 in Comins, MI. Green Funeral Home obituary, married Lois (Pichler) Read 28 May 1954, two children (Karl Read of Sparta MI, Karen Read of Bad Axe MI). His obituary explicitly names parents **"the late Albert and Harriet (Driver) Read"** of South Lyon and a sister Lois Smith of South Lyon and a brother Loren (deceased).

Three live signals from this:

1. The maternal surname **Driver** is now in evidence for Allie's wife Harriet — Mary Ellen's oral history had only "Harriet."
2. Leslie Read's later move to **Comins, Michigan** (Oscoda County, north-central Lower Peninsula) confirms the oral history's note that "a couple of Allie's children moved" — though to northern Michigan, not Florida as Mary Ellen recalled. The southward Florida move may belong to a different cousin or be an oral-history drift.
3. None of the three obituaries that surfaced (Leslie's full text; Loren's name and dates; Lois's name and dates) reference an Aunt Editha, an Uncle Roy Dean, or a cousin named Alvin Read. **Editha is unmentioned in the in-laws' obituaries**, which is consistent with — though not proof of — the inheritance rift Mary Ellen described.

### 2.3 Editha's parents Albert and Addie Read

The oral history names them. The Wikipedia / National Register chain of title independently corroborates **Albert Read** as the farm's owner from 1900 (when his mother Mary Read died) until his own death (described in the Wikipedia article as occurring "1939 or 1940"). His wife is not named in the Wikipedia entry, but the oral history's "Addie" plus a 1900 succession pattern (Albert taking the farm at age old enough to operate it; he would have been at least in his late 20s, given his daughter Editha was born 1894) means Albert and Addie Read had an active household at Rushton Road across the entire 1900–1940 period — exactly the window in which a child Alvin would have been born to their daughter Editha.

The "Addie" name in the oral history is most likely a familiar shortening of **Adeline** or **Adelia** or **Adelaide** — these are the standard 19th-century given names that contracted to "Addie." None of these are confirmed yet for Editha's mother specifically; the oral history is the only source naming her at all.

### 2.4 Editha and Roy Dean — still incompletely documented

Despite multiple search angles, Stream A11 did **not** find:

- A Find-a-Grave memorial for Editha Read Dean 1894–1980 in Michigan.
- An obituary for Editha Read Dean in South Lyon, Detroit area, or any Michigan obituary collection accessible by general web search.
- A marriage record for Roy Dean and Editha Read in Michigan 1920s.
- An obituary for Roy Franklin Dean in South Lyon area.

This silence is not surprising for a 1980 death in a small town — many such records are only behind paid genealogy databases (Ancestry, Newspapers.com) or in physical archives at Lyon Township Public Library, Salem-South Lyon District Library, or the Genealogical Society of Washtenaw County. They are findable, but not via the open web.

It is, however, the silence on Editha's side that becomes meaningful when we turn to Alvin himself.

## 3. The search for Alvin — what came back, and what didn't

### 3.1 No Alvin Read of the right profile surfaced on the open web

The most direct candidate name — **Alvin Read**, born Michigan c. 1910–1920, mother Editha Read — does not appear in any web-accessible obituary index, Find-a-Grave Michigan listing, or publicly indexed family tree that I could reach in this stream. The closest accidental-name match was an Alvin L. Reid (1915–1955) buried at Mears Cemetery, Oceana County, MI — but his name spells Reid, his location is on the western Lower Peninsula far from Salem Township, and his vital dates and parents do not fit. He is not our Alvin.

### 3.2 No Alvin Dean of the right profile surfaced either

A natural alternative — that Roy Dean adopted Alvin and gave him the Dean surname when he married Editha — would predict an **Alvin Dean** in the Salem / South Lyon area, eventually with relations to Del Dean. No such record surfaced via open web search.

### 3.3 No Alvin under any other plausible Read-family-adjacent surname

Checked variants — Alvin Reed, Alvin Reid, Alvin in connection with Driver (Harriet's family), Alvin in connection with the Olds family who had farmed before the Reads — no fitting match.

### 3.4 What this silence likely means

Several things, and they are not mutually exclusive:

1. **He genuinely existed but is buried in a paid-database record set.** Michigan vital records 1910–1925 (the most likely birth window) and 1990s–2010s obituaries (the most likely death window, since Alvin would now be in his late 90s if alive, or already dead) sit primarily in Ancestry.com, FamilySearch's authenticated collections, Newspapers.com, the Library of Michigan vital records, and Oakland County / Washtenaw County / Livingston County offices. Open web indexing is thin for births before 1915 (where Michigan only had a partial state-level registry) and for ordinary obituaries pre-2000.

2. **He may have been raised under a different surname.** This is the scenario that most directly fits Mary Ellen's oral history. If Editha's parents excluded her from the inheritance because she had Alvin, but Editha kept Alvin and raised him alongside her later Dean children, he might appear in census records as Alvin Read, Alvin Dean (taking his stepfather's surname after Editha's marriage), or as Alvin under whatever surname Editha and Roy Dean's household legally settled on. The oral history tells us he didn't know he was illegitimate — which means he grew up using a surname that *felt* like his real surname. That is most likely **Dean** if Roy formally or informally claimed him, or possibly **Read** if Editha kept him as a Read at the farm.

3. **He may have been raised by someone else.** A 1910s–1920s rural Michigan response to an out-of-wedlock grandchild was sometimes for the maternal grandparents to raise the child as their own — calling Alvin a late "son" of Albert and Addie rather than a grandson by Editha. In that scenario, Alvin would appear in census records as an Albert Read household child without being marked as anyone's grandson. The 1910 and 1920 censuses would show a much-younger child in Albert and Addie's household than their other children, which is the standard signature of this kind of arrangement. (Mary Ellen's account that Alvin only learned the truth after Editha's 1980 death is at least compatible with this scenario — if "Read" had been his lived surname from infancy, he might not have had reason to question parentage.) But it sits oddly with the inheritance exclusion: if Albert and Addie had effectively raised Alvin as their own son, why exclude Editha and not Alvin from the will? Possible answers: they did exclude Alvin too; or the exclusion happened in Editha's name only because Alvin was already being supported as a household member; or the inheritance rupture is a 1920s-era family story that was about something narrower than the eventual will. The oral history does not resolve this.

4. **He may have been formally adopted out.** Less likely given that Alvin was alive and in contact with the family at Editha's 1980 death and learned the truth then — adoption usually severed contact in early-20th-century Michigan. But not impossible if it was an informal in-family adoption.

5. **He may have predeceased Editha.** Mary Ellen's account places Alvin alive at Editha's 1980 death, learning the truth then, and being upset. So this is ruled out for that date — but it leaves open whether Alvin lived another year, another decade, or another forty years afterward. If he was born ~1912–1918, he would be ~62–68 at Editha's death, and would die between, roughly, 1985 and 2010 in the most likely actuarial window. That puts his obituary in the era when Michigan small-town obituaries were spottily indexed online.

The cleanest read of the open-web silence is: **Alvin existed; his existence was protected within the family's day-to-day surface to such a degree that whatever surname he carried, he carried it without flagging the anomaly to the wider community.** That protection pattern — give the child a surname, raise him as belonging to that surname, suppress the origin story — is exactly the pattern that produces the "did not learn until after Editha's death" structure of the oral history.

## 4. Where the documentary record almost certainly does sit

This stream's purpose is not to wave hands at "more research needed" but to point precisely at where the answer lives. The closest paths to closure:

### 4.1 Michigan birth records 1910–1920 for Editha Read as mother

The **Library of Michigan** maintains the statewide birth registry. Michigan's 1905 vital-records modernization meant that by 1910, in-county births were generally recorded with both parents named (where known). A birth in Salem Township or Lyon Township for a mother **Editha Read** (without a father named, or with a father named but no marriage on record) would be the cleanest single document that confirms Alvin's existence and birth date. This requires either a Library of Michigan request (mail or in-person, fee-based), an Ancestry/FamilySearch authenticated session, or a county clerk request to **Washtenaw**, **Oakland**, or **Livingston** county clerk's offices.

### 4.2 Census records 1920, 1930, 1940, 1950 for Albert + Addie Read household at Rushton Rd

Each decennial census for Albert Read's household at the Rushton farm would list everyone resident. If Alvin was raised by Albert and Addie in their household, he appears as a "son" or "grandson" in 1920, 1930, 1940. The 1950 census (the most recent one publicly released) would show a 30-something Alvin, and would tell us whether he stayed in the area into adulthood or left. **This is the single highest-yield search** because it requires only Ancestry / FamilySearch open-database access with the Rushton Rd / Salem Twp filter, and the household composition is unambiguous.

### 4.3 Oakland County probate file for Editha Read Dean (1980)

Editha's probate file would name her heirs. If Alvin appears as an heir, that's confirmation he was acknowledged by Editha at her death (which fits Mary Ellen's account that he learned the truth then). Alternatively, his absence from the file would tell us whether the family withheld his name from the formal probate record too. Oakland County Circuit Court Probate Division holds these.

### 4.4 Oakland County or Washtenaw County probate files for Albert Read (d. 1939–40) and any subsequent will of Albert Read Jr.

The original "exclusion from inheritance" — the act that the oral history says caused the rift — would be visible in **Albert Read's will** (probated c. 1940). Was Editha simply not named, or was she explicitly disinherited with cause cited? Wills of that era sometimes contained the cause language. This document would be at Washtenaw County Probate (since the farm is in Salem Twp, Washtenaw side of the line) or Oakland County Probate (depending on Albert's residence at death).

### 4.5 South Lyon Cemetery and Salem-area cemetery surveys

Find-a-Grave lists 5,122 memorials at South Lyon Cemetery; the Read family farm tradition makes that cemetery the most likely burial place for Albert Sr., Addie, Albert Jr. ("Allie"), Harriet Driver Read, and possibly Editha and Roy Dean. **A direct walk of South Lyon Cemetery, Olds Cemetery (Salem Township), and the Methodist-affiliated cemetery in the area** would surface marked Read graves and could surface a marked Alvin Read grave if he is buried among them. (This is the kind of work the local genealogical society or a hired Find-a-Grave volunteer can complete in a single visit.)

### 4.6 Lyon Township Public Library local family histories

The Lyon Township Public Library is a Family History Center affiliate with scrapbooks of local birth, marriage, and death records and family histories. **A Read family file or scrapbook would almost certainly be there**, given the National-Register status of the family's farm. This is a librarian-mediated search; phone or email inquiry suffices.

### 4.7 Mary Ellen Donahoe Dean and Paulette Dean themselves

Mary Ellen and Paulette are still the strongest sources. A second-pass conversation specifically about Alvin — not as a side anecdote but as the focal question — would likely surface:

- Alvin's full name and surname-as-used.
- Approximate birth year.
- Whether Alvin had children (the most consequential question, because Alvin's children would be Brady's living half-second-cousins).
- Where Alvin lived (the same Salem / South Lyon corridor? Detroit? Out of state?).
- When Alvin died, if known.
- Who at the family knows him.

The oral history is from 2020 and is rich on the inheritance-rift dimension but thin on Alvin's adult life. A targeted ask to Mary Ellen or Paulette, framed gently, would close this in one conversation rather than fifty hours of archive work.

## 5. What I am willing to claim, and what I am not

### Claims supported by this stream

1. The Read farm on Rushton Road of the oral history is the **Albert Read House at 10084 Rushton Rd, Rushton, MI** — National Register listed since 1972 — and the family chain of title from William Read (1863) → Mary Read → Albert Read → Albert Read Jr. → Harriet Driver Read is documented.
2. The first-cousin generation Mary Ellen named — Loren Albert Read, Lois (Read) Smith, Leslie M. Read — are all confirmed by funeral home obituaries with parents Albert and Harriet (Driver) Read of South Lyon, MI.
3. Harriet's maiden surname is **Driver** (a detail not in the oral history but recovered from Leslie Read's 2021 obituary).
4. Editha Read Dean is **not** mentioned in any of her in-laws' findable obituaries — consistent with the inheritance rupture Mary Ellen described, though by itself not proof of it.
5. Albert Read Sr. died **1939 or 1940** per the National Register history — meaning the inheritance event of the oral history is most likely the probate of his will c. 1940.
6. **Alvin's existence is supported only by the oral history at this time.** No web-accessible vital record, census record, obituary, Find-a-Grave memorial, or family tree posting that I could surface in this stream names a child of Editha Read born out of wedlock in Michigan c. 1910–1920.

### Claims I am not making

1. I am not asserting Alvin's full name, exact birth date, birthplace, marital status, descendants, or burial place. The oral history names him as "Alvin"; no documentary record I reached confirms even the first name beyond Mary Ellen's testimony.
2. I am not asserting which surname he carried in life — Read, Dean, or another. Each has prior plausibility; none has documentary support yet.
3. I am not asserting that Alvin had no descendants. **If he did, those descendants are Brady's living biological half-second-cousins** — which is a meaningful relational fact the family deserves to know honestly. But the question is open in this stream.
4. I am not asserting that Alvin is dead. The actuarial window suggests he most likely died between 1985 and 2010, but that is statistical, not documentary.

### What this stream does establish about the *story* even without Alvin's name

Even with Alvin's specific identity unrecovered, the oral history's framing has been validated by Stream A11 to a degree it had not previously been:

- The **physical setting** is real and registered (the farm).
- The **named people on the family side** are real and confirmed by independent obituary records (Albert Sr., Albert Jr., Harriet, Loren, Lois, Leslie).
- The **timing** of the inheritance event lines up with a c. 1940 probate (Albert Sr.'s death).
- The **silence around Editha** in the in-laws' findable obituaries is consistent with the rupture Mary Ellen described.

Mary Ellen's oral history therefore continues to score Credibility 5 for the structural facts and Credibility 4 for Alvin specifically (one source, no documentary corroboration yet, but with the surrounding scaffolding matching independently).

## 7. A note on tone for the whitepaper

When this thread reaches the v2 *Virginia Deans and Donahoes* whitepaper, I would handle it this way:

- Name Alvin in a single dedicated sub-section, not in a list of branches alongside Pop and the cousins. The structural fact — that Editha had a son before her marriage to Roy Dean, that her parents excluded her from their inheritance because of him, and that Alvin himself did not learn the story until after his mother's death — is the human heart of the Dean / Read side of this family. It deserves a paragraph that handles it with care rather than three lines in a bulleted family map.
- Do not publish details (full name, residence, descendants) that the family has historically kept private, until you have confirmed those details with Paulette and Mary Ellen and asked their consent. The oral history was given to Brady as family history; it was not given as material for a public document. This is consistent with the project-level note on Alvin's privacy in the Phase 0 instructions for this stream.
- Where the record is silent, say so plainly. "Alvin's full name, dates, and adult life are not yet documented at this time" is a defensible sentence in a family whitepaper. It is more honest than an inferential narrative built from the oral history alone.
- Treat the Read farm as the geographic anchor of this side of the family, the same way the lost Bull farm anchors the Donahoe side. Both are real places; both are central to the family's self-understanding; one is still standing and one is gone. That symmetry is itself part of the story.

## 10. Conclusion

Alvin is real in the family's memory and is structurally consistent with everything else this stream confirmed about the Dean / Read household at Rushton Road. He is also genuinely hidden in the documentary record I could reach — appropriately so, given that he was hidden from himself. The path to him runs first through Paulette and Susan, and second through Michigan probate and census images. He is not a mystery so much as a private fact that the family kept private, including from him, for sixty-some years. The kindness in this research is to find him without breaking the discretion that defined his place in the family — which means asking the family first, and only then opening the public archives.

— End of Stream A11.
