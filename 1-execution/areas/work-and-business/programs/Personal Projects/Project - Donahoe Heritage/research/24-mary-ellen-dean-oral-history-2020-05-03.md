## 1. The inheritance exclusion — Editha and Alvin

Editha Read had Alvin out of wedlock. As a result, her parents Albert and Addie excluded her from their inheritance entirely. **Alvin did not learn of this until after Editha's death, at which point he was very upset.** The family had kept the circumstances secret from him throughout his life.

Despite being cut out of the inheritance, Editha and her brother Allie maintained a cordial relationship. The inheritance rift generated hard feelings between Pop's siblings and Allie's children (Loren, Lois, Leslie) — the first-cousin generation — but did not fully sever the Editha-Allie connection.

---

## 2. The Read farm on Rushton Road

Allie (Albert Jr. Read) was a dairy farmer and lived on the **Read farm on Rushton Rd**. This is the family's anchor property. Mom and Pop visited the Read farm in adult life on occasion for family reunions, despite the underlying inheritance tensions between branches.

After some point (timing unspecified), a couple of Allie's children moved to Florida following the sale of the Read farm. The specific sale date and circumstances are not recorded in this oral history.

---

## 3. Family dynamics and relationships

- Allie "liked Mom (Mary Ellen) very much for some reason" — Mary Ellen's own characterization, with some bemusement at the specificity of his regard.
- The harsh feelings about the inheritance ran between **Pop's siblings and Allie's children**, not primarily between Editha and Allie themselves. The sibling relationship appears to have held.
- The family-reunion gatherings at the Read farm created a social context that bridged the rift partially.

## 4. Geographic / institutional context

The Michigan reference is confirmed by other research (Susan Elzey's autobiography notes Del was on Army recruiting duty in **Jackson City, Michigan** when Susan was born in 1953). The Read farm on Rushton Rd and the University of Michigan arrowhead assessment (see section 5) reinforce a Michigan-based Dean / Read family geography.

---

## 5. The arrowhead collection — Roy Dean's legacy

**Roy Dean collected arrowheads over the years from the Read farm.** The family context suggests he gathered them across decades of visits to the farm, as no one else showed interest. When Pop (Delbert) held onto them, it was because he was the only one who cared to keep them.

At some point the collection was taken to the **University of Michigan** and assessed. The assessment reportedly identified **one arrowhead as very valuable** — but the family no longer knows which one it is.

The arrowheads are currently in the possession of the **Smallwood children** (Brady's generation).

**Research actions this opens:**
- U of M archaeology / anthropology department may have assessment records if it was a formal evaluation; the date is unknown but likely mid-20th-century.
- The Read farm's location on Rushton Rd (Michigan) is a search thread: Great Lakes–area arrowheads collected on a working farm are typically Woodland-period or Archaic-period indigenous material from the region. This is a separate indigenous heritage thread from the Monacan / Algonquian research for the Donahoe and Clark lines.
- Determining the current whereabouts and condition of the collection; any surviving assessment documentation.
