## Headline finding

**Two women, not one.** The Mary "Molly" Kimburrow (FSID LRKY-41Q) on Brady's FamilySearch tree appears to be a conflated profile combining at least two distinct historical women:

1. **Mary Dorcas Kimbrough Wilkins** (christened 30 Jan 1703/4 at St. Peter's Parish, New Kent VA; died 1747; married a Wilkins; 6 documented children, none named Woodrum)
2. **Mary "Molly" Bays/Baze Woodrum** (married John Woodrum 15 Nov 1761 at St. James Northam Parish, Goochland VA; bore John 7+ children 1764–1774; died 4 Nov 1775)

The 1703 christening record is a **REAL primary source** — but it's the wrong woman. The 1761 marriage and 1764–1774 baptism records are also REAL primary sources — but they prove a different identity (Bays/Baze, not Kimburrow). These two record sets have been welded together on FamilySearch with no evidentiary bridge, then layered with Native-ancestry tradition that cannot be locked to either set.

**Practical consequence for the whitepaper:** the Native-ancestry claim is salvageable but must be substantially rewritten. The Bible-record tradition (Mowhemencho / Monacan) attaches to the Bays/Baze ancestor, not to the Kimbrough Wilkins ancestor. The 1703 St. Peter's Parish christening should be removed from the Native-ancestry citation chain entirely — it documents the wrong person.

## 1. The Douglas Register entry — verbatim text from the 1928 publication

Source: William Mac. Jones (transcriber/editor), *The Douglas Register, being a detailed record of births, marriages and deaths together with other interesting notes, as kept by the Rev. William Douglas, from 1750 to 1797. An index of Goochland wills. Notes on the French Huguenot refugees who lived in Manakin-town* (Richmond, VA: J.W. Fergusson & Sons, 1928), 412 pp. Digitized at Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/douglasregisterb00doug.

**Important publication context (from the Foreword):** Douglas was the minister at St. James Northam Parish, Goochland County, from 12 October 1750 until February 1798. He began keeping records 15 September 1753 and procured the manuscript book in 1756. Many marriages from before 1756 (and births back to 1705) were inserted retroactively from parents' verbal records. The original manuscript was held in 1892 by Robert W. Lewis (great-grandson of Douglas's daughter Peggy Douglass Merriwether). The current location of the original manuscript was not confirmed in this research — Brady's project should query the Library of Virginia (LVA call number FS Library Book 975.5455 V2d 1977).

## 2. The St. Peter's Parish christening — wrong woman

Source: *The Parish Register of Saint Peter's, New Kent County, Virginia from 1680 to 1787*, transcribed and published 1904 by the National Society of the Colonial Dames of America in the State of Virginia (Richmond: Wm. Ellis Jones), facsimile reprint 1988 by Heritage Books, Inc. Digitized at Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/parishregisterof00stpe.

### The christening entry verbatim:

> "Mary dau' of Jn° Kimburrow Ju' ye 30 Jany. 170[2/3]."

(Adjacent entries: "John Kimburrow son of Jn° Kimborow Jir bapt. 21 Dec, 1701" and "Major son of John Kimburrow baptised 29, 170[2/3]" — these are her brothers John III and Major. Note: in the period the year often switched at March 25, so "30 January 1702/3" Old Style = "30 January 1703" New Style, and FamilySearch's "1703" date is correct under New Style.)

### Who was this Mary Kimburrow?

Per WikiTree profile **Kimbrough-164** (John Kimbrough II, ~1661–1743), the daughter is fully known as:

- **Mary Dorcas Kimbrough Wilkins** (born 30 Jan 1703/4 New Kent VA; died **1747** — 28 years before "Mary Bays Woodrum" died)
- Married a Wilkins
- Children: Anna Catherine, John, Thomas, William, Richard, Susannah (Wilkins) Stovall — **none named Woodrum, none born in St. James Northam Parish Goochland**

### Her siblings (also baptized at St. Peter's, all listed in the Vestry Book of St. Peter's Parish):

- Rebecka (b. 10 Aug 1699)
- Bulkley (b. 17 Nov 1699)
- John III (bapt. 21 Dec 1701)
- Major (bapt. 2 Jan 1703/4)
- Mary Dorcas (b. 30 Jan 1703/4) — **the woman in the christening record**
- Thomas Henry (b. 1707)
- Marmaduke Sr. (b. 1710)
- Bradley C. (b. 1715)
- Margaret (b. 1715, m. Harris)

### Why this is the wrong woman for Brady's tree

Mary Dorcas Kimbrough Wilkins lived 1703/4–1747 and married a Wilkins. The Mary Baze who married John Woodrum in 1761 had her **first** child in 1764, suggesting she was likely born in the 1730s–1740s (women bearing first children in their 20s was the norm). If Mary Baze were born in 1703, she'd have been **61 years old at the birth of her first recorded child** — biologically improbable, and inconsistent with bearing 7 children over the next decade.

The 1703 christening date attached to the Mary Kimburrow / Bays Woodrum profile on FamilySearch (LRKY-41Q) is **the wrong birth year for the right woman**. The correct birth year for Mary Bays who married John Woodrum 1761 is more plausibly ~1735–1745 — but this birth has not been documented in any primary source recovered in this research run.

The Mary Kimburrow / Wilkins of 1703/4 is a real ancestor of *some* Kimbrough lineage, but she is NOT the woman who married John Woodrum, NOT the Native ancestor in the Bible record, and NOT Brady's 7th great-grandmother through this line.

## 3. Mowhemencho Village — what's actually documented

Three independent primary and secondary sources confirm the historical existence and fate of Mowhemencho:

### Source A: Captain John Smith, Map of Virginia (1612)

Mowhemencho appears as one of five Monacan towns mapped by John Smith in the Piedmont upstream of the Fall Line of the James River. The other four were Monahassanugh, Rassawek, Monassukapanough (Monasukapanough), and Massinacack. Mowhemencho is on the south bank of the James River in extreme eastern Powhatan County, between Bernards Creek (east) and Jones Creek (west). Sources: Encyclopedia Virginia "Smith Map" entry; Library of Virginia *Document Bank of Virginia* item #53; National Park Service "John Smith's Map of Virginia: A Closer Look"; Monacan Indian Nation "Step Back in Time" timeline.

### Source B: Virginia Department of Historic Resources Marker O-3 (erected 2008)

Verbatim inscription:

> **Mowhemcho-Manakin Town**
>
> Near here stood the Monacan Indian town of Mowhemcho, noted on John Smith's map of 1612. The Monacan homeland encompassed much of Virginia's Piedmont. **In 1670, Indian townspeople welcomed explorer John Lederer's party with celebratory "volleys of shot". Colonists destroyed the town and most of the Indian people were killed.** French Huguenots settled here in 1700, and Monacan survivors visited them at the site, then called Manakin Town, to trade. By 1722, the Monacan had moved west, along the James River. Today, the Monacan Indian headquarters is just north of the James, near Lynchburg.

Location: 37°33.942'N, 77°42.535'W, on Huguenot Trail 0.2 miles east of Manakin Town Ferry Road (Powhatan County, VA). Source: https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=21142.

### Source C: Monacan Indian Nation "Step Back in Time" timeline (drawing on Helen Rountree's *Pocahontas's People: The Powhatan Indians of Virginia Through Four Centuries* (1990, University of Oklahoma Press))

Key details:
- 1608: John Smith records Monacan towns on his map
- 1630: Five towns located on the James and Rivanna rivers (Mowhemenchouch, Massinacock, Rassawek, Monahassanaugh, Monasukapanough)
- 1670: John Lederer's expedition welcomed at "Mowhemencho, or Manakin" with "Volleys of Shot"
- 1670: Colonists destroyed the town shortly after Lederer's visit — "possibly by trader William Claiborne, and most of the Indians were killed"
- 1700: French Huguenots settled at the abandoned site, renamed it Manakin Town
- 1702: Francis Louis Michel reported Indians visiting "Manigkinton" bringing game and corn; "the Indians who were not present at the time of the attack still camped nearby in the summer"
- 1702 contemporary observation (Michel): "Monacans lived in the Manakin town until, having inflicted injury of Christian colonists, Col. Born all but destroyed them and their settlement"
- 1722: Monacan had moved west along the James River
- 1729: Documentation that "all Virginia Indians except the Pamunkeys had left the state"
- 1730: Spotsylvania court records describe "some Saponis living on Spotswood's land near Fox Neck" — remnant population persisting

Source: https://www.monacannation.gov/step-back-in-time.html (read 2026-05-08)

### How this lines up with the Bible-record claim

The Bible note as transcribed by Marjorie Fluor Moore (27 July 1983) says, of Mary "Molly" Bays/Baze: "she was an Indian. All the males in her tribe (Monacan) had been killed by the settlers. The Indian Village was called 'Mowhemencho.' They occupied 10,000 acres of land close to present day Richmond on the south side of the James River in Virginia."

**Geographically accurate.** Mowhemencho is on the south side of the James, near present-day Richmond (Powhatan County is immediately west of Richmond), and 10,000 acres for a major Monacan town is in the right order of magnitude for a Piedmont village's traditional territory.

**Chronologically plausible-but-strained.** If Mary Bays was born ~1735–1745 (per the marriage and child-bearing window), she was born **65–75 years after the destruction of Mowhemencho in ~1670** and **35–45 years after the Huguenots took the site in 1700**. Her Monacan ancestry, if real, would have to come through a **survivor woman** (since the Bible note explicitly says all males were killed) who either remained in the area or was absorbed into the colonial population. The 1702 Michel observation that survivors "still camped nearby in the summer" supports the existence of remnant Monacan in the area; the 1730 Saponi-near-Fox-Neck record shows scattered individuals persisting.

**One specific chronological problem with the Bible's wording:** "all the males in her tribe had been killed by the settlers" reads as if happening in Mary's lifetime ("her tribe"). But the documented destruction was 60+ years before her birth. The most generous reading is that the Bible records *family memory* of an event that traumatized Mary's grandmother or great-grandmother — events told as "her tribe's story" because Native identity passed matrilineally through that lineage. This is consistent with how Bible-record genealogy normally compresses generational memory.

**This is not invented folklore.** It is grounded in documented colonial-Native history. But it is a family-tradition claim that survives in oral memory and a 19th-century Bible — not in any 18th-century court, parish, or land record naming Mary Bays as Indian.

## 6. The "9614 Miller Roll" claim — fraud-pattern verification

### What the user `zettaBaker` claimed (FamilySearch note, 28 June 2025)

> "Mary Molly Malden Ellen Maiden Baze was from the Monakan Tribe but she Cpt Cherokee on the miller roll. 9614"

### What's documented about Eastern Cherokee Application 9614

The Eastern Cherokee Applications under the Act of 28 June 1906 (the "Guion Miller Roll") cover real numbers from 1 to ~45,856, distributed across National Archives microfilm M1104 reels 1–348. Each reel covers a known range (e.g., reel 010 has applications 779–859; reels 283–348 cover applications 36,051–45,856). **Application 9614 falls within the documented numbering range** (likely on reel 116-118, covering ~9,500–9,800).

So application 9614 is a **theoretically valid** application number — it is not fabricated as a number. **However:**

### The fraud-pattern signature

The deeper Native-fraud literature (Geni, WikiTree "Shawnee Heritage Fraud" project, Bill Deyo's Patawomeck commentary) shows that Don Greene and Noel Schutz in *Shawnee Heritage* (Volumes I–IV, 2007–2014) repeatedly attached **specific Eastern Cherokee Application numbers** to fabricated ancestors as a way of giving fictional Native genealogies the appearance of primary-record citation. This is the same pattern as:
- The deleted FamilySearch profile **Solomon John "Rappahanno…" Kimbrough** (FSID GLXB-NKG, deleted by merge 10 Feb 2024), which had Custom Event "Cherokee Gunion Miller Roll 9614"
- The deleted FamilySearch profile **"Sharp As Fox" John Spiller** (FSID LKRW-Y8S)
- And other deleted Shawnee Heritage entries

The *signature* of the fraud is that the **same Miller Roll number** (9614) is being recycled across multiple unrelated fictional ancestors — which is impossible if the number actually corresponded to a real application by a specific individual.

### Verification path

To definitively prove or disprove fraud-network contamination:
1. Pull Eastern Cherokee Application **9614** from National Archives microfilm M1104 (or via Fold3.com paid subscription, or via Ancestry.com)
2. Read the actual applicant's name, residence, and claimed Cherokee ancestry
3. Compare to the alleged ancestor's known biography

If the real Application 9614 applicant has no documentable connection to the Kimbrough, Bays, or Woodrum lines — **it's fraud-pattern contamination** and should be discarded.

If the real Application 9614 applicant IS a Kimbrough or Bays or Woodrum descendant — that's a separate, smaller-grade evidence of *some* family member's Cherokee claim, but it does not retroactively prove Mary Bays/Baze was Cherokee.

**Recommended action:** Request the Eastern Cherokee Application 9614 file from the National Archives (Washington DC reading room, or via Fold3 paid pull). Until done, treat the 9614 claim as **fraud-pattern signature, default-skeptical**.

### Genealogytrails / "Cherokee" tradition assessment

The fraud-pattern signature plus the surface-level surname mismatch (Cherokee tradition vs. the Monacan tradition the Bible records) suggests the "Cherokee" tag was layered onto Mary Bays/Baze's profile by **someone confusing or merging multiple Native-ancestry traditions.** The Monacan tradition in the Bible record is internally coherent and geographically accurate. The Cherokee tradition is incoherent (geographically wrong: Cherokee homelands are in NC/TN/GA/SC, not Powhatan County VA) and traces to fraud-pattern citation. **The Cherokee tag should be discarded; the Monacan tradition retained as family-tradition-only evidence with appropriate caveats.**

> **Mary "Molly" Bays / Baze (married name Woodrum)** married John Woodrum on 15 November 1761 at St. James Northam Parish, Goochland County, Virginia. >
> According to a family tradition recorded in a Bible held by a Tennessee descendant of the Woodrum family — and transcribed in a memo dated 27 July 1983 by Marjorie Fluor Moore, copies of which are referenced in the Woodrome family history page on Genealogy Trails — Mary "Molly" came from the Monacan Indian village of Mowhemencho, on the south side of the James River near present-day Richmond, Virginia. The Bible recorded that "all the males in her tribe had been killed by the settlers." Mowhemencho is a documented Monacan town that appears on Captain John Smith's 1612 map of Virginia. Historical records (the Virginia Department of Historic Resources Marker O-3, the Monacan Indian Nation's official timeline drawing on Helen Rountree's 1990 *Pocahontas's People*, and Francis Louis Michel's 1702 contemporary account) confirm that Mowhemencho was destroyed by Virginia colonists circa 1670, that survivors continued to camp nearby through the early 1700s, and that French Huguenots took over the site in 1700 — renaming it Manakin Town. The Bible's account of Mary Bays/Baze's Monacan origin is therefore geographically accurate, chronologically plausible (the destruction occurring 60+ years before her assumed birth in the 1730s–1740s, with descent through a survivor woman), and internally consistent with documented Monacan history.
>
> However, the claim is unverified by any 18th-century court, parish, or land record that names Mary Bays/Baze as Indian, and the Bible itself has not been independently authenticated in this research run. The family tradition stands as plausible-but-unproven Native ancestry, citable as such.
>
> **Important note on identity:** Earlier versions of this research (and the FamilySearch profile LRKY-41Q "Mary Kimburrow") conflated this Mary Bays/Baze Woodrum with a Mary Dorcas Kimbrough (christened 30 January 1703/4 at St. Peter's Parish, New Kent, Virginia; daughter of John Kimbrough Jr.; died 1747; married a Wilkins). These were two different women. The 1703 christening record at St. Peter's Parish, while a valid primary-source document, refers to the Kimbrough Wilkins woman, not to the Bays/Baze Woodrum woman. The two have been incorrectly merged on FamilySearch, with the Kimbrough birth date and the Bays/Baze marriage date forced into a single profile. The corrected family-tradition Native-ancestry claim runs through the **Bays/Baze surname**, not the Kimbrough surname. The Kimbrough connection should be removed from Brady's whitepaper Native-ancestry section unless an independent primary source links Mary Bays/Baze to a documented Kimbrough parent — which does not currently exist in any surviving record found in this research run.

## 11. Net-net for Phase 5 of the Donahoe Heritage project

The Native-ancestry claim survives this primary-source verification run, but it lands very differently than the prior project notes suggested.

**What's gained:**
- Primary-source-confirmed 1761 marriage record at St. James Northam Parish (Douglas Register)
- Primary-source-confirmed 6+ baptism records of John Woodrum + Mary Bays/Baze children, 1764–1774
- Primary-source-confirmed historical destruction of Mowhemencho ~1670 by Virginia colonists

**What's lost:**
- The "Mary Kimburrow christened 1703 at St. Peter's Parish New Kent" — that's a different woman (Mary Dorcas Kimbrough Wilkins, 1703/4–1747, married Wilkins). The 1703 christening should be removed from Brady's Native-ancestry citation chain.

**What remains family-tradition-only:**

- The 27 July 1983 Marjorie Fluor Moore memo's accuracy in transcribing the Bible
- The chain of custody from the original Bible (allegedly held by Jacob E. Woodrum in Wilson County TN) to today

**The whitepaper claim is honest, sourced, and properly caveated — it just needs the surname correction (Bays/Baze, not Kimburrow) and the removal of the wrong-woman St. Peter's Parish christening.** That's a meaningful correction but not a retraction.
