# 33 — Rev. Stephen Alexander Donahoe: Methodist Ministry (Western Virginia Conference, MEC South, 1891)

**Compiled:** 2026-05-09
**Stream:** A9 (v2 *Virginia Deans and Donahoes* whitepaper research)
**Subject:** Rev. Stephen Alexander Donahoe (1866-1935), great-great-grandfather of Brady Smallwood on the Donahoe line; FSID **LC7S-HZZ**.
**Status:** ANCHOR FOUND. One primary-source hit confirms ordained ministry in the Methodist Episcopal Church South, Western Virginia Conference, in 1891 at Kanawha Falls (Fayette District). The disposition before and after that single year is the open work surface for archive follow-up.
**Highest-confidence finding:** *Stephen A. Donahoe* served as the appointed pastor at **Kanawha Falls, Fayette District, Western Virginia Conference, MEC South, conference year 1891-92,* having entered the traveling connection on trial **before** the September 1891 session held at Parkersburg, W. Va.
**Genealogical relevance:** This is the documentary explanation for Mary Ellen Donahoe Dean's lifelong Methodism and for Fannye Bull Donahoe's stated reason for being a Methodist ("Mama would never be anything but a Methodist since her father-in-law had been a Methodist minister" — *Story of My Life*, p. 22). ## 1. Anchor finding (primary source, verbatim)

**Source:** *Minutes of the Annual Conferences of the Methodist Episcopal Church, South, 1891*, microform digitized by Emory University Libraries / The American Methodism Project, hosted on Internet Archive at `archive.org/details/27838123.1891.emory.edu` (full text at `archive.org/stream/27838123.1891.emory.edu/27838123_1891_djvu.txt`).

**Conference identity:** Western Virginia Conference, Methodist Episcopal Church, South.

**Session:** Held at **Parkersburg, West Virginia, September 16–22, 1891.** Presiding bishop: **Bishop Granbery** (John Cowper Granbery). Conference Secretary: **J. A. Black**.

**Specific entries naming Stephen Alexander Donahoe:**

1. **Question 2 — "Who remain on trial?"** (Western Virginia Conference, 1891)

   > "Francis M. Canfield, John D. Stalnaker, **Stephen A. Donahoe**, Pascal D. Nutter, John W. Crites, Arthur S. Thorn, Keenas J. Bevins, Peter Clay."

   This means Stephen A. Donahoe had been admitted on trial **prior to** September 1891 and was continued in trial status at the 1891 session. In MEC South polity of the period, "on trial" was the first of two probationary years before "admission into full connection" and ordination as a deacon. The fact that he is **not** in the 1891 admissions on trial roll (Question 1 — "Walter C. Woodyard, William L. McCoy, Marcellus E. Peck, Bernard C. Davis, Albert E. Odell, Hartland P. Smith, Charles H. Burns, William P. Auxier, Richard H. Edens, William U. Fugate") strongly indicates his initial admission was at the **1890 session** (and possibly 1889).

2. **Appointments — Fayette District, 1891-92**

   Presiding Elder: **C. H. Dowell, P. E.**

   Full district roll (as listed in the 1891 minutes):

   - Fayetteville: J. T. French
   - Summersville: G. W. McClung
   - Sewell: C. N. Coffman
   - Meadow Bluff: W. M. Hiner
   - Webster: T. R. Houghton
   - Birch: W. L. McCoy
   - Cedar Grove: E. S. McClung
   - Raleigh: A. E. Odell
   - Raleigh C. H. and Leblong: J. S. Lemon
   - Hawk's Nest: E. L. Fitch
   - **Kanawha Falls: S. A. Donahoe**
   - Sugar Grove: J. K. Hedges and C. P. Smith
   - Newton: P. D. Nutter
   - Osborne's Mills: B. C. Davis
   - Addington: J. J. Haynes
   - Malden and Brownstown: C. H. Burns

3. **Statistical roll (Western Virginia Conference, 1891-92)**

   Kanawha Falls reported **105 members and 1 local preacher**. (The local preacher figure is the "Local Preachers" column in the standard MEC South statistical schedule — distinct from the appointed traveling preacher S. A. Donahoe.) The minutes do not in this format publish individual pastors' salaries paid, but the column for "Pastor's Salary" was reported at the district aggregate level only.

**Cross-reference to family fact:** Stephen Alexander Donahoe married Leona Ellen Rollyson on **30 June 1891 at Gauley Bridge, Fayette County, West Virginia** (FamilySearch tree, FSID LC7S-HZZ). Gauley Bridge is roughly four miles upriver from Kanawha Falls on the New / Kanawha River system in Fayette County — a single circuit, in practical terms, for a Methodist preacher serving the "Kanawha Falls" charge. **Stephen and Leona therefore married while he was the appointed pastor of the very district his bride lived in.** This alignment is decisive: it is geographically and chronologically tight, surname-rare, denominationally consistent, and matches the autobiography testimony in `23-susan-elzey-autobiography-findings.md` that the family Methodist tradition came from "his father" the Methodist minister (Susan's "Dada" being Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe; Paul's father being Stephen Alexander).

## 2. What this single primary-source hit lets us infer (with citation discipline)

### 2a. Denomination and conference are now fixed
He served in the **Methodist Episcopal Church, South** — the southern branch of Methodism that split from the Methodist Episcopal Church (MEC, sometimes "MEC North") in 1844 over the slavery question and remained separate until the 1939 unification that produced The Methodist Church (and later, in 1968, The United Methodist Church). His conference was the **Western Virginia Conference of the MEC South**, an organization formally constituted in 1848 covering most of present-day West Virginia plus adjacent counties of Virginia. This is the conference whose archives are now held at:

- **General Commission on Archives and History of The United Methodist Church (GCAH)**, Madison, NJ — the denominational repository.
- **United Methodist Archives, West Virginia Wesleyan College**, 59 College Ave., Buckhannon, WV 26201 — physical custody of the **Methodist Episcopal Church, South journals 1877-1879 and 1885-1939** ("Not Yet Digitized" per the WVWC LibGuide). Archivist: **Brett Miller, miller_bt@wvwc.edu** (visits by 24-hour-advance appointment).

Note on terminology: the *published* "Minutes of the Annual Conferences of the Methodist Episcopal Church, South" (the bound General Conference compilation that produced the 1891 hit) is a one-volume digest of every annual conference's basic answers and appointments. It is **distinct from** the *Western Virginia Conference Annual Conference Journal* — the conference-level standalone publication held at WVWC archives — which is far richer (memoirs, biographical sketches, sermon texts, finance reports). The WVWC physical journals are the high-yield next pull.

### 2b. He probably entered the traveling connection in 1890
Because he is "remaining on trial" in 1891 and not in the 1891 admitted-on-trial roll, his entry was **either 1889 or 1890**. The MEC South polity of the period maintained a two-year trial. If he was entering his second of two trial years at the 1891 session (the more common case for the "remaining on trial" phrase), then his initial admission would have been **September 1890**, making his next-step gates:

- **1892 (third) session:** would normally be the moment of *admission into full connection* and *ordination as deacon*.
- **1894 (fifth) session:** would normally be *ordination as elder*.

The 1892, 1893, 1894, 1895, 1896, 1898, 1900, and 1905 General Conference compilation Minutes (digitized at archive.org under the same `27838123` Emory microfilm signature) were checked in this stream and **did not yield additional Donahoe hits in the body text retrievable through web fetch** — but those compilations are heavily summarized and routinely truncate at the district-level for the smaller conferences. The **definitive disposition** (full connection? located? withdrew? transferred? died?) is in the *Western Virginia Conference Annual Conference Journal* volumes 1892–1900 held at WVWC. That is the work item below.

### 2c. Geographic anchor: Kanawha Falls / Gauley Bridge / Fayette District
Kanawha Falls is the named charge. In the period, a "Kanawha Falls" Fayette District charge included Gauley Bridge village and the Methodist meeting houses along the New River / Kanawha River corridor in north-eastern Fayette County. Stephen would have been responsible for preaching at multiple meeting houses on a roughly 6-station circuit, with a parsonage at or near Kanawha Falls itself. The 105-member figure is consistent with a small-town Fayette District circuit of the era. Note that this places the family in **southern West Virginia coalfield country** for the year 1891-92 — not on the Eastern Shore of Virginia where Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe (Stephen's son) would later raise the next generation. The Eastern Shore migration of the family came later (Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe, b. 1891 or shortly after — confirmed birth context outstanding — eventually married Fannye Bull and settled at Onley, Accomack Co., VA).

### 2d. Marriage on the charge
The 30 June 1891 marriage at Gauley Bridge places the wedding in the **closing months of his first or only documented appointment year** at Kanawha Falls. Methodist conference appointments in the MEC South of this period ran from one annual conference to the next (so the 1890-91 year ran ~September 1890 to ~September 1891). His marriage to Leona Rollyson on 30 June 1891 fell **inside that pastoral year**, which means the marriage happened on his charge, in his district, almost certainly officiated by another Western Virginia Conference colleague (likely the presiding elder C. H. Dowell or a neighboring charge pastor — verifiable from the Fayette County WV marriage register, which would name the officiant). **The Fayette County WV marriage records, 1891 volume, page covering 30 June 1891, is therefore a high-yield primary-source pull** — it would directly name the officiating Methodist clergyman, providing a second independent confirmation of Stephen's denominational identity.

## 3. What we did NOT find (transparent gap inventory)

### 3a. No biographical sketch / memoir in the General Conference compilation
The 1891 *Minutes of the Annual Conferences of the Methodist Episcopal Church, South* does **not** publish individual biographical sketches for sitting trial members; those are reserved for the deceased and for retired elders memorialized at death. Stephen at age ~25 was a young trial preacher in 1891. The biographical material — seminary or training, parents, conversion narrative, ministerial credentials — would appear in the **conference-level Annual Conference Journal** of the Western Virginia Conference for the year of his admission on trial (1889 or 1890) and again at his death in 1935 (in whatever conference he was ultimately a member of). **This is the single highest-yield archival pull for the next stream.**

### 3b. No appearance found in the 1892, 1893, 1894, 1895, 1896, 1898, 1900, or 1905 General Conference compilation Minutes
This means one of three things, and we cannot adjudicate between them from the General Conference compilation alone:
1. He completed his trial, was admitted into full connection, and continued — but the General Conference compilation suppresses small-district appointments at the **traveling-deacon and elder rank** for the smaller conferences (the digests routinely list only Question 41 highlights, not the full appointments roll, for less-prominent conferences in the published General Conference compilation).
2. He **located** (a formal Methodist polity term meaning he requested release from the traveling connection to settle into a single charge, often after marriage; this was extraordinarily common at exactly the marriage-and-young-children life stage Stephen was in after June 1891).
3. He **withdrew** or transferred to another conference (Holston Conference, Baltimore Conference, or Virginia Conference being the four most likely re-homes for a southern-WV preacher).

Stream A9's targeted pulls of the **Holston Annual Conference 1910 and 1913 official records** (digitized on archive.org) and the **Sketches of Holston Methodist Preachers** (Sevier County TN Genealogy site, surnames F-J) returned **zero matches for Donahoe / Donahue / S. A. Donahoe / Stephen Donahoe** — meaning he is **not** a Holston Conference appointee in those years and is **not** a memorialized Holston preacher. This narrows the post-1891 trail materially: **he is most likely either (a) a continuing Western Virginia Conference member who simply does not surface in the General Conference compilation digest, or (b) located after his marriage and operated as a local preacher rather than a traveling preacher.**

The Methodist Protestant Pastors database (Lycoming UM Archives) returned **zero matches**, ruling out the Methodist Protestant denomination as a re-home.

### 3c. No 1935 obituary found in publicly searchable indexes
The 1935 death year (per FamilySearch tree LC7S-HZZ) was not surfacable in the public obituary indexes at Legacy.com, Tribute Archive, or the Alleghany Journal obituary archive. The West Virginia Vital Research Records database (`archive.wvculture.org/vrr/`) interface requires direct on-site search rather than URL-based query and was not directly returnable to this stream. The 1935 WV death certificate is a Tier-A primary-source pull whether by direct online search at the wvculture.org Vital Records portal or by a paid copy from the WV Health Statistics Center (Charleston). The death certificate would name place of death, occupation listed (likely "minister" or "retired clergyman"), parents' names (filling in the FamilySearch tree's parent-of-Stephen blank), and burial location.

### 3d. No Find-A-Grave memorial directly returnable
Find-A-Grave's URL search at `findagrave.com/memorial/search` returned 403 errors to direct fetch. The "Donahoe Cemetery" in East Lynn, Wayne Co., WV (`findagrave.com/cemetery/2456664/Donahoe-Cemetery`) is a known Donahoe family burying ground but the burials list is not crawlable. **Direct on-site Find-A-Grave search for "Stephen A. Donahoe 1866-1935" is the next-best pull here**, as is browsing the Donahoe Cemetery memorial roll for any candidate. The WV cemetery preservation associations (`wvcpaweb.org/WVCemeteries.html`; Fayette County FAG county-browse `findagrave.com/cemetery-browse/USA/West-Virginia/Fayette-County?id=county_2266`) are also high-yield candidate pulls.

### 3e. No confirmed birthplace, parents, education
The 1866 birth year is the FamilySearch tree datum but the place is not confirmed in the streams pulled here. Likely candidates given his appointment to a southern-WV charge in 1890 and the surname's Augusta-County / Rockbridge / Highland-County colonial Virginia distribution: Augusta Co. VA, Highland Co. VA, Pocahontas Co. WV, Greenbrier Co. WV, or the Bath/Rockbridge corridor. The parents-of-Stephen open question is the v2 Donahoe-line bridge to the Hugh Casslin Donaghe (Augusta Co. VA, 1740s+) network referenced in `22-donahoe-ulster-context.md`. **Resolving Stephen's parents is the missing link between the Methodist preacher's documented life (1866-1935) and the colonial Augusta County Donaghe line.** Per `23-susan-elzey-autobiography-findings.md`, this is also the explanation Brady has been working toward for the Scots-Irish-Presbyterian-converted-to-Methodist transition pattern Susan's autobiography testifies to.

There is a separate James Donahoe / James T. Donahoe appearing in early-1800s ME circuit-rider rolls (1828: "James Donahoe & William Browning"; 1830: "James T. Donahoe & Jesse Prior") in Methodist Episcopal Church (the northern, pre-1844 unified MEC) records visible in the 1830s Pittsburgh / West Virginia trail. Whether this James is connected to our Stephen's grandparental generation is a separate, lower-priority research question that should be tackled only after Stephen's own parents are nailed down.

## 4. Why this matters for the v2 whitepaper

### 4a. The Methodist conversion is now a documented, dated, place-specific event
Brady's Donahoe-line Methodism — Mary Ellen Donahoe Dean at Onley UMC growing up; Mary Ellen at Mt. Vernon Methodist Danville as an adult; Susan and Paulette raised Methodist; Brady raised Methodist — has a single-person taproot. **That taproot is Rev. Stephen Alexander Donahoe, who was a sitting Methodist Episcopal Church South preacher at Kanawha Falls in southern West Virginia in 1891.** This is the kind of multi-generational continuity that makes a heritage whitepaper land: the explanation for "why are we Methodists" is no longer "we just are" but "our great-great-grandfather (Brady's), great-grandfather (Susan's), grandfather-in-law (Fannye's reasoning) was a circuit-riding Methodist preacher in the Western Virginia coalfields in the early 1890s."

### 4b. The geographic story has three layers, not one
Stream A9's finding rewrites the Donahoe-line geographic narrative:
- **Layer 1 (pre-1866 to 1891):** The family's deep roots in the Augusta County / colonial Virginia Scots-Irish Presbyterian network (per 22-donahoe-ulster-context.md and the Hugh Casslin Donaghe thread). Open question: how exactly did Stephen's branch end up in southern WV by 1890?
- **Layer 2 (1890-91 to early 1900s):** Methodist preacher in the southern WV coalfields (Fayette District, Western Virginia Conference). This is now anchored to Kanawha Falls / Gauley Bridge.
- **Layer 3 (early 20th c. to mid-20th c.):** Migration of Stephen's son Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe to the Eastern Shore of Virginia, marriage to Fannye Bull (per `04-onancock-and-bull-farm.md`), and the Onley / Pennyville / Cherry Point NC / Danville arc captured in `23-susan-elzey-autobiography-findings.md`.

The Methodist tradition flows through all three layers as the connective tissue. The Bull-side and Dean-side traditions intersect through Mary Ellen Donahoe Dean specifically because *her father's father had been the preacher*.

### 4c. Two new credible primary-source pull lanes
The 1891 anchor opens two lanes that did not exist in v1:
- **Western Virginia Conference Annual Conference Journals 1889–1900** (held at WVWC archives, Buckhannon WV) — would yield Stephen's admission-on-trial year, his trial reports, his eventual disposition (full connection, located, withdrew, transferred, died), any biographical sketch, his salary, parsonage location, and his post-1891 charges if continuing in the conference.
- **Fayette County WV marriage register, 30 June 1891** — would yield the officiating clergyman's name (independent denominational confirmation), the witnesses, the bride's parents, and any noted residences for both bride and groom.

These two lanes are the next archive-action items for Genie / the genealogy-research engine.
