## 1. DAR eligibility — the rules in plain language

DAR (Daughters of the American Revolution, founded 1890, HQ Washington DC) admits women 18+ who can prove unbroken bloodline descent from a documented "Patriot" — anyone who provided service to the cause of American independence between **19 April 1775 (Lexington & Concord) and 26 November 1783 (when British troops left New York)**. The bar for what counts as service is famously **low**:

### What counts as Patriot service

Per the DAR's own guidelines (services.dar.org/Public/DAR_Research/, "Acceptable Service" reference):

- **Military service** — Continental Army, Continental Navy, state militia, privateer, French allied forces, Spanish allied forces. Any rank, any duration. Even one muster appearance counts.
- **Civil service** — Held a public office under the new revolutionary government. Examples: justice of the peace, sheriff, constable, member of Committee of Safety, signer of the Articles of Association, member of state legislature, delegate to Continental Congress.
- **Patriotic service** — *This is the wide door.* Includes:
  - Signing an **Oath of Allegiance** to the new United States or to one of the states (Virginia required oaths from 1776 onward; Accomack County oath lists exist at LVA)
  - Paying **supply tax** to support the war (the "Specific Tax" or "Public Service Tax")
  - **Furnishing supplies** — grain, beef, horses, wagons, blankets, lead — with or without compensation. Documented in Virginia Public Service Claims (LVA).
  - Serving on a **Committee of Safety, Committee of Correspondence, or Committee of Observation**
  - **Rendering aid to soldiers** — sheltering, feeding, nursing
  - **Active participation** in the Boston Tea Party, the burning of the Peggy Stewart, etc.
  - Membership in certain religious bodies that took collective patriotic stands

### What does NOT count

- Loyalist (Tory) service — disqualifies that ancestor (though descendants of Tories are not personally barred; they just can't use that ancestor)
- Service to the British Crown
- Anyone who took the oath of allegiance and then later supported the British (proven Tory acts)
- Service before 19 April 1775 or after 26 November 1783 (with narrow exceptions)
- Native-American service that was on the British side
- Civilians who simply lived through the war without active support

### Bloodline rules — strict

- **Continuous biological descent.** No adoptions count. No step-relations. Every generation must be a documented biological parent-child link.
- **Each generation documented by a primary source** — birth certificate, baptismal record, marriage license, will (naming the child), deed (naming the heir), census (showing the household with correct ages). Bible records are accepted if the Bible itself is described and dated.
- **Patriot must have lived in one of the 13 colonies, Vermont, Maine (then part of Massachusetts), Kentucky (then part of VA), or recognized adjacent territories.** Virginia qualifies in full.
- **One Patriot ancestor is enough.** Multiple is decoration. Susan and Paulette only need one.
- **Already-approved chains are inheritable.** If a cousin already proved descent from Patriot John Dean of Accomack through a verified lineage, Susan and Paulette only need to prove their link from the common ancestor onward — they don't re-prove the older generations.

### What the application packet contains

- Application form (downloaded from DAR.org)
- A "Lineage" page listing every generation from applicant to Patriot
- One **primary-source document per generation** (or a citation to an already-approved DAR application that established that generation)
- Patriot service documentation (military record, oath signature, tax record, etc.)
- Application fee (~$165 currently) + first-year chapter dues (~$40, varies by chapter)
- Sponsorship by two existing DAR members (the local chapter registrar arranges this — it's not a barrier, it's a formality)

Processing: 6–12 months at the National Society level after the chapter registrar accepts the packet. Faster if the chain is mostly inherited.

**Plain-language summary:** if Susan or Paulette can show that ONE direct biological ancestor (parent → grandparent → great-grandparent → … → Patriot) lived in Virginia and at any point between 1775 and 1783 either signed an oath, paid a tax, served a day of militia, or sold a horse to the army, they qualify. The bar is that low. The challenge is documenting every generation in between.

## 2. Two paths to the Patriot

### Path A — Donahoe maternal line (Mary Ellen → her parents → her grandparents → ...)

**Donahoe surname itself: low probability.** If the Donahoe immigrant arrived in the Famine wave (1845–1855, the most likely window for an Irish-Catholic Donahoe → Eastern Shore VA), then no Donahoe in the line was in America during 1775–1783. The Donahoe surname cannot have a DAR Patriot.

**Caveat:** A small number of Irish were in Virginia before 1776 (indentured servants, Ulster Presbyterians especially). If the Donahoe immigrant turns out to be earlier than 1820, this opens up — but the cultural pattern (Catholic, Eastern Shore arrival, surname intact through to Mary Ellen) strongly implies post-1840 arrival. Phase 2 Agent 3 is resolving this question in `03-irish-origins-and-odonahoe.md`. Until proven otherwise, treat the Donahoe surname as Famine-era and DAR-disqualified.

**Mary Ellen's mother's line: real possibility.** Mary Ellen Donahoe was Donahoe by maiden, but her own mother was someone else — likely an Eastern Shore VA woman from one of the long-established colonial-VA surnames. THAT line could easily run back to a Patriot. The Eastern Shore was settled 1620s+, and the same families (Bagwell, Bayly, Bell, Bird, Bowman, Bull, Bundick, Burton, Custis, Drummond, Edmunds, Fisher, Garrison, Gunter, Hall, Hopkins, Justice, Kellam, Lewis, Mason, Mears, Melson, Nottingham, Parker, Powell, Pruitt, Rew, Savage, Scarburgh, Scott, Selby, Smith, Stockley, Taylor, Topping, Turlington, Walker, West, White, Wise, Young) appear continuously from 1660s through 2000s. Every one of those surnames has multiple DAR Patriots.

**Action:** find the maiden name of Mary Ellen's mother. Census records (1900, 1910, 1920) will list her. Marriage record to Mary Ellen's father (a Donahoe) is on file at Accomack County Circuit Court if the marriage was post-1853 (when VA started requiring civil marriage records county-wide).

**Same logic for the next generation up** — Mary Ellen's father (a Donahoe) probably married once (Mary Ellen's mother), but Mary Ellen's *grandmothers* on both sides could be old-VA surnames. Each grandmother is a new bloodline Highway leading back to a possible Patriot.

### Path B — Dean paternal line (Brady's maternal grandfather → his father → ...)

**This is the highest-probability path.** Eastern Shore Deans appear in:

- **Accomack County tax lists 1782–1787** (the post-Revolution lists, indexed at LVA — these list everyone who paid the public service tax, which is itself qualifying patriotic service)
- **Northampton County deeds and wills 1700s** (multiple Dean families)
- **Virginia Public Service Claims** indexed at LVA — claims filed by Eastern Shore residents who provided supplies, livestock, or wagonage to the army 1779–1782
- **DAR GRS approved Patriots** — multiple Deans/Deanes from Accomack and Northampton are already in the system (see Section 3 below)
- **Accomack County Order Books 1770s** — names of men taking oaths of allegiance, serving on juries, holding minor offices

The Dean surname in Eastern Shore VA traces continuously to at least the 1680s. By the 1770s there were multiple Dean households in Accomack and Northampton, almost certainly across several branches. Susan and Paulette's specific Dean line can almost certainly be traced back to one of these households — and at least one of them produced a Patriot.

**Phil's prior on this:** if you randomly pick a multi-generational Eastern Shore VA family with the surname Dean, Bagwell, Custis, Parker, Walker, or Wise, the probability that there is at least one DAR Patriot in the bloodline within 6–8 generations is north of 90%. The Eastern Shore was small, intermarried, and almost every adult male of military age in 1776–1783 either signed an oath, paid a tax, or mustered.

**Action:** name Mary Ellen's husband (the Dean) and his father. From there, every additional generation backward is a 30-minute Accomack County records pull. By 4–5 generations back you're in 1776–1783 territory.

## 4. Virginia Revolutionary records — what's available

### Library of Virginia (LVA), Richmond

**The single most important physical archive for this project.** LVA holds:

- **Virginia Public Service Claims (1779–1782)** — claims filed by Virginia residents for supplies, services, wagonage, livestock provided to the war effort. **Partially indexed online** at lva.virginia.gov; complete indices available on-site. Critical: a successful claim filed by an ancestor IS itself qualifying patriotic service for DAR. Search by surname + county.
- **Auditor of Public Accounts records** — claims, vouchers, oaths, and revolutionary military pay records. LVA Accession 528 series.
- **Virginia Militia Muster Rolls (1775–1781)** — county-by-county. Accomack and Northampton militia rolls survive in fragmentary form. Not fully digitized; in-person research at LVA recommended.
- **Oath of Allegiance lists, Accomack County (1777, 1779, 1782)** — Virginia required adult males to take an oath of allegiance to the new state government. Multiple oath-taking events; lists survive at LVA. **Signing an oath IS qualifying patriotic service for DAR.** This is the easiest Patriot service to document because it's just a signature.
- **Revolutionary War Public Claims (Eastern Shore)** — separate sub-collection. Accomack and Northampton residents who filed claims for supplies provided.
- **Virginia State Library Personal Property Tax Lists 1782–1787** — the first surviving statewide tax records. List every adult male taxpayer. Paying tax during this window is qualifying service.

LVA is reachable in person (Brady's already planning a Monacan-project trip — add Accomack queries). Online catalog: lva.virginia.gov. Many records are also on FamilySearch under "Virginia Revolutionary War Records."

### National Archives and Records Administration (NARA), Washington DC

NARA holds the federal Revolutionary War records:

- **Pension Files — Series M804** (Revolutionary War Pension and Bounty-Land Warrant Application Files, 1800–1900) — every veteran (or widow) who applied for a federal pension. **Series numbers W (widow), S (survivor), R (rejected), BLWt (bounty land warrant).** Fully digitized at Fold3 (paid; free at most libraries) and partially at FamilySearch.
- **Pension Files — Series M805** (selected pension files, abridged) — quicker to search but less detail.
- **Compiled Service Records — Series M881** (CMSR for Revolutionary War) — every soldier's military service file, indexed by name and state.
- **Virginia Continental Line records** — 1st through 15th Virginia Regiments. Eastern Shore men are most likely to appear in **1st Virginia Regiment, 9th Virginia Regiment, and Eastern Shore VA Militia.**

### Eastern Shore-specific units (1775–1783)

Men from Accomack and Northampton served primarily in:

- **Eastern Shore Battalion of Militia** — local defense force, highest concentration of Eastern Shore men. Captains included John Cropper, Edmund Bayly, Levin Joynes, William Snead.
- **9th Virginia Regiment (Continental Line)** — recruited heavily from Tidewater and Eastern Shore. Saw action at Brandywine, Germantown, Monmouth.
- **1st Virginia Regiment (Continental Line)** — earliest formation, included some Eastern Shore men.
- **Cropper's Independent Company** — Colonel John Cropper of Accomack raised this unit. Cropper himself is a documented Patriot and a famous Eastern Shore Revolutionary figure (his home, "Bowman's Folly," still stands near Accomac).

If a Dean (or any Donahoe-line ancestor) served, he most likely served in one of these three. Pension files and muster rolls are the proof.

### How to access (priority order)

1. **DAR GRS online** — free, fast. Start here. Search every surname that surfaces in the Dean and maternal lines.
2. **FamilySearch.org** — free. Search "Virginia, Revolutionary War" → "Public Service Claims," "Pension Files," "Militia Records." Often has digitized images.
3. **Fold3** — paid (free at libraries). Best for pension files (full PDFs of original applications, often 20–40 pages each — they read like memoirs).
4. **LVA online catalog (lva.virginia.gov)** — free for catalog lookup, request digitization for ~$0.50/page or visit in person.
5. **In-person LVA research trip** — the Monacan project already plans this; bundle Accomack/Dean queries into the same trip.

## 5. Application path — what Susan and Paulette would actually do

### Step 1 — Identify the Patriot

Resolved by completing the genealogy work above. Need: name, dates, county, type of service, and primary-source citation for service.

### Step 2 — Build the lineage chart

Eight to ten generations from Susan/Paulette back to the Patriot. Each generation needs documentation:

| Generation | Person | Document needed |
|------------|--------|-----------------|
| 0 | Susan Dean / Paulette Dean | Birth certificate (state of VA) |
| -1 | Mary Ellen Donahoe + husband (Dean) | Marriage license (Accomack VA) + birth certificate of Susan/Paulette listing both parents |
| -2 | Husband's father (Dean) | Husband's birth certificate naming father |
| -3 | His father | Marriage record + census + will |
| -4 | His father | Ditto |
| -5 | His father | Ditto |
| -6 | The Patriot (or further) | Will naming him as son of generation -7, plus primary service doc |

Each step is a form-filing or a cousin's already-approved DAR application that can be inherited.

### Step 3 — Find a chapter registrar

DAR membership is administered through local **chapters**. Susan in Bentonville AR and Paulette (location TBD) each join their own local chapter — they don't have to join a Virginia chapter even though the Patriot is Virginia. The relevant registrar helps prepare the application.

**Likely chapters:**

- **For Susan in Bentonville AR** — the closest active chapter is the **Mary Wethers Chapter NSDAR** (Bentonville/Rogers/Bella Vista area). Or **Sequoyah Chapter** (Springdale/Fayetteville). Both active. Easy outreach — chapter websites have registrar contact info.
- **For Paulette** — depends on her current residence. If she's still on the Eastern Shore, the closest chapters are **Accawmacke Chapter NSDAR** (Accomac/Onancock — yes, there is a local chapter, and it would be the most relevant for the Eastern Shore Dean line because the chapter registrar will personally know the Dean families and may already have inherited applications on file). If she's elsewhere, find by zip at dar.org.
- The **Adam Thoroughgood Chapter** in Norfolk-Virginia Beach is large and active but more distant from Eastern Shore than Accawmacke.

**Recommended:** Susan joins Mary Wethers (local Bentonville). Paulette joins Accawmacke (Eastern Shore — they will be the most help on the Dean genealogy). Paulette's chapter registrar becomes the genealogy partner; Susan inherits the proven chain through cousin sharing.

### Step 4 — File the application

- Application packet submitted through chapter registrar to NSDAR National in Washington DC
- Fee: ~$165 application + first-year chapter dues (~$40/yr, varies)
- Processing time: 6–12 months at National. Inherited chains (where the registrar can cite prior approved applications for older generations) move faster, sometimes 3–6 months.
- Result: Membership approval, official "National Number," chapter affiliation, attendance privileges at all DAR events nationwide.

### Step 5 — Once approved

- Annual dues continue (~$40/yr at chapter level; some additional national dues bundled)
- Membership is for life if dues are kept current
- Susan + Paulette can then sponsor others (their daughters, granddaughters, nieces, etc. — including Brady's daughters when they're 18+). The chain becomes *Brady's daughters' inheritance* once Susan or Paulette is in.

**Total time from "name the husband" to "membership approved":** realistically 9–18 months. If a cousin's prior application exists for the Dean line (which is likely), cut that to 6–9 months.

**Total cost for Susan + Paulette filed together:** ~$330 application fees + ~$80 first-year dues + ~$50 vital records + ~$0–200 in genealogy assistance if needed. Call it **$450–700 all-in**.
