**Joane / Jane Pierce — L9T1-JVH (does not exist) → correct ID is L5T1-JVH**
- The ID L9T1-JVH returns "Person Not Found." The actual record is **L5T1-JVH (Jane Pierce, 1595–1650)**, a real, well-documented historical figure.
- Female, b. about 1595, England; d. February 1650, Isle of Wight, Virginia, British Colonial America.
- Married names: Joane Peirce Rolfe; Mrs Joane Smith; Jane Rolfe.
- 17 sources, 10 memories. Quality flagged with research alert ("Important research has been done on this person").
- Immigration: 11 August 1609, Jamestown — sailed from Plymouth, England aboard the *Blessing* on 9 June 1609 with her mother as part of the Third Supply; her father William Pierce was on the *Sea Venture*, shipwrecked in Bermuda.
- Survived The Starving Time (Nov 1609 – May 1610, only 60 of 340 colonists survived) and the Powhatan Massacre of 22 March 1622 (347 killed colony-wide).
- 1st marriage: about 1619 to **John Rolfe** (LBN5-B59, 1585–1622) — yes, the John Rolfe formerly married to Pocahontas. Daughter: Elizabeth Pierce Rolfe (1620–1635).
- 2nd marriage: 1623 to **Captain Roger Smith** (LC8G-43C, 1572–1629). Sons: John Smith (LZZ2-PYC) and Francis Smith.
- Parents: William Pierce (LJ2G-45H, 1580–1647) + Joan Phippen (LH2L-X71, 1580–1660), m. 1595 in England.
- They appear in the 16 Feb 1623 "List of the Living and the Dead" muster at West and Shirley Hundred Island. By 1625 muster they had a 96-acre plantation at Archer's Hope. They adopted Sarah Maycock, a Jamestown massacre orphan.
- Source link cited: libertyletters.com/resources/jamestown/jane-pierce.php.

**Capt. Adam Thorowgood (Thoroughgood) — L5FW-RK1**
- Male. Born 14 July 1604, Grimston, Norfolk, England (christened St. Botolph's, Grimston). Died 27 April 1640, Norfolk, Virginia. Buried Old Donation Episcopal Church / Lynnhaven Parish, Lower Norfolk Co. Will dated 17 February 1639, probated 27 April 1640.
- 42 sources, 31 memories.
- Immigrated 1623 in the *Charles* (or "God's Gift"). 1624/5 muster age 18, listed as servant of Mr. Edward Waters at Elizabeth City. Indentured originally to Col. Edward Waters of Kiccoughtan.
- Burgess for Elizabeth City 1629, 1629–30 and 1630–32. Member of Governor's Council 1637–38. Justice of Court. Captain in militia. Started first ferry service in Hampton Roads (1636, on the Elizabeth River) — operated until 1952.
- 24 June 1635 patent: 5,350 acres at Lynnhaven (largest in the area; later New Norfolk County 1637 → Lower Norfolk → Princess Anne 1691 → Virginia Beach 1963). Patent was "for his personal adventure, for that of his wife Sarah and for transportation at his own charges of 105 persons into the colony between 1628 and 1634."
- The 105 transported include named individuals such as Augustine Warner (progenitor of George Washington and Robert E. Lee), Thomas Keeling, John Harris, Thomas Boyer, Edward Palmer, Casandra Underwood, Merciful Halley, Dorothy Wheeler, etc.
- Helped name New Norfolk County (1637) after his English home and the Lynnhaven River.
- Spouse: **Sarah Offley** (28MK-WGM, 1609–1657), m. 18 July 1627, St. Anne's Church, Blackfriars, London. Children: Anne (1630–1704), Sarah (1631–1658), Elizabeth (1633–1670), Elizabeth Mourning (1633–1690), Robert (1635–1702), Lt. Col. Adam Thorowgood Jr. (1638–1686).
- Parents: Rev. William Thorowgood (LVWG-7CY, 1554–1625) + Anne Edwards (L8KH-ZHS, 1561–1610), m. 5 October 1586, Grimston, Norfolk. (A second father record exists: William Thoroughgood MSY9-SC9.)
- Sources cited: Meyer & Dorman, *Adventurers of Purse and Person, Virginia, 1607-1624/5*, 3rd ed. (Dietz Press, Richmond 1987), pp. 459, 607–608; Findagrave Memorial #192224436; the MilesFiles compilation v23 ID I21549; Stell/Skinner/Wilkins/Traynor Thorowgood research on ghotes.net (Feb 2005).

- 51 sources, 16 memories. (Highest source count of all on this list.)

- Three husbands:
  1) Capt. Adam Thorowgood (m. 18 July 1627, St. Ann's Blackfriars, London) — 6 children listed above.
  2) Capt. John Gookin (L885-7S5, 1615–1643), m. 2 May 1641 Lynnhaven Parish. Daughter Mary Gookin who later married 1) William Moseley, 2) Anthony Lawson.
  3) Col. Francis Yeardley (93FY-H81, 1623–1655), m. 20 November 1647.
- Tombstone (January 1647 inscription): "Here lyeth ye body of Capt. John Gookinh & also Ye body of Mrs. Sarah Yeardley, who was wife to Capt. Adam Thorowgood…" Engraved with the Offley arms.

- Sources cited: *The Virginia Magazine of History and Biography*, Vol. 12, No. 2 (Oct. 1904), pp. 201–02 (JSTOR); Cox/Middleton "Thorowgood, Ghotes of Virginia"; *VMHB* Vol. 5, No. 4 (Apr 1898), p. 435 (Gookin Family); *VMHB* Vol. 5, No. 3 (Jan 1898), pp. 329–30 (Lower Norfolk and Princess Anne Counties).

Her will directed that her best diamond necklace and jewel be sold in England to buy six diamond rings and two black tombstones for herself and John Gookin.

**Maj. Gen. John Custis II Esq — 9J1J-CNC**
- Male. Born 1630, Rotterdam, South Holland, Netherlands. Died 29 January 1696, Northampton, Virginia. Buried 1696 at the Custis Tombs, Cape Charles, Northampton, Virginia.
- 5 sources, 3 memories.
- Alt name: Major General Custis.
- Took oath of allegiance to the Commonwealth of England (no King/House of Lords) on 30 March 1651, Northampton Co.
- Came to Virginia 1651/52 with his sister Ann Custis, who was the second wife of Argoll Yeardley (son of Sir George Yeardley). In the early 1670s built **Arlington Mansion** on the south shore of Old Plantation Creek in Northampton Co. During Bacon's Rebellion (1676), Governor William Berkeley fled Jamestown and took refuge with John Custis II at Arlington — making it briefly the capital of the Virginia Colony.
- Will dated 18 March 1691, probated 10 February 1695/96 Northampton Co. by Maj. John Robins, Daniel Neech and Margaret his wife. Names mansion "Arlington" and "Mockon Island"; mentions slave Gabriel Jacob who was to work on the sloop for 4 years and then be free; Smith's Island and "the great Dutch press and gilded looking glass at Arlington" went to grandson John Custis. Witnesses: Francis Nicholson, John Robins, Daniel Neech, Esther Robins, Frances Waterson, Margaret Neech, Elizabeth W Waterson.
- Spouses: (1) Elizabeth Robinson (L2SG-RHY, 1626–1654), m. 16 June 1651 Northampton — son Capt. John Custis III (LHKW-694, 1654–1713). (2) Alice Traveller (G2B1-6FW, 1610–) m. 1656 Accomack. (3) Tabitha Scarborough (L898-YS5, 1639–1717) m. after 1675 Accomack. (4) a second Elizabeth Robinson (P75L-CDH, 1633–).
- Parents (preferred): John Custis I (PSYP-57D, 1612–) + Joanne Powell (PSYP-XBP, 1612–). Alternative parent set listed: Henry Custis (G543-V91, 1596–1661) + Joan Powell/Whittington (G543-8D1, 1592–1661); MilesFiles describes Henry as a clothier/merchant/innkeeper in Amsterdam, Holland with 13 children by Joan Whittington.
- Sources cited (MilesFiles): Whitelaw, *Virginia's Eastern Shore* (Peter Smith, Gloucester, MA 1968) p. 971; Revell, *Randall Revell* (Penobscot Press 2007) Custis Family Lineage Appendix; Meyer & Dorman *Adventurers of Purse & Person* 3rd ed.; McDaniel emails Oct–Nov 2007; Marshall, *Wills & Adm Northampton Co. 1632–1802* (Picton Press, Camden ME 1994) pp. 34, 151, 43; Arlington Foundation Custis Genealogy Chart; Nottingham, *Accomack Co. VA Wills & Administrations 1663–1800* p. 67; *Acc Land Causes 1728–1825* p. 10; Sheppard, *Ayres-Dawson & Allied Families Vol. 2* (American Historical Co. 1961) p. 28.

**Col. William Coulbourne — 997F-NJ3**
- Male. Born 19 January 1630, Westminster, City of Westminster, Greater London, England (christened 19 January 1630, St. Martin-in-the-Fields). Died 21 January 1689, Marion, Somerset, Maryland. Buried 22 January 1689, Pomfret Plantation, Marion, Somerset, MD.
- 21 sources, 1 memory. 15 name sources, 11 sex sources.
- In Northampton Co., Virginia by 25 March 1652 — signed (with 116 others) the "Engagement tendered to ye Inhabitants of Northampton County" pledging fidelity to the Commonwealth without King or Lords.
- Two Northampton Co. land patents of 350 acres each: 4 March 1652/3 and 28 February 1662/3.
- Sympathized with the Quaker movement: 12 April 1661 the Northampton Court recorded an order of recantation against him for entertaining Quakers — but the side note reads "This refused to bee subscribed by Wm. Colborne." Reportedly arrested with Henry White, Thomas Leatherbury, and Ambrose Dixon for giving succor to Quakers. Eleven years later George Fox (founder of the Society of Friends) called on Coulbourne and held a service at his house.
- Settled at Annemessex on the lower Eastern Shore of Maryland between October 1663 and 27 January 1663/4. Patented 1,400 acres in June 1679, named "**Pomfret**" — between Jones' Creek and Coulbourne's Creek. The Coulbourne family owned Pomfret through 9 generations until sale in 1921; listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1984.
- Military: Lieutenant 26 March 1664 (foot company under Capt. William Thorne); Captain of foot Manokin to Pocomoke Feb 1669/70; Capt. and commander of a horse troop Oct 1673; Colonel of foot raised in Somerset and Dorchester Counties (1681); still "Coll: of the foot" December 1688.
- Politics: nominated 2nd sheriff 1667; Lord Proprietor's commissioner & justice of the peace 22 February 1672; Sheriff of Somerset October 1673 & November 1675; Commission of the Peace Feb 1670–1673 and again from March 1676 until death.
- July 1687 designated by Lord Proprietor to head the commission to draw up a treaty with the **Nanticoke Indians** — the treaty was signed 11 August 1687.
- 28 November 1689: signed loyalty oath to King William & Queen Mary.
- Spouses: (1) Anne Margery Huxton (LTCT-15Z, 1630–1670) m. July 1656 Northampton, VA — children: Capt. William Coulbourne Jr. (1658–1701) m. Anne Revell; Mary (1661–1689); Solomon (1663–1689, b. Annemessex 27 Jan 1663/4); Anne (1665–1730) m. John Taylor; Robert (1667–1698) m. Rebecca Revell. (2) Margaret Cooper (273F-RX8, 1634–1700) m. 1670 Somerset — daughter Penelope Coulbourne who m. Michael Holland.
- Parents: Charles Coulbourne (G7JM-5XZ, 1603–1662) + Audrey (9VRC-CFR, 1600–) m. about 1621 St Martin in the Fields, Westminster, London.
- Source cited: Clayton Torrence, *Old Somerset on the Eastern Shore of Maryland: A Study in Foundations and Founders* (HathiTrust uva.x000311271). Pomfret Plantation NR file: apps.mht.maryland.gov/medusa/PDF/NR_PDFs/NR-820.pdf. *Harleian Society Publications*, Vol. XI, p. 26 (1623 visitation of Somerset, England) gives a possible English pedigree from William Colborne of Wythehill → John → Thomas → William of Braton (1623), m. Mary Topp; arms "Argent, on a chevron sable between 3 bugle horns or, stringed of the second, 3 mullets of the third."

**Richard Kellam II — LQR6-LDT**
- Male. Christened 30 August 1618, St. James Parish, Aslackby, Lincolnshire, England. Died 1 June – 6 October 1703, Accomack, Virginia. Buried 1703, Virginia.
- **69 sources** (very heavily documented), 6 memories.
- Immigrated 27 July 1635 aboard the ship *Primrose* (Capt. Douglas), entered at Gravesend, England as a 16-year-old passenger. Indentured to Nicholas Waddelowe, a Quaker farmer.
- 6 May 1647: deed from "King Tabutiabum of Nandau" — purchased a neck of land on north side of Occahannock Creek called Quinduit; recorded 2 Aug 1649, witnessed by Tho Johnson, Manatuke, Rich Workeman.
- 28 July 1649 land patent for 300 acres north side Occahannock Creek for transporting 6 persons including his future wife Sarah Ainsley.
- 30 March 1651: signed oath of allegiance to the Commonwealth of England.
- 8 March 1654 warrant for contempt of court — committed for affronting justice during suit by "Andyaman and other Indyans," reportedly saying "if he had the Indyan at ye door he would cut his throat."
- Multiple subsequent patents: 200 acres north Occahannock 10 June 1654 (re-patented 20 October 1661); 400 acres at head of Matchepungo Creek 26 March 1662; 800 acres Matchipungo 18 November 1663; 900 acres for transporting 18 persons 19 July 1664 (including his son John Kellam, Edward Bird, Sarah Ainsley); 350 acres for 7 persons 16 July 1666. Lord Berkeley granted him a total of about 2,400 acres. In 1663 he traveled north to the Pocomoke River in Somerset County, Maryland and bought 500 acres known as "Kellam's Folly."
- 23 May 1663: confessed to killing Richard Buckland's cow "Butterflower" — ordered to pay 1,000 lbs tobacco fine plus another 1,000 lbs and a new cow to Buckland (full deposition text on his page).
- 1655 Act of Assembly designated his land as a court/church/prison/public-mart site at Hungars Creek divide, where courts were held for several years.
- Tithables lists from 1662 through 1695 are recorded annually on his page.
- Sold land to his sons in 1693–97 (William, Richard Jr., Edward).
- Will signed 1 June 1703, "very aged and weak in body" — daughter Rose 60 acres on Russell's branch; son William 50 acres on Beaver Dam Branch; wife Sarah his horse mill and remainder; children Edward, William, Richard, Sarah Curtis, Ann Wallis: 5 shillings each.
