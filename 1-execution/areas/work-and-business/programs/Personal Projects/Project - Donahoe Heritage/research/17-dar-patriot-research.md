## 1. Headline finding

**Capt. William H. Parker (FSID 9SKY-DJ3, Accomack Co., Virginia, b. ~1717, d. before 30 March 1784) is the cleanest DAR Patriot filing path on Susan Caylor Dean's direct line.** Primary-source documentation of his Revolutionary War service is verbatim in the *Accomack County, Virginia, Court Order Abstracts, Volume 28*, abstracted by JoAnn Riley McKey in 2017 from the original 1777–1780 court order book on microfilm at the Eastern Shore Public Library:

> **30 July 1777, Accomack County Court** (p. 9 of the abstracts, manuscript p. 17): "The following were recommended to the Governor to be commissioned officers of the county militia: Charles Marshall (captain) **William Parker (captain)** Robinson Custis (lieutenant) Thomas Slocomb (lieutenant) Southy Copes (lieutenant) Ismael Andrews (lieutenant)... [and the entry continues]"
>
> Same court, same day (manuscript p. 17): "Commissioned as officers of the county militia, the following were sworn in: **William Parker**, Solomon Smith, Joseph Kelly, George Justice, George Truet Taylor, Ismael Andrews, Francis Savage, Thomas Young, Zorobabel Watson, John Dix and Southy Copes."
>
> **26 August 1777, Accomack County Court** (p. 10, manuscript p. 19): "Ordered that the following administer the oath of allegiance to companies of militia: ... **James Arbuckle — to the companies under Captains William Parker, Young, Pettigrew and Burton**..."

That is the documentary chain a DAR application requires: **commissioned by the Governor, sworn in, and listed by name as a captain commanding a militia company in Accomack County during the Revolution.** It is the same kind of evidence DAR routinely accepts under Patriotic Service category "civil officer / militia officer commissioned during the Revolution," which is one of the lowest evidentiary bars DAR applies (Lt. Samuel Black below is approved on near-identical evidence).

Brady's Susan-direct descent path through Capt. William H. Parker, established in file 15 verbatim from the FamilySearch relationship calculator and confirmed in file 16, is:

> Capt. William H. Parker (1717–1784) → Ruth Parker (LZR4-MQP, 1742–1815) → Francis Boggs III (L4HS-B3C, 1775–1823) → Eliza Evins Boggs (KHZS-J1M, 1813–1880) → William Frances Rodgers (KZNX-SJ5, 1847–1895) → Mary Eliza Rogers (LC7S-HXW, 1878–1946) → Fannye Rogers Bull (LHNT-HK4, 1906–1982) → Mary Ellen Donahoe (LF86-MZT, Living) → Susan Caylor Dean / Paulette Dean.

That is **eight intermediate generations** between Capt. Parker and Susan/Paulette. Each generation must be documented by a vital record (birth, marriage, or death certificate; tombstone; church record; will; deed; or census). All eight are pre-1900 and most are pre-1850, which means the documentation will lean on church registers, marriage licenses, wills, and pre-1850 census schedules. **This is doable but it is not trivial.** The application packet will run thick.

The other candidates in the brief — Elijah Bull, Seth Dean, Hugh Casslin Donahoe, Lt. Samuel Black, Francis Boggs II — are weaker by varying margins, summarized below.

**Recommended filing path:** Capt. William H. Parker, Accomack Co. VA militia, via Boggs–Rogers–Bull–Donahoe descent, filed by Paulette through the Accomack-area chapter (Eastern Shore of Virginia Chapter, NSDAR, Onancock VA — *not* a separate "Accawmacke Chapter," that chapter does not exist). Susan files a "supplemental application" or a fresh application through her local chapter (James Bright Chapter, Bentonville AR — *not* a "Mary Wethers Chapter," that name does not appear in the Arkansas DAR roster).

## 2. Per-candidate analysis

### 2.1 Capt. William H. Parker — Accomack Co. VA, ~1717–1784 — STRONGEST

**Brady-line relationship to Susan:** 6th great-grandfather (file 16 confirmed).

**Evidence of Revolutionary War service (primary source):**

- **Accomack County Court, 30 July 1777**: William Parker recommended to the Governor of Virginia as captain of a county militia company; sworn in the same day along with Solomon Smith, Joseph Kelly, George Justice, George Truet Taylor, Ismael Andrews, Francis Savage, Thomas Young, Zorobabel Watson, John Dix, and Southy Copes. *Source: Accomack County, Virginia, Court Order Abstracts, Volume 28 (27 May 1777 to 1 March 1780), abstracted by JoAnn Riley McKey, 2017, p. 9 (original manuscript p. 17). Online at* `https://esplheritage.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Accomack-County-Court-Order-Abstracts-Volume-28-1777-to1780.pdf`.
- **26 August 1777**: Justice James Arbuckle ordered to administer the oath of allegiance to the militia companies under Captains William Parker, Young, Pettigrew, and Burton. *Same source, p. 10 (manuscript p. 19).*
- **FamilySearch profile 9SKY-DJ3** records Lt. 1776 → Capt. 1778–1781. The 1776 lieutenancy is not confirmed in the 1777-onward Accomack court abstracts (Volume 28 begins May 1777); it would be in Volume 27 of the McKey abstracts if that is published, or in the underlying Accomack court order book at the Library of Virginia (LVA microfilm). The Captain commission is documented above.
- **MILESFILES** (Eastern Shore Public Library genealogy database): Capt. William H. Parker, b. abt. 1720 Accomac, d. bef. 30 Mar 1784. The 30 March 1784 will probate date is the standard FFV death-floor for a man who left a will. *Source:* `https://espl-genealogy.org/getperson.php?personID=I24967&tree=1`.
- **DAR/SAR status:** No published DAR Patriot record was returned for William Parker of Accomack VA in any of the searches run for this file (DAR Genealogical Research System, SAR Patriot Research System, Virginia SAR Burial Book, Forgotten Patriots Program). **This is actually the normal state for a Patriot whose service has not yet been "proven" via a DAR application.** DAR will accept a fresh application if the documentary chain is clean. Brady should expect the application to be routed through DAR's Office of the Registrar General as a new ancestor record, which will assign a new ancestor number (typically a five- or six-digit sequence such as A0XXXXX).

**Strength assessment:** **HIGH for service evidence** (verbatim primary source court order naming him as Captain of militia, sworn in by date, confirmed by the same court ordering oath-of-allegiance administration to his company a month later). **MEDIUM-HIGH for descent evidence** — the eight intermediate generations are all in FamilySearch and reasonably well-documented per file 16, but each generation will need a vital record citation, and a few of the generations (notably the early-1800s Boggs and the mid-1800s Rogers transitions) may require courthouse pulls in Augusta / Greenbrier / Rockbridge / Botetourt counties. **HIGH for project significance** — Capt. Parker is also the Coulbourne-line bridge (his wife was Frances Colburne, daughter of Robert Coulburne), so the DAR filing simultaneously cements the Coulbourne FFV line and the Parker FFV line for the *Virginia Deans and Donahoes* whitepaper.

**Practical reality:** Generations 4–8 (Rodgers/Rogers/Bull/Donahoe/Dean) should be pulled from already-documented family papers and BPS-era vital records — these are the easy ones. Generations 1–3 (Parker/Boggs/Boggs/Boggs) are the hard ones. They will require:

- Accomack County, VA marriage records 1758–1815 (for Ruth Parker → Boggs marriage and her children)
- Augusta or Rockbridge County, VA marriage records 1790s–1820s (for Francis Boggs III → Eliza Evins)

- Pre-1850 census records (1810, 1820, 1830, 1840) — these do not name household members beyond the head, but they do confirm household composition by age/sex bracket
