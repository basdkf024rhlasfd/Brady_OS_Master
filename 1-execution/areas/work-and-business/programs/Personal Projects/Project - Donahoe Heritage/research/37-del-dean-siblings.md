## 1. The marriage-window constraint that bounds the sibling set

The single most important new constraint surfaced in this stream is in the comprehensive walk file at line 9:

> "Roy" Franklin Dean (2WFL-B1C, 1886–1964) + "Editha" Addie Read (KHLX-5DT, 1894–1980) — **m. 9 May 1929**

**Roy and Editha married 9 May 1929.** Del was born 24 August 1930 — about **15 months and 15 days** after the marriage. This is the keystone fact for sibling enumeration:

1. **All siblings of Del that are biological children of both Roy and Editha must have been born between 1930 and ~1939.** Editha was already 35 at marriage and 36 at Del's birth. The biological window after Del closes by Editha's mid-40s, so the latest plausible full-sibling birth is ~1939–1940. The full-sibling set is therefore **at most a small handful of children spaced 1930–1939**.
2. **Editha's pre-marital child Alvin (b. before 1929)** is Del's **half-brother on the maternal side** — same mother, different (or unknown) father. Alvin is being researched in Stream A11 separately and is not enumerated here. He is older than Del — likely b. early-to-mid 1920s based on the 1929 marriage date and the fact that Editha's parents excluded her from inheritance in response (file 24).
3. **No half-siblings on the Roy Dean side are evident in the FamilySearch walk.** Roy was 43 when he married Editha — older than typical for a first marriage in 1929, which raises the open question whether Roy had a prior marriage or prior children. The FamilySearch tree as walked shows only Editha as Roy's spouse, but the walk did not enumerate Roy's marriage history; this is a residual uncertainty.

The marriage-window is the single most useful filter: **the 1930 US census (taken April 1930) catches Roy + Editha as a four-month-old marriage with Editha visibly pregnant with Del; the 1940 US census catches the family fully formed; the 1950 US census catches it after Del has likely left for Army service.** Three census touchpoints that should fully resolve the sibling list, plus Editha's 1980 obituary which would name surviving children at her death.

## 6. Cross-reference with the "hard feelings" oral-history thread

Mary Ellen's 2020 oral history records that **the inheritance rift generated hard feelings between Pop's siblings and Allie's children (Loren, Lois, Leslie)**. This is the only living-memory data on Del's siblings that survives into 2026. Reading it carefully:

1. The plural "siblings" makes a single-sibling reading unlikely. **Del had at least two full siblings** by Mary Ellen's testimony. (Strict English usage of "siblings" plural would not apply if there were only one; Mary Ellen was a thoughtful speaker.)
2. The phrase positions the rift between "Pop's siblings" (multiple) and "Allie's children" (named — Loren, Lois, Leslie — three). The symmetry of the construction suggests Mary Ellen had a comparable count in mind; **a 2-3 sibling count for Del is consistent with the oral phrasing**.
3. The rift was not severe enough to sever the Editha-Allie connection (the parental generation), but reached the cousin level. This means the Dean siblings of Del were old enough to form independent opinions about the inheritance — i.e., **they were adults by the 1948–1950 Read-parent-death/inheritance window** (Albert d. 1924, Addie d. 1938, but the inheritance distribution likely concluded around 1938–1940 since Addie was the last surviving parent). Del himself would have been a teenager — 8 years old at Addie's 1938 death. **The siblings carrying the strongest grudge would be the older ones — in particular Alvin (the half-brother), who learned of his exclusion only after Editha's 1980 death and was "very upset."** The "hard feelings" are most plausibly led by Alvin and any older full-siblings of Del who lived to be aware of the inheritance question.
4. **Loren / Lois / Leslie Read's own life dates and surviving-descendant trail (Stream B / future) would let cross-referenced cousin-cousin obituaries name the Dean siblings on the other side of the rift.** A Loren Read obituary, for example, would likely list "preceded in death by his cousin Alvin [Read/Dean] of Michigan" or similar — Michigan obituary culture in this period was generous with cousin-naming.

**Inferred scenario most consistent with the oral history:** Editha's pre-marital child Alvin + a small Dean sibling cluster (Del + 1–2 younger) inherited the "Editha branch was cut out" story and held it against the Allie branch's Loren/Lois/Leslie line into the 1950s–1980s. The rift never became a full break because the Allie-Editha parental friendship held. After the Read farm sale (timing uncertain, "after some point"), some of Allie's children moved to Florida and the geographic distance further softened the friction.

Editha's brother Allie's Read farm on Rushton Rd is the **physical setting** where the inheritance dispute centers and where Roy Dean collected arrowheads. Documenting the Dean siblings is the Editha-side of this dispute; the Read-cousin side is documented in Stream B (or future) work on Allie's descendants.

The Read farm on Rushton Rd is therefore at the township boundary and may show up in either Washtenaw or Oakland county records. Editha was born "Rushton, Livingston Co., MI" per the pedigree file 12, but Rushton is more commonly associated with the Washtenaw / Oakland boundary; this may be a mis-county on the FamilySearch record (Rushton is on the **South Lyon-Salem-Lyon corridor**, not in Livingston County proper). **A County-level deed search for "Read, Albert" or "Read, Albert Jr." in Salem Twp Washtenaw or Lyon Twp Oakland during the 1900s–1950s would be the path to confirming the farm location and the post-sale transfer.**
