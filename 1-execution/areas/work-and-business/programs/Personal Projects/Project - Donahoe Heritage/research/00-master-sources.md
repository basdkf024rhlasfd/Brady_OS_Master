## Free public sources

### Deep Virginia / Jamestown / Colonial
- **Library of Virginia (LVA)** — Richmond. Largest single VA archive. Online catalog: lva.virginia.gov. Land Office grants, Northern Neck grants, court records, will books. ARK-style accessions.
- **FamilySearch** — free, world's largest genealogy DB. ARK pattern: `https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1/XXXX-XXX`. Indexed VA marriage / death / will / census back to 1600s for many counties.
- **Encyclopedia Virginia** (encyclopediavirginia.org) — Virginia Humanities project. Tribes, Plecker, Jamestown, Pocahontas, Eastern Shore.
- **Virtual Jamestown** (virtualjamestown.org) — searchable database of every documented Jamestown-era settler 1607–1650, ship lists, headright grants.
- **Pocahontas descendants registry (Order of First Families of Virginia)** — well-documented genealogy of John Rolfe + Pocahontas line. Most "Pocahontas descendant" claims fail on this database; a few real ones exist.
- **First Families of Virginia** (FFV, 1607–1624.5) — list of immigrant ancestors who arrived before 24 May 1624. Membership orgs maintain documented lines.
- **Virginia Land Office (Patents and Grants 1623–present)** — searchable at LVA. Headright system rewards (50 acres per imported person) are a key 1607–1700 record set.

### Accomack County / Eastern Shore
- **Accomack County Circuit Court** — courthouse in Accomac, VA. **Holds one of the oldest continuous court record series in the United States — 1632 to present.** Deeds, wills, orders, marriages. Many indexed at FamilySearch.
- **Accomack County Historical Society** — Onancock area. Local genealogists, surname files.
- **Eastern Shore of Virginia Historical Society** — Eastville (Northampton County, sister county). Holds Northampton court records from 1632 (split into Accomack 1663). Both are gold mines.
- **Northampton County Court Records 1632–** — pre-1663 records cover the entire Eastern Shore including what became Accomack. Same sequence as Accomack, slightly older.
- **Library of Congress: Virginia Eastern Shore collections** — colonial-era manuscripts.
- **Hornsby Family / various Eastern Shore family genealogies** — many published locally, available at Onancock or Eastville libraries.

### Native American — Eastern Shore Algonquian (NOT Monacan)
- **Smithsonian National Anthropological Archives** — Frank G. Speck papers. Speck did fieldwork on Gingaskin descendants at Indiantown VA in early 1900s.
- **Helen Rountree's books** (UNC Press) — *Pocahontas's People*, *The Powhatan Indians of Virginia*, *Eastern Shore Indians of Virginia and Maryland* (1996, with Thomas Davidson). **Definitive academic source on Eastern Shore tribes.** Rountree is the leading scholar; if she didn't document it, it's probably not documented.
- **Gingaskin Reservation records** — the Gingaskin (Accomac) reservation at Indiantown (Northampton Co) was one of the longest-surviving Indian reservations in Virginia (1641–1813 dissolution). LVA has the reservation dissolution records. Surnames associated: Press, Drighouse, West, Beckett, Carter, Stephens.
- **Walter Plecker 1943 surname list** — same source as Monacan project. Plecker targeted Eastern Shore "Indian-mixed" families too. List is at LVA, fully transcribed in the Monacan project at `Project - Monacan Heritage/research/02-plecker-era.md`.

### Irish Origins / Donahoe
- **Donohoe / Donahoe / O'Donoghue surname** — Gaelic *Ó Donnchadha* (descendant of Donnchadh = "brown warrior"). Multiple distinct septs: Glenflesk (Kerry), Ross (Kerry), Cavan, Tipperary, Limerick. The "O'" was almost universally dropped during 1700s Penal Era and not reclaimed by most American emigrants.
- **Irish Genealogy** (irishgenealogy.ie) — civil registration 1864+, Catholic parish records 1700s+ free.
- **National Archives of Ireland** — Tithe Applotment Books 1820s, Griffith's Valuation 1847–1864 (key Famine-era property survey).
- **Ellis Island / Castle Garden** — passenger arrival records 1820+. NARA holds.
- **Famine emigration records** — peak 1845–1855, the most likely arrival window for an Irish-immigrant Donahoe → Eastern Shore.

### Civil War
- **National Park Service Civil War Soldiers and Sailors System (CWSS)** — searchable index of every Union and Confederate soldier. Free.
- **Fold3** (paid, but free at most public libraries) — Civil War service records, pension files.
- **Eastern Shore of VA Civil War context** — Accomack + Northampton voted to secede with VA in 1861 BUT were occupied by Union forces in November 1861 (Lockwood's expedition) and remained under federal control for the rest of the war. **Practical effect: very limited Confederate enlistment from Eastern Shore VA, residents lived under Union occupation.** Some men slipped across the Chesapeake to enlist with mainland VA units; most stayed home. This is a unique sub-history worth its own file.

### Revolutionary War / DAR
- **DAR Genealogical Research System (GRS)** — searchable at services.dar.org/Public/DAR_Research/. Lists every documented Patriot ancestor used in approved applications.
- **DAR Patriot Index** — published index, also online.
- **DAR application paths** — to qualify, Susan Elzey or Paulette Dean must show unbroken descent from a documented "Patriot" — anyone who provided service (military, civil, patriotic) to the cause of American independence between 1774–1783. Service can be very minimal (oath of allegiance, paying tax to support war, supplying provisions).
- **Virginia Revolutionary War records** — pension applications (NARA), militia muster rolls (LVA), public claims (VA Public Service Claims, indexed and partially online at LVA).
- **Sons of the American Revolution (SAR)** — parallel to DAR; sometimes documents lines DAR doesn't and vice versa. SAR Patriot Research System is searchable.

### Tribal contact (if Native ancestry surfaces)
- **Nansemond Indian Nation** — federally recognized 2018, southeast VA
- **Pamunkey Indian Tribe** — federally recognized 2015, King William Co
- **Accohannock Indian Tribe** — Maryland-recognized, Eastern Shore MD (across state line from Onancock); the closest culturally-continuous Eastern Shore Algonquian community today
- Note: The Gingaskin (Accomac VA) tribe was officially dissolved in 1813 when the General Assembly authorized the sale of the reservation. Descendants remain but no formally-recognized Virginia successor tribe exists.

## Working assumption on geography and lineage

Eastern Shore VA = **Algonquian territory** (Powhatan paramount chiefdom included Accomac and Accohannock chiefdoms). Onancock specifically was within the Accomac territory. This means:

- Native ancestry, if real, is **Eastern Shore Algonquian** (Accomac / Gingaskin / Accohannock), not Monacan
- Pocahontas connection is geographically plausible (her father Wahunsenacawh's confederation included the Eastern Shore tribes) but biological descent is rare and well-documented elsewhere
- The Monacan question Brady raised is a **category check** — much like the Clark line's "Cherokee that turned out to be Monacan." Here, if there's any tribal angle, it would be Algonquian. Worth saying so explicitly in the whitepaper.

Irish-Donahoe immigrant likely arrived 1820s–1850s (Famine wave most likely) and reached the Eastern Shore by mid-1800s. This rules out the Donahoe surname being part of the 1607–1650 Jamestown wave — but it does NOT rule out **OTHER** ancestors in Mary Ellen's line being Jamestown-era. The line through her mother / grandmother could easily go back to 1607–1650 even if her father's surname is Famine-era Irish.

This is exactly the same structural pattern as the Clark/Monacan run: paternal surname (Anglo Clark) tells one story, maternal lines tell another. Apply the same lens here.
