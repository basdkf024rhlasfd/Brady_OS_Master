### Daniel Bull's 1781 Will — verbatim extract (p. 19 of document)

**Source:** Accomack County Wills 1780–1784, p. 175. Credibility: 5.

> "In the Name of God Amen, the 3 day of December 1781, I Daniel Bull in the county of Accomack Virginia being of sound mind and memory... do make this my last will..."

Children named in the will:
- **Albert Bull** — devised "one hundred acres of land, being the dwelling plantation"
- **Daniel Bull [Jr.]** — "all the remainder of my land"
- **Spencer Bull** — "my still and an [unclear]"
- **Fanny B[ull]** — "furniture and my negro one [to be hers for life]"
- **William Bull** — four pounds hard money
- **Ezekiel Bull** — four pounds hard money; also "my negro James whom I have to Ezekiel during [his life] for the support of [his] youngest children as he shall think proper"

Residue split equally among Spencer, William, Ezekiel, and daughter Fanny, except James the enslaved person.

Witnesses: Thomas S. Littleton, Willie Littleton, Robert Bull.  
Proved at court: Accomack County, 27 March 1782.

**Research note:** Daniel Bull held enslaved people (named "James" and at least one unnamed female) as of 1781. This closes part of the A3 Slave Schedule thread for the Bull line above 1850 — the Bull family was a slaveholding family in the late colonial / early American period. The A3 task (1860 Slave Schedule) picks up at Susan Elizabeth Kellam Bull / Edward S. Bull's estate after 1856.

### Map

BullsofAccCo includes a hand-drawn map of Virginia's Eastern Shore. Division line between Accomack Parish and St. George Parish is marked. Bullscrape Creek (near the Maryland line on the bayside) is labeled. Key communities relevant to the Bull family geography are visible.

### DAR/SAR path from this line

The Carey document notes all descendants of the Accomack Bull line may qualify, but does not claim a specific Patriot. The V2-QUEUE DAR path (Section C1) runs through this line via:  
**Frances Colburne → Ruth Parker → Francis Boggs III → Eliza Evins Boggs → William Frances Rodgers → Mary Eliza Rogers → Fannye Bull → Mary Ellen → Susan/Paulette**

Note: Fannye Bull's father (the Bull who married Mary Eliza Rogers) has not yet been identified in these documents. Finding his identity is the critical link between this published genealogy and the Donahoe Heritage direct line.

### Curtis Bull — Revolutionary War pensioner (DAR/SAR eligibility)

The foreword states explicitly: *"All descendants of Curtis Bull are eligible to join national organizations as the Sons of the American Revolution and the Daughters of the American Revolution, for he was a Revolutionary War pensioner."*

Curtis Bull's position in the Bagwell Bull Line chart needs to be verified against the V2-QUEUE's specific DAR path (through Captain William H. Parker, Accomack) — if the Bagwell-line Curtis Bull is a direct ancestor of Fannye Bull, this is a cleaner DAR path than the Parker route.

**Research action:** Locate Curtis Bull in the BullBagwell charts (pp. 33–34) and determine his relationship to the Bagwell Bull line and to Fannye Bull's father.

### Context note on the Bagwell name

"Bagwell" appears as both a given name (unusual) and a surname on the Eastern Shore. The Bagwell Bulls are a distinct enough branch that Carey compiled a separate 47-page document; this suggests a significant body of court records distinguishes this line from the main-line Tobias I → Charles Maurice line in BullsofAccCo.

## 3. BullsofMessongoCreek — Supplement, Southern Accomack

### Geographic focus

Messongo Creek is in **lower (southern) Accomack County**, in the Helltown area. The creek runs northeast, eventually becoming Collins Creek, which empties into the Pocomoke River on the Maryland line. Helltown had a post office between 1851 and 1885. The nearest substantial community is between Sanford and Eaxis on US-13.

**Why this matters:** The V2-QUEUE B2 task identifies a "Bull-family triangle" in southern Accomack within an eight-mile area: GS-199 (Bull Farm near Melfa), GS-88 (Bull-Pennyville), GS-188, GS-25, GS-20. Messongo Creek falls squarely in this geography. The BullsofMessongoCreek document is therefore the most geographically specific source for the community where Fannye Bull's family would have farmed.

### Informants named in this document

- Mrs. Hilda Jean Bull White and Mr. Herbert J. Bull Jr., of Sanford
- Mrs. Agnes Bell Taylor of Oink Flat
- Mrs. Joanie Slater (sister of Sam Church), raised by grandparents J. T. Rustall and his wife "Lacey Bull [Brawl/Brall?]"
- Mr. Marvin L. Hamblin of Crisfield, Maryland (descendant; his mother Alice Taylor Hamblin was a sister to Villie Florence Taylor, who married William Carroll Bull in 1952)
- Miss Karen L. Simkins of Milford, Delaware (descendant; daughter of William Lee Simkins 1921–1977 and Dethie Bull, daughter of Wallace E. Bull)

These living-informant names could serve as collateral contact leads for any Smallwood/Donahoe family working their Bull line in the same area.
