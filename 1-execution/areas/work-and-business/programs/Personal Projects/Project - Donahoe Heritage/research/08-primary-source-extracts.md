## Headline summary — what we found, what's still open

The Phase-2 sweep against the priority record sets returned **three documentary anchors and four open queries**:

**Anchors (documented):**
1. **The "Bull farm" Brady's family folklore names is a real, indexed Accomack County place.** The Eastern Shore Stuff cemetery project lists **Bull Burial Ground / Bull Farm near Melfa** as cemetery GS-199, plus a Bull Plot (GS-188), Bull Site (GS-6), Bull Plot near Nelsonia (TUA-249), Bull-Pennyville (GS-88), and Bull Cemetery east of Horsey (TUA-79). 2. **The Catholic Eastern Shore is a Pennsylvania Railroad / late-19th-century phenomenon, not a colonial one.** Onancock from 1687 was Presbyterian (Francis Makemie's church). The first Catholic church on the entire Eastern Shore was St. Charles Borromeo in Cape Charles, 1890. The second was St. Peter the Apostle in Onley (5 mi south of Onancock), built 1948. Father Edward "Ned" Mickle wrote in May 1930 that **"the Pennsylvania Railroad [is] bringing in many Catholics"** — explicitly identifying the NYP&N rail extension (built south through the Eastern Shore in 1884, taken over by PRR 1920) as the immigrant-Catholic vector. **This is the structural fit for a Famine-era / post-Famine Irish Donahoe arrival on the Eastern Shore: not colonial, but post-1884.**
3. **The Accomack County primary record series is exactly as deep as the Phase 0 brief promised.** Per the FamilySearch Accomack County wiki, the courthouse holds: deeds 1663+, wills 1663+, court orders 1663+, marriage records 1784+, death/birth registers 1853+, divorce 1850+, and tax/tithables back to 1663. Deeds and wills are full-text searchable on FamilySearch; chancery causes 1718–1912 are imaged at the Library of Virginia. Pre-1663 Eastern Shore records are at Northampton County (Eastville).

## 1. U.S. Federal Census, Accomack County VA (priority set #1)

**Status: structure-only at this draft.** No specific Donahoe / Donohoe head-of-household has been pinned in Accomack County VA via web-indexed search alone. This is consistent with two scenarios:

- **(a) Late arrival.** If the Donahoe immigrant ancestor came in via the Pennsylvania Railroad after 1884, the family might first appear in Accomack as late as the 1900 or 1910 census, possibly under a slightly different spelling (Donaho, Donoho, Donahue, Donohue, Donaghy). The Eastern Shore Catholic-immigrant vector lines up with this timing.

## 3. Accomack County Will Books and Probate Records (priority set #3)

**Source:** Accomack County Circuit Court will books, **continuous 1663–present**.
**Online:** FamilySearch full-text search; Ancestry collection 62347 (1652–1900); Library of Virginia EAD finding aid `lva/vi01659.xml` for Will Books 1673–1761; `lva/vi01671.xml` for combined Deed/Order/Will books 1632–1757 (the pre-1663 Northampton/Eastern Shore series).
**Credibility: 5** — primary court record.

### What a Donahoe / Dean will would establish

Wills in 19th- and 20th-century Accomack County typically named:
- The testator's spouse (frequently with reference to maiden name)
- All living children (married daughters often named with husbands)
- Specific parcels of land devised — naming farms, often by traditional name ("the Bull tract", "the home farm", "the marsh lot")
- Personal property and slaves (pre-1865) / household goods (post-1865)
- Executor, often a son or son-in-law

**For the Donahoe project specifically:** if the Bull farm was lost mid-1950s, the *prior* Bull will or deed transferring it to a Donahoe (as in-laws, perhaps via the Bull→Donahoe marriage) is the missing-link document. Search Accomack will books 1900–1955 for any Bull testator naming a Donahoe / Donohoe / Dean child or grandchild as devisee.

### Pre-1850 wills (deep ancestry potential — Mary Ellen's maternal lines)

The Library of Virginia EAD finding aids for Accomack:
- *Will Books 1673–1761* (LVA accessions 1146298–1146299): https://ead.lib.virginia.edu/vivaxtf/view?docId=lva%2Fvi01659.xml
- *Deed, Order and Will Books 1632–1757* (LVA accessions 1146285–1146290 and 1146292–1146296; reels 1–11, 51–52, 78–79): https://ead.lib.virginia.edu/vivaxtf/view?docId=lva/vi01671.xml — this is the combined pre-1663 Eastern Shore series, the oldest continuous county records in America.

Pre-1850 Donahoes in Accomack are unlikely (too early for Famine wave). Pre-1850 Deans, Bulls, and Mary Ellen's maternal-line surnames are quite likely. **FILL once Mary Ellen's mother's maiden surname is known.**

## 5. Virginia Death Index and Bureau of Vital Statistics (priority set #5)

**Source:** Virginia Department of Health, Office of Vital Records.
**Coverage:** Birth/death/marriage records 1853+ (with statewide registration mandatory from 1912).
**Holding institution:** Library of Virginia for pre-1912 / non-current; VDH for current. Eastern Shore Health District / Accomack County Health Department issues certified copies for Accomack-county events.
**Online:** FamilySearch collections 4149585, 1708697, 2377565, 5000046; Ancestry indexed abstracts.
**Credibility: 5** — primary government records.

### What a Mary Ellen Donahoe death certificate (post-1912) would establish

Standard Virginia death certificate content:
- Decedent's full name, race, sex, marital status
- Date and place of death
- Date and place of birth
- Father's name and place of birth
- Mother's maiden name and place of birth
- Spouse's name (if applicable)
- Occupation and last place of residence
- Cause of death
- Burial place and date
- Informant's name and relationship (often a child or sibling — names another family member)

**Value to this project:** the death certificate of Mary Ellen Donahoe (Dean) would in one document name her parents (closing the most important open question), her spouse (her Dean husband, naming Brady's maternal grandfather), her birthplace (resolving whether she was Accomack-born or arrived as an immigrant or migrant child), and her burial place (likely a specific Onancock-area cemetery — Mt. Holly, Fairview Lawn, Cokesbury, or one of the Bull cemeteries).

**Plecker-era caveat (1924–1946):** for any Eastern Shore family with even partial Native American or non-white ancestry, the death certificate's "race" field during the Plecker years (1912–1946) was actively manipulated by the Bureau of Vital Statistics. The Clark/Monacan project documents this in detail. If Mary Ellen or her parents are listed as "white" on a Plecker-era certificate but the 1860/1870/1880 census shows mulatto, that's a Plecker reclassification. *This is a low-prior hypothesis for the Donahoe line* (Eastern Shore Algonquian + Famine Irish is a different demographic than the Monacan-Siouan Clark line) but it's part of the standard genealogy-research discipline to check.

## 7. Holy Trinity Catholic Church Onancock — CORRECTION (priority set #7)

**This is a Phase 0 / 00-master-sources.md correction.** The master-sources file lists "Holy Trinity Catholic Church Onancock" as a parish-records target. **This is incorrect.** Holy Trinity in Onancock is **Episcopal**, not Catholic — founded 1881, consecrated 1893, currently in the Diocese of Southern Virginia. There is no Catholic church in Onancock proper.

### The actual Catholic Eastern Shore record set

| Parish | Founded | Location | Diocese | Notes |
|--------|---------|----------|---------|-------|
| **St. Charles Borromeo** | 1890 (under Rev. Edward "Ned" Mickle) | Cape Charles, VA (Northampton County, ~50 mi south of Onancock) | Diocese of Wilmington 1868–1974, then Diocese of Richmond | **The first and only Catholic church on the Eastern Shore until 1948.** Any Eastern Shore Catholic baptism / marriage / burial 1890–1948 was at this parish OR via a circuit-riding priest from this parish. |
| **St. Peter the Apostle** | 1948 | Onley, VA (Accomack County, 5 mi south of Onancock) | Diocese of Wilmington until 1974, then Diocese of Richmond | The second Catholic parish on the Eastern Shore. **Most likely parish for any Mary-Ellen-Donahoe-era sacrament from 1948 onward.** |
| **St. Andrew the Apostle** | (later) | Chincoteague Island, VA (Accomack County) | Diocese of Richmond | Third parish; serves Chincoteague community. |

### Father Mickle's 1930 letter — the framing extract

Father Edward "Ned" Mickle, pastor at St. Charles Borromeo Cape Charles 1890–1930, wrote in May 1930 of his region:

> "the Pennsylvania Railroad bringing in many Catholics."

Bishop Alfred A. Curtis (Diocese of Wilmington) had appointed Mickle in 1890 with the charge:

> "go to the peninsula south of Delaware, live there among the people, make your own tracks through the woods and gather what stray Catholics you may find."

**Why this matters:** these two passages, together, document the Eastern Shore Catholic experience as a railroad-era immigrant story 1884–1930s, not a colonial story. **The structural fit for an Irish Donahoe → Eastern Shore Catholic family is exactly this window: Famine-wave immigrant arrives in the Northeast 1845–1860s, family migrates south to follow PRR construction or potato/strawberry/oyster work 1884–1920s, settles in Accomack near Onancock / Onley / Melfa, baptizes children at St. Charles Cape Charles or via circuit priest until St. Peter Onley opens in 1948.**

### Diocesan archives to query

- **Diocese of Wilmington Archives** (Wilmington DE) — holds Eastern Shore VA sacramental records 1868–1974. **Most likely repository for any Mary-Ellen-Donahoe-era baptism, marriage, or burial pre-1974.**
- **Diocese of Richmond Archives** (Richmond VA) — holds 1974+ records and pre-1868 records (when Eastern Shore was originally part of Richmond Diocese).
- **St. Charles Borromeo parish, Cape Charles VA** — original parish books may still be at the parish.
- **St. Peter the Apostle parish, Onley VA** — original parish books from 1948 onward.

### Single named family in the available history

The St. Peter Onley parish history names exactly one family by name at the founding-era: **Dr. Campbell and his Catholic wife, who moved to Onancock in 1928** (Dr. Campbell was identified as the new Health Officer at the time). No Donahoe / Dean / Bull surnames appear in the available parish history excerpt. **Action: contact St. Peter the Apostle Onley directly to request access to baptism/marriage registers under the surnames Donahoe, Donohoe, Dean, Bull.**

## 11. What the primary sources establish definitively, and what's still open

### Established (cite-ready)

1. **Geography of folklore.** The Bull farm Brady's family folklore names is grounded in real Accomack geography — six distinct Bull-family burial / farm sites are documented in the county, with Bull Burial Ground/Bull Farm near Melfa (GS-199, 4 miles south of Onancock on the railroad corridor) the most likely candidate.
2. **Religious framing.** The Catholic Eastern Shore is a post-1884 immigrant story tied directly to the Pennsylvania Railroad NYP&N extension. Holy Trinity Onancock is Episcopal, not Catholic — Brady's master-sources file is corrected on this point. The parish-records target for any Mary-Ellen-era Catholic Donahoe family is **St. Charles Borromeo Cape Charles (1890+) or St. Peter the Apostle Onley (1948+), records archived at the Diocese of Wilmington for the 1868–1974 period.**
3. **Record-set depth.** The Phase 0 brief's claim that "Accomack County has one of the oldest continuous court record series in the United States — 1632 to present" is fully verified. Deeds, wills, court orders run continuously from 1663 (with pre-1663 records at Northampton). Marriages from 1784. Vital records from 1853. This is genuinely a gold-mine record set, and most of it is online or microfilmed.
4. **Surname presence in Accomack.** The Donahoe / Donohoe surname is currently present in Onancock (Joani Donohoe 2022, Michelle Donahoe 1987) and one Donoho researcher contact is listed in the Accomack USGenWeb. The surname is *not* yet documented in the available web-indexed pre-1912 Eastern Shore marriage notices, consistent with a post-1884 arrival.

### Still open (FILL — Brady-input or further research)

1. **Mary Ellen Donahoe's vital dates and parents' names.** The single highest-leverage open question. One question to Susan / Paulette Dean closes most of the rest.
2. **Mary Ellen's husband (Brady's maternal grandfather)** — name and dates. Required to start the Dean-side research.
3. **Specific Donahoe / Dean head of household in any Accomack census 1880–1950.** Closeable via FamilySearch search once Mary Ellen's dates are known.
4. **Marriage record of Mary Ellen Donahoe to her Dean husband.** Closeable by direct courthouse search once dates / approximate year are known.
5. **The Bull farm conveyance** — when did it enter the Donahoe / Dean family, and how was it lost mid-1950s? Closeable via Accomack deed index 1900–1958 plus *Eastern Shore News* sheriff-sale archive.
6. **Mary Ellen's maternal line** — the branch likely to include any deep-Eastern-Shore (pre-1850, possibly colonial) ancestry, and the most promising path to DAR eligibility for Susan / Paulette. Closeable once Mary Ellen's mother's maiden name is known.
7. **Native American ancestry test (Eastern Shore Algonquian).** Lower prior probability than the Clark/Monacan line, but worth a categorical check via Brady's existing AncestryDNA file + a search of Mary Ellen's maternal-line surnames against the Gingaskin Reservation dissolution records (1813 LVA) and the Indiantown families documented by Frank Speck (Smithsonian).

The sources stack up — what's missing is one or two specific Brady-input data points to convert structural research into specific records.
