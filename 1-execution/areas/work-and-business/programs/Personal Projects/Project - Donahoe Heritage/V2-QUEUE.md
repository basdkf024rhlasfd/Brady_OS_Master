### B1. Donahoe migration corridor: Augusta VA → Pendleton/Highland VA → Fayette WV (1809–1891)

V1 traces Hugh Casslin Donaghe (1728–1809 Augusta) → James H. Donahoe (1806) → William Penn Donahoe (1842, m. Lucinda Caylor) → Stephen Alexander Donahoe (1866, m. Leona Rollyson 30 June 1891 Gauley Bridge, Fayette WV) → Paul Riley Kidd Donahoe (1896 Ripley, Jackson WV).

The county-level migration in this corridor is unmapped. Specifically:
- When did the family leave Augusta? Which county did they move to first — Pendleton, Highland, Bath, Greenbrier, Pocahontas, or directly to Fayette?
- Are there Donaghe / Donahoo land entries in those mountain counties' tax rolls 1810–1880?
- **Civil War loyalty oaths from those mountain counties** — Scots-Irish mountain families were often Union-sympathizing. If the Donahoes signed Union loyalty oaths, that's a useful contrast to the Eastern Shore occupation story and a real new finding.
- Stephen Alexander Donahoe was a Reverend (per the FamilySearch profile). What denomination? Where was he licensed/ordained? Methodist Episcopal Church South records, Presbyterian, Disciples of Christ?

**Action:** Pull tax lists, deed indexes, Civil War loyalty oaths for Pendleton / Highland / Bath / Greenbrier / Pocahontas / Fayette counties WV across 1810–1891. West Virginia became a state in 1863 specifically over Union loyalty; the 1864 oath records are well-preserved and online via the West Virginia Archives.

### B2. Bull line above Elijah (b. 1748)

V1 dead-ends at Elijah Bull because the FamilySearch tree carries no parents for him. But ten Bull-family cemeteries are catalogued in southern Accomack within an eight-mile triangle (GS-199, GS-88, GS-188, GS-25, GS-20, etc.), and the Accomack tithables for 1663–1695 are published (Nottingham). Cross-referencing the published gravestone names against the tithables would identify which Bulls were landholding heads-of-household in the 1660s–1690s and could bridge to Elijah via 1700–1748 marriage records, deeds, and parish entries from St. George's Parish, Pungoteague.

The 1812 Bull-veteran cemetery 0.5 mi east of Pennyville confirms ancestral Bull homeplace at Pennyville goes back to early 1800s minimum — meaning Elijah's father was almost certainly a Pennyville Bull.

**Action:** Cross-reference *Graven Stones* gravestone names against *Accomack County Tithables 1663–1695*. Build a Bull-family pedigree chart for the 1660s–1748 generations from the convergent evidence. Pull St. George's Parish Pungoteague vestry book (1763–1787 surviving at LVA) for any pre-1763 Bull baptisms / marriages / burials retroactively recorded.

### B3. Dean line above Nathaniel Dean (b. 1693, Connecticut)

V1 dead-ends at Nathaniel Dean (b. 1693, Connecticut, no parents linked). The path forward:

- **Barbour Collection** — the canonical compilation of pre-1850 Connecticut town vital records. Fully digitized. Searches by surname yield town-by-town hits. A "Nathaniel Dean" born ca. 1693 should appear as a baptism in one of the Connecticut towns; that locates him geographically and unlocks parents.
- **Fisher line geographic anchor** — Anthony Fisher Sr. and Jr. were in Dedham, Norfolk County, MA after 1637. Nathaniel Dean married Johanna Fisher in 1716 in Massachusetts Bay Colony. Their eastern-Connecticut / western-Massachusetts overlap zone is the search area: Hartford, Tolland, Windham, New London Counties, plus Worcester / Norfolk MA.
- **Connecticut Congregational church records** 1680s–1700s — Yale Divinity School and the Connecticut State Library hold extensive runs.
- **Ancestry Dean DNA Project** and other surname DNA studies may already have the line solved through Y-DNA matches.

**Action:** Run the Barbour Collection search for "Nathaniel Dean" b. ~1693 across all CT towns. Cross-check against Massachusetts vital records. If a parents-named entry surfaces, walk back into the standard New England Great Migration databases (NEHGR, Anderson's *Great Migration* series).

### B4. Donaghe line pre-1635 in County Down

V1 correctly says pre-1700 Co. Down records were largely destroyed in the 1922 PROI fire. But three specific source sets survive and bear targeted searching:

- **Church of Ireland parish registers for Kilkeel** — some start in 1816, but a few earlier transcriptions survive
- **1630 Muster Roll of Ulster** — surviving in PRONI as D1759/3C/1, covers able-bodied males 16+, lists Down men
- **Presbyterian Historical Society of Ireland (Belfast)** — Kirk Session books that occasionally predate official parish registers; their Kilkeel/Mourne holdings should be queried directly
- **Ulster Plantation grant lists** — for Co. Down (private settlement, not Plantation proper, but Hamilton-Montgomery and Earl of Antrim's MacDonnell estates left grant records that survive in PRONI)
- **Donaghy / Donaghey / Donachie surname Y-DNA project** — Big-Y testing on a male-line Donahoe descendant could place the haplogroup in the Ulster Scots-Irish R-M269 sub-branches and identify cousin matches whose paper trails go further back

**Action:** Targeted query of PRONI for "Donaghe" or "Donaghey" in the 1630 Muster Roll for Co. Down. Query the Presbyterian Historical Society of Ireland for Kilkeel Kirk Session pre-1700 holdings. Survey the Donaghy surname Y-DNA project for Big-Y data.

### B5. Caylor / Köhler German Palatine line — completely untraced

Lucinda Caylor (b. 1845, m. William Penn Donahoe; d. 1881) is the entry point for an entire untouched German Palatine branch.

The Caylor / Köhler / Kayler surname is studied by the **Pennsylvania German Society** and the **Palatines to America German Genealogy Society**. German Palatine families in the Shenandoah Valley and West Virginia mountains are extremely well-documented through Reformed and Lutheran church records, most of which are on FamilySearch.

Key questions:
- **Where was Lucinda Caylor born?** Augusta, Rockingham, Shenandoah, Page, Pendleton VA? Or already in West Virginia?
- **Who were her parents?** (Not yet linked in the FamilySearch tree per the comprehensive walk file 12.)
- **John Caylor** (FSID P75J-RWX, Lucinda's father per the comprehensive walk) — when and where born/died?
- **Reformed or Lutheran church records** in the Shenandoah Valley counties for "Caylor" / "Kayler" / "Köhler" baptisms 1750–1850

**Action:** Pull Lucinda Caylor's FamilySearch profile (FSID likely findable from the comprehensive walk source folder). Identify her birthplace and parents. From there, work the standard Pennsylvania German Society and Palatines to America databases. The line could go deep — many Palatine families in the Shenandoah trace cleanly to specific German villages (Pfalz region) via the standard PA Dutch sources.
