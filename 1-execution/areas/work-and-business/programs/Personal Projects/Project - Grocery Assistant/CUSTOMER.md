# Grocery Assistant — What Success Looks Like

## Who This Is For
The Smallwood family — Brady and five kids, Bentonville AR.

## The Problem We're Solving
Feeding a family of six well takes real work: picking meals everyone will eat, making sure the nutrition isn't garbage, staying on budget across groceries and dining out and school lunch funds, remembering what's already in the pantry, and getting input from kids who have opinions but no system to voice them. Today this is all manual — scattered across texts, mental notes, and whatever Brady remembers standing in the Walmart aisle.

## What You Get

**Smart Grocery Lists** — AI-generated weekly shopping lists built from the meal plan, cross-referenced against what's already in the fridge and pantry (via photo scanning). Categorized, with Walmart deep links, quantity estimates, and a running price total.

**Meal Planning with Guardrails** — Weekly meal plans that balance what the kids actually like (based on their scores), what's nutritionally sound for a household this size, what fits the budget, and what's realistic to cook on a weeknight. Includes eat-out nights and takeout.

**Budget Tracking** — One view of total family food spend: groceries + dining + school lunch funds. Weekly target with alerts when you're trending over.

**Kid Voice** — A simple interface where Lily, Faith, and the rest can request items and rate meals. Their input directly shapes future meal plans.

**Dining Intelligence** — Restaurant recommendations for family-friendly and kids-eat-free spots in NWA. Chrome-automated ordering for takeout nights.

**Recipe Cards** — Each meal links to a recipe with a one-tap "Cook with me" button that opens an AI chat with custom instructions for making dinner engaging and educational.

## How We Measure Success

- **Before:** Brady spends 30+ min/week planning meals and building lists from memory, with no visibility into total food spend or nutrition quality.
- **After:** Meal plan and list generated in < 5 min of review. Total weekly food budget visible in one place.

- **Before:** Kids have no input into meals except complaining at the table.
- **After:** Kids rate meals and request items through a simple interface. Their preferences visibly shape what gets cooked.

- **Before:** No idea if the family is eating a balanced diet or spending more than necessary.
- **After:** Nutrition guardrails flag calorie-heavy or unbalanced weeks. Price comparison surfaces savings.

- **Before:** School lunch fund balances are a mystery until someone runs out.
- **After:** Balances visible in the food budget dashboard, auto-topped-up when low.

## What This Is Not
- Not a diet app or calorie counter for individuals
- Not a cooking instruction platform (recipes link out to AI chat)
- Not a replacement for Brady's judgment — the system recommends, Brady approves

## How It Works
Each week: the system proposes a meal plan based on family preferences, nutrition targets, budget, and what's in the pantry. Brady reviews and tweaks in < 5 min. The grocery list auto-generates. Kids add requests anytime. After meals, kids score them. The system learns and improves every week.

- Phase 3: Budget tracking + price intelligence
