## V. The Divorce Decree Question

Brady states the divorce decree (from Gene's prior marriage to Brady's mother) was supposed to give the children 50%. This is the central legal claim that would need to be traced.

**What to look for:**
- Virginia divorce decrees are filed with the Circuit Court of the county where the divorce occurred. If Gene divorced Brady's mother in, e.g., Mecklenburg, Halifax, or another Virginia county, that decree is a public record.
- Specifically look for any of these provisions in the decree:
  1. **Life insurance requirement** — Gene was required to maintain life insurance naming the children as beneficiaries
  2. **Testamentary provision** — Gene was required to leave a will with specified shares to children
  3. **Property settlement** — Gene agreed to leave specific assets to the children
  4. **QDRO** — retirement accounts divided at divorce (though those would apply to accounts at divorce, not at death)
- A provision in a divorce decree requiring Gene to name children as beneficiaries IS enforceable against an estate in Virginia, but the children or their mother would typically have needed to enforce it during Gene's lifetime or immediately after death. The statute of limitations matters.

**What is NOT automatically true:**
- A divorce decree doesn't by itself override a will or survivorship designations
- If Gene set up joint accounts and survivorship title with Debi AFTER a divorce decree that required estate planning for the kids, the kids may have had a claim against Gene during his lifetime — but that window has largely passed

## IX. Legal Context — Virginia Fiduciary Law

Under Virginia Code §64.2-1200 et seq.:

- **Executor's duty:** Fiduciary duty to all beneficiaries and creditors; must act in good faith, keep accurate accounts, and distribute according to the will
- **Annual accounting:** Required under §64.2-1300; failure is a default; the Commissioner of Accounts can compel
- **Surcharge:** If an executor misappropriates estate assets or fails to account, beneficiaries can seek a court order compelling an accounting and surcharg the executor personally
- **Statute of limitations:** Virginia generally has a 5-year statute of limitations for claims of fiduciary breach, running from discovery of the breach
  - **This is the critical issue.** If the estate was never closed and accounting never provided, the limitations clock may run from when Brady/Mike reasonably could have discovered the breach — potentially 2017 when the reversal happened, giving a window through ~2022
  - If the estate was closed without notice to the children, different rules may apply
  - **If the SOL has run, legal recourse is likely foreclosed without further analysis**
- **Divorce decree enforcement:** Virginia courts can enforce divorce decree provisions against estates, but the aggrieved party typically must act promptly after the decedent's death

> **This is not legal advice. Brady should consult a Virginia probate/estate attorney to assess any remaining claims, particularly regarding the SOL.**

## XII. The House Sale — Do the Children Have a Claim? (Added 2026-05-09)

**Question:** Does the November 2023 sale of 1165 Lake Point Drive for $585,000 create any claim for Gene's children?

**Answer: Almost certainly no.**

### Why Survivorship Title Is a Complete Bar

The inventory listed the residence as "Part 4 — other real estate" with a value of **$0.00 to the estate**, specifically noting it was "owned with Deborah B Smallwood with survivorship." Under **Virginia Code §55.1-136**, property held as joint tenants with right of survivorship passes automatically to the surviving joint tenant at the moment of the first tenant's death — before probate opens, before a will is read, before any creditor or heir can assert a claim. It is not an estate asset. It never enters the court process.

Debi owned that house outright the moment Gene died on December 1, 2014. The 2015 inventory correctly valued it at $0 to the estate. She sold it in November 2023 for $585,000 and kept all proceeds. No executor duty, no fiduciary obligation to heirs, and no court process attached to any of that.

### The Only Possible Legal Theory — and Why It Fails

The one arguable path: if the divorce decree from Gene's first marriage contained language specifically prohibiting Gene from re-titling real property in a way that excluded his children (or required him to maintain certain assets for them), then placing the house in survivorship with Debi could have been a breach of that decree. A court *could* in theory impose a **constructive trust** — requiring Debi to hold a portion of the sale proceeds for the children as remedy for Gene's breach.

For this to work, all of the following would need to be true simultaneously:

1. **The decree has specific restrictive language** — uncommon. Most divorce decrees address assets held at the time of divorce, not future acquisitions or future titling decisions. Brady needs to read the actual decree.
2. **The house was acquired or re-titled after the decree** in a way that specifically violated that language.
3. **A claim was filed within Virginia's statute of limitations** — generally 5 years from the date the claimant knew or reasonably should have known of the breach. If Brady knew something was wrong by 2017, the window closed around 2022 — a full year before the house even sold.
4. **Debi still has the money and is collectible** — she is ~76 years old, proceeds from a one-time real estate sale, no guarantee of assets remaining.

None of these can be confirmed without the divorce decree. But the SOL problem alone likely forecloses the theory regardless of what the decree says.

### Where the Better Argument Lives

The house is the cleanest asset Debi ever had — it was always hers by law, never subject to her executor duties. The more viable (though also likely time-barred) claim is in the **probate estate**: specifically Debi's conduct as executor of Affordable Properties of VA Inc. over 9 years, with no final accounting delivered, no transparency to heirs, and a $585K personal windfall from a survivorship property running concurrently with her fiduciary administration of the corporate assets.

The fiduciary duty claim is the right frame. The house sale is not.
