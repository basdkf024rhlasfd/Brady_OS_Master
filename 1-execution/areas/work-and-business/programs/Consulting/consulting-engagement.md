## Purpose

Delivering a paid advisory or consulting engagement — from scoping through deliverable handoff. Lives under the Consulting program.

## Dual-Facing Structure

Every consulting engagement produces two manifests:

| File | Audience | Purpose |
|------|----------|---------|
| `PROJECT.md` | Internal only | Brady's goals, team, authority, financials, learning loop, dirty details. The client never sees this. |
| `CUSTOMER.md` | Customer-facing | Customer's goals, what they receive, how success is measured from their chair. Could be shared directly with the client. |

**Why this matters:** You can't build a good product by optimizing for your own KPIs. The product is designed around the customer's goals. Internal goals (retainer conversion, revenue, efficiency) are lagging indicators of customer goals being met. If the customer wins, you win.

**Rule:** When writing the scoreboard, define the customer's Key Results first. Then define your internal KRs. Then show how they connect — internal KRs should be downstream of customer KRs.

## Roles Needed

| Role | Description | Suggested Draft From |
|------|-------------|---------------------|
| Product Owner | Owns what gets built and whether it's good enough from the customer's perspective. Holds the team accountable to customer goals, not internal goals. | Domain-specific agent (e.g., Musashi San for STIHL) |
| Builder | Creates deliverables (UI, reports, models, specs). Builds to Product Owner's spec. | Claudine (Code mode) or relevant specialist |
| Reviewer | Pressure-tests deliverables before client delivery. Asks "does this actually help the customer?" | Phil |
| Ops / Scope Guard | Guards scope. Flags creep. Keeps delivery realistic. | Bo |

Minimum viable team: Product Owner + Builder.

## Interaction Rules

- Product Owner owns all content decisions — nothing ships to the client that PO hasn't approved
- Builder produces to spec, does not freelance on scope
- Reviewer sees final deliverable before it goes to client
- Brady is the sole client communication channel — no agent sends anything externally
- Debrief after engagement closes: what worked, what to reuse, what to change

## Dual Scoreboard

### Customer Goals (define first)
- What does the customer need to achieve?
- How will they measure whether this engagement helped?
- What does "success" look like from their chair — not yours?

### Internal Goals (define second)
- Delivery timeline and scope
- Revenue and retainer conversion
- Reusable frameworks captured in Layer 3
- Efficiency gains for future engagements

### Connection
- How do internal KRs follow from customer KRs?
- If the customer wins, does that naturally lead to internal wins?
- If there's a conflict between customer KRs and internal KRs, customer KRs win.

## Recursive Learning

Every engagement with ongoing delivery must include a learning loop:

1. **Observe** — What did the customer use, ignore, request, reference?
2. **Capture** — Record observations with metadata (date, source, signal, implication)
3. **Synthesize** — Monthly Learning Brief: what patterns emerge, what to change
4. **Adapt** — Structural and content changes based on synthesis
5. **Deliver** — Ship the improved version with at least one visible learning-driven improvement

Full framework template: see the STIHL project's [recursive-learning.md](Project%20-%20Stihl%20Insights/plans/recursive-learning.md)

**Why this matters:** For consulting engagements with ongoing delivery, the compounding effect is the actual product. The system getting smarter over time is what makes maintenance cheap and what differentiates this model from a freelancer doing one-off work.

## Authority

- **Day**: Builder makes formatting/execution decisions. Product Owner makes content/product decisions.
- **Cycle**: Product Owner can adjust deliverable scope within client agreement. Learning adaptations happen here.
- **ARC**: Brady approves new engagements, pricing, and scope changes that affect the client relationship.

## Goals & Success Criteria

- Customer goals met (as defined in CUSTOMER.md)
- Deliverable meets agreed scope and timeline
- Client satisfied (would engage again or refer)
- Revenue collected per agreement
- Reusable frameworks or templates captured in Layer 3 for future engagements
- Learning loop produces measurable improvement over time (for ongoing engagements)
