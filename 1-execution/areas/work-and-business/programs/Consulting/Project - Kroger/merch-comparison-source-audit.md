## PRICING & PROMO STRATEGY

**What the diagram says:** Over-reliant on supplier-funded promos, merchants don't negotiate, "price as promised" failures.

**Bill's whitepaper (recs #2 and #4d):** Kroger was historically a "hybrid EDLP" operator — asking suppliers to invest trade funds in lower everyday prices while reserving some for promos. That hybrid was powerful because everyday prices weren't "offensive," driving loyalty and consistent large baskets. Over time, as Kroger lost households and share, they became increasingly reliant on promotional tactics — "constantly looking for the next complex promotion to drive shoppers back in store." Shoppers now cherry-pick promos without full baskets. Merchants are "far too reliant on [suppliers] to bring funding for the next promotion" and "behave more like promotional planners than like true buyers."

**"Price as promised" (rec #4d):** Most frequent customer complaint — promos and digital coupons not applying correctly online. Two technical causes: (1) substitutions breaking "buy 5 save 5" type deals with no customer explanation, (2) promo calendar crossover — orders placed Tuesday night for Wednesday delivery lose Tuesday's deals, affecting 5-8% of weekly orders.

**Accuracy: ✅ Fully sourced from Bill.** "Price as promised" framing, 5-8% stat, and supplier reliance language are nearly verbatim.
