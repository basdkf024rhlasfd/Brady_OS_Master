# §2.1 Financial Snapshot: FY25 Results & FY26 Positioning

## Situation

Kroger finished FY25 with solid operational performance — identical sales without fuel grew, EBITDA management held firm — but the year was also a financial reckoning. The Ocado partnership, which was supposed to unlock a future of automated fulfillment, instead consumed $2.6 billion in impairment charges when management recognized the bet had failed. This wasn't a one-time write-off buried in quarterly results. It was a statement: the next phase of Kroger's fulfillment strategy looks nothing like the previous one.

The numbers: FY25 sales reached $133.6 billion. Identical sales without fuel growth came in at 1.3% to 1.5%. Adjusted EBITDA held at approximately $5.8 billion. Operating cash flow remained positive. But adjusted earnings per share reflected the weight of the Ocado charge and the broader restructuring — the company moved forward despite a multi-billion-dollar setback that would have crippled some organizations.

The capital position tells the real story. Kroger's net debt-to-EBITDA ratio sits at 1.76x, compared to 1.79x the prior year. That's tight. Manageable. But not loose. After the merger failure in 2024 and a decade of selective M&A and portfolio optimization, Kroger has limited dry powder for major capital bets. The company can invest, but it cannot swing big and miss again. The Ocado lesson is expensive enough.

## Complication

FY26 guidance reveals why the Ocado writedown, while painful, was actually strategic clarity. Kroger guided to identical sales growth of 1.0% to 2.0% — a modest slowdown from FY25 — but with a more powerful revenue story underneath. E-commerce is moving toward profitability. Store-based fulfillment is lowering cost-to-serve. Third-party delivery partnerships with DoorDash, Instacart, and Uber Eats are expected to deliver over $1.5 billion in sales in 2026.

The guidance also carries a 130 basis point headwind from the Inflation Reduction Act, which limits the apparent upside on identical sales. Adjusted FIFO operating profit is guided to $5.0 to $5.2 billion — slightly down from FY25 levels — but adjusted EPS guidance of $5.10 to $5.30 came in above consensus estimates of $5.00. This signals confidence that the margin story and operational efficiency gains more than offset a slowing top line.

What's not in the guidance is what came *before* Foran: six months of capital allocation paralysis under interim leadership. Votes instead of decisions. A $2.5 billion Ocado anchor that no one wanted to cut. A McKinsey engagement approved only after an exodus of internal capability. These are structural problems, not cyclical ones. The numbers are clean. The organization that produced them was broken.

## Resolution

Foran arrived in February 2026 and immediately signaled operational clarity. On the March earnings call, he credited the prior team for "some really good decisions around Ocado" — executive shorthand for "I'm glad someone already started unwinding this mess" — and laid out a stores-first fulfillment roadmap. Pleasant Prairie, Frederick, and Groveland CFCs are closing. Charlotte was canceled outright. The remaining two CFCs will be evaluated. Kroger will pay Ocado $350 million in compensation for the accelerated exit.

The strategic pivot is brutal but coherent. Store-based picking and in-store automation are capital-light. Delivery partnerships scale without Kroger bearing the unit economics burden. Gross margins improve as fulfillment costs decline. The company trades a centralized, high-capex bet (Ocado) for a distributed, asset-light model (stores + partnerships). Foran has done exactly this before at Walmart US. The playbook is known.

Capital discipline is now the operating rhythm. E-commerce reaching profitability while still growing is the new bar. Procurement efficiencies and productivity gains are the levers — not major capex or portfolio swaps. Kroger's balance sheet can support this. The question is whether the organization can execute it at the speed required to narrow the competitive gap with Walmart, whose omnichannel model has already won the customer mindset.

The FY26 guidance implies Foran has room to move. A 1% to 2% identical sales growth band suggests confidence in the operational plan, not optimism about a macro recovery. The adjusted EPS beat on consensus is meaningful — it says the margin story is intact, possibly even improving. But the real measure will come in quarters two and three, when the benefits of CFC closures and store-based fulfillment start to show in comparable metrics. That's when Foran proves whether operational turnaround works in grocery, where Walmart's head start is measurable and the customer's willingness to switch is low.

For now, the balance sheet holds. The strategy is clear. The capital is allocated to fixing operations, not pursuing transformative deals. Foran has bought the time to move.
