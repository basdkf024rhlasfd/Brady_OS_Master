# §5.4 — AI Talent and Hiring Strategy

## Situation

Michael Pfaffenberger — principal data scientist at Walmart, creator of Code Puppy, featured in Walmart's "Ideas in Action" series on MLOps and enterprise governance — represents the archetype of engineer Kroger needs to recruit at scale. Not a consultant. Not a specialist. A builder with taste, who cares about governance and scaling, who works in public (open-source contributions), who drives adoption through example rather than mandate.

Walmart's talent pipeline runs through Global Tech, now with 17 geographic hubs (Silicon Valley, Northwest Arkansas, Dallas, Atlanta, Toronto, India, plus others). They hire recent bootcamp graduates, sponsor internal academies (Walmart Academy, Global Tech Academy, Tech Fellows), and explicitly recruit from non-traditional pipelines. This creates a talent flywheel: engineers who want to work on hard retail problems know where to apply.

Kroger doesn't have an equivalent pipeline. KTD is known for process overhead and risk aversion — not for shipping hard things. The internal reputation is "bureaucratic and slow," not "hard problems, smart people, your code ships to 2,800 stores." That reputation compounds: you don't recruit your best people to a place known for friction.

## Complication

Recruiting Walmart Global Tech alumni requires offering something Kroger doesn't have yet: infrastructure optionality. A Walmart engineer leaving for Kroger needs to believe they'll have more autonomy and faster shipping, not just different work. Kroger's track record on technology doesn't support that claim yet.

Salary is not the constraint. Retention is. Engineers at Walmart who've shipped with WIBEY and Element know what good infrastructure looks like. A move to Kroger feels like a step backward unless the CEO and board visibly commit to rebuilding.

Additionally, Kroger's KTD organization is still north of 1,000 people and delivering below expectation. Adding senior AI talent into that structure without changing the structure itself creates frustration for the new hire. They see dysfunction, they leave.

## Resolution

**Tier 1: Immediate hires (next 6 months)**

1. **Chief AI Infrastructure Officer** — reports to CTO, owns developer AI, agent orchestration, model training. This is your MVP hire. Recruit directly from Walmart Global Tech (EVP level or principal engineer equivalent). Non-negotiable. Budget: $350-500K.

2. **Retail ML Director** — owns demand forecasting, pricing, inventory optimization models. Someone who's shipped AI features in grocery or similar high-volume retail. Walmart Global or Amazon Retail ML. Budget: $250-350K.

3. **Data Platform Lead** — owns integration of 84.51° data into real-time systems. Someone who's built data pipelines at scale (Shopify, DoorDash, Instacart engineering). Budget: $250-350K.

**Tier 2: 6-month hiring wave (months 6-12)**

- 4-6 senior ML engineers (forecasting, personalization, supply chain specialists)
- 6-8 platform engineers (data infrastructure, agent orchestration, deployment)
- 2-3 retail domain experts (who understand what merchants actually need)

Total Tier 1 + Tier 2: ~$3.5M in annual salary + benefits. Non-trivial. Worth it.

**Recruitment playbook:**

1. **Direct recruit from Walmart** — have the CTO call Walmart Global Tech leaders, propose talent movement at senior levels. Offer: "Lead AI infrastructure at Kroger — board commitment, CEO backing, $50M budget, 18-month turnaround to parity with Walmart."

2. **Build external reputation** — publish engineering blog posts about hard problems Kroger is solving (demand forecasting at 2,800 stores, agentic supply chain). Get people talking. "Kroger is doing interesting stuff" becomes the narrative.

3. **Create a "Kroger Engineering Fellowship"** — partner with a bootcamp (Year Up, General Assembly) to recruit and train 10-15 junior engineers per cohort for placement into Kroger. Walmart does this. Create pipeline.

4. **Offer equity + bonus** — in a public company, restricted stock units at 4-year vest are powerful. Pair with annual bonuses tied to shipping milestones (10% of comp for hitting infrastructure targets).

5. **Protect new talent from KTD dysfunction** — stand up a "Platform and AI Division" separate from traditional KTD, reporting to the CTO, with its own operating cadence (weekly shipped features, not quarterly OKRs). Make it a place where things move.

**Retention strategy:**

- Quarterly all-hands from the CEO on AI progress and competitive positioning
- Direct access to the CTO for infrastructure decisions (no middle layer)
- Shipped features are visible (board reports on them, business celebrates them)
- No year 2 Transformation Management Office overhead — keep it lean

The Pfaffenberger archetype isn't rare. It's just not attracted to places known for slow movement. Change the reputation. Hire the talent. Move fast.
