# Greg Foran — Deep Oppo Research Dossier

**Date:** 2026-04-11
**Depth:** Deep

## Executive Summary

Greg Foran is a 64-year-old New Zealand-born retail operator who has spent his entire career in consumer-facing businesses — from stacking shelves at a Woolworths supermarket in Hamilton, NZ at age 17 to running the largest grocery chain in America. He is Kroger's first-ever externally hired CEO, appointed February 9, 2026, after an 11-month search following predecessor Rodney McMullen's forced resignation over an ethics violation. Foran's signature move is operational turnaround — he ran the same playbook at Walmart US (lower prices, invest in stores and people, fix fresh, drive comp sales) and is expected to repeat it at Kroger. The market loves the pick (KR +7.7% on announcement day). But his Air New Zealand tenure (2020–2025) reveals a more complicated picture: a hierarchical, command-and-control style that triggered a mass executive exodus, internal friction with NZ culture, declining stock performance, and the nickname "the grumpy grocer." If you go work for this man, know that you're getting a demanding, operationally obsessive, calm-under-pressure retail lifer who expects results and doesn't tolerate ambiguity — and who may struggle with cultural fit in organizations that don't share his intensity.

## Key Findings

1. **No university degree.** Foran never attended university. He holds a Diploma in Management from the NZ Institute of Management and completed executive programs at Harvard and UVA — but his education is the school of hard knocks in retail operations. [1][2]

2. **33 years at Woolworths, passed over for CEO.** He spent three decades rising through Woolworths Australia/NZ, was the leading internal candidate for CEO at age 48, and was passed over. That rejection sent him to Walmart. [2][3]

3. **"The greatest retailer on the planet" — Doug McMillon.** At Walmart US (2014–2019), Foran delivered 20 consecutive quarters of comp sales growth, remodeled 500+ stores, raised wages, and grew ecommerce 44% in a single year. WMT stock appreciated ~55-60% during his tenure. [4][5][6]

4. **He was also passed over at Walmart.** Despite elite performance, Foran was caught in a power struggle with ecommerce chief Marc Lore. He was never in line for the top Walmart Inc. CEO role. He left for Air NZ, framing it as a homecoming. [7]

5. **Air NZ was a mixed bag.** He navigated COVID (cutting 30% of staff and 85% of long-haul capacity), returned the airline to profitability by 2023, but stock declined every single year of his tenure. He later admitted he "cut too much staff in 2020." Five senior executives departed within months of his arrival. [8][9][10]

6. **His management style is polarizing.** Described as "slightly scary," a "quiet achiever," and an "operator's operator." Also called "the grumpy grocer" and "Grandad" internally at Air NZ. His North American hierarchical style clashed with NZ's more collegial corporate culture. [10][11]

7. **Kroger comp package: ~$17M+ annually.** $1.5M base salary, up to 200% annual cash incentive, $12M long-term equity grant, expatriate benefits, $200K/year in company aircraft use. First external CEO in Kroger history. [12][13]

8. **He's running the Walmart playbook at Kroger.** On his first earnings call, Foran signaled margin compression of 2-3 percentage points, reinvested into lower prices and employee compensation — the exact strategy that worked at Walmart US. [14]

9. **Family: wife Ondrea, four children.** Three older children (Natalie, Liam, Kieran) are adults based in Australia. Youngest son Ryan was born ~2014. Parents Pat and Glenda Foran are retired school teachers from Papamoa, NZ. [15][16]

10. **Estimated net worth: $50–110M.** Primarily from Walmart stock grants and executive compensation across a 45-year career. [17]

## Career Arc: Five Eras

### ERA 1: The Woolworths Apprenticeship (1978–2011) — "The Long Climb"

**Theme: Patient operator, built from the ground up, ultimately passed over.**

Greg Stephen Foran was born July 22, 1961 in New Zealand to Pat and Glenda Foran, both school teachers from Papamoa. He grew up in Hastings and Hamilton, attending Twyford School, St John's College (Hastings and Hamilton), and Hillcrest High School in Hamilton.

At 17, his mother pushed him to apply for a part-time job stacking shelves at a Woolworths supermarket in Hamilton. He got it. Three years later, at 20, he was managing one of the chain's stores. He never looked back — and never went to university.

Over the next 30+ years, Foran rose through virtually every operational role at Woolworths across New Zealand and Australia. He ran stores, districts, and ultimately major divisions of the company. By his late 40s, he was the presumptive heir to the Woolworths Australia CEO role.

Then, at age 48 (~2009), he was passed over. The board chose someone else.

This moment is foundational to understanding Foran. A man who spent three decades building inside one organization, was the best internal candidate, and got rejected. It clearly drove his next move and may shape how he thinks about loyalty, institutional politics, and proving doubters wrong.

**Compensation:** Not publicly disclosed for Woolworths roles. Australian executive pay at that level typically ranged A$2-5M in total comp.

---

### ERA 2: Walmart International — The Proving Ground (2011–2014) — "Going Global"

**Theme: Rapid ascent through international operations, earning the top US job.**

After the Woolworths rejection, Foran joined Walmart in 2011. The move took him from the Southern Hemisphere to the world's largest retailer. His trajectory was steep:

- **2011–2012:** Senior role in Walmart international operations
- **2012–2014:** President & CEO of Walmart China — managing one of the most complex retail markets in the world
- **Spring 2014:** Promoted to lead all of Walmart Asia
- **July 24, 2014:** Announced as President & CEO of Walmart U.S. — the crown jewel, overseeing 4,500+ stores and 1.6 million associates

Doug McMillon, who had just become Walmart Inc. CEO, handpicked Foran for the US role. The message was clear: Foran was an operator who could fix things, and US stores needed fixing.

**Stock context (WMT):** When Foran was announced as Walmart US CEO in July 2014, WMT traded at approximately $75-77/share.

---

### ERA 3: Walmart US CEO — The Glory Years (2014–2019) — "The Turnaround Artist"

**Theme: Operational excellence, wage investments, ecommerce growth — but politically outmaneuvered.**

This is Foran's masterpiece. When he took over Walmart US, the division was coming off five consecutive quarters of negative comp sales. Stores were tired, employees were demoralized, and Amazon was eating retail alive.

Foran's playbook was classic operator:

**Store operations overhaul:**
- Remodeled 500+ stores by 2017 — wider aisles, lower shelves, better sight lines
- Acknowledged publicly that ~50% of stores "needed improvement" (2015)
- Implemented "clean, fast, and friendly" service mandate
- Brought back Sam Walton's 10-foot rule (greet every customer within 10 feet)
- Visited nearly 200 locations personally
- Added 8,000 regional managers for localized accountability

**Employee investment:**
- Raised starting wage to $11/hour (2018), average full-time wage to $13.85/hour
- Built employee training academies
- This strategy was later published as a Harvard Business School case study (2025)

**Results:**
- Broke the 5-quarter comp sales decline streak
- Delivered **20 consecutive quarters of positive comp sales growth**
- Achieved "highest comp sales growth in nine years" by fiscal 2018
- U.S. ecommerce sales grew 44% in fiscal 2018

**The cost:** The February 2015 wage increase announcement spooked investors. WMT stock fell 10% in a single day, wiping out $21.5 billion in market value. The stock finished 2015 down 26.65%. But the strategy was vindicated: WMT recovered to +15.73% in 2016 and +47.54% in 2017.

Foran's Walmart comp over 5 years likely totaled **$60-75M** in salary, bonuses, and equity grants.

**The political failure:** Despite being called "the greatest retailer on the planet" by McMillon, Foran was never in the running for the top Walmart Inc. CEO job. The real tension was with Marc Lore, Walmart's ecommerce chief (brought in via the Jet.com acquisition). According to Jason Del Rey's book "Winner Sells All," Foran was "miffed" that Lore's division received public credit for growth that Foran's stores were driving. The relationship "soured" by July 2019. Foran departed October 2019, replaced by Sam's Club CEO John Furner.

Foran framed his exit as a desire to go home and lead something as the top executive — with "no one above him." The Air NZ opportunity presented itself.

### ERA 4: Air New Zealand CEO — The Complicated Homecoming (2020–2025) — "The Outsider at Home"

**Theme: COVID crisis manager, cultural misfit, mixed financial results, polarizing tenure.**

Foran took over Air New Zealand in February 2020. Within weeks, COVID-19 shut down global aviation. This era tests the "Foran as turnaround artist" narrative.

**COVID response (2020–2022):**
- March 2020: Cut 85% of long-haul capacity, significant domestic cuts
- Announced 30% workforce reduction (~3,500-4,000 jobs) and $150M wage bill reduction
- Grounded aircraft, closed routes, restructured for a much smaller operation

**His own retrospective (May 2025 interview):**
> "We cut too much staff in 2020... I think all of us had never experienced what it was like to shut down an airline, nor had we experienced what it was like to start one up — and my learnings on that is that it's a lot easier to shut it down than to start it up."

He said he would have "burned more cash" rather than cutting so aggressively, especially in call centres and engineering roles.

**Financial performance:**

| Year | Pre-Tax Profit (NZD) | Notes |
|------|---------------------|-------|
| 2020 | Significant losses | Border closures |
| 2021 | Significant losses | Continued restrictions |
| 2022 | Significant losses | Third year of losses |
| 2023 | ~NZ$574M | Return to profitability |
| 2024 | ~NZ$222M | -65% from prior year |
| 2025 | ~NZ$189M | Continued decline |

The 2024-2025 decline was driven by Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce engine supply chain issues, forcing Air NZ to ground up to 11 aircraft and cut capacity.

**Stock performance (AIR.NZ on NZX):**

| Year | Annual Return |
|------|--------------|
| 2020 | **-42.24%** |
| 2021 | **-13.88%** |
| 2022 | **-21.61%** |
| 2023 | **-15.07%** |
| 2024 | Continued weakness |

As of his departure, AIR.NZ traded at ~NZ$0.44 (52-week range: NZ$0.41-0.63). **Stock declined every single year of Foran's tenure.**

**Compensation at Air NZ:**

| Year | Total Comp (NZD) |
|------|-----------------|
| FY2022 | ~NZ$3.6M (~US$2.2M) |
| FY2023 | ~NZ$4.0M (~US$2.4M) |

A massive pay cut from Walmart-era earnings. Base salary was NZ$1.8M with the rest in bonuses.

**The cultural friction — this is the oppo research gold:**

Foran's hierarchical, North American management style grated badly with Air NZ's collegial culture:

- **Mass executive exodus:** Within months of Foran's arrival, five senior executives departed: Nick Judd (Chief Strategy), John Whittaker (Air Operations), Mike Tod (Marketing/Customer), Cam Wallace (Chief Commercial, 19 years tenure), and Jeff McDowall (CFO). Multiple sources said "McDowall simply could not work with Foran."

- **Internal nicknames:** Called "the grumpy grocer" and "Grandad" by staff.

- **Social misjudgment:** Declined to attend a farewell event for departing executives — described as a "snub" inconsistent with the airline's culture.

- **Toxic atmosphere:** Insiders reported "the tension is so thick you could cut it with a knife" in the C-suite.

- **Staff fear:** Employees worried about becoming "a Walmart of the Sky."

- **Aviation inexperience:** Analyst David MacKenzie publicly questioned whether Foran had the aviation-specific experience needed.

- **Saudi Navy contract (February 2021):** Air NZ's Gas Turbines subsidiary was found to be performing engine work for the Royal Saudi Navy. Foran claimed he was unaware. PM Jacinda Ardern ordered an investigation. Foran apologized to Parliament. PwC review found employees followed procedures but highlighted governance gaps.

**The counterpoint:** Union leaders initially reported favorable impressions. He visited staff at ground level, sought feedback from 1,200 airline leaders, and joined sustainability initiatives. And he did bring the airline back to profitability after three years of losses — no small feat in aviation.

**Departure:** Announced March 6, 2025. Effective October 20, 2025. Stated "the time is right" to "take on a new challenge." The Kroger appointment came four months later.

### ERA 5: Kroger CEO — The Redemption Play (2026–Present) — "One Last Turnaround"

**Theme: External hire into a wounded company, running the Walmart playbook one more time.**

**Context — what Foran inherited:**

Kroger was reeling from two blows:
1. **The failed Albertsons merger.** Kroger's $24.6 billion acquisition of Albertsons — announced October 2022 — was blocked by a federal judge siding with the FTC in mid-2024. Over two years of strategic planning, legal costs, and management distraction — for nothing.
2. **The McMullen scandal.** CEO Rodney McMullen (11 years in the role) resigned March 3, 2025, after a board investigation found his "personal conduct was inconsistent with Kroger's Policy on Business Ethics." The specific conduct was sealed but unrelated to business operations and did not involve Kroger workers. McMullen forfeited ~$11M in unvested equity and bonus.

Ron Sargent (former Staples CEO, Kroger lead director) served as interim CEO for 11 months while the board searched. Foran was announced February 9, 2026 — Kroger's first externally hired CEO ever.

**Market reaction:**
- KR jumped 7.7% to $72.67 on announcement day
- Barclays called it "best case scenario"
- Evercore ISI analyst Michael Montani praised Foran for "instant credibility"
- As of April 2026: KR trades ~$70-73, near all-time high of $75.60 (March 13, 2026)

**Employment agreement (SEC 8-K, February 9, 2026):**

| Component | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Base salary | $1,500,000/year |
| Annual cash incentive target | 200% of base ($3,000,000) |
| Long-term equity incentive target | $12,000,000/year (starting March 2026) |
| Special performance unit grants | $1,000,000 each for 2024-2026 and 2025-2027 LTI cycles |
| Company aircraft | Up to $200,000/year |
| Expatriate benefits | Included (NZ citizen) |
| Change-in-control severance | 2x salary + target bonus |
| **Estimated total annual comp** | **~$17M+** |

**Stated strategy (Q4 2025 earnings call):**
- Lower prices through margin compression (2-3 percentage points)
- Reinvest savings into lower shelf prices and employee compensation
- Improve store operations to attract more customers
- Leverage practical advice from store operations teams
- This is the Walmart playbook, word for word.

**Key challenges ahead:**
- Kroger's workforce is heavily unionized — a very different dynamic than Walmart
- Stretched consumer spending environment
- Need for significant capex to compete with Walmart, Amazon, and Costco
- No Albertsons merger synergies to lean on
- Managing as an outsider in a promote-from-within culture

## Leadership Profile — What It's Like to Work for This Man

**Strengths:**
- **Operational obsessive.** Visits stores constantly. Knows the details. Expects you to know them too.
- **Calm under pressure.** His own words: "I've learnt to panic slowly. In many cases if you can buy yourself a little more time you will find a better solution."
- **Results-driven.** 20 consecutive quarters of comp growth at Walmart speaks for itself.
- **Integrity-focused.** "Every short cut we take matters... you'll never need to look over your shoulder."
- **Curious listener.** Former colleagues say he has a "fascinating ability to listen, to add, and to provide inspiration and direction all at the same time."

**Risks:**
- **Hierarchical / command-and-control.** This is not a consensus-builder. He expects respect for the chain of command. At Air NZ, this "grated" with the more collegial culture.
- **Senior talent churn.** Five C-suite departures at Air NZ within months. If you're not aligned with his approach, you're out — or you leave.
- **Cultural blind spots.** His North American management style created real friction in New Zealand. At Kroger, he'll be managing a unionized workforce and a promote-from-within culture as the first outsider CEO. Watch for similar friction.
- **Not an innovator.** Foran is an operator, not a visionary. His playbook is proven but singular: fix stores, lower prices, invest in people, drive comp sales. If Kroger needs a digital transformation or strategic pivot, that's not his wheelhouse.
- **Ecommerce tension.** At Walmart, his relationship with ecommerce chief Marc Lore "soured." He may struggle to balance physical store operations with digital investment at Kroger.

**His own philosophy (Pinnacle Post, July 2025):**
> "From my experience, the most outstanding leaders earn their position through hard work, integrity, being curious and having an interest in the detail."

> "Attributes are often much more critical than skills. Skills are something you can teach and learn, while attributes are guidelines and features that are more inherent."

> "Darwin got it right... It's the one that is most adaptable to change."

## Implications for Brady

- **If you go work for Foran at Kroger**, you're signing up for an intense, operationally focused culture. He'll want to see results quickly. He values people who know the details, work hard, and don't need hand-holding.
- **Your ops/analytics/finance background is exactly what he values.** Foran doesn't come from a finance or MBA background — he respects people who bring analytical rigor to operational problems.
- **Watch the union dynamic.** Kroger's UFCW relationships are complex. Foran's Walmart background (anti-union reputation) could create internal tension. How he navigates this will define the early culture.
- **The first 12-18 months will be aggressive.** Expect cost reductions, margin compression, store remodels, and employee investment — all at once. It'll be intense.
- **Cultural fit question:** Foran is New Zealand-born, direct, and operational. He's not a "corporate politics" guy. If you thrive in a meritocratic, results-oriented environment with a demanding boss, this could be great. If you need consensus-building and political air cover, it might be rough.
