## The Ocado Thesis (From the Inside)

Ocado was always a bad idea. There was never anything more than crazy ideas to try and save it. They got extra money just for Florida — maybe $25M. The Ocado Zooms were doomed to fail. They wanted Kroger to spend millions on something that would lose money, while Instacart was offering to build comparable infrastructure on their own dime — profitable from day one.

It may not have been the right decision to do these things with Instacart, but with the cards on the table, **it was the only play.**

### What Was On the Table

- **Uber** offered ~$140M in "marketing funds"
- **DoorDash** — explored co-branded Dashmarts, etc.
- **Instacart** — wanted to deploy a fleet of hundreds of MFCs, all on their dime

I originally tried to spearhead the Instacart path.

### The Disintermediation Trap

I understand the risk of customer disintermediation, and I applaud the types of leaders who stand against prevailing winds, protect the brand/institution, and drive more customer loyalty long-term. But **Rodney got distracted by technology and forgot what people love about Kroger the most.** He spent billions trying to hold onto customer value he thought he owned, not realizing the market had already moved on.

### What Kroger Should Do Now

1. **Become the anti-big-FC grocer that has better food.** Capital investments must be geared around becoming relevant in AI — not legacy fulfillment infrastructure.
2. **Partner with Uber/DoorDash/Instacart** as the largest player willing to play ball. Leadership must think harder about this than Rodney did. He only thought about customer disintermediation and the loss of value in the long run — he missed the bigger picture.
3. **Sue Ocado** — but recognize that Ocado is going to be running out of cash before long. In reality, they'll probably face lawsuits soon and have to declare bankruptcy around the same time to protect what cash they have.
4. **Acknowledge the cost of cleanup.** Kroger is far, far behind on all of this, and it will cost hundreds of millions just to shut down the Ocado operations.

### Internal Support

I would come in with strong support from the internal team — including people like **Brittain Ladd**.
