What to Listen For

1. Tariff Math Update

  • The $800M number may move. The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariff authority in February 2026. The replacement — a 15% global tariff under Section 122 — may be lower or higher than the original IEEPA rates on specific categories. Listen for whether SBD revises the $800M figure up or down.
  • Section 232 metals tariffs (effective April 6): 50% on aluminum, 25% on derivatives. SBD uses aluminum extensively in housings and components. This is a NEW cost layer on top of the $800M.
  • STIHL read: Every tariff dollar SBD absorbs or passes through is a dollar STIHL doesn't face on 60% of its BOM. If SBD revises tariff exposure upward, the STIHL advantage gap widens.

2. Pricing Realization

  • +2% enterprise-wide pricing planned for FY 2026, weighted to H1. Q1 will show the first real data on whether consumers are absorbing the increases or trading down.
  • Listen for: Volume/mix commentary. If revenue is flat-to-down while pricing is +2%, that means units are declining — potential demand destruction from price increases.
  • STIHL read: If SBD signals consumer pushback on pricing, STIHL has the option to hold price and take share through the dealer channel. The tariff math gives STIHL room SBD doesn't have.

3. Outdoor Segment Transition

  • Gas-powered walk-behind outdoor lines shifting to licensing model mid-2026. Expected revenue hit: $120–140M for the year. Q1 may show early effects as retailers adjust orders.
  • DeWalt 60V MAX outdoor expanding aggressively: Top-handle chainsaw ($419–$579), backpack blower (160mph/855 CFM), hedge trimmers, pro string trimmer + edger (~$800 each). Premium pricing positions DeWalt as a contractor-grade outdoor brand.
  • STIHL read: SBD is simultaneously abandoning gas outdoor AND pricing battery outdoor at professional levels. Both moves create space for STIHL — gas loyalists lose a mass-market option, and DeWalt's $800 string trimmers make STIHL's dealer-channel battery offering look like the value play.

4. Gross Margin Trajectory

  • Q4 2025 adjusted gross margin: 33.3% (+210 bps YoY). Full-year target: ~150 bps expansion. But Q1 is the peak tariff quarter — margin expansion may stall or reverse temporarily.
  • Listen for: Whether the $2B cost transformation program is offsetting tariff headwinds or just treading water.

5. Channel Inventory & Consumer Demand

  • Q4 outdoor revenue was up 2% organically, driven by strong preseason ordering. The question is whether sell-through is matching sell-in, or if retailers are sitting on elevated inventory heading into spring.
  • Listen for: POS (point-of-sale) commentary, retailer inventory levels, and any signs of promotional activity to clear inventory — which would signal demand weakness.

Prediction Scorecard

Seven specific calls to score against the actual Q1 results. This is the system getting smarter — predictions that hit build credibility, misses sharpen the model.

P1 Q1 revenue comes in at or below the $3.7B guidance. Rationale: tariff-driven price increases are dampening consumer demand in the $200–$500 power tool range. Preseason outdoor ordering may have pulled forward some Q2 revenue into Q4 2025.
P2 Outdoor segment shows organic revenue decline in Q1. Rationale: gas licensing transition creates channel uncertainty. Retailers may be holding off on gas orders they know are moving to a licensed model mid-year.
P3 Management announces additional pricing actions for H2 beyond the +2% already planned. Rationale: Section 232 metals tariffs (effective April 6) create a new cost layer not fully captured in February guidance. Aluminum at $3,557/ton (+48.5% YoY) amplifies the pressure.
P4 DeWalt battery outdoor is highlighted as a growth bright spot. Rationale: the gas→battery pivot is the strategic narrative SBD wants to tell. 60V MAX outdoor line expansion will be framed as the future, even as gas outdoor revenue declines from the licensing shift.
P5 Tariff exposure number is revised — either up (adding Section 232) or recalculated under Section 122. Rationale: the IEEPA → Section 122 transition fundamentally changed the tariff math. Plus, Section 232 metals tariffs create a new exposure layer. The old $800M figure is almost certainly stale.
P6 Q1 gross margin expansion misses the full-year 150 bps pace. Rationale: management explicitly said Q1 carries peak tariff P&L expense. The $2B cost transformation program may not fully offset in the seasonally softest quarter.
P7 Management references supply chain diversification progress — specifically Mexico and US capacity. Rationale: the tariff environment makes onshoring/nearshoring a centerpiece of investor narrative.
