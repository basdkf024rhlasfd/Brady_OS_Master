## Executive Summary

This document lays out the go-to-market strategy for Brady Smallwood's fractional AI consulting practice. The core thesis: there is a massive underserved market of mid-market companies ($10M–$500M revenue) that see the AI opportunity but lack the internal bandwidth and cross-functional capability to capture it. Brady's differentiation is the rare combination of executive operational experience (COO, board member, GM) and hands-on technical building capability (automation, AI agents, data science)—not one or the other, but both in the same person.

The strategy follows a deliberate sequence: build credibility through free work with trusted contacts, convert those into paid engagements and portfolio pieces, then expand outward with content and community as air cover. The practice is structured to generate near-term consulting revenue while quietly building the infrastructure for a more ambitious platform vision (mception) that can scale beyond one person's time.

This is not a plan to build a venture-backed startup on day one. It's a plan to build a sustainable consulting practice that funds and validates the bigger idea. The timeline runs from March 2026 through year-end, with a major inflection point in May when public marketing goes fullbore.

## 1. Strategic Context

### 1.1 The Problem You Solve

Most mid-market companies have strategists who can't build and builders who don't understand the business. The person who can diagnose the operational problem is usually not the same person who can open a laptop and build the solution that afternoon. This gap is widening as AI capability accelerates faster than organizational structures can adapt.

The problems that AI solves best in these companies aren't the headline-grabbing moonshots. They're the hundred small-to-medium problems sitting on someone's to-do list—each one too small to justify a consulting engagement, too technical for the ops team, and too operational for the tech team. Collectively, they're eating the company alive.

### 1.2 Your Positioning

**Tagline:** General Manager · Strategic Analyst · AI Practitioner

You're not an AI consultant. You're an operator who builds. The distinction matters. AI consultants sell strategy decks. You sell working solutions built by someone who's sat in the seat—COO, board member, P&L owner—and understands what actually matters in execution.

**Key language constraint:** All materials should read as "selectively taking on engagements," not as having left corporate permanently. This keeps the door open for the Walmart (Archimedes) relationship and any future corporate opportunities.

### 1.3 Target Market

The primary target is mid-market companies in food, CPG, foodservice, and retail—industries where Brady has deep domain credibility and an existing network. Secondary targets include PE-backed portfolio companies (via the American Capital/Gauge Capital network) and any company in the $10M–$500M range that has operational complexity and no dedicated AI function.

The initial go-to-market is entirely network-driven. The first 44 outreach contacts are the primary channel. Everything else—LinkedIn, Substack, white papers, community involvement—is air cover designed to make those direct conversations land harder.

## 4. Messaging Architecture

Every piece of content and every conversation should reinforce the same core narrative. Below is the messaging hierarchy—from the one-line pitch down to supporting proof points.

### 4.1 Core Narrative

**Elevator pitch (10 seconds):** "I help mid-market companies solve operational problems with AI. I'm the person who can sit in the executive meeting AND open a laptop and build the solution that afternoon."

**Expanded pitch (60 seconds):** "After 16 years as an operator—finance, analytics, pricing strategy, eCommerce P&L, COO—I've always been the technical person in the executive room and the business person in the engineering sprint. Now I'm taking that combination and applying it to the AI problems that mid-market companies can see but can't solve. The ones too small for McKinsey, too technical for the ops team, and too operational for the tech team. I show up, understand the problem, and build something that works."

### 4.2 Key Messages

**Message 1: The Capability Gap**
Most companies have strategists who can't build and builders who don't understand the business. I'm both in one person. That's not common, and it's exactly what AI projects need right now.

**Message 2: The Bandwidth Problem**
The AI opportunity isn't one big project—it's a hundred small ones. Each too small to justify a hire or a consulting engagement. But collectively they're transformative. I handle them the way they need to be handled: fast, hands-on, one at a time.

**Message 3: Operator, Not Consultant**
I've been a COO, a board member, a P&L owner. I'm not giving you a strategy deck and walking away. I'm building the solution and making sure it works after I leave.

**Message 4: Lasting Value**
I build solutions designed to run with little to no maintenance. The value compounds long after the engagement ends. You're not renting my time—you're buying an asset.

### 4.3 Language Rules

These rules apply to ALL external communications—outreach messages, content, pitch deck, website, everything.

- **Use:** "Selectively taking on engagements." Direct, low-pressure, easy out. Confident and specific.
- **Avoid:** "Leveraging," corporate vocabulary, anything that reads as having permanently left corporate. No AI hype language.
- **Tone:** Human and rough, not polished and AI-generated. Reference standard: Brady's self-drafted Jorge Azevedo text (tight, personal, not templated).
- **Outreach pattern:** "Would love to catch up either way"—always give the person an easy out. Never make it feel like a sales call.

## 6. Team & Collaboration Model

### 6.1 Near-Term Structure

This is not a company. It's a network of people side-hustling together on a project-by-project basis. Everyone maintains their own thing. Brady is the hub. Deal flow comes through Brady. Work gets allocated based on fit and availability.

### 6.2 Compensation Framework (To Be Developed)

The near-term model is simple: Brady scopes and sells, collaborators deliver their piece, everyone gets paid per project. The long-term model needs more thought—particularly around:

- Revenue sharing on recurring/retainer engagements
- How to compensate collaborators who bring in their own deals vs. work on Brady-sourced deals
- IP ownership for tools and templates built during engagements
- What happens if the mception platform vision materializes—how do early collaborators participate

This doesn't need to be solved before May. It needs to be solved before the third or fourth collaborator joins.
