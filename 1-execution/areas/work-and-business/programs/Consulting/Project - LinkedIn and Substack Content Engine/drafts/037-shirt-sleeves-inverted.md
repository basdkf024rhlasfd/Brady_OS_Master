## LinkedIn Opinion Post

When I was a teenager, I found a stack of movie posters in the back of my dad's video stores — King Video, Southern Virginia — and started selling them on eBay. They were free. Just sitting there. I made thousands of dollars before the marketplace got flooded and the window closed.

Normal small-town hustle. Nothing special about it except the timing.

Now my nephew makes posters using AI. Not finding them in the back of a store. Generating them. Different tools, same instinct — see something no one else is paying attention to and move on it.

There's an old saying: "shirt sleeves to shirt sleeves in three generations." The idea that a family builds wealth, gets comfortable, and loses it. Earned, kept, lost.

But I'm watching something different happen. The tools are compressing the timeline in the other direction. A kid with a laptop and the right curiosity can now access capabilities that would've required a design firm and a print shop when I was selling those posters.

The barrier isn't capital anymore. It's not even knowledge — that's basically free now. The barrier is whether someone in the house shows you it's worth trying.

We need a new version of that saying. One that captures what happens when each generation doesn't just inherit the wealth — they inherit the tools to build faster than the last one did.

I don't know what the phrase is yet. But I know the pattern is real.
