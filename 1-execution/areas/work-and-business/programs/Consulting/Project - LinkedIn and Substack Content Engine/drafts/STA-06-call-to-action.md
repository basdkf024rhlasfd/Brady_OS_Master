I keep thinking about a question that won't leave me alone.

What if a thousand people read this post and went and did one small AI project for someone in their town?

Not a startup. Not a product. Just — a weekend.

Help a church put together an employee manual. Help a local gym make social media posts. Help your cousin's landscaping company draft better estimates. Build a simple tool for your neighborhood nonprofit.

I've done versions of all of these. Most take a weekend. Some take an hour.

I made a song for my local humane society. Took sixty minutes. PETA noticed. I built AI agents for a friend's real estate business. Took a weekend. Saves him six hours a week.

These aren't complex technology projects. They're conversations with a tool that's finally smart enough to help.

It reminds me of trying to set up Dropbox for my dad years ago. The value was so obvious to me — but the technology wasn't accessible enough for it to stick. That gap doesn't exist anymore. The tools meet you where you are now.

Your town has a hundred organizations that could use this kind of help. Your cousin has a business that's drowning in manual work. Your kid's school has a process that makes everyone miserable and could be fixed in an afternoon.

You don't need a product. You don't need a business plan. You don't need to learn to code. You just need to ask your AI a question and see what comes back.

So here's my ask: What's the nonprofit, the small business, or the neighbor in your town that could use a weekend of your time?

Go build something small. Tell me what happens.
