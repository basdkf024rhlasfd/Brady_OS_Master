## LinkedIn Story Post

My nine-year-old son got genuinely angry at an ice cream truck last week.

Not because he couldn't get one. Because of the margins.

"Dad, I can get a whole box of those from Walmart for what they charge for one bar." He's not wrong. So over spring break, he and his buddy sat down and vibe-coded an entire go-to-market strategy for an ice cream wagon business. Pricing. Routes. A marketing plan I actually had to review.

The only bottleneck? Adults. I have to get the dry ice. I have to approve the flyers.

Meanwhile, one of my daughters has been using AI to study Caitlin Clark's playing style — pulling up analyses of her footwork, her shot mechanics, building herself custom practice plans. She's nine.

Here's where it gets weird for me. I got in trouble at Southside Elementary for selling Jolly Ranchers on the school bus for a dime each. My dad Gene — he actually drove an ice cream truck at one point. So my kid wanting to run an ice cream business isn't new. The tools are new.

I don't have exact numbers, but I'd bet the average kid's ratio of Instagram hours to ChatGPT hours is way greater than one right now. I think that ratio needs to flip — and fast.

My kids aren't prodigies. They're just not afraid of the tools. That's the part worth paying attention to.

The generation that treats AI like a calculator instead of a magic trick is already here. They're just stuck waiting on us to buy the dry ice.
