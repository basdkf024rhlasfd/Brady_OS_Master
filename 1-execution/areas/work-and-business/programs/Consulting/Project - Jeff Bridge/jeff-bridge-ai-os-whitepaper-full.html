US ethane-based resin producers are the clear winners in this market. They run on natural gas liquids, not naphtha. While naphtha breached $1,000/MT, ethane-based US production costs moved much less. The result:

  • Dow (+25% YTD), LyondellBasell (+88% YTD): Market is pricing in their structural cost advantage. These companies — and their downstream resin distribution — are your most stable supply sources.
  • Naphtha-dependent imports: More expensive than domestic right now. The math has flipped — imported resin may actually cost more than domestic for the first time in years.
  • Westlake: They're a competitor AND a potential resin supplier (they sell resin to third parties). But they're also restructuring — closing North American vinyl facilities — which reduces available supply.

Historical Context

Two precedents are relevant:

  • 2008 oil spike: Crude hit $147 in July 2008. PVC resin spiked. When oil collapsed in H2 2008, PVC resin dropped 40-50% within 6 months. Fabricators who bought at the peak took significant inventory writedowns. But that was a demand-driven collapse — the financial crisis killed demand simultaneously. The current crisis is supply-driven with demand still intact (IIJA, data centers).
  • 2021-2022 pandemic disruption: PVC resin hit all-time highs due to freeze-related outages and demand surge. Prices corrected 30-40% over 18 months. The reversion was gradual, not sudden.

The current crisis is structurally different from both: it's geopolitical, not weather or demand-driven. Recovery depends on a political resolution that does not appear imminent. And demand remains strong (see Section 5). This is not a scenario where demand and supply both collapse simultaneously.

6. Freight

PVC pipe is heavy, bulky, and expensive to ship. Freight is your second-largest cost line after resin. Diesel surged 20.3% month-over-month in February 2026.

Oldcastle Infrastructure is piloting a TMS at Mansfield and Northlake, TX (announced Feb 2026). Unclear when that rollout reaches National Pipe.

The Opportunity

With two plants on the East Coast, every order is a routing decision: Endicott or Greensboro? The variables that determine optimal routing:

  • Customer proximity: Shorter haul = lower freight. But it's nonlinear with pipe dimensions and weight.
  • Product availability: If Greensboro is running the product and Endicott isn't, proximity doesn't matter.
  • Plant loading: If one plant has excess capacity, routing there may be cheaper total cost even with longer freight — because the alternative is overtime at the loaded plant.
  • Mode options: Rail vs. truck breakeven for long hauls. Rail saves 30-40% per ton-mile on lanes over 500 miles, but adds 3-7 days transit.
  • Backhaul matching: Pipe shipments are outbound-heavy. Carriers needing eastbound loads may offer better outbound rates.

A lane-by-lane freight analysis across both plants — benchmarking current rates against market, identifying consolidation opportunities, and optimizing plant-to-customer routing — would require shipment history data from National Pipe. With 12 months of shipment data (origin, destination, carrier, weight, cost), this analysis can identify 5-15% freight cost savings. On a heavy-freight business like PVC pipe manufacturing, that's material margin recovery.

Fuel surcharge audit: Carriers apply surcharges indexed to the DOE diesel price. When diesel moves 20% in a month, there's a lag before surcharges adjust — and sometimes they adjust asymmetrically (up fast, down slow). A surcharge audit against the actual diesel index often recovers overcharges.

9. Strategic Options That Changed Math in This Market

The resin spike makes several strategic options significantly more attractive than they were 60 days ago. These are not theoretical — each has specific data behind it.

PVC-O: Use 15-40% Less Resin Per Meter

Molecularly oriented PVC (PVC-O) aligns polymer molecules during manufacturing, producing pipe that uses 15-40% less resin per meter at higher pressure ratings.

The math at current resin prices: If resin is 60% of COGS and you reduce resin usage by 25%, you reduce material cost per unit by 15%. At pre-crisis resin prices, this was a nice-to-have. At current prices (+41%), a 15% material cost reduction is the difference between margin and no margin. PVC-O is not a sustainability play right now — it's a direct cost hedge.

Market status: PVC-O is well-established in Australia, Europe, and Latin America. US adoption is growing. AWWA has standards for PVC-O pipe. The gating factor is municipal acceptance and the capital investment in orientation equipment (12-18 month implementation timeline).

CPVC: Higher Margin Product on Existing Lines

Chlorinated PVC serves hot water, fire sprinkler, and industrial applications. Higher margin than commodity PVC pressure pipe. Some of it runs on existing extrusion equipment.

Data center connection: Some data center cooling applications use CPVC for hot water circuits. If you're already pursuing data center conduit, CPVC cooling pipe is a cross-sell opportunity.
