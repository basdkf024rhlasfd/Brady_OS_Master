M&A and Capital Strategy

B.1 The thesis in one paragraph

1915 South is already a roll-up, not a single-site retailer. Two closed deals in three years and a named VP of Corporate Development & Strategy mean the M&A muscle is in place. The Southeast is currently the single most target-rich region in the country for furniture M&A — Franchise Group's 2024 collapse emptied roughly 900 storefronts across 1915 South's core states; several multi-generation independents are at succession age; multiple Ashley licensees in adjacent territory are sub-scale; and Havertys, the public competitor, is executing a pure real-estate-scavenging expansion and leaving the customer-acquisition play on the table. 1915 South can credibly run two to three deals per year starting now, with a path to ~48 stores inside 18 months and a real-estate dividend that compounds the operating story.

B.2 Anchors

Roll-up cadence

Two acquisitions in three years (Fineman Jacksonville 2022, Yulee FL 2025). Stated path to ~48 stores in 18 months. The team has built the muscle.

Regional dislocation

~880 Southeast storefronts vacated 2024–2025 from Franchise Group / Badcock / Conn's / American Freight. Once-a-decade real-estate window inside the 1915 South footprint.

Manufacturer posture

Ashley CEO Todd Wanek: $2B innovation commitment, GMROI Excellence Framework Dec 2024, mainland China sourcing ceased April 2025. Resident Home acquired for ~$1B March 2024.

PE re-entry

B.4 Why now

  • Manufacturer cover. Ashley corporate's AI mandate creates top-down support for showcase licensees who move on operating-model modernization in tandem with footprint expansion.
  • Once-a-decade real-estate window. Box-for-box availability in the 1915 South footprint is the highest it has been in five-plus years and is unlikely to persist at this scale into 2027.
  • Operator-pricing reset. Distressed transaction comps have re-anchored what franchisee tuck-ins should clear at — often well below the prior multiple regime.
  • M&A velocity already exists. Justin's named VP Corp Dev creates a permanent owner of the pipeline; codifying the playbook now (while Fineman and Yulee are fresh) costs a fraction of what it will cost in 12 months.

B.7 The FRG / Badcock real-estate dividend

Between July 2024 and March 2025, Franchise Group and its consumer-retail subsidiaries collapsed: Conn's HomePlus (which had acquired Badcock in 2023) filed Chapter 11 in July 2024; Franchise Group parent followed in November 2024 with American Freight (357 stores) closing immediately. Net effect: roughly 900 retail storefronts vacated across the FRG footprint, with the customer base concentrated in low-prime credit tiers — the same customer segment Ashley HomeStores serve.

Real estate

Badcock's Mulberry FL footprint, Conn's TX/LA/FL/AL/GA footprint, and American Freight's national footprint sit on landlord re-lease lists at distressed terms. Havertys is already executing this play.

Customer base

FRG served roughly the same furniture customer as Ashley HomeStore. Those customers are now shopping somewhere; capture cost at re-banner is signage and local advertising rather than full LTV-led acquisition.

Talent

Experienced furniture-retail store managers, delivery drivers, and sales associates are available in 1915 South's core markets — an unusual window to upgrade bench depth without paying market premium.

The play. Treat the FRG-footprint dividend as a distinct sub-thesis inside the buy-side roll-up. Build a box-by-box opportunity map within a 45-mile radius of every existing 1915 South store. Score boxes on trade-area demographics, lease terms, and proximity to existing DC infrastructure. Target 3–5 new boxes in 2026, 5–8 in 2027. Capital requirements are modest — these are mostly lease take-overs; fit-out capex per box runs $500K–$1.5M with 24–36 month payback at historical store unit economics.

B.9 Strategic partnerships — universe map

The partnership lanes 1915 South can deepen or restructure, ordered roughly by strategic leverage. Each is a no-regret move that does not depend on a capital event.

Ashley corporate

The relationship is not a partnership in the contractual sense — it's the parent-company dynamic — but every licensee has a defined elevation path. Top-tier status brings preferred territory assignments, innovation-pilot access, and margin-friendly terms. The play: position 1915 South as Ashley's AI + GMROI showcase licensee. Propose a quarterly Wanek-level review meeting; bring specific metrics from the new operating layer to every one. This is positioning, not a pitch for capital.

Final-mile delivery

The Bassett / Zenith Freight Lines template (JB Hunt acquired Bassett's in-house delivery in 2017 with a long-term master services agreement) is the playbook reference. Possible structures: (a) contract a 3PL for all final-mile, (b) divest in-house delivery assets to a 3PL under a long-term MSA, or (c) maintain a dual-provider model for resilience. Data-driven SLAs on damage rate, first-time delivery success, and NPS as explicit contract terms.

Sleep / mattress vertical

Tempur Sealy / Mattress Firm closed February 5, 2025. Tempur Sealy committed to reserve 25% of MFRM floor space for third-party brands for five years (75% of that for >$1,500 mattresses) as a regulatory concession — a signal of strategic willingness to partner at scale with adjacent retailers. The play: a direct Tempur Sealy + 1915 South conversation about co-branded sleep destinations within Ashley stores, with preferred pricing and marketing support.

Consumer financing

The Koalafi / Synchrony waterfall (announced January 2026) is a single-application offer covering Synchrony store card + Koalafi lease-to-own — already live at The Wellsville Group (top-10 Ashley licensee) and Miskelly Furniture. The play: immediate adoption, with revenue-share mechanics integrated into the Ashley data platform so approval rates and lifetime value roll up into store-level P&L.

Data platform scope

Existing data foundation (Zapsight) is shipping the pipes layer well; agentic capabilities are being built on top. The opportunity is a clean RACI plus a refreshed SOW: explicit definition of what the data vendor delivers (pipes, ingestion, agents) versus what 1915 South owns (decision layer, strategy, vendor management). Data-ownership terms should give 1915 South full optionality.

B.10 Adjacencies and vertical integration

Ashley Care Plus — service as product

Innovation Workshop concept A5. 40K annual deliveries × 30% attach × $120 AOV ≈ $1.4M high-margin revenue. Furniture-industry attach rates run 30–50% at high-performing retailers during product presentation versus <10% at checkout — the lever is training and presentation, not the plan itself. Vendor options span Montage Furniture Services, Allstate Protection Plans / SquareTrade, Guardian Protection Products, OnPoint Warranty. Pattern reference: Best Buy Geek Squad runs 40–50% gross margin on $2B+ annual revenue, ~4% of total Best Buy revenue.

B2B / hospitality / contract channel

Ashley Contract, RH Contract, West Elm Contract, and Bernhardt Contract exist as dedicated B2B arms for competitors. A Southeast-focused contract arm could serve multi-family (Camden, Greystar), senior living (Watermark, Sunrise), student housing (American Campus, Scion), short-term rental (Vacasa, Sonder regional operators), and hospitality (Kimpton, InterContinental). Margin is thinner per unit, but volume is higher and more predictable, and it leverages existing DC and sales infrastructure.

Trade-in / circular pilot

AptDeco, Kaiyo, Chairish, FloorFound, and Revivn are developing retailer-partnership models. IKEA has run trade-in. A pilot offering trade-in credit toward new purchase within 90 days of delivery — with used product flowing through a dedicated outlet channel — is margin-neutral but drives repeat-purchase velocity.

B.13 Risks to monitor

  • Manufacturer approval mechanics. Every M&A move involving an Ashley licensee requires AFI approval; the contractual mechanics aren't public. Mitigation: run informal Wanek-level conversations on any material move early — not after LOI.
  • Integration capacity ceiling. Buyer-side bandwidth is the real governor on deal cadence. Mitigation: stage VP Corp Dev plus the redesigned Role Design + Applied AI seat before deal #2 and #3 close; model one new deal per integration-quarter as the ceiling.
  • Seller's market as rates ease. Lincoln International expects H2 2025 to be when momentum breaks loose; valuations on attractive targets may rise 0.5–1.5× EBITDA. Mitigation: run two deals in the next 12 months; do not wait for perfect.
  • FRG-footprint dividend is time-limited. Landlords will re-lease the vacant boxes across 2026–2027. Mitigation: 45-mile-radius opportunity map complete in 60 days; 3–5 boxes committed by end of 2026.
  • Tariff volatility. Lincoln International flags tariffs as the 2025 wildcard; impacts COGS on anything not domestically sourced. Mitigation: scenario-model 10% / 20% / 30% tariff increases on non-US product mix.

Section C · Role Design and Applied AI

Role Design and the AI Decision Layer

Role architecture for the seat sketched in Justin's April 20 note — a CFO-shaped role with technology and applied AI in scope, sitting above the existing data infrastructure rather than displacing it. The aim: a small set of crisp accountability statements, an applied-AI roadmap that compounds on existing investments, and a finance function that absorbs roll-up complexity without slowing it down.

C.1 Role anchors

Scope of accountability

Accounting and controllership (incl. inventory). Deal support and modeling. Entity design and structure. Management and operating framework. Applied-AI roadmap. Technology and IT.

AI decision layer

Existing data foundation provides implementation. The decision layer that turns data into operating choices and margin actions is the seat the role owns.

Manufacturer context

Ashley corporate (Theo / FrontlineIQ for floor coaching, AshleyDirect for ordering and assets) is operating at the floor and pipes layer. The 29-store decision layer remains an open seat.

GMROI directive

Ashley's December 2024 Excellence Framework is a positioning document with commercial enforcement (product access, co-op eligibility) — not a contractual numeric target.

C.2 The three operating layers

A useful way to read the AI landscape inside an Ashley licensee operation: floor, pipes, and decisions.

Floor layer · already shipping

Sales-rep coaching at the showroom (Theo / FrontlineIQ via Ashley AI Advantage). Lands inside store sessions, free at the licensee level. Useful and underutilized — but bounded.

Pipes layer · in build

Data foundation, ingestion, model and dashboard infrastructure. Largely owned by the existing data vendor. Fast progress recently; agentic capabilities being built on top.

Decision layer · open seat

The translation of data into operating choices that move GMROI, gross margin, sell-through, and labor productivity. Connects pipes to specific weekly and quarterly merchandising, pricing, inventory, and operations decisions. This is where the Role Design + Applied AI seat sits.

C.3 Year-1 outline

  • Inventory and GMROI lever map. 80/20 SKU audit, order frequency / depth tuning (Ashley's MOQ-of-1 enables this), structured promotional clearance design.
  • Pricing and assortment cadence. Weekly pricing rhythm with explicit decision rights and a single source of margin truth tied back to the data foundation.
  • Last-mile economics. Independent assessment of the in-house delivery operation against 3PL alternatives at current and post-acquisition footprint scale, focused on capital and complexity not just unit cost.
  • Finance modernization. Faster close, cleaner inventory accounting, and a forecasting cadence that holds up post-AGR integration.
  • Applied-AI roadmap. Sequenced capabilities (call-center triage, demand forecasting layer above corporate, agentic decision support for merchants, etc.) with explicit coordination with the existing data vendor.

C.4 Why this role exists now

  • Two-side mandate from Ashley corporate. Both the AI commitment and the GMROI directive create a multi-year window where modernization and franchisee economics are explicitly on Wanek's stage.
  • Roll-up scale. A path to ~48 stores in 18 months changes the finance, IT, and decision-cadence requirements faster than incremental hiring can absorb. The role exists to absorb that complexity.
  • Vendor seam. The pipes layer is well underway; the decision layer is the next build. The role exists to turn pipes into outcomes, not to rebuild pipes.

C.6 What the role explicitly does not own

  • Replace the existing VP of People or VP of Sales / Operations.
  • Store-level HR policy, labor relations, or compensation committee.
  • Marketing execution (a VP Marketing exists).
  • Project-managing the data vendor (vendor-side role; managed but not owned by this seat).
