## The carved-out Badcock precedent (for the M&A brief)

The **"Badcock of South Florida"** story is worth studying independently:

- Original Badcock was a dealer network — individual dealers owned their stores, Mulberry corporate was the nerve center
- When corporate collapsed Oct 2024, the South Florida dealer entity (W.S. family operated) retained independent ownership of 10 stores and continued operating under the Badcock brand name
- This is a model for **how a regional operator can carve out licensee operations from a failing parent** — relevant precedent if any Ashley licensee ever goes distressed

**M&A implication for 1915 South:** if another Ashley licensee is in trouble, 1915 South could potentially acquire operating stores without inheriting the parent entity's liabilities. Precedent now exists at ~10-store scale.

## Known limits of this research

- **Lease vs owned:** most FRG boxes were leased; the question of which landlord holds each lease and whether it's available varies box-by-box
- **B. Riley Retail Solutions role:** B. Riley is managing some of the liquidation and is itself under SEC investigation — could complicate direct-to-landlord negotiation if B. Riley still has influence over lease assignments
- **Competitor activity:** Havertys is already pursuing a similar real-estate strategy (Joann / Big Lots / Bed Bath & Beyond boxes). Some of the best FRG boxes may already be under LOI or leased.
- **Trade-area risk:** FRG's customer was non-prime credit. A box that served non-prime Conn's customers may or may not map to 1915 South's customer base — requires demographic analysis per box, not just geographic proximity.
