## Detailed Analysis

### 1. Ashley Furniture Industries — Company & Manufacturer Context

Ashley Furniture Industries is one of the largest furniture manufacturers in the world, headquartered in Arcadia, Wisconsin. Revenue estimates vary by source: $4.2B annually (2026 estimate, ECDB) vs $11B total revenues cited in a November 2024 report, likely reflecting consolidated family holding company revenue including retail affiliates. The company is privately held and family-run — Ron Wanek founded it, his son Todd Wanek serves as President & CEO.

The company runs ~1,075 HomeStore locations globally, all individually owned by franchisees/licensees. 1915 South is one of the larger US franchisees. Todd Wanek's key imperatives for the retail network are clear: GMROI optimization, AI adoption, and e-commerce growth ($348M on ashleyfurniture.com in 2024). He has been vocal that the next 3-5 years are existential for AI adoption.

Ashley was named to Fortune's America's Most Innovative Companies 2025 based on product, process, and innovation culture. This is important context for any conversation about what the brand expects from its franchisee operators.

Sources: [6][7][10][14]

### 2. Todd Wanek — Leadership Style & Strategic Bets

Wanek's operating philosophy is captured in one phrase: "Skate to where the puck is going to be." He prizes humility, curiosity, honesty, and continuous improvement — not flashy pivots. His communication style is warm (weekly "Gratitude Sunday" emails to the organization during hard times) and metrics-driven (he cites specific ROI figures, forecast accuracy improvements, job creation numbers).

His three current strategic bets are: (1) domestic manufacturing expansion and supply chain diversification away from China, (2) aggressive AI/automation investment — not just robots, but agentic AI across forecasting, AP, scheduling, and e-commerce, and (3) retailer success through GMROI discipline. He is not a trends chaser; he frames AI as table stakes infrastructure.

This matters for Brady because any role or engagement touching the Ashley franchise network will operate inside a performance culture with explicit KPIs and a CEO who pays attention to store-level economics.

Sources: [8][9][11][15]

### 3. Justin Woods — Role, Background, Relationship

Justin joined 1915 South as President in 2025. He is accountable for strategy, growth, and operational performance. His prior career is analytically dense: Head of Pricing Product at Walmart (2016-2019), Omnichannel Growth & Pricing at Advance Auto Parts (2020-2021), VP Omnichannel Pricing Strategy at Ashley Global Retail (2021-2023). U of Michigan Ross MBA.

His move to 1915 South President is significant — he went from a corporate VP role inside Ashley Global Retail to running the largest region-specific Ashley franchisee. This suggests he either has an equity/ownership stake, or sees a path to one, and/or was recruited by Russell Turner specifically to build the management layer the company has been missing.

Brady's connection to Justin is through their shared background: both have deep analytics, pricing, and retail ops roots. Justin knows the Walmart pricing stack; Brady knows retail analytics and COO-level operations. The language will match immediately.

Sources: [1][2][3][4]

### 4. Russell Turner — Owner & CEO of 1915 South

Russell Turner is the second-generation owner of what was originally a hardware/general retail business founded in 1915 in Pelham, GA. He returned to the family business in 1991 and transformed it into a furniture retail powerhouse. He opened his first Ashley HomeStore with his father Scott on January 1, 2002 — now 29 stores strong, ranked #50 in the US.

Russell is described as a civic leader and Thomasville community anchor. The organization has a deep Southeast identity. He brought in Justin Woods in 2025 as President — the first time the company appears to have had a non-family president — which signals a maturation of the management layer and likely readiness for more structured growth.

For Brady: Russell is the decision-maker. Any FTE or advisory engagement at 1915 South runs through him. Justin is the internal champion who can get Brady in the room.

Sources: [4][5][16]

### 5. Ashley Competitive Landscape & Industry Signals

Ashley's primary retail competitors: Rooms To Go, Havertys, La-Z-Boy, Value City, and Wayfair. At the price/segment level where 1915 South plays (mainstream to upper-mainstream), the competition is regional and fragmented. The national players are struggling with the same headwinds: post-pandemic furniture demand normalization, inflation-squeezed consumers, and China tariff disruption.

Industry-wide AI tailwinds are large: 89% of retailers now actively use or pilot AI. In furniture specifically, AI is reshaping demand forecasting, pricing, in-store AR (virtual room planning), and supply chain. 1915 South — like most regional multi-location furniture retailers — is significantly behind the curve on this. The gap between what Ashley Industries is doing at the manufacturer level and what a 29-store regional franchisee has operational capacity to implement is Brady's opportunity.

The GMROI pressure from Ashley corporate will intensify. As the manufacturer shifts away from China sourcing and experiences cost pressure, they will push GMROI metrics harder onto franchisees. 1915 South's trajectory of rapid store acquisition (29 stores now vs. fewer 3 years ago) means operational complexity is outpacing system maturity — a classic inflection point for bringing in outside ops/analytics talent.

Sources: [10][12][13][17]

## Implications & Recommendations

1. **The opportunity is with 1915 South (Russell Turner's franchisee), not Ashley Industries corporate.** This changes the scope and compensation expectations significantly — a regional $100M+ retailer, not an $11B manufacturer. Brady should calibrate role expectations accordingly.

2. **Justin is the path in, Russell is the yes.** Brady should use the Justin relationship to generate a warm intro conversation, then aim to meet Russell directly. Any FTE or material advisory role will require Russell's conviction.

3. **Brady's most credible pitch is operational analytics + AI enablement.** Justin's gap (going from corporate analytics to running a full retail P&L) is exactly where a COO-level operator with AI experience adds immediate value. Brady fills the operating layer that Justin doesn't have.

4. **GMROI is the language to use.** Ashley corporate is laser-focused on GMROI at the store level. Walking into any conversation using this framework signals institutional alignment and credibility.

5. **The Innovation Workshop angle is real.** A Product + Operations Innovation Workshop for 1915 South would be a natural consulting engagement — helping them identify where AI and process improvement can drive GMROI. This is a $25-50K consulting entry point that could convert to an FTE or ongoing advisory.
