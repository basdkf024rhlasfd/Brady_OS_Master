Read on succession. Sam Zeidan started in independent furniture retail in 1988. He is in his mid-60s. The pattern of new holdco formation late in an operator's career — particularly with a Roanoke-side counsel as agent — is consistent with three scenarios: (a) preparing for a sale or recapitalization, (b) succession planning to family / next-gen, or (c) consolidating multiple operating LLCs for tax/estate purposes. Any of the three are entry windows.

Read on legal posture. No criminal records, no federal litigation, no state court litigation, and no BBB complaint patterns surface against Bassam Zeidan, Bob Young, or any of their corporate vehicles. AFHSVA and Providence both maintain BBB profiles in good standing. This is a clean operator universe.

2. AFHSVA / Bassam "Sam" Zeidan — the deep file

Identity & origin

  • Legal name: Bassam Zeidan. Goes by "Sam."
  • Heritage: Lebanese descent. The Zeidan surname is common across Lebanese, Palestinian, and Levantine diaspora communities. Multiple unrelated Zeidan families operate in Virginia (Leesburg, Virginia Beach, Hampton, Sterling) — these are NOT Sam's branch.
  • Home base: Hanover County, Virginia (Mechanicsville). Hanover County government describes him as a "longtime Hanover resident and businessman." He has lived there long enough to be considered native by local press.
  • Career arc:
  • Pre-1988: Furrier retail (the fur trade — coats, accessories). The 2009 Roanoke Times profile is explicit: "Before furniture, he was in the furrier retail business." This is unusual and worth noting — fur retail tracks closer to high-end specialty than mass furniture, suggesting Zeidan came up in a relationship-driven, high-margin operating model.
  • 1988: Enters independent furniture retail. Opens Home-Makers Furniture in Mechanicsville (~20,000 sq ft). Still operating today at 8235 Mechanicsville Turnpike.
  • 2008: Founds CanonSkye Corp. — incorporated in Virginia March 30, 2009; alternate names include "Canons Skye Corp."; operating since 1/1/2008.
  • April 2009: Acquires the failed Roanoke Ashley HomeStore (45,000 sq ft) at 5004 Valley View Blvd NW. The prior franchisee had collapsed in late 2008, leaving customers with paid back-orders and no merchandise. Ashley Furniture Industries promised to deliver back-orders directly. Zeidan stepped in, agreed to honor the prior store's one-year warranties, and rehired some prior staff. He did not disclose the acquisition price.
  • September 2010: Opens Lynchburg Ashley (2116 Wards Road, next to Burlington Coat Factory).
  • February 2012: Opens Harrisonburg Ashley (1866 E. Market St) — described in local press as his "third Ashley furniture storefront."
  • 2019–2024: Continues organic expansion. By 2026 the network is 8–9 stores (Roanoke ×2, Lynchburg, Harrisonburg, Staunton, Waynesboro, Blacksburg, Bluefield, Wytheville).
  • September 19, 2019: Forms Wythe Furniture, LLC at 7424 Bell Creek Rd, Mechanicsville (a corporate / warehouse address adjacent to the original Mechanicsville store). Likely vehicle for the Wytheville location.
  • September 25, 2025 — six months before this report: Forms The Zeidan Group, LLC at the same 7424 Bell Creek Rd address, with Compton M. Biddle Esq. of Roanoke as registered agent. This is a holding-company structure, not operational. The forming of a holdco late in an operator's career is the single strongest entity-level succession signal available in Virginia public records.

The 2025 AED donation — the philanthropy signal

In February 2025, Zeidan donated $50,000 to Hanover County, Virginia, to fund ten AEDs in county parks. The donation was triggered by hearing about a 74-year-old, Jack Mullikin, who had cardiac arrest playing in a Hanover Senior Softball League game at Pole Green Park and was saved by an existing AED. Zeidan heard the story from Hanover Supervisor Sean Davis, who he describes publicly as "my friend." The county fast-tracked installation across all ten parks.

This data point matters for three reasons:

  1. Liquidity. $50,000 in cash is consistent with an operator generating $5–10M+ in pre-tax personal income from a $50–80M revenue retail portfolio.
  2. Civic anchor. His public identity is rooted in Hanover, not Roanoke, despite Roanoke being his largest store. His personal community is Mechanicsville-Hanover. Outreach paths in his hometown will land harder than in his store footprint.
  3. Relationship style. He calls a county supervisor "my friend." He responds to specific human stories rather than impersonal asks. Approach paths should be personal-narrative, not corporate-deck.

3. Providence Enterprises / Bob Young — the comparison set

Providence is positioned in this dossier not as a target, but as the structural and behavioral peer — the operator who's already done what AFHSVA's owner might be considering doing next: scaled an Ashley footprint via tuck-in acquisitions.

Identity & origin

  • Robert "Bob" Young — Founder, Owner, CEO, "Team Leader" (his preferred internal title) of Providence Enterprises.
  • Born and raised in Morristown, Tennessee. Mother and sisters still live in the area. Personal narrative is rooted in East Tennessee homecoming.
  • Education: MBA from University of Tennessee–Knoxville (1991–1992).
  • Pre-furniture career: Lane Home Furnishings (the Tupelo, MS-based legacy furniture brand, now part of Heritage Home), Product Manager from January 1999 through June 2005 — six and a half years. Came up on the manufacturing side, not retail. This makes him operationally fluent in product, supply chain, and category management — not just floor sales.
  • Home: Teays Valley / Scott Depot, West Virginia.

Growth philosophy (Bob Young's own words): "We try to grow opportunistically; we don't force it. We love the idea of growing, but we're not growing for the sake of growing. We're doing it because it makes sense."

The Zak's deal — the tuck-in template

The August 2025 acquisition of Zak's Home is the mechanical template for any Ashley licensee tuck-in in this region:

  • Independent operator with retiring owners (Scott & Jennifer Bowman)
  • Geographic adjacency to acquirer's existing footprint ("It's in our backyard in East Tennessee")
  • Operator-to-operator personal relationship pre-dating the deal ("had a few interactions with them over the years")
  • Speed: "48-hour courtship before we shook hands"
  • Owner rollover into the integration ("Scott Bowman will remain on board for a while to help in the transition")
  • Cultural framing ("They liked us and felt we would be good stewards of the family business")
  • ERP migration to Storis (industry standard for Ashley operators)

Read for any operator considering a tuck-in: The deal closed because Bob Young had earned the seller's trust by being a known, respected adjacent operator before the deal happened. The deal structure honored the seller's legacy. The valuation was below-market by traditional standards — Young got it because he was the right buyer, not the highest one.

Vision and values

Providence's stated vision: "Be Prosperous and Giving." The corporate identity is explicitly Christian — "bringing honor and glory to God" — and the Hope to Dream charitable program (donating beds to children in need) is deeply integrated into store operations. Any conversation with Bob Young that does not engage the values dimension will land flat.

Providence has only two Virginia stores: Bristol and Wise. Both are in the far southwestern tip of Virginia, geographically aligned with East Tennessee, not the Roanoke/Lynchburg/Danville corridor. Their distribution centers in Nitro WV and Gray TN do not naturally service Central or Southern Virginia. Providence is not a competitor to AFHSVA in any meaningful corridor. The two operators co-exist with negligible territorial overlap (Bluefield is the closest pair-point, on the WV/VA border).

4. Trivett's Furniture — the central Virginia wildcard

A previously under-recognized operator. Per the Home News Now 125 list, 2024 ranking #82:

  • Revenue: $106.7M (2024), up from $100.7M (2023) — +6.0% growth in a sector where 75% of Top 100 retailers posted negative numbers
  • Stores: 13 total (12 in 2023). Operates one Trivett's branded store + 11 Ashley HomeStores + 1 Ashley Outlet
  • Headquarters / footprint: Central and Eastern Virginia
  • Recent expansion: January 2024 — opened a new Ashley in Colonial Heights, VA (Petersburg metro) in a former Toys R Us building. Aggressive real estate — repurposing dead big-box retail into Ashley showrooms is exactly the FRG-collapse playbook adjacency.

Read. Trivett's is the largest Ashley operator in Virginia by revenue. They are growing while peers contract. They have a Trivett's-branded legacy store + Ashley licenses. They sit in the geographic gap between AFHSVA (SW) and Broad River (NoVA).

No public-record link to Zeidan. No common counsel, no overlapping LLCs, no shared management. This is a separate, parallel operator.

Surface area for research follow-up: Trivett's is meaningfully larger than AFHSVA on revenue ($106.7M vs ~$70M est.) but operates a comparable store count (13 vs 8–9). Their per-store productivity is higher. Worth a separate deep-dive if expansion strategy contemplates Central VA.

6. The Corrie Teague Bobe / Danville Regional Foundation angle

This is the highest-leverage relationship dimension in this dossier.

Who she is

Corrie Teague Bobe (note: Corrie with two r's, not Cori) is a native of Danville, Virginia. Pamplin College of Business (Virginia Tech) graduate, Marketing Management. Joined the City of Danville Office of Economic Development in November 2009 as a marketing and research manager. Ascended through every level of the department — project manager (2012), assistant director (2015), interim director (early 2020), director (July 2020).

What she did at the City of Danville (2009–2026)

In her 16+ years at the city, she landed:

  • Caesars Virginia $650M resort casino (open late 2024)
  • Tyson Foods new manufacturing facility
  • AeroFarms ~140K-sq-ft aeroponics farm at Cane Creek Centre (158 jobs)
  • Avio USA manufacturing
  • Microporous manufacturing
  • Staunton River Plastics manufacturing
  • River District redevelopment — multiple branding processes, $30M+ private investment, 180+ jobs in restored historic buildings
  • Dan River Falls — $85M public-private mixed-use redevelopment of the shuttered White Mill textiles factory
  • Danville tourism program (created 2022)
  • 5,600+ acres of industrial land developed and marketed

She has been on the Virginia Business 500 Power List for five consecutive years (2020–2024+) and was a 2025 Women in Leadership honoree (one of 25 statewide).

What just changed — March 25, 2026

She announced her resignation from the City of Danville to take a new role at the Danville Regional Foundation (DRF) as Director of Economic Development and Regional Collaboration. The DRF service area is Danville, Pittsylvania County (VA), and Caswell County (NC) — a tri-county footprint covering exactly the Southern Virginia / North Carolina border corridor.

DRF is a regional foundation that funds nonprofits and government entities pursuing economic development in the Dan River region. It has a long track record of co-investing with city and county economic development agencies to attract new businesses. The 2019 Regional Economic Development Strategic Plan was produced jointly with the City of Danville and Pittsylvania County.

Why this matters strategically

  1. She is the most influential economic development professional in Southern Virginia. Five years on the Virginia 500. Caesars + Tyson + AeroFarms is a track record almost no economic developer in a city of Danville's size has matched.

  2. Her new role is regional, not municipal. As a city director, her influence stopped at the Danville city line. As DRF's regional director, her remit covers Pittsylvania County, which surrounds Danville on three sides, and Caswell County NC just across the state line. Any Ashley footprint in Danville is now in her region of focus. Any expansion in Lynchburg → Roanoke is adjacent to her work and benefits from her introductions.

  3. DRF can co-invest in economic development. Per their FAQ, they will partner with governmental entities to recruit businesses where their funds and city/county funds can be combined. This is unusual for a regional foundation — it gives Bobe direct influence on incentive packages.

  4. She is now non-governmental. Her conversations are no longer constrained by procurement law, ethics statutes, or political optics. She can have informal strategic conversations that a sitting city director cannot.

  5. She is a personal relationship. A high school connection from many years ago is the rarest kind of warm intro — pre-professional, pre-transactional, identity-based. This is the strongest single relationship advantage in the entire Virginia operator universe.

What to do with the relationship

The Bobe relationship is strategic, not tactical. It is not a path to Sam Zeidan directly (different geographies, different industries). What it IS:

  • A direct line into the Danville business community's collective intelligence about who is doing what, who is succession-stressed, who is liquidity-stressed
  • A potential co-investor if any future expansion in Pittsylvania County or Caswell County wanted local incentive support
  • A credibility multiplier for any Southern Virginia retail expansion conversation
  • A referral source to her successor at the City of Danville Office of Economic Development (currently a vacancy as of late March 2026 — new director needed)
  • A connection to The Launch Place, Danville Pittsylvania County Chamber of Commerce, and the Industrial Development Authority of Danville — all of which she has worked closely with

The right opening conversation is not "I want to acquire Zeidan." The right opening is "I'm watching the corridor between Roanoke and Danville. What do you see from where you sit, and where would I be useful?"

8. Recommendations — what to do with this

This dossier is reconnaissance only. No outreach should be initiated until:

  1. A clear strategic intent is set — is the goal acquisition, partnership, intelligence-gathering, or relationship-building?
  2. The right entry path is selected — Hanover personal, Roanoke professional, Danville regional, or industry-peer
  3. A go-slow posture is adopted — Sam Zeidan is a legacy seller, not a financial seller. Bob Young is a deeply religious, values-driven operator. Both will reject any approach that feels transactional.

Three concrete next-step recommendations

  1. Open the Corrie Bobe relationship before the DRF transition fully settles. She starts her DRF role any day. This is the right moment to reconnect and ask what she's seeing across the Dan River region. The conversation pays off on every dimension: relationship maintenance, regional intelligence, Danville-anchored credibility, and a soft pre-introduction to anything that develops in Southern Virginia retail.

  2. Develop a peer-credibility relationship with Bob Young at Providence. He is the structural template for anything that might happen with Zeidan. He has done the playbook. He is publicly accessible. A learning conversation with him — operator to operator — is low-risk, high-information, and builds the standing required to ever credibly evaluate a tuck-in deal of comparable structure.

  3. Triangulate Sam Zeidan through Hanover, not Roanoke. His civic anchor is in Hanover County. The most-natural path into his trusted circle is local — Hanover Supervisor Sean Davis, Hanover County Chamber, possibly through a charitable / civic engagement framing rather than a commercial one. Patience matters. The Zeidan Group LLC was formed only six months ago. If something is going to happen, it isn't this quarter — it's 18–36 months from now.
