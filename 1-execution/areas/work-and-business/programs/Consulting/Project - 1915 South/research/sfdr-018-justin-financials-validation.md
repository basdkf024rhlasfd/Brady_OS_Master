## Executive Summary

1. **The 13.5% EBITDA margin Justin is showing is at the very top of the public furniture-retail comp set and is structurally above Havertys (HVT FY25: ~10% adj. EBITDA) and Ethan Allen.** It is plausible only because 1915 South does not carry public-company overhead, runs an owned-DC model that is partially absorbed in COGS rather than SG&A, and operates Ashley's higher-margin SSB/America's Mattress vertical inside a single P&L. The number is real but unusual — a private operator's premium, not a benchmark sustainable for a public peer. Top quartile for an Ashley licensee at this scale is a credible 12-14% range; 13.5% sits at the top.

2. **The 2024 → 2025 EBITDA jump ($17.5M → $25M, +43%) on roughly flat revenue almost certainly came from three things, in order: (a) FY24 was depressed by the Thomasville HQ DC ramp + ERP-pre-launch costs, not by a structural margin gap; (b) Ashley GMROI / mattress mix expansion lifted gross margin 100-200 bps; and (c) Yulee/SS adjustments + expense control closed the rest.** The 2025 number is the real baseline. The 2024 number is the noisy comparison year. Brady should anchor every margin conversation to 2025, not the YoY delta.

3. **The 14% AGR target is aggressive but achievable inside 12-18 months, not in year one.** AGR is being acquired from Ashley corporate (per Justin's email — "they will also be acquired from Ashley") — meaning these are stores currently underperforming the licensee top-quartile because Ashley itself is a structurally worse retail operator than a focused licensee. The integration uplift (operations, expense rationalization, mattress attach, owned-DC absorption) is the standard post-licensee-takeover playbook. Fineman 2022 and Yulee 2025 are the precedents. **Realistic 2026 AGR EBITDA: 9-11%. 14% is the steady-state the second year, not the first.**

4. **The "Total Store Operating at exactly 4.9% across all scenarios" is a model artifact, not real-world physics.** Store payroll has meaningful operating leverage above $4.5-5M revenue per store. At the aggressive scenario ($339M / 42 stores = $8M+ avg revenue/store), real Total Store Operating should compress to 4.4-4.6% of sales, adding **~$1.0-1.7M of additional EBITDA** Justin's model doesn't capture. The 13.5% margin floor is genuinely a floor; the upside in the aggressive case is 50-100 bps higher than what's in the table.

5. **At $34M post-AGR EBITDA (Aug 2026 run-rate), $700K all-in maps to ~2.06% of EBITDA. At $38M post-all-deals (mid-2027), it maps to ~1.84%.** A $400K base + 0.9-1.0% of EBITDA bonus + LTI of ~$200-250K annual issuance gets to $700K cleanly at the $34M EBITDA mark. That is the negotiating anchor. Anything below it requires an LTI compensator that puts the structural ceiling materially higher.

### 1.1 Is 13.5% sustainable at this footprint?

**Read:** 1915 South's 13.5% sits **above** Havertys (the closest structural comp at ~10%), **above** Ethan Allen, and **at parity** with Arhaus (which has a fundamentally different premium-custom model). This is a real outlier on the high side of furniture retail.

**What makes it credible:**
- **No public company overhead.** SOX, IR, public-board comp, audit fees, public-company legal — Havertys carries 100-150 bps of margin Justin doesn't. That alone closes most of the gap.
- **Owned-DC model absorbs distribution into COGS rather than SG&A.** Per SFDR-015, ~$23-25M of distribution cost is in the P&L; in a pure-resale licensee, this would be a separate line, not buried in the gross margin calc. The 55.4% gross margin in Justin's table is consistent with this — Havertys at 60.7% has different cost geography, not a structurally better business.
- **SSB / America's Mattress vertical mix.** Bedtimes Magazine / Ashley have publicly stated Ashley's bedding strategy is built around GMROI uplift through inventory turn at key price points. A licensee with disciplined mattress attach gets 200-400 bps of margin lift over a furniture-only operator.
- **Family-owned operator discipline.** No quarterly Wall Street pressure means no margin-destroying promotions to hit a number. This is structurally worth 50-100 bps over time.

**What makes it suspect:**
- **Top-quartile Ashley licensee benchmark is ~14%.** SFDR-003 estimated this directionally; the public peer set above suggests 12-14% is the realistic range, not 14%+. Sitting at 13.5% means 1915 South is already at the top — there is very little room above.
- **Justin's "all scenarios held at 13.5%" is the tell.** Any operator who genuinely believed margin scaled with size would model 14.0-14.5% at $339M. The fact that he held it flat means either (a) he is being honest that there's no operating-leverage assumption baked in, or (b) the 13.5% has hidden risk he isn't surfacing.

**Verdict:** Believable for one or two years, defensible against any reasonable peer, but not a number that gets better without active work. Brady should treat 13.5% as the steady-state ceiling, not the floor for a stretch case.

### 1.2 The 2024 → 2025 EBITDA Jump

**The math:**
- 2024: $17.5M EBITDA / ~$185M est. revenue = ~9.5% margin
- 2025: $25.04M EBITDA / $185.5M revenue = 13.5% margin
- **Delta: ~$7.5M EBITDA on ~flat revenue. That's a 400 bps margin expansion in one year.**

400 bps of margin expansion in one year on flat revenue is a major industrial event. It does not happen organically. It happens because of one of these:

**A. The 2024 base was depressed by one-time costs.** This is the most likely explanation.
- The Thomasville HQ DC opened June 2023 (per SFDR-015) — meaning 2024 was the first full year carrying $1.5-2M/yr of incremental D&A + ramp inefficiency on the new DC.
- ERP migration costs are pre-launch; if June 2026 is go-live, FY24-25 likely carried $500K-1.5M in implementation expenses absorbed in OpEx.
- Yulee acquisition closed in 2025; the 2024 P&L would not have captured its annualized contribution, but 2024 might have carried preparation/legal/diligence costs.

**B. Mix shift (mattress + GMROI directive).** Ashley's December 2024 GMROI Excellence press release (per SFDR-003) coincides exactly with the timeline. If 1915 South leaned in early on Ashley's mattress GMROI playbook in 2024-2025, 100-200 bps of gross-margin lift is a real number. Combined with assortment rationalization, you can get to 250 bps of GM lift quickly.

**C. Operational expense control.** Justin's email explicitly attributes Q1 2026 EBITDA expansion to "strong expense control." If that discipline started mid-2024 and ran through 2025, it would close a meaningful piece of the gap.

**D. Owned-DC absorption.** Once the new Thomasville DC reached run-rate utilization in late 2024, the per-unit distribution cost dropped sharply. This is the "operating leverage" the model artifact in section 1.4 doesn't capture but that is real in the data.

**Verdict on durability of 2025 baseline:**
- The 2025 number is real and durable. It reflects a stable-state operation at top-quartile licensee margin.
- The YoY delta is misleading because 2024 had structural noise, not because 2025 is inflated.
- **Brady should specifically validate this with Justin: "What was the FY24 incremental D&A from the Thomasville DC, and what were the FY24 ERP and M&A diligence costs?" If those add to $5-7M, the entire jump is explained by 2024 noise plus modest organic expansion. If they add to $2-3M, there's genuinely $4-5M of structural improvement Brady should understand.**

### 1.3 Is 14% on AGR Believable?

**Context from web research:** AGR is most likely an Ashley corporate-owned or Ashley-affiliated retail entity (Ashley Global Retail LLC is registered to Ashley corporate; "AGR" in the Justin email is a 12-store package being acquired *from Ashley*). The pattern matches the 2019 Olinde's → Dufresne Spencer Group transaction in Louisiana, where Ashley-corporate-influenced stores moved to a focused licensee for a margin uplift. This is not a hostile or distressed acquisition — it is Ashley moving real estate to a stronger operator.

**What we know about AGR's current performance from the email:**
- $69M revenue / 12 stores = **$5.75M revenue per store** — meaningfully *above* 1915 South's current $185.5M / 30 = $6.18M/store. AGR is not subscale on revenue per box. (This is an important data point — the integration uplift is operational, not throughput.)
- Justin's modeled "conservative" case: $79.2M from AGR (+8% traffic, 23.5% close rate). Base case: $89.6M (+15%). Aggressive: $107.2M (+25%, 27.0% close rate).
- Justin claims "clear line of sight to ~14% EBITDA on that business."

**Why AGR EBITDA today is likely below 14%:**
- Ashley corporate's operating-company economics are well-known to be weaker than top-quartile licensee economics. Per Ashley's own published licensee FAQ (cited in SFDR-003), the model is set up to make licensees more profitable than the parent — that's the whole structural reason 850+ stores are licensee-operated and only 118 are corporate.
- The Olinde's-DSG transition (2019) is the closest precedent. Industry reporting from that period noted DSG immediately drove margin expansion through Ashley assortment standardization, mattress attach, and DC consolidation — a 12-18 month uplift, not a day-one number.
- A reasonable AGR baseline today is **8-10% EBITDA** — meaning Justin's "14% line of sight" implies 400-600 bps of integration uplift.

**Where the integration uplift comes from:**
1. **Mattress attach + GMROI.** AGR may not be running the SSB/America's Mattress playbook at 1915 South's level. 100-200 bps lift achievable in year one.
2. **DC consolidation.** AGR stores in MS/LA can pull from 1915 South's existing Loxley AL DC + new build, sunsetting any AGR-owned DC infrastructure. 100-150 bps.
3. **Expense rationalization (corp + advertising + back-office).** Eliminating duplicate corporate functions: 100-150 bps.
4. **Pricing discipline + promotional cadence alignment.** Tightening up margin-destroying promos: 50-100 bps.
5. **Owned-DC absorption** (if the AGR business doesn't currently run owned distribution): 100-200 bps.

**Total integration uplift available: 450-800 bps. Justin's 14% target requires capturing 400-600 bps. Conservatively achievable, but not in 2026.**

**Realistic timeline:**
- 2026 (Aug close, partial year): AGR contributes at 9-10% EBITDA (close-to-current state, modest mattress lift).
- 2027 (first full year): AGR at 12-13% (DC + expense rationalization mostly complete).
- 2028 (steady state): AGR at 14% (full integration, mattress program at maturity).

**This matters for the bonus pool conversation.** If bonus is paid quarterly on EBITDA, and AGR runs below the 13.5% blended assumption in Year 1, the actual 2026 EBITDA will likely come in at the low end of Justin's $25-28M range — possibly even below $25M if Q2 supply chain drag is severe. Brady should price the 2026 bonus conservatively.

### 1.4 The 4.9% Total Store Operating Constant — Real or Artifact?

**The model artifact:** Across all 5 scenarios in Justin's table (As Reported / Annualized +$6.5M / Conservative +0% traffic / Base +15% / Aggressive +25%), Total Store Operating holds at exactly 4.9%. Total Store Payroll holds at 12.4%. These are dollar-for-dollar scaling, not operating-leverage modeling.

**The real-world physics:** Furniture-retail store payroll has a meaningful fixed-cost component (store managers, salaried roles, base staffing). Variable comp (commission) scales with revenue, but total payroll does not. The HFA-published industry norm is that **store payroll declines ~50-100 bps as a % of sales when per-store revenue rises from $5M to $8M+** (see also: SFDR-003 GMROI sources, HFA performance metrics).

**What this means in dollars:**
- Aggressive scenario: $339M / 42 stores = $8.07M avg revenue per store. At 1915 South's $185.5M / 30 = $6.18M/store today.
- A 50 bps payroll efficiency at $339M = $1.7M of additional EBITDA.
- A 30 bps Total Store Operating efficiency at $339M = $1.0M.
- **Combined: $1.0-2.7M of EBITDA upside in the Aggressive case that Justin's model holds back.**

This is real money. It also means the 13.5% EBITDA margin floor is genuinely a floor. The aggressive case at $339M/42 stores is closer to **15.0-15.4% EBITDA**, not 14.9%.

**What's the offsetting risk?** Justin may be holding 4.9% flat because he has new-store ramp-up cost embedded (Lake City new build Jan 2027 carries pre-opening + ramp costs that depress margin in year 1). That's a legitimate offset. But it doesn't fully explain a constant across the entire revenue range.

**Negotiating use:** "I'd want to model store-level operating leverage explicitly. Conservative case probably is what the table shows, but the aggressive case has 50-100 bps of additional margin you're not surfacing — and that's the case where the bonus pool gets interesting."

### 4.3 Calibration to market (per SFDR-012)

CFO target bonuses for mid-market private companies ($100M-$500M revenue) are 50-59% of base salary. Per CAP / Bennett Financials / JM Search 2025:
- A $400K base CFO with a 50% target bonus = $200K target bonus = $600K target cash
- A $450K base CFO with a 60% target bonus = $270K target bonus = $720K target cash

**A bonus structured as a flat % of EBITDA is non-standard for CFO comp.** It's much more common in CEO / GP / sponsor comp. The flat % structure has two implications:
1. **Asymmetric upside (good for Brady).** If Brady drives EBITDA from $34M to $42M, the 0.88% becomes $370K instead of $300K — a 23% bonus increase off operational leverage.
2. **Asymmetric downside (bad for Brady).** A 2026 Iran-war Q2-Q3 disruption that takes EBITDA to $23M would cut the bonus to $200K (0.88% × $23M) — a 33% bonus haircut for something Brady didn't cause.

**The negotiating principle:** A flat % of EBITDA is fine **if** it has a floor (a minimum bonus regardless of EBITDA, calibrated to roughly 50% of base) AND an accelerator above target (e.g., the bonus % goes from 0.88% to 1.10% above $40M EBITDA). This is sometimes called a "kicker" structure.

### 4.4 The structural bonus framework Brady should propose

**Based on $400K base, $700K target all-in at $34M post-AGR EBITDA:**

| Component | Amount | Mechanic |
|---|---|---|
| Base salary | $400,000 | Fixed |
| Target cash bonus | $200,000 | 0.88% of EBITDA, with floor of $100K and target of $200K at $34M EBITDA |
| Above-target bonus accelerator | up to +$100,000 | Bonus % rises to 1.20% of EBITDA above $40M |
| Long-term incentive | $200,000 annual issuance, 5-year vest | Annual grant of phantom equity / synthetic equity at OpCo enterprise value |
| **Target all-in cash + LTI value** | **$800,000 (target) / $700K (run-rate)** | |
| **Floor (worst year)** | **$500,000** | Base + floor bonus + LTI vesting |

**Variant — $450K base:**

The choice between these is a function of Brady's risk tolerance and how confident he is that 2026-2027 EBITDA will land in the $34M+ range.

### 4.5 What about LTI mechanics?

Justin's email: "Long term incentive issued annually and earned out in equal increments over 5 years." This is a synthetic equity / phantom stock structure.

**Critical questions Brady should ask:**
1. **Is the LTI grant value fixed in dollars per year, or fixed in equity units?** Dollars-per-year means inflation-protection at the company growth rate. Equity-units means Brady captures upside at exit. The latter is much more valuable in a high-growth scenario (and rationally is what Justin should propose if he believes his own growth case).
2. **What's the valuation basis for the equity units — most recent EBITDA × multiple, or formal third-party valuation?** A 5x multiple cap is very different from a 7-8x cap.
3. **What happens at a liquidity event (sale of company, recap, IPO)?** Single-trigger acceleration on change of control is standard.
4. **What happens on termination without cause vs for cause?** Continued vesting on without-cause is the norm; forfeiture on for-cause is standard.
5. **Is there a put right (Brady can force company to repurchase vested units at fair value after some period) or only the company holds the call right?** A 5-year cliff with no liquidity is materially worse than annual put windows.

A clean LTI structure: 5-year vest, annual put right at fair value (defined as trailing 12-month EBITDA × an agreed multiple), single-trigger acceleration on change of control, continued vesting on termination without cause.

A weak LTI structure: 5-year vest, no put right, company-only call, valuation defined by Russell at his discretion, forfeiture on any termination. This is effectively "promises" not LTI.

## Part 5 — Wild Cards Brady Should Probe

### 5.1 What's NOT in the plan

- **Real estate.** Russell Turner owns multiple buildings 1915 South operates from. The OpCo P&L pays rent to Russell-owned RE entities; the rent is a real expense to OpCo and a real income to Russell. This is the largest "outside-the-bonus-pool" Russell income stream and is structurally normal for family operators.
- **Aviation.** Per industry research, Russell may operate aircraft through Sycamore-style holding entities (analogous to Wanek family). This is a corporate expense that shows up in OpCo SG&A as travel — and should be probed.
- **Family office investments.** Diversified Russell-personal investments outside 1915 South. Not in the bonus pool, but they're the reason Russell can be patient with capital allocation.
- **The "EBITDA" definition.** Standard adjustments at private retailers: addback of owner compensation above-market, addback of personal expenses (vehicle, club membership, family payroll), addback of one-time M&A costs. Brady should ask: "Is the EBITDA in your model the reported number or an adjusted number? If adjusted, what are the addbacks?" A $25M reported / $27M adjusted EBITDA company is a different bonus pool than a $25M adjusted / $24M reported.

### 5.2 The "$300M+ topline" comment — what's the path beyond June 2027?

Justin: "real top line is likely to exceed $300M fairly quickly." The plan tops out at $283.5M at 48 stores. To get to $300M requires either:
- **Comp growth** above flat (a return to housing-recovery market by 2028)
- **One additional acquisition** (the SW Virginia Ashley licensee or a Southeast tuck-in, per prior M&A research)
- **Format extension** (mattress-only stores, outlet stores, B2B/hospitality channel)
- **Per-store revenue growth** (mattress vertical maturity, new lines like SSB)

This is the 2027-2028 story Justin hasn't fully laid out. **The bonus pool implication: the LTI 5-year vest mechanic implies Justin sees a 5-year horizon, meaning he likely has acquisition / capital strategy moves planned for 2028-2030 that aren't in the email.** Brady should explicitly ask: "What's the next leg of the plan after Beaumont? Is there a 2028-2030 vision I should be building toward?"

### 5.3 Was 2024 depressed, or is 2025 inflated?

This is the recurring question. The evidence above (section 1.2) supports "2024 was noisy due to DC ramp + ERP pre-spend" — meaning **2025 is the durable baseline**. But Brady should validate by asking:
- "What were the FY24 incremental D&A from the new Thomasville DC?"
- "What pre-implementation ERP costs flowed through FY24 and FY25?"
- "What were FY24 M&A diligence and integration costs (Yulee prep)?"

If those total $5-7M, the 43% YoY EBITDA jump is fully explained by the noise of 2024. If they total $2-3M, there's $4-5M of structural improvement worth understanding (likely mattress + GMROI mix).

### 5.4 The return rate gap (Gross Sales $194.7M vs Net $185.5M = 4.7%)

A 4.7% returns/allowances rate is **better than industry average** for furniture (HFA performance metrics + post-pandemic data show 8-12% returns at many retailers). This is a positive signal for 1915 South operations.

But Brady should track this trend. If returns are creeping up YoY (e.g., 3.8% in 2024 → 4.7% in 2025), it could signal:
- Mattress attach increasing (mattresses have higher return rates than case goods)
- Online order share increasing (online has 2-3x higher returns than in-store)
- Damage/quality issues during DC ramp

The other side: **if 1915 South is under-marking returns** (e.g., issuing store credit or doing free re-deliveries that don't flow through the returns line), the real number could be higher. Worth a 30-minute conversation with Leah on returns accounting policy.

### 5.5 Capex needs over the 5-year horizon

The 5-year LTI vest + the $300M+ topline + the AI/Zapsight + ERP implies meaningful capex and OpEx investment. Brady should be ready with a sense of what's needed:

- **DC capacity expansion.** 4 DCs today (per SFDR-015). Adding $98M revenue requires either a 5th DC (Beaumont needs Texas-area distribution), JB Hunt outsourcing (per SFDR-016), or extension of existing DCs. Capex: $5-15M depending on path.
- **ERP follow-on costs.** Post-launch integration with EDI partners, custom reporting, BI layer, training. $1-3M over 12-18 months post-go-live.
- **AI/Zapsight + decision layer.** Justin specifically called out the "applied AI roadmap" in Brady's scope. Real budget: $500K-2M annual SaaS + integration.
- **Store remodels / Lake City new build.** $5-10M per new store. ~$2-3M per remodel (1915 South has ~30 boxes that may need refresh cadence over 5 years).
- **JB Hunt transition** (per SFDR-016): one-time $2-5M cost, $5-8M capital liberation from delivery fleet sale.

**Total 5-year capex envelope likely: $40-70M.** Funded primarily from operating cash flow (likely $15-20M/year free cash flow at $35M EBITDA), partially from acquisition financing for AGR / Tupelo / Beaumont, and possibly minority recap if Russell wants liquidity.

This matters for bonus pool because: **EBITDA includes D&A addback by definition, but the cash flow available to fund growth is real and constrained.** If Russell wants minimum dividends to support family liquidity, the bonus pool is calculated off EBITDA but the actual ability to pay generous bonuses is constrained by cash.

## Part 7 — Bottom Line

**Justin's plan is internally consistent and credible.** The 13.5% EBITDA margin is at the top of the realistic range for an Ashley licensee but defensibly so given the structural advantages (no public co overhead, owned-DC absorption, mattress vertical, family operator discipline). The 14% AGR target is achievable in 2 years, not 1. The $300M topline by mid-2027 is realistic only if comp store growth returns or one additional acquisition lands. The 4.9% Total Store Operating constant is a model artifact that hides 30-100 bps of upside in the aggressive case.

**For comp purposes, Brady's anchor should be:**
- **$400-450K base** (mid to upper range of SFDR-012 market data)
- **0.85-1.10% of EBITDA quarterly bonus** with a floor (~50% of target) and an accelerator above $40M EBITDA
- **$200-250K annual LTI grant**, 5-year vest, with single-trigger acceleration on change of control and a defined valuation mechanic
- **Target all-in: $750-850K at the $34M post-AGR run-rate; $900K+ at the $40M+ post-all-deals run-rate**

This sits roughly 7-15% above Justin's informal "$700K post-acquisition" number but is the structurally correct ask given the scope (CFO + CSO + applied AI roadmap + entity strategy + technology architecture, per the email). Brady should not move below $700K all-in at the $34M EBITDA level. Above $34M the formula should pay him more.

**The biggest risk to the bonus pool is 2026 EBITDA landing at $23-25M (low end of range) due to Iran-war + ERP + AGR integration overhang.** Brady should specifically negotiate a 2026 floor regardless of formula.

**The biggest opportunity is the LTI structure.** If Justin agrees to unit-denominated phantom equity at a fair valuation multiple (say 6.5x trailing EBITDA), and Brady is part of driving EBITDA from $25M to $45M over 5 years, the LTI value at exit could be $1.5-3M+ on the cumulative 5-year grants — meaningfully above what cash bonus alone would pay.
