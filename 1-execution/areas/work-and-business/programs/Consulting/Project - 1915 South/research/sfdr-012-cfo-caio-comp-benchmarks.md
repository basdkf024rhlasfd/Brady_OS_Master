# SFDR-012 — CFO + CAIO/CSO Compensation Benchmarks (CLOSED)

**Date researched:** 2026-04-24
**Status:** Closed — confirmed from public proxy filings, Raw Selection survey (2025), CTAIO/CDAO Insights benchmarks (2026)
**Unlock:** Brady's $250-380K expected range is defensible but potentially anchored too low for a combined CFO + CAIO + entity/structure scope. Market data supports $280-350K base for a traditional CFO at this revenue scale — but the AI roadmap and expanded scope add a documented premium. Floor should not move below $280K. Ceiling can reach $380-420K if the scope is argued cleanly.

---

## Part 1 — Public Company CFO Proxies (Furniture / Specialty Retail)

These are publicly filed base salaries for CFOs at comparable specialty retail/furniture companies. All from SEC DEF 14A proxy statements.

| Company | Revenue | CFO Name | Base Salary | Source / Year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Havertys Furniture (HVT) | $723M | Richard B. Hare, EVP & CFO | **$465,000** | Havertys DEF 14A, FY2024 |
| Arhaus (ARHS) | $1.27B | Dawn Phillipson (outgoing CFO) | **$492,752** | Arhaus DEF 14A, FY2024 |
| Arhaus (ARHS) | $1.27B | Michael Lee (new CFO, May 2025) | **$550,000** | Arhaus 8-K, April 2025 |
| Ethan Allen (ETD) | ~$740M | Matthew J. McNulty, SVP & CFO | **~$380,000 base** (total cash $443,927) | Salary.com / ETD proxy FY2024 |
| La-Z-Boy (LZB) | ~$2.1B | Taylor E. Luebke, SVP & CFO | **$440,000** | La-Z-Boy 8-K, October 2024 (hire) |

### Key observations from public comps

- Havertys and Ethan Allen are the most structurally comparable to 1915 South — regional specialty furniture, lower complexity than Arhaus/La-Z-Boy.
- Ethan Allen's CFO at ~$380K base is the most useful anchor: $740M revenue company, established specialty furniture, SVP-level title (not pure C-suite), strong comp committee discipline.
- **Scale adjustment for 1915 South ($182M revenue vs $740M Ethan Allen):** Public company CFOs carry additional complexity (SEC filings, investor relations, public market compliance). A private company CFO at 25% of Ethan Allen's revenue would typically earn 50-65% of the public comp. That puts a "like-for-like traditional CFO" at **$190-250K base** for a straight 29-store private retailer.
- **But this role is not a straight CFO.** See Part 3 for scope premium.

| $50M–$150M | $200,000 | $275,000 | $350,000 |

### PE-backed CFO average (Heidrick & Struggles, 2025)

Heidrick & Struggles reports: PE-backed CFOs in the US earned an **average of $604,000 total compensation** in 2025 (base + bonus, excluding equity). This is across all sizes; skews larger. The 5% YoY increase was driven by longer hold periods. PE-backed CFOs negotiate base upfront aggressively because equity timing is uncertain.

### Key positioning for 1915 South

1915 South is a family-owned private operator — not PE-backed — which is a meaningful difference. PE-backed CFOs get equity kickers that often represent the majority of long-term compensation value. A family-owned company CFO typically gets higher base to compensate for lack of meaningful equity upside. **The private family-business premium on base salary vs. PE-backed comps is typically +10-20%.**

Applied to the "over $150M" band:
- PE-backed median: $350K (retail) / $380K (consumer)
- Private family-owned equivalent: **$385-455K** (with family-business premium applied)

**Bottom line from private benchmarks: $280-380K base is the defensible market range for a traditional CFO at 1915 South's revenue scale. The Raw Selection and Heidrick data confirm Brady's intuitive range is approximately right for a traditional CFO — but conservative for a combined CFO + CAIO scope.**

| Christian & Timbers (2026 study, 50+ CHROs) | AI C-suite roles, total comp | — | $380-520,000+ | AI role comp +10% YoY in 2025 |

### The CAIO market reality for a private specialty retailer

A standalone CAIO at a $182M private specialty retailer is **not** the same as a CAIO at a tech company or financial services firm. The retail/consumer CAIO market is earlier-stage. Most comparable operators don't have a standalone CAIO at all — the role is being absorbed into CFO, COO, or CTO scope.

**What this means for Brady's negotiation:**

The "Applied AI Roadmap" component of Brady's scope is not a tech-company CAIO role. It is closer to:
- A "VP of Applied AI" or "Director of Digital Transformation" at a furniture retailer
- The internal champion for Zapsight + agentic decision-layer + AI-enabled reporting

Comp benchmarks for that framing:
- CDAO Insights CDO/Retail: $240-350K total cash
- Mid-market VP AI/ML (CTAIO.dev): $200-350K total comp

**The AI scope is NOT worth $200-250K additive comp on top of a traditional CFO base.** At this company scale, it justifies a **20-35% premium** over a traditional CFO — roughly $55-120K on base — depending on how central the AI mandate is to the company's strategic direction.

## Part 4 — Scope Premium Analysis

### Brady's proposed scope vs. traditional CFO

| Scope Element | Traditional CFO? | Brady's Scope | Market Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounting / controllership | Yes | Yes | — |
| Deal support / financial modeling | Yes | Yes | — |
| Entity design / structure (trust, RE, aviation, ventures) | Partial (at this size, often outsourced) | Yes — fully owned | +$20-40K |
| Management / operating framework | Rarely | Yes — full ownership | +$25-50K |
| Applied AI Roadmap / Zapsight oversight | No | Yes — architect + owner | +$30-50K |
| Technology / IT oversight | No | Yes | +$15-30K |

**Additive scope premium over traditional CFO: $90-170K.**

However, this is not how executive comp works in practice — especially at private family businesses. Justin's logic is "capable thinkers justify comp by building better AI-enabled ways of working." The right framing for Brady is:

> This is a CFO/CAIO/CSO hybrid role. The market for this scope does not exist at your company size — you're not going to find a benchmark. The correct anchor is: what would it cost to hire a traditional CFO at this revenue scale ($280-320K) AND a VP-level AI/Technology lead ($200-250K) separately? You can't hire two people for less than $480-570K in combined base. A single hire who owns both scopes, with proven operational and AI credentials, should come in at a discount to that — but not below $300K.

## Part 5 — Synthesized Comp Range for Brady

### Traditional CFO alone, at 1915 South's scale

| Scenario | Base Range | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative (like-for-like private family retailer) | $240-280K | Scale-adjusted from public comps + family business discount |
| Market median (Raw Selection private/consumer >$150M) | $300-380K | PE-backed + family premium |
| Top-of-market traditional CFO at this scale | $380-420K | Top quartile private consumer + family premium |

### With full CFO + CAIO + CSO scope

| Scenario | Base Range | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | **$280-320K** | Traditional CFO floor + minimal AI premium (AI roadmap treated as secondary scope) |
| Market-defensible | **$320-380K** | Traditional CFO median + 20-25% AI/expanded scope premium |
| Full-scope justified | **$380-420K** | Top-quartile private CFO + CAIO scope premium + entity/structure complexity |

### Brady's stated range ($250-380K) — assessment

- **Floor of $250K is too low to defend.** Market data puts traditional CFO at this scale at $280-350K median. Accepting $250K signals Brady is pricing himself as a controller-level hire, not a CFO.
- **Floor should be $280K minimum.** That's the bottom of the Raw Selection "over $150M" private consumer band.
- **Target should be $310-350K base.** This is the defensible market median for a private family business CFO at $150-200M revenue with expanded scope.
- **Ceiling of $380K is achievable** if Brady frames the scope correctly (CFO + CAIO hybrid) and if Russell sees the combined value clearly. This is top-quartile but within the documented range.
- **Above $380K at this company:** Extremely difficult without meaningful equity or profit-sharing component. Public company CFOs at Havertys ($465K) and Ethan Allen ($380K) have SEC complexity, investor relations burden, and public company governance overhead that Brady won't have — and Justin will cite this. If Brady wants more than $380K, the conversation needs to shift to bonus or equity structure.

### Bonus / TDC benchmarks

- Traditional CFO at this scale: **50-75% target bonus** of base (per Raw Selection: most PE-backed retail CFOs below $400K base are in 30-50% bonus band)
- At $320K base + 50% bonus target: TDC = $480K
- At $350K base + 50% bonus target: TDC = $525K
- At $380K base + 60% bonus target: TDC = $608K
- These TDC numbers track favorably against the Heidrick & Struggles PE-backed average of $604K

**The strongest negotiating posture:** Accept a base in the $300-340K range if there is a meaningful bonus target (50%+) and at least one structural upside element (equity, profit sharing, or enterprise value participation). Don't optimize base if the variable comp can close the gap.

## Part 6 — What to Say If Justin Challenges the Number

### If Justin says "our last CFO made $X" (expect: $180-240K)

> "That's what a controller-level CFO in a family business at your scale typically earns. What you're describing — entity design, the AI roadmap, the technology layer, the management framework — is a different scope. The market for a person who can own all of that simultaneously doesn't really exist at your revenue size. Which is why you're talking to me instead of posting on Indeed."

### If Justin says "we can't justify more than $X based on EBITDA"

> "I understand the math. Your EBITDA is around $25M. A CFO at 1.5% of revenue is $2.7M over three years — that's my loaded cost if you're at $280K base plus benefits. The distribution transition, the GMROI work, or one clean acquisition adds more than that in value in year one. The number is justified by the decisions it enables, not by the P&L slot I fill."

### If Justin wants to underprice because "you get equity-like upside if the business grows"

> "I'm open to a comp structure that includes upside — profit sharing, phantom equity, something tied to enterprise value. But that's additive, not a substitute for market-rate base. If the business grows the way you're planning, I want to participate. But I'm not taking below-market base on the promise of upside I don't control."
