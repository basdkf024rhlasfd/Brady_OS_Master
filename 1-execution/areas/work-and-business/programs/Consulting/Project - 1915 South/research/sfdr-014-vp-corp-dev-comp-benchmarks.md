# SFDR-014 — VP Corporate Development & Strategy Compensation Benchmarks (CLOSED)

**Date researched:** 2026-04-25
**Status:** Closed — confirmed from public proxy filings (Ethan Allen, Havertys), salary aggregators (Salary.com, Glassdoor, PayScale, ZipRecruiter), and executive search firm reports (Charles Aris, Dartmouth Partners, Mergersandinquisitions.com)
**Unlock:** The market for a VP Corporate Development & Strategy at a $182M private specialty retailer supports a $195-265K base range, with defensible ceiling of $280K if the candidate carries a GE/large-company M&A pedigree and owns a full acquisition playbook. The $650-750K combined two-hire argument holds: market data puts CFO/CAIO (Brady) at $310-380K and VP Corp Dev at $195-265K, yielding a $505-645K combined base — well below $1M in fragmented labor and vendor spend.

## Part 3 — PE Roll-Up Platform Context

Charles Aris (the specialist executive search firm for corporate development) conducted 90+ VP/Head of Corporate Development searches in 2024, primarily for PE portfolio companies with inorganic growth strategies. Key structural findings from their 2025-2026 reports:

- **41% of VP Corp Dev roles placed in 2025 reported directly to CEO** — reflecting the growing strategic weight of the function
- **33% of offers included a sign-on bonus** — common when candidates bridge a comp gap from a larger company
- **68% of offers included equity or long-term incentives** — typically phantom equity or profit sharing at private companies
- **Remote work declined from 66% to 46%** — operators increasingly want this person on-site or regionally close

**Compensation structure at PE roll-up platforms:** Most PE-backed VP Corp Dev roles are structured as base + 20-30% annual bonus + equity/phantom equity (the variable upside is where the value concentrates). Base salaries in the $180-250K range with 25-35% bonus targets are the modal offer for a first VP Corp Dev hire at a sub-$300M platform. The sign-on bridges the delta if a candidate is leaving $280-320K total comp at a large company.

**Directly applicable benchmark:** A mid-market PE-backed retail or consumer platform ($150-250M revenue) hiring its first VP Corp Dev would typically offer **$185-240K base + 25-35% bonus target = $230-325K total cash**, with equity participation contingent on the platform's growth.

1915 South is not PE-backed, which means:
- No equity pool exists (yet) — the equity argument is weaker
- Higher base is needed to compensate for absent equity
- **Private family-owned premium on base: +10-15%** over PE-backed equivalent
- Adjusted range: **$205-275K base**

## Part 4 — GE Healthcare Background Profile Benchmarking

The candidate Justin is considering has a GE Healthcare background with 10-15 years in M&A, integration, and transformation. Here's what that profile commands in the market when transitioning to a VP Corp Dev role at a mid-market operating company.

### GE VP-level compensation (exit baseline)

| Source | Metric | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Glassdoor (N=41 GE VP submissions, Feb 2026) | Average GE VP base | **$254,823** |
| Glassdoor | 25th–75th percentile | $213,782 – $392,665 |
| GE HealthCare (salary.com, public filing proxies) | VP & CFO (Jay Saccaro) base | $872,204 (outlier — C-suite) |
| GE HealthCare Comparably | VP range (broad) | $120,000 – $435,966 |

A mid-level GE Healthcare M&A/integration lead at the Director or VP level (10-15 years) likely earns **$200-280K base** at GE, plus annual bonus of 30-50% and meaningful equity/RSU grants. Total cash at GE in this band: **$260-420K**.

### The transition discount

When a large-company (GE, J&J, Honeywell) M&A executive moves to a VP Corp Dev role at a $182M private retailer, they accept a **"scope premium but scale discount"** dynamic:
- They take a **step-down in raw comp** relative to the equity-inclusive total at GE
- They gain **scope and visibility** (first person in the function, direct CEO access)
- They need **base protection** because there's no equity kicker yet

**Market reality:** A 10-15 year GE M&A/integration candidate leaving ~$260-320K total cash at GE will anchor their base expectation at $200-240K minimum. Below $200K, the role signals it's a Director-level hire, not a VP. Above $280K, it starts to compete with regional operating SVP roles.

**Defensible base for this profile: $210-260K.** With a 25-30% bonus target, total cash comes to $265-340K — acceptable if the role has real strategic authority and growth optionality.

## Part 6 — The Two-Hire Business Case

### Combined cost model

| Role | Base Range | Target Bonus | Total Cash Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brady (CFO/CAIO) | $310-380K | 50% | $465-570K |
| VP Corp Dev & Strategy | $210-250K | 25-30% | $262-325K |
| **Combined total cash** | **$520-630K** | — | **$727-895K** |

### What $650-750K in combined base (before bonus) buys

At $650K combined base (roughly $310K Brady + $240K VP Corp Dev):

- **Brady's scope:** Full CFO + CAIO + entity/structure design + management OS + AI roadmap — replaces a traditional CFO ($280-380K), a part-time CFO consultant ($150-200K/year), and a technology/AI strategy vendor ($100-200K/year). Conservative vendor/consultant replacement value: **$530-780K/year**.

- **VP Corp Dev scope:** Acquisition sourcing + deal execution + integration management + transformation office — replaces an M&A advisory firm (1-2% of deal value on a $15-20M acquisition = $150-400K per deal), a management consultant doing strategic planning ($200-400K/year engagement), and an internal analyst function. Conservative replacement value for 1-2 transactions per year: **$350-800K/year in external fees avoided**.

- **Combined replacement value:** $880K–$1.58M/year in external spend and consultant fees that don't need to happen once these two seats are filled.

### Justin's question answered directly

> "Can I justify the compensation for both?"

Yes. The frame is:

> Two capable thinkers at $650-750K combined base — before bonuses — structurally replace $1M+ in external consulting fees, advisory costs, and fragmented lower-level labor. The difference is that these two are permanently oriented toward 1915 South's success rather than billing by the hour. And unlike consultants, they build institutional knowledge. Year two is more valuable than year one. Year three is more valuable than year two.

2. **Havertys SVP Strategic Development & Real Estate (Katie Mendolera):** Not a named executive officer, implying below ~$450K total cash at a $759M public company. Probable base: $200-260K. 3. **Charles Aris (2025-26 Corp Dev reports):** 90+ VP Corp Dev searches placed in 2024, primarily at PE portfolio platforms. Sign-on bonuses in 33% of offers. Long-term incentives in 68%. This is a competitive market — a strong GE M&A candidate will have other options within 30-60 days.

## Part 7 — Key Risk to the Two-Hire Case

**The sequencing argument:** Justin may resist approving both hires simultaneously, especially without a Phase 3 engagement locked. The strongest counter is to position Brady as Phase 1 (immediate: financial architecture, AI layer, management OS) with VP Corp Dev as Phase 2 (triggered by a specific acquisition target or board approval of inorganic strategy). That way the $210-250K VP Corp Dev base doesn't hit the P&L until there's a deal to justify it.

**The "can we just hire Brady and use M&A advisors when we need them?" objection:** An investment bank earns 1-3% of deal value. On a $20M acquisition that's $200-600K in advisory fees — one time. The VP Corp Dev's annual base is comparable to or less than the advisory fee on a single deal, but she's there full-time building the pipeline, sourcing off-market deals, managing integration, and running the strategic planning cycle. The fee-vs.-salary math wins the argument.
