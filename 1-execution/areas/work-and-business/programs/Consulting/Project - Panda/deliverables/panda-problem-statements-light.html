Operating Model · Three Options

The question isn't what Panda should build — it's who owns the build. Three archetypes; one fits Cherng governance.

Option A · Embedded

VP Ops Innovation inside COO org

Report to COO Jeff Wang within Operations function. Owns kitchen automation, drive-thru, labor productivity, construction velocity as part of ops P&L.
Pro: Fast decision cycles. Aligned incentives with ops P&L. Low org change. Respects decentralization.
Con: Risk of absorption into "run-the-business." No authority over digital, format, supply chain. Signals low priority.
Fit for Cherng Culture: Moderate
Option B · Standalone

Chief Innovation Officer, CEO-reporting

SVP-level ($350K–$550K base + bonus + equity). Independent P&L. Direct reporting to Andrew Cherng. Cross-functional authority across ops, digital, format, supply chain.
Pro: Executive authority across silos. Highest-priority signal. Matches Domino's/Starbucks trajectory.
Con: New reporting line departs from decentralization norm. Walmart Store No. 8 failure mode risk. Turf conflict with James and COO.
Fit for Cherng Culture: Lower probability
Option C · Dual-Mode ★

Embedded + Center of Excellence

Embedded: VP Ops Innovation reports to COO — owns execution, piloting, ops KPIs.
Strategic: Chief Innovation Officer (fractional → permanent) reports to Andrew — owns portfolio prioritization + cross-functional sponsorship.
Governance: Quarterly steering. CIO decides what; embedded VP decides how. Clear rights, no turf.
Pro: Balances execution + strategy. Leverages family advisory model. Proven at Chipotle, CFA, Sweetgreen.
Fit for Cherng Culture: Highest ★

Point of View

The dual-mode model works because it respects how Cherng decisions actually get made: Andrew as chair/advisor, not day-to-day operator. A CEO-reporting CIO would trigger the Cherng immune system. A VP buried in ops would signal "low priority" and get absorbed into run-the-business work. Option C gives James a senior peer (CIO) who can unblock cross-functional resource conflicts — without displacing the COO, without creating a venture arm, and without asking Andrew for a structural overhaul. It's the smallest governance change that unlocks the biggest execution velocity.

Methodology

Fourteen parallel deep-research threads executed April 17–18, 2026. Each thread scoped for Tier-A source preference (SEC filings, earnings calls, primary interviews, regulatory disclosures) with Tier-B sources (industry press, analyst reports) acceptable for directional context. Tier-C sources (uncited blog posts, speculative vendor claims) used only as supporting color, never load-bearing. Candidate problem statements (~60) extracted per thread, then clustered across threads, then subjected to the 5-criteria Stage-0 sharpness gate. 20 statements passed; the rest were merged, dropped, or held for Phase 2B reconsideration. All addressable $ figures use Panda-specific financial data (DR-07) cross-referenced to peer 10-K filings. Every dollar estimate carries a methodology note in the underlying synthesis.

Known Limitations & Caveats

Peer benchmarks (AUV, margins) derived from public SEC filings, investor presentations, and Investor's Business Daily reports (TTM 2024). Margins represent restaurant-level P&L and company-level normalized figures; corporate overhead allocation varies by franchisor mix. Panda proprietary data (time study by station, rework tracking, franchisee economics) unavailable — estimates based on industry proxy. Automation adoption figures reflect announced deployments only; scale and rollout status may lag announcements as of Q1 2026. Panda Home, North Platte, and delivery-only unit economics unpublished. Format maturity rating (1–5 scale) is a qualitative assessment based on breadth of formats + tech integration + documented innovation cadence, not independently validated. International unit percentage (Panda 33%) reflects mainland China, Hong Kong, and select Asian markets via JV or license; significant gap in Western expansion vs. Taco Bell and McDonald's.
