## Frameworks Brady Articulated

**NPS as catch-all:**
Start with NPS (or closest proxy) as the top-level customer metric. Every sub-initiative maps back to moving total NPS — don't let an innovation win on its own metric while total NPS stays flat.

**Top-down + bottom-up planning → OKRs:**
High-level leadership alignment on capital allocation → empower business owners bottoms-up with their own innovation plans → meet in the middle with judgment → track against OKRs. Branded it (space theme at Kroger), ran monthly town halls and road shows to keep the flywheel turning. Key principle: make it "so stinking objective on the math that people don't have a chance to hide unsuccessful things that go on too long."

**Champion operator strategy:**
The actual unlock isn't the strategy — it's finding the one operator who will own the project, raise their hand, and pull the rest of the field along. Brady's best Kroger partnership: the ops counterpart who became his "best work friend." That guy is now #2 at Smith's, about to be president.

**Simplicity beats automation:**
At Walmart China, the team solved missed freezer items with a clothespin on the ticket (have to return it before completing the order). Worked better than expensive automated alternatives. Lesson: before you build a system, find the clothespin equivalent.

**Data before solutions:**
Don't solve until you can slice the data. Find stores that are doing well, cross-pollinate their process. The hidden insight never surfaces from watching the process — it surfaces from the data.
