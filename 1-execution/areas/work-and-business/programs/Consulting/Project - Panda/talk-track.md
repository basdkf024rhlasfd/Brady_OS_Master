# Talk Track — James Ku Conversation

**Prep for:** Brady Smallwood ↔ James Ku (CDO, Panda Restaurant Group)
**Context:** Warm reconnection via LinkedIn. Former Walmart colleagues (overlap Jan 2019–Dec 2020). James mentioned "innovation team" at Panda Express and openness to consulting on "leadership, strategic planning, ops experience."

---

## Mindset Frame

This is a **reconnection, not a pitch.** James reached out. He mentioned consulting. Brady's job is to:
1. Rebuild the personal connection
2. Demonstrate CDO-relevant depth (prove you understand his world)
3. Listen for pain points and opportunities
4. Plant seeds — don't harvest

---

## 2. Discovery — James's World (15-20 min)

### Must-Ask Questions (CDO-Specific)

**Build pipeline:**
- **"What does your build pipeline look like? How many new stores per year?"**
  - (You know: ~123 planned for 2025. But let him tell you.)
- **"What's the split between new builds vs. remodels?"**
- **"How long from site selection to opening day?"**

**Format evolution:**
- **"I saw the Panda Home prototype in Dripping Springs. How is that performing vs. legacy format?"**
- **"Are you testing anything beyond Panda Home? Any next-gen prototypes?"**
- **"How are you thinking about the drive-thru buildout? 75% of new builds is aggressive — what's the goal?"**

**The innovation team he mentioned:**
- **"You mentioned the innovation team on LinkedIn. What's the mandate? What are they working on?"**
- **"Is that format innovation, kitchen innovation, or broader?"**

**Kitchen and automation:**
- **"I've been tracking what Sweetgreen is doing with the Infinite Kitchen — robotic makeline. Are you looking at any of the automation stuff for Panda's kitchen?"**
- **"The automated woks are interesting. How far can you push that?"**

**International perspective:**
- **"You had the China experience at Walmart. Are you looking at what's happening in Asian markets for restaurant innovation? The robotics stuff in China is years ahead."**

**Smart building / facilities:**
- **"The GridPoint deployment is smart. How is that performing? Are you seeing the energy savings you expected?"**

### Listen-For Cues

| Signal | What It Means | Follow-Up |
|--------|---------------|-----------|
| Frustration with **speed of construction** | Pipeline bottleneck — modular/prefab opportunity | "Have you looked at modular construction? Some chains are cutting 30-50% off build time." |
| Desire for **data-driven site selection** | Still using traditional RE methods | "There's some interesting AI work happening in trade area analysis. I've been looking at this." |
| **Kitchen throughput** challenges | BOH is the constraint, not demand | "That's where the format innovation really matters — the kitchen is the factory." |
| **Standardization vs. localization** tension | Scale vs. relevance debate | "How do you think about the Tea Bar — is that everywhere eventually or market-specific?" |
| **Cost pressure** on new builds | Capital efficiency focus | "Modular construction + smart energy management could meaningfully change the per-unit economics." |
| **Talent/contractor** challenges | Execution bottleneck | "That's one of the hardest parts of scaling development. The Walmart facility services background must help." |

## 3. Brady's Angle — Where AI/Analytics Meets Store Development (10 min)

**Bridge from his answers. Do NOT monologue. Plant seeds based on what resonated.**

Possible threads:

- **AI-driven site selection:** "There's a version of trade area analysis where you feed the model not just demographics but traffic patterns, competitor proximity, weather data, delivery density — and it predicts AUV within 10% before you break ground. That's an area where I've been doing work."

- **Predictive new store performance:** "If you're building 120+ stores a year, even a 5% improvement in site selection accuracy is worth millions in avoided mistakes."

- **Kitchen layout optimization:** "With the KDS data and throughput metrics you're collecting, there's a way to optimize kitchen flow — equipment placement, station sequencing — based on actual production data rather than historical assumptions."

- **Competitive intelligence engine:** "One of the things I've built for other clients is an automated competitive intelligence system — tracks what Chipotle, Chick-fil-A, Sweetgreen, Taco Bell are doing with format innovation in real time. It's like having a research team running 24/7."

- **Construction project management:** "AI-powered scheduling and cost tracking for the build pipeline. When you're managing 100+ construction projects simultaneously, the data-driven approach to contractor management and timeline prediction is a real unlock."

**Calibrate to his energy.** If he lights up on one topic, go deeper. If he's polite but not engaged, move on.

## 5. Do-Not List

| Do NOT | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Lead with a consulting pitch | This is a reconnection. James reached out. Let it breathe. |
| Pretend to know Panda's internals | You have zero insider knowledge. Everything you know is public. |
| Talk about Kroger or other clients by name | Confidentiality. Say "another client" or "a retailer I work with." |
| Oversell AI as a silver bullet | James builds physical stores. He's a construction/RE guy. Keep it grounded and practical. |
| Confuse CDO with CTO/CIO | James is not a tech executive. He builds physical things. Respect the domain. |
| Talk too much about yourself | Discovery mode. 70/30 listening-to-talking ratio. |
| Push for commitment on the first call | Let the relationship develop. The ask is a follow-up conversation, not a contract. |
| Mention his Wharton/Harvard credentials | Don't be the guy who name-drops someone's resume at them. |
