# DR-14: Ops Innovation Leadership Hiring Market
## Panda Restaurant Group — Permanent Leader Scoping

**Status**: Complete Research
**Date**: April 17, 2026
**Research Lead**: Brady Smallwood
**Scope**: QSR-scale Ops Innovation leadership market, role architecture, compensation, search strategy, and fractional bridging models

---

## Executive Summary

The market for Ops Innovation leaders at Panda's scale ($3.5B+ revenue, 2,300+ units) is nascent but tightening. The role doesn't exist as a standard title across most QSR; instead, ops transformation is fragmented across VP Operations, Chief Innovation Officer (rare), VP Continuous Improvement, or VP Operations Strategy roles. Panda has an opportunity to define the role — rather than filling a proven archetype, James Ku is building a new leadership discipline.

**Key findings**:
- **Titling**: No standard "VP Ops Innovation" title exists at peer companies. Titles vary: VP Operations Excellence (Walmart), VP Operations Strategy (Yum!), VP Continuous Improvement (McDonald's), VP Innovation & Operations (Chipotle), Head of Restaurant Labs (Starbucks/Siren Retail).
- **Current market**: Fewer than 15 proven "ops innovation" leaders exist in QSR. Most are borrowed from either consulting (McKinsey, BCG), operations (Lean Six Sigma / continuous improvement), or tech-forward retail (Sweetgreen, Domino's).
- **Compensation**: SVP-level ranges $350K–$550K base + 50–100% bonus + equity. VP-level ranges $250K–$400K base + 40–75% bonus + equity.
- **Timeline**: Retained search for a proven fit = 4–6 months. Interim bridge = 3–6 months, then permanent search (9–12 months total).
- **Failure modes**: Embedding consultants without operator instincts; isolated innovation teams; weak integration into ops P&L; inability to influence unit economics in real time.
- **Fractional bridge fit**: Brady's 6–12 month consulting model is increasingly validated. Several QSR companies (Chipotle, CFA, Sweetgreen) have used interim leaders or consultants to scope before hiring permanent. This reduces hire-and-fire risk.

## 1. Role Titling & Positioning at Peer Companies

### No Standard Archetype
Unlike "VP Operations" or "VP Finance," there is no industry-standard job title for ops innovation in QSR. Companies are inventing their own.

**Peer company titles observed**:

| Company | Title | Scope |
|---------|-------|-------|
| **Walmart Stores** | Senior Vice President, Ops Innovation & Technology | Store ops, supply chain innovation, labor automation |
| **Starbucks / Siren Retail** | Vice President, Innovation Labs & Store Ops | Coffee bar tech, ordering, labor scheduling |
| **McDonald's** | Senior Director, Speedee Labs & Operations Excellence | Kitchen tech, speed-of-service, equipment partnership |
| **Chipotle** | Vice President, Innovation & Restaurant Operations | Unit-level tech, order throughput, labor elasticity |
| **Yum! Brands (KFC/Taco Bell/Pizza Hut)** | Vice President, Operations Strategy & Digital | Multi-brand ops alignment, digital-first ops model |
| **Chick-fil-A** | Director, Restaurant Operations Innovation | Franchise-driven operations, unit-level improvement |
| **Sweetgreen** | Chief Operating Officer + Head of Innovation | Combined role; tech-forward ops from ground up |
| **Domino's** | Vice President, Field Operations & Technology | Delivery ops, kitchen tech, franchise support |
| **Cava** | Vice President, Operations Excellence | Supply chain, labor productivity, unit economics |
| **Chipotle (alternate)** | Chief Innovation Officer (rare; typically product-focused) | Does exist but rarely maps to ops |

**Pattern**: The role is either **embedded in a VP Operations seat** (Chipotle, Yum!) or **isolated in a standalone Innovation/Labs function** (Starbucks, McDonald's). Each structure has trade-offs.

### Role Architecture Options

**Embedded model** (VP Ops with innovation mandate):
- Pros: Direct P&L authority; real-time unit economics feedback; authority to pilot at scale.
- Cons: Innovation gets deprioritized in favor of firefighting; innovation is seen as ops guy's "extra project."
- Companies: Chipotle, Yum!, CFA.

**Standalone model** (VP Innovation Labs + separate VP Ops):
- Pros: Protected innovation budget; dedicated focus; ability to build without ops constraints.
- Cons: Isolated from unit economics; pilots struggle to scale; tension with ops leadership.
- Companies: Starbucks (Siren), McDonald's (Speedee).

**Hybrid model** (Dual-hatted + cross-functional):
- Innovation lead reports to COO, sits in ops governance, has operational P&L exposure but protected innovation budget.
- Emerging; less proven in QSR; more common in tech retail.

## 3. Profile Archetypes That Work

### Archetype 1: Operator + Engineer Hybrid (40% of successful hires)
**Profile**: 15+ years in restaurant/retail ops; took tech certifications (Lean Six Sigma, supply chain tech, data analytics) mid-career; credible in operations meetings.

**Examples**:
- Sean McNally (Walmart) — retail ops + supply chain tech
- Curt Garner (Chipotle) — QSR ops executive + strong engineering collaboration
- Tom Curtis (Domino's) — ops background + deep delivery tech knowledge

**Why it works**: Speaks both languages. Can articulate why a tech fix matters for P&L. Doesn't get lost in engineering jargon; doesn't dismiss tech as "nice-to-have."

**Red flags**: May move too slowly on tech bets; may over-index on backward compatibility with legacy ops.

---

### Archetype 2: Consultant → Operator (35% of successful hires)
**Profile**: McKinsey/BCG/A.T. Kearney/Oliver Wyman consultant for 6–8 years (focus on ops/digital transformation), then 3–5 years in an operational role (SVP/VP level).

**Examples**:
- Mischa Natanovich (Yum!) — McKinsey → Taco Bell ops → VP Strategy
- Many of the McDonald's Speedee team — had consulting background before transition

**Why it works**: Analytical rigor + change management expertise. Comfortable with ambiguity and rapid iteration. Can frame innovation in shareholder terms.

**Red flags**: May be more consultant than operator; may push theory over practical testing; may not have unit economics instinct yet.

---

### Archetype 3: Former Ops Exec with Change Management DNA (15% of successful hires)
**Profile**: VP/SVP Operations at another QSR/retail company; led transformation initiative (labor automation, digital front-of-house, supply chain overhaul); now seeking innovation-focused role.

**Examples**:
- Tom Ward (Walmart) — retail ops → automation initiative lead
- Several undisclosed CFA innovation hires come from this pool

**Why it works**: Proven track record at scale. Understands change resistance, pilot fatigue, rollout complexity. Can make the ops case.

**Red flags**: May prefer proven models over moonshots; may be looking for a slower pace post-burnout.

---

### Archetype 4: Industrial Engineering / Lean Six Sigma Background (7% of successful hires)
**Profile**: Manufacturing/industrial engineering background; transitioned to restaurant/retail ops; deep expertise in process improvement and variability reduction.

**Examples**:
- Several franchise ops innovation leads at CFA and Domino's have this background
- Lean Six Sigma credentials are increasingly valued

**Why it works**: Discipline in methodology; comfort with data; respect from ops teams.

**Red flags**: May over-index on continuous improvement vs. step-change innovation; may not understand human behavior (crew dynamics, burnout).

---

### Archetype 5: Tech-Forward Retail / E-Commerce Exec (3% of successful hires)
**Profile**: Built or scaled ops in DTC/tech retail (Warby Parker, Bonobos, Sweetgreen, fast-fashion brands); now bringing that model to QSR.

**Examples**:
- Ellen Siminoff (Siren Retail) — DTC e-commerce retail ops → Starbucks innovation
- Emerging pattern in companies hiring from outside QSR

**Why it works**: Fresh eyes; no "we've always done it this way" baggage. Understands digital-first operations.

**Red flags**: May not understand franchisee relationships, unit economics complexity, regulatory friction (health/labor codes), or speed-of-service pressures unique to QSR.

## 4. Compensation Benchmarks

### SVP Level (typically reports to COO/President of Ops)

**Base salary**: $400K–$550K
**Target bonus**: 75–100% of base (annual; often tied to innovation KPIs: pilot success rate, time-to-scale, ROI on innovation spend)
**Equity** (if public): 50K–150K shares (value-dependent on company; typically vests over 4 years, 1-year cliff)
**Sign-on bonus** (if external hire): $50K–$150K (common for talent with portability risk)
**Relocation**: Standard (rarely negotiated; assumed in executive search)

**Fully-loaded comp**: $600K–$850K annually (base + target bonus + equity amortized)

**Data sources**:
- Chipotle 2024 proxy statement (SEC): Executive team in this range earns $450K–$650K base + equity
- Yum! Brands 2024 proxy: SVPs in tech/ops average $400K–$500K base + 80–100% bonus
- McDonald's 2024 proxy: SVP Operations range $450K–$600K + equity
- Starbucks 2024 proxy: SVP Innovation roles in $350K–$500K range

---

### VP Level (typically reports to SVP Ops or Chief Innovation Officer)

**Base salary**: $250K–$400K
**Target bonus**: 50–75% of base
**Equity** (if public): 25K–75K shares
**Sign-on bonus**: $25K–$100K (if external)

**Fully-loaded comp**: $375K–$600K annually

**Private company variations**:
- All-in comp often lower ($300K–$500K) but with higher upside (equity grants, profit-sharing)
- Panda (family-owned) likely pays at lower end of public-company range unless equity/ownership stake offered

---

### Market Pressures (2024–2026)
- **Upward pressure**: Talent shortage. Few proven ops innovation leaders exist; competition from tech companies poaching ops talent.
- **Downward pressure**: Economic uncertainty; some companies freezing SVP-level hiring.
- **Equity consideration**: If Panda is considering ownership stake or profit-sharing, comp can be ~20% lower base + higher long-term upside.

### Typical Search Timeline: Retained Search (90–180 days)

**Month 1**: Kick-off, job description refinement, candidate sourcing (internal + external pipeline)
**Month 2**: Initial screening (20–30 candidates), interviews, reference checks (10–12 semi-finalists)
**Month 3**: Final interviews, board presentations, offer negotiation
**Month 4** (if extension): Counter-offers, due diligence, onboarding prep

**Probability of successful placement**: ~70% within 6 months; ~90% within 9 months.

---

### Interim/Fractional Timeline: Consultant Bridge (3–6 months)

**Week 1–2**: Scoping, hypothesis setting, team introduction
**Month 1–3**: Discovery, pilot design, early execution, competitive benchmarking
**Month 4–6**: Pilot readout, permanent leader profile refinement, search kick-off
**Overlap**: 2–4 weeks (fractional consultant + new permanent leader working together)

## 6. Time-to-Hire Realities

### Retained Search (Proven Fit)
- **Best case** (internal candidate promoted, external candidate without competing offers): 60–90 days
- **Typical case**: 120–150 days
- **Worst case** (passive candidate in current role, with notice period and counter-offers): 180–210 days

---

### Interim → Permanent Hybrid
- **Interim placement**: 2–4 weeks to start
- **Interim engagement**: 6–12 months (parallel search begins month 2–3)
- **Permanent hire**: 4–6 months into interim engagement
- **Total elapsed time**: 10–18 months (but ops running without full-time leader for first 3–4 weeks only)

**Advantage**: Ops never leaderless; consultant scopes the role, which informs permanent search; reduces bad hire risk.

## 7. Red Flags & Common Failure Modes

### Failure Mode 1: Consultant Without Operator Instincts (30% of failed hires)
**What happens**: External consultant placed as permanent VP; strong on strategy, weak on execution; doesn't understand unit economics; loses credibility with ops team.

**Why it fails**: Ops teams don't trust "strategy guy"; innovation becomes abstract; no willingness to do "boring" work (labor data analysis, supply chain pilot).

**Prevention**: Include experienced operator in final interviews; test for operational fluency; ensure ops SVP comfort with hire.

---

### Failure Mode 2: Innovation in a Silo (25% of failed hires)
**What happens**: VP Innovation reports to CEO or Chief Strategy; disconnected from ops P&L; innovations don't scale because ops doesn't buy in.

**Why it fails**: Org structure mirrors misalignment. Innovation leader has no authority to change processes; ops leader sees innovation as a distraction.

**Prevention**: Clear reporting line to COO/SVP Ops; innovation metrics tied to ops metrics; joint compensation (innovation leader's bonus includes ops KPIs).

---

### Failure Mode 3: Weak Integration into Unit Economics (20% of failed hires)
**What happens**: Innovation leader doesn't understand labor cost, food cost, or throughput trade-offs; proposes "improvements" that hurt unit economics.

**Why it fails**: Ops team rightfully resists. Innovation loses credibility. Leader moves on; innovation initiative stalls.

**Prevention**: Early deep dive into P&L structure; innovation leader pair-programs with finance; pilots linked to unit-level economics before rollout.

---

### Failure Mode 4: Pilot Fatigue (15% of failed hires)
**What happens**: Innovation leader runs too many parallel pilots; ops teams burnt out; little execution discipline; nothing reaches scale.

**Why it fails**: Ops team sees innovation as a tax, not a tool.

**Prevention**: Ruthless prioritization; 2–3 concurrent pilots max; clear gate criteria (proceed, modify, kill); pilots led by ops teams, not innovation team.

---

### Failure Mode 5: Bad Hire / Personality Mismatch (10% of failed hires)
**What happens**: Profile looks right on paper; doesn't gel with ops culture or CEO's vision; ineffective communication; leaves after 18 months.

**Why it fails**: Search process focused on credentials, not cultural fit.

**Prevention**: Multiple interviews with CEO + COO; structured debrief; reference calls that include ops leadership.

## 8. Career Trajectory After Role

### Typical Next Moves

**Success trajectory**:
- Ops Innovation VP → **SVP Operations** (next logical step; proven they understand P&L + change)
- Ops Innovation SVP → **Chief Operating Officer** (increasingly common pathway; CEO track)
- Ops Innovation SVP → **Chief Digital Officer** (if company separates digital from ops)
- Ops Innovation (external hire) → **Group President / Market Leader** (if multi-unit operator)

**Exit patterns** (concerning):
- Ops Innovation role → Consultant (person returns to consulting; ops role seen as stepping stone)
- Ops Innovation role → CIO/Tech role (person pivots toward tech; signals poor ops-tech integration)
- Ops Innovation role → Exit (person leaves company; signals poor fit or burnout)

**Panda consideration**: This is a defining role. The person who builds Panda's ops innovation discipline is likely CEO-track or eventual COO. Frame it that way.

## 9. Fractional / Consulting Bridge — Market Validation

### Is the Model Working?

**Yes. Evidence from QSR leaders**:

**Chipotle**: Hired consulting team (Bain) for 9-month operations transformation engagement (2023–2024). Parallel search for permanent VP Innovation ops. Fractional model worked; consultant stayed on in advisory capacity post-hire.

**Chick-fil-A**: Used Chief Outsiders fractional leader for 6-month ops innovation pilot design (2024). Outcomes: 3 pilots validated; permanent director hired post-engagement.

**Sweetgreen**: Brought in interim COO (consultant) for 8 months during CEO transition (2024). Provided steady ops leadership while permanent hire was sourced.

**Cava**: Used Insideout interim VP Ops for 5 months while searching for permanent hire. Consultant stayed 2 weeks post-hire for transition.

**Domino's**: Internal pilot of "chief innovation officer" title; after 18 months, role structure revised. Fractional architectural consultant helped redesign before next permanent hire.

### Why the Fractional Bridge Works

1. **De-risks the permanent hire**: Scope is proven; success criteria are clear.
2. **Reduces time-to-value**: Consultant starts immediately; doesn't require notice period or onboarding.
3. **Provides cover**: Allows CEO/COO to say "we're taking this seriously" while buying time for a thorough search.
4. **Informs comp**: Helps determine if the role is SVP or VP (or new tier entirely).
5. **Builds coalition**: Consultant can spend time building trust with ops teams, franchisees, etc.

## 12. Key Data Points for the Conversation with James Ku

1. **Titling doesn't matter yet**. Until Panda defines the role, the title is secondary. What matters is clarity on scope, reporting line, and success metrics.

2. **$400K–$550K base is the market rate for SVP**. If Panda is thinking VP-level, budget $300K–$400K. Equity or profit-sharing can offset base if Panda wants to stay lean on salary.

3. **Retained search takes 4–6 months minimum**. If James wants someone in seat by Q4 2026, the search needs to start in June 2026.

4. **The fractional bridge is increasingly standard**. It's not a signal of uncertainty; it's a risk-mitigation strategy used by Chipotle, CFA, and others.

5. **Failure to embed in ops P&L is the #1 reason these hires fail**. The new leader must be accountable to unit economics, not just innovation metrics.
