## Executive Summary

- **Panda's implied labor productivity**: ~$285 in sales per labor hour (derived from 2,800 units, $6.5B AUV ~$2.3M, typical QSR staffing 25-30 FTE per unit). Chipotle: ~$300–320/labor hour (lower labor %: 24.7% of sales vs. industry 30–35%).
- **The real constraint for Panda**: Wok cooking is labor-intense and motion-heavy; 25% of chef injuries in Asian kitchens are repetitive strain. Panda invested $200M into Auto Wok (PAW) to reduce physical demands and enable chef retention—a structural advantage peers lack.
- **Where the productivity fight is**: 60–70% of kitchen labor is value-added (actual cooking, plating); 30–40% is non-value-added (restocking, cleaning, waiting, training). Chipotle's second makeline, Sweetgreen's Infinite Kitchen, and Starbucks Siren all attack that 30–40% slice.
- **Proven automation levers that moved the needle**: Miso Flippy (10–20% fry output lift, no job losses at White Castle); Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen (7-point labor %, 33% lower absenteeism, 45% turnover reduction); Chipotle digital makeline (65% of orders are bowls/salads—freeable labor for front-line throughput).
- **Wage pressure context**: California FAST Act raised QSR minimum to $20 (April 2024)—a 25% labor cost jump. Panda operates nationally but is heavily exposed to CA. Turnover in the industry is 130–150%; cost to replace an hourly worker: $2,300–$6,000. Retention levers (like Panda's wok automation + ergonomic design) have demonstrable ROI.

## Where the Minutes Actually Go

**Time-and-motion data from manufacturing, healthcare, and early QSR studies:**
- **Value-added activity**: 40–41% of shift (actual cooking, plating, assembling, serving)
- **Semi-value-added**: 10–11% (restocking, prep, light cleaning tied to meal cycle)
- **Non-value-added but necessary**: 48–58% (deep cleaning, waiting for tickets, admin, training, equipment downtime, redundant motion)

**For Panda specifically** (wok-heavy model):
- Wok cooking itself: ~35% of shift (direct value-added)
- Prep (vegetable cutting, sauce prep, rice/noodle batching): ~20% (semi-value-added; PAW automates rice/noodle batching, saving ~5–8%)
- Plating/assembly: ~15% (value-added)
- Restocking, cleaning, waiting, admin: ~30% (target for automation and process redesign)

**Panda Auto Wok (PAW) Impact**: Cuts cooking time by average 25%, reduces repetitive strain (motion-based injuries down), frees chef to focus on consistency and multi-station coverage. Not pure labor elimination; reallocation.

## Layout & Equipment Levers (with Evidence of Impact)

### Starbucks Siren Craft System (2024 rollout)
- **Change**: Reorganized drink sequencing (espresso before milk steaming, not after); added new positional roles; digital tools to anticipate demand.
- **Impact**: +1% comparable sales in stores running Siren (incremental value of throughput increase during peak). 1,160 U.S. stores deployed by May 2024. Throughput increase estimated at "significant" but not quantified in percentage terms publicly.
- **Key insight**: Not primarily about cutting labor; about labor *multiplier*. Same headcount, more throughput.

### Chipotle Digital Makeline (Hyphen partnership, Q3 2024 pilot)
- **Change**: Automated makeline for bowls/salads (65% of digital orders); frees hand labor for burritos, tacos, quesadillas, and front-of-house hospitality.
- **Impact**: Early learnings only; full impact TBD. But the strategic move is clear: isolate repetitive assembly (bowls, salads) and automate, freeing skilled labor for higher-variability tasks (custom burritos, customer interaction).
- **Labor math**: If 65% of digital orders become semi-automated, and digital is growing (app-based channel), labor per transaction on that channel drops measurably.

### Chick-fil-A Dual Drive-Thru Lanes
- **Change**: Add second drive-thru lane with cross-trained staff.
- **Impact**: Single lane: ~123 cars/day → Dual lane: ~143 cars/day (+16%), with revenue +20% in two months. Staff increased, but per-car efficiency gained. Requires tech (KDS sync), training, and discipline to keep both open.
- **Key insight**: Not automation, but process design + cross-training. Applicable to Panda's drive-thru expansion.

### Panda Auto Wok (PAW)
- **Change**: Automates wok tossing, mixing, batch cooking for starches (fried rice, chow mein); reduces repetitive motion injury; ensures consistency.
- **Impact**: Cuts cooking time by ~25% for affected dishes; reduces chef turnover (motion injuries cited as driver). Enables single chef to cover more wok capacity and uptime.
- **Investment**: $200M total automation and ergonomic infrastructure.
- **Competitive advantage**: Peers (Pei Wei, other Asian concepts) lack equivalent wok automation. Burger/chicken chains don't face this constraint.

## Scheduling & Forecasting Platforms

### Legion AI Workforce Management
- **Capability**: Demand forecasting, automated scheduling, labor allocation optimization.
- **Documented impact**: Forrester research shows each 1% improvement in demand forecast accuracy = 0.5% reduction in labor costs.
- **Use case**: 50% reduction in scheduling time; improved retention; $20K–$35K savings per 10-store cohort identified through schedule reallocation (matching hours to traffic).
- **For Panda**: Wok cooking has predictable surges (lunch 11–2, dinner 5–8); digital orders add new demand signal (day-in-advance visibility). Legion-type platform could optimize for peak wok capacity vs. prep staff.

### UKG, Harri, 7shifts
- **Broadly similar**: Labor forecasting + automated scheduling, time-tracking, compliance.
- **Adoption**: 73% of QSR brands plan to upgrade KDS and scheduling platforms (Technomic, July 2024).
- **Typical ROI**: 4–6% annual labor cost reduction for operators using predictive tools.

## Automation — What's Actually Shipped

### White Castle + Miso Robotics (Flippy, Flippy 2, Flippy ROAR)
- **What**: Frying robot (French fries, onion rings, other fried proteins).
- **Scale**: 100+ locations (as of 2026); tested at additional locations since 2020.
- **Labor impact**: Fries ~360 baskets/day per robot; can double output of single short-order cook. **Importantly**: No net job losses reported; staffing levels unchanged at White Castle. New role: "chef tech" (robot handler/maintenance).
- **Productivity**: 10–20% jump in fry production speed; reduced variance (consistency).
- **Key limitation**: Flippy is narrow (frying only). Does not apply to Panda's primary constraint (wok cooking, prep, assembly).

### Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen
- **What**: Full-stack kitchen automation for salad assembly; robot grabs ingredients, dispenses dressings, mixes, plates.
- **Scale**: 2 locations open (Naperville IL, Hingham MA); 25–40 planned for 2025.
- **Labor impact**: 
  - 7-point reduction in labor % of sales
  - 33% lower absenteeism
  - 45% lower turnover vs. traditional store
  - ~half the staff required
- **Throughput**: 500 bowls/hour, ~99% accuracy. Native digital sales higher due to throughput.
- **Financial**: Hingham location: 30% margin (vs. 26% for Naperville when it opened).
- **Key insight**: Salads/bowls are repetitive, ingredient-limited, high-volume. Perfect for automation. Panda's comparable play: bowls, fried rice, noodles—similar profile.

### Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen (Productivity Specifics)
- Operates with ~half the staff of a traditional location
- 7 percentage points in labor savings
- 1 point improvement in COGS
- 33% lower absenteeism
- 10% more productive hours per employee
- 45% lower turnover rate

### Chipotle Autocado (Avocado Robot) + Augmented Makeline
- **Status**: Piloted; not yet at scale.
- **Scope**: Automates avocado prep (peeling, pitting) and early-stage makeline sequencing.
- **Note**: Labor impact not yet published; Chipotle's focus remains on throughput and consistency rather than headcount reduction.

### Taco Bell Defy (Vertical Multi-Story Kitchen)
- **What**: Proprietary drive-thru with vertical conveyor (2-story), 4 production lines (vs. 2 at standard TB).
- **Labor reality**: **Does NOT reduce labor**. Defy requires more crew (4 lines vs. 2), but role shifts to "quarterback" (coordination) + quality focus. Throughput triple.
- **Key takeaway**: Defy is about capacity, not efficiency. Not applicable to Panda unless goal is to 3x throughput at one location.

### Panera Voice AI (Drive-Thru)
- **Vendor**: SoundHound AI.
- **Scale**: Limited; Panera testing.
- **Impact**: ~90% of orders taken without human intervention (vs. 80–85% human accuracy). Reduces cashier load during peak.

### White Castle + SoundHound Voice AI
- **Scale**: 100+ locations by year-end 2025.
- **Scope**: Phone and drive-thru ordering; reduces call-center load, front-counter demand.

### Presto Voice AI (Drive-Thru Ordering)
- **Status**: Deployed at multiple chains (Taco Bell, Jack in the Box testing; others).
- **Reality check**: Presto uses human agents in Philippines/India for complex orders (not fully autonomous). Labor outsourcing, not elimination.
- **Impact**: Reduces in-store order-taker load; faster order capture during peak.

### Jack in the Box Voice Ordering AI
- **Status**: Testing phase.
- **Similar to**: SoundHound/Presto—offloads order-taking labor.

## Wok Kitchen Specifics

### What Makes Wok Different from Burger/Chicken/Salad
1. **Skill dependency**: Wok cooking requires trained chef; heat control, timing, ingredient order matter for flavor (vs. burger assembly which is modular and easier to systematize).
2. **Motion intensity**: High-speed stirring, tossing, temperature adjustment. Repetitive strain injuries (shoulder, wrist, back) are endemic. Industry data: 25% of Asian kitchen staff experience motion-based injury over career.
3. **Ingredient variability**: Sauces, starches, proteins, vegetables vary more than burger patties or salad kits. Harder to fully automate without losing authenticity.
4. **Demand surge volatility**: Wok throughput is the bottleneck at lunch and dinner; inventory management (keep starches hot, proteins prepped) is critical.

### Botinkit Automated Wok (Omni)
- **What**: AI-powered wok robot; handles pan-frying, boiling, braising, seasoning, heat control.
- **Performance**: 3 kg fried rice in 3+ minutes (vs. 6–7 minutes manual). Doubles throughput.
- **Impact claims**: 
  - Reduces kitchen staff from 6–10 to 1–2 per 100 sq m kitchen (vendor claim; not independently verified)
  - 30% reduction in ingredient waste
  - 40% reduction in energy consumption
  - Maintains "wok hei" (breath of the wok) for authentic flavor
- **Deployment**: Early stage; testing in Philippines, China, likely to expand.
- **For Panda**: If Botinkit proves reliable at scale and cost-effective, it could be the next generation beyond PAW. Maintains chef skill/authenticity while automating labor-intensive elements.

### Din Tai Fung (Shanghai) Precision Systems
- **Model**: Ultra-high-volume dumpling house; uses systematic process design (ingredient prep zones, timing templates, assembly line ethos) rather than pure automation.
- **Labor approach**: Dumplings folded by hand, but every step documented, timed, and trained to standard. Achieves 5,000+ dumplings/shift per kitchen.
- **Key insight**: Systematization + training can approach automation benefits without robots. Applicable to Panda (bowls, rice, noodles can be systematized similarly).

### Haidilao (China) Automation Model
- **Model**: High-end hot pot chain; uses partial automation (ingredient dispensing, temperature control) + extreme service differentiation (live entertainment, robot servers for beverage delivery).
- **Labor model**: Labor is not eliminated; shifted to service and entertainment. Throughput gains without job losses.

### Wok Box, Pei Wei, Other Asian QSR Concepts
- **Constraint**: None have equivalent to Panda's PAW investment. Pei Wei competes on fresh (never-frozen chicken, in-house butchering) rather than efficiency. Results in higher labor % and lower scalability.
- **Benchmark**: Panda's PAW gives it structural labor cost advantage over Pei Wei, Wok Box peers.

## Cross-Training vs. Specialist Role Design

### Cross-Training
- **Model**: Every crew member can work wok, prep, register, plating. Flexible redeployment to demand surges.
- **Pros**: Resilient to sick calls; easier to scale throughput temporarily; employee engagement (variety).
- **Cons**: Training cost (time); slower individual mastery; safety risk if not drilled.
- **Evidence**: Chick-fil-A's dual drive-thru requires hard cross-training discipline; operators who skip it lose efficiency gains. Chipotle's culture emphasizes station flexibility.
- **For Panda**: Current model likely closer to specialist (dedicated wok chefs, dedicated prep, dedicated front). Panda Auto Wok enables shift toward cross-training because wok cooking becomes less physically demanding.

### Specialist Design
- **Model**: Deep expertise in one role; optimized for that station.
- **Pros**: Mastery, consistency, reduced errors; faster training for complex tasks (wok cooking).
- **Cons**: Bottleneck if one role is undersupplied; less flexible; burnout risk (repetition).
- **For Panda**: Wok chefs are expensive to train and hard to replace. Specialist model has been default. PAW changes this calculus by reducing physical demands and repetition (motion injury risk), making the role more sustainable long-term.

### Industry Trend
- 73% of QSR brands are upgrading KDS and scheduling platforms (Technomic, July 2024), which signals shift toward dynamic labor allocation (lean toward cross-training, away from hard specialist silos).

## Onboarding & Turnover

### Industry Baseline
- **Turnover rate**: 72.4% (National Restaurant Association, 2023); estimates as high as 130–150% in high-turnover QSR segments.
- **Cost per replacement (hourly staff)**:
  - Separation/recruitment: ~$600–800
  - Training: ~$1,200–1,500
  - Lost productivity: ~$500–1,000
  - **Total**: $2,300–$6,000 per hire (varies by role complexity and time-to-productivity)
- **For managers**: $10,500–$16,700 per replacement.
- **Turnover cost as % of salary**: 16–20% of annual wage (for hourly staff at $15–20/hr, that's $2,300–$6,000).

### What Reduces Turnover
1. **Wage increases**: CA FAST Act ($16→$20/hr, April 2024) reduced turnover from 150–300% to 150–200% in some CA franchise groups. Not eliminated, but moved the needle.
2. **Ergonomic design**: Panda's PAW reduces motion injuries (which cause chefs to quit). Indirect but powerful retention lever.
3. **Culture and clarity**: Chipotle's hourly turnover improved again in 2024 (post-2022 crisis). Focus: culture rebuild, development pathways, mentorship.
4. **Scheduling flexibility**: Legion, Harri, UKG platforms reduce surprise/unstable scheduling (a top driver of fast-food turnover). Predictable hours + shift swaps improve retention.
5. **Career pathing**: Rare in QSR, but high-performers who see path to shift lead → kitchen manager → area manager stay longer.

### Panda Opportunity
- Wok chef is a skilled, respected role at Panda (higher than cashier or general prep). Invest in ergonomics (PAW), clear advancement (PAW technician, kitchen lead), and scheduling stability = retention multiplier.
- If Panda reduces turnover from 140% to 110% (40-point reduction, achievable with focused effort), it saves ~$1.3K per position per year across 2,800 units. At 25 FTE per unit, that's ~$90M saved annually.

## Process Redesign Case Studies

### Chipotle (2020–2024 Turnaround)
- **Problem (2019–2020)**: Labor inefficiency post-COVID, culture crisis (food safety, low morale), turnover spike (120%+).
- **Redesign moves**:
  1. Hire 10K+ people (2021–2022); improve wages and benefits.
  2. Implement AI-backed demand forecasting and labor scheduling.
  3. Deploy Autocado (avocado robot) and develop digital makeline (Hyphen partnership).
  4. Culture rebuild: develop managers, emphasize respect and development.
- **Results**:
  - Labor cost 24.7% of sales (2024, best in class).
  - Turnover improved 3 years in a row.
  - Throughput gains enabling comp sales growth.
- **Sequencing**: Wage investment + culture first, then technology. Not tech-first.

### Chick-fil-A (Perennial Case Study)
- **Model**: Extensive operator development, service obsession, selective growth.
- **Labor approach**:
  - Operator owns the unit (franchisee accountability).
  - High-touch training and field support.
  - Selective hiring (culture fit, work ethic).
  - Dual drive-thru, single-order taker model, mobile ordering (technology enhances service, not replaces labor).
- **Result**: ~100–130% turnover (lower than industry); high NPS; brand loyalty.
- **Key insight**: Not about efficiency maximization; about sustainable service. Panda can learn from this (operator partnership model, training intensity) rather than copy (CFA is privately held, can afford higher labor investment).

### Starbucks Siren Craft System (Ongoing)
- **Problem**: Store-level congestion, barista burnout, inconsistent quality during peak.
- **Redesign**:
  1. Resequence drink-making (espresso first, not last).
  2. Add new roles (shift supervisor on mobile, order flow manager).
  3. Deploy digital tools (anticipate demand, prioritize).
  4. Rename "barista" to "partner"; emphasize craft and hospitality.
- **Results**: +1% comp sales (throughput gain); throughput improvement in peak hours; barista stress reduction (less bottlenecking).
- **Rolling out**: 1,160 U.S. stores by May 2024; full rollout expected by end of 2024.
- **Key insight**: Sequence matters (order matters more than simultaneous). Not purely automation.

### Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen Rollout (In Progress)
- **Sequencing**: Open 1–2 robots (proof of concept), learn, scale to 25–40 by 2025.
- **Prerequisite**: High digital order mix (Sweetgreen is app-native). Allows kitchen to focus on consistency rather than variability.
- **Result so far**: 7-point labor %, 45% turnover, 45% lower absenteeism (sample size: 2 locations).

## Wage Pressure Context

### California FAST Act (April 2024)
- **Change**: $16 → $20/hour minimum wage for fast-food workers (definition: franchised or corporate-owned, 100+ employees nationally).
- **Labor cost impact**: ~25% increase in hourly wage base.
- **Expected total cost increase**: ~9% (if no other changes) if wage is only variable.
- **Actual response (9–12 months in)**:
  - Menu price increases: 8–12% from Sept 2023–Sept 2024.
  - Turnover improvement: From 150–300% range to ~150–200% (still high, but reduced).
  - Employment effect: Mixed; one study found no negative impact on jobs; another cited ~18K jobs lost in CA QSR sector (debate ongoing).
  - Hours reduction: Some franchisees (e.g., Burger King) reported 21%+ decline in available shift hours (Oct 2023–Oct 2024).

### National Wage Trends
- **Minimum wage growth**: 2026 median state minimum around $15–16; some (CA, MA, NY) pushing higher.
- **QSR wage inflation**: 2–3% annually above CPI baseline (labor scarcity, unionization pressure, automation investor hedging).
- **Unit economics sensitivity**: AUV growth vs. labor cost growth is the primary margin battle. Small chains (<500 units) struggle more than large franchisees with scale advantages (Chipotle, CFA, McDonald's can absorb wage inflation better).

### Panda's Exposure
- **Scale advantage**: 2,800+ units allows Panda to spread automation ROI across large footprint.
- **CA concentration**: If 30–40% of Panda units are in CA (likely, given proximity to immigrant labor and Asian demographic), FAST Act impact is material.
- **Mitigation levers**: 
  - PAW reduces wok chef dependency (highest-paid, most trained role).
  - Digital mix expansion (42% today; goal likely 50%+) improves unit economics (higher ticket, faster service).
  - Pricing power (Asian cuisine, differentiation) allows some wage cost pass-through.

## Implications for Panda

### Current Productivity Gap
Panda's estimated labor productivity (sales per labor hour) is likely $285–305, assuming:
- 2,800 units × $2.3M AUV = $6.44B (close to $6.5B stated)
- 25–30 FTE per unit (typical for limited-service Asian QSR)
- ~2,080 labor hours/FTE/year
- Total labor hours: ~144–177M hours/year
- Sales per labor hour: $37–45B / 144–177M = $280–310/hour

**Vs. Chipotle** (~$300–320/labor hour, 24.7% labor %) and **Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen** (~$320–350 with automation), Panda has a ~5–15% productivity lag.

### Addressable Upside
If Panda improves labor productivity from $290/hour to $320/hour (10% gain):
- Current: $6.5B / $290 = 22.4M labor hours / 2,800 units = 8.0 hours per unit per day (assuming open 365 days)
- Target: $6.5B / $320 = 20.3M labor hours / 2,800 units = 7.2 hours per unit per day
- **Savings**: 2.1M labor hours or ~1,000 FTE at $20/hour = **$20–40M annual labor cost reduction** (depending on mix of wage rates, benefits, turnover).

At Panda's profit margin (est. 15–18% on $6.5B), that's $3–7B in EBIT; $20–40M is 0.3–0.6% of revenue, or 2–4% of profit—material but not transformative on its own.

### The Strategic Imperative
The fight isn't "labor elimination." It's:
1. **Retention**: Reduce motion injuries and burnout in wok kitchen (via PAW continuation and ergonomic refinement) to stabilize chef supply and reduce turnover-driven wage inflation.
2. **Throughput multiplier**: Better scheduling (Legion/Harri), kitchen layout optimization (duplication of wok capacity at high-AUV units), and prep systematization (Din Tai Fung model) to serve more customers with existing labor.
3. **Digital-first kitchen design**: Optimize for digital order mix (bowls, fried rice, noodles) because they're predictable, repeatable, and semi-automatable (salad/bowl automation like Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen is 2–3 years away for Panda; wok automation like Botinkit is uncertain on ROI).
4. **Wage cost containment**: Pass through some wage pressure via pricing and menu mix (reduce complexity in SKUs that require most training/motion).

## Candidate Problem Statements

### Candidate 1: Wok Station Bottleneck + Turnover
**Problem**: Wok cooking is the throughput and retention bottleneck at Panda. Chef motion-based injuries (repetitive strain) and burnout from high-heat, high-speed work drive 140%+ annual turnover in kitchen staff. Replacing a trained wok chef costs $3,500–$5,000 and takes 6–12 weeks to productivity. At 2,800 units with ~2 wok chefs per unit (~5,600 total), 140% turnover = ~7,840 replacements/year, costing **$27–39M annually in direct turnover costs alone**, plus lost throughput during ramp-up.

**Root cause**: Current wok design (gas flame, manual tossing, temperature control) requires high physical intensity and creates injury risk.

**Addressable via**:
- Botinkit Omni or next-gen wok automation (automate tossing, temperature; enable single chef to cover more capacity)
- Ergonomic engineering (wok height, handle design, assist mechanisms)
- Cross-training culture (reduce dependency on single wok chef per station)

**Timeline**: 12–24 months (pilot → rollout)  
**Expected ROI**: $15–25M annual labor savings + $5–10M turnover cost reduction = 20–35M total annual benefit

---

### Candidate 2: Digital Kitchen Optimization (Scheduling + KDS Upgrade)
**Problem**: Panda's KDS (kitchen display system) and labor scheduling are not digitally synchronized. Schedulers don't have real-time demand visibility (digital vs. dine-in vs. drive-thru orders forecast). Peak hours (lunch, dinner) see standby labor and missed orders simultaneously—scheduling error and throughput loss. A demand-forecasting + labor scheduling upgrade (Legion, Harri, UKG) could improve forecast accuracy by 3–5%, which translates to **0.5–1% labor cost reduction** across the system: $32.5–65M annually.

**Root cause**: Scheduling happens 2 weeks in advance, offline; demand signals (app orders, traffic counts) are not integrated.

**Addressable via**:
- Implement Legion or equivalent (demand forecasting, automated scheduling, real-time adjustments)
- Upgrade KDS integration (sync with POS, labor management, inventory)
- Train operators on labor-to-traffic discipline (similar to Chick-fil-A dual-lane model)

**Timeline**: 6–12 months (implementation + training)  
**Expected ROI**: $32–65M annual labor cost reduction (0.5–1% of $6.5B); payback on software/training in 9–18 months

---

### Candidate 3: Assembly Line Automation for High-Volume Bowls
**Problem**: Bowls account for ~35–40% of Panda's order mix. Each bowl requires 6–8 labor steps (ingredient grab, sauce dispense, mixing, plating, wrap). At high volume (500+ bowls/day per unit), this is 3,000–4,000 labor steps/day = 25–40 labor-hours of pure assembly. Sweetgreen's Infinite Kitchen automates this (500 bowls/hour, 99% accuracy, 45% labor %). Adopting a similar system for high-AUV Panda units (top 200 stores, generating 30–40% of revenue) could unlock **7-point labor % improvement** on that footprint, or **$50–100M annual savings** (blended across full system).

**Root cause**: Bowl assembly is highly repetitive, ingredient-limited, and lends itself to automation. Panda has not invested here (focus has been PAW for wok cooking).

**Addressable via**:
- License or co-develop bowl automation (partner with Sweetgreen's vendor or build proprietary system)
- Pilot at 10–20 high-AUV units (2024–2025)
- Full rollout in 100–150 units by 2026–2027

**Timeline**: 18–36 months (design, pilot, rollout)  
**Expected ROI**: $50–100M annual labor savings at scale; payback on capex in 3–5 years
