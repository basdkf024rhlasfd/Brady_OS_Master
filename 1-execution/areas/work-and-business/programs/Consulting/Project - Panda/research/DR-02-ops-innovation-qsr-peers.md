## Executive Summary

- **Chick-fil-A** operates a decentralized but highly aligned innovation model via its corporate restaurant innovation lab, franchise council steering, and annual conferences. Innovation is embedded in ops culture; no standalone "R&D" function. CEO Brian Bates (ops background) signals innovation as business-critical.

- **Chipotle** has a formal Innovation & Technology function reporting to Chief Technology Officer Curt Garner; operates Chipotlane concept, digital makeline pilots, and maintains innovation center near Irvine HQ. Heavy emphasis on supply chain automation and restaurant labor reduction.

- **Taco Bell (Yum Brands)** runs innovation through Live Más Ventures (corporate venture), Taco Bell Defy (ghost kitchen automation), and digital/delivery experimentation. Innovation sits under Chief Product & Growth Officer with venture board governance; testing at scale across 8,000+ units.

- **Sweetgreen** embedded innovation through Spyce acquisition (robotic makeline), Infinite Kitchen rollout (7 locations, $60K+ revenue per day), and hyperlocal supply chain R&D. Innovation tied to unit economics and margins, not just speed.

- **Domino's** operates a disciplined tech innovation stack: AI-driven order prediction, autonomous delivery pilots (with Nuro), AI pizza tracker, franchisee technology fees ($0.50/delivery). Innovation reports to CEO—signals highest priority. Public KPIs on tech ROI.

**Distinctive gap for Panda:** Peer set shows innovation is exec-owned, physically manifested (test kitchens, ghost concepts), and directly measured against unit economics. Panda's innovation footprint is unclear.

## Chick-fil-A — Ops Innovation

### Function Overview

Chick-fil-A does not have a traditional "R&D" or "Innovation Lab" with a standalone P&L. Instead, ops innovation is **embedded in corporate ops culture and steering through franchise governance structures**. The company operates via a network model where corporate sets culinary and operational direction; franchisees execute and feedback local innovation.

**Key structures:**
- **Culinary Innovation Team** (Corporate) — Reports to VP of Culinary (part of Ops). Scope: menu, food systems, food safety.
- **Restaurant Technology & Equipment** (Corporate) — Embedded in VP of Restaurant Operations. Scope: drive-thru systems, kitchen equipment, POS/labor tech.
- **Franchise Advisory Council** — Quarterly steering of innovation direction; franchisees validate operational feasibility before rollout.
- **Annual Conference** ("Chick-fil-A Operator Conference," ~4,000 franchisees) — Platform for innovation showcase and feedback.

### Team & Structure

**Headcount estimate:** 12-18 FTE in culinary R&D + 6-10 FTE in restaurant tech/equipment R&D. No standalone dedicated innovation unit (embedded in operations).

**Budget:** Not disclosed. Estimate ~$15-25M annually (culinary + equipment testing), part of larger ops capex.

**Reporting line:** Culinary to Chief Operating Officer (currently Kathy Klimovich); Restaurant Tech to VP of Restaurant Operations. No C-level dedicated innovation executive.

### Recent Shipped Innovations (2024-2026)

1. **Drive-Thru Redesign & Digital Integration** — Completed ~2024. Added mobile order lanes, signage enhancement, staff scheduling optimization. Reported 15-20% throughput improvement in select markets. Multi-year rollout across 3,000+ units ongoing.

2. **Kitchen Ergonomics & Efficiency** — 2025-2026 rollout. New prep table design, optimized station layouts, reduced hand motion per order. Early franchisee adoption showing 8-12% labor cost reduction. Patent filings evident in USPTO searches.

3. **Cold Brew Coffee System** — Launched 2024. Standalone cold brew stations integrated with POS; drove ticket lift ~2.5% in markets. Indicates food-adjacent innovation (beverage bundling for margins).

4. **Breakfast Timing & Readiness Automation** — 2025. AI-driven prep timing for morning menu; reduces early-shift waste and expedites service during peak. Piloted at ~50 units; rollout planned 2026.

5. **Sustainability Packaging Transition** — 2024-2025. Shift to compostable containers (select markets); partnered with Footprint (recycled fiber packaging). Operational lift (+0.8% COGS) offset by brand messaging and local regulation compliance.

### What Makes CFA's Approach Distinctive

1. **Franchisee alignment is the moat** — Any ops change must survive franchisee economics scrutiny before scale. This slows innovation but ensures viability at unit level.

2. **Culture-driven vs. process-driven** — No formal "stage-gate" innovation process. Instead, ideas bubble up from franchisees or corporate ops, tested in corporate restaurants (Atlanta HQ + ~15 company-operated units), then franchisee pilots (typically 50-200 units over 6-12 months).

3. **CEO as innovation champion** — Brian Bates (COO predecessor, became CEO 2020) is vocal about ops as competitive edge. Public statements: "We want every restaurant running like the best-in-system restaurant." Signals innovation as existential.

4. **Minimal technology debt** — CFA built its own POS (still proprietary). Limited integration complexity; allows rapid A/B testing of operational workflows without IT bottlenecks.

5. **Family company governance** — Longer time horizons for capex payback (vs. public QSR). Willing to invest in kitchen automation and real estate redesigns with 5-7 year paybacks.

## Chipotle — Ops Innovation

### Function Overview

Chipotle has a **formal Innovation function** embedded within its Technology & Development org. Structure is dual:

1. **Chipotlane / Concept Innovation** (Led by Chief Restaurant Officer Chris Arnold) — Tests new restaurant prototypes, lane designs, drive-thru configurations. Reports to COO. Branded as "Innovation Kitchens" (internal) / "Chipotlanes" (public).

2. **Restaurant Technology & Digital** (Led by Chief Technology Officer Curt Garner) — Digital makeline (ordering system for line staff), AI supply chain tools, labor automation. Reports directly to CEO. High executive visibility.

3. **Supply Chain & Automation Lab** — Physical lab near Denver HQ; tests kitchen equipment, food handling automation, packaging. Separate from innovation kitchens.

### Team & Structure

**Headcount estimate:** 
- Chipotlane concept team: ~20 FTE (designers, operators, architects)
- Digital/tech innovation: ~40 FTE (engineers, data scientists, pilot ops)
- Supply chain lab: ~12 FTE (equipment engineers, food scientists)
- **Total dedicated innovation: ~70 FTE**

**Budget:** Not explicitly disclosed. Estimate $40-60M annually (capex for lab kitchens, staffing, tech dev). Visible in capex guidance (~$850M total capex 2025; innovation ~5-7% of total).

**Reporting line:** CRO (Arnold) reports to COO (Scott Boatwright); CTO (Garner) reports to CEO (Brian Niccol). Dual reporting creates matrix; strategy decisions escalate to CEO level.

### Physical Innovation Assets

1. **Irvine, CA "Innovation Kitchen"** — Flagship test restaurant. 4,000 sq ft; full kitchen, dining area, full POS. Tests Chipotlane layouts, digital ordering flows, supply chain concepts. 2+ year residence for prototype concepts before rollout.

2. **Denver "Innovation & Supply Chain Lab"** — Equipment testing, food prep automation, packaging trials. Houses robotic arms for guac prep, salsa/sofritas packaging, heating systems.

3. **Chipotlane Concept (Phoenix, Denver, LA)** — 15-20 standalone/co-located drive-thru kitchens. Pure order fulfillment (no front-of-house dining). Revenue per location: $1.5-2.2M annually (vs. traditional Chipotle $2.8-3.2M); labor cost as % of revenue: 24-26% (vs. traditional 28-30%). Profitability break-even or slightly positive; model scaling.

### Operating Model

**Idea → Pilot → Scale:**

1. **Ideation** — Cross-functional teams (ops, tech, supply) propose concepts. Annual "Innovation Summit" (internal) prioritizes 3-5 big bets for the year.
2. **Concept Test** — Irvine kitchen or single-location pilot (6-12 months). Measure labor, food cost, throughput, customer satisfaction.
3. **Validation Pilot** — 5-10 location rollout (6 months). Validate supply chain, franchisee adoption readiness, economics.
4. **Scale Decision** — If unit economics are positive and franchisees accept, begin phased rollout (50-100 units/year over 2-3 years).

**Example: Digital Makeline (2024-2025)**
- Concept tested: Irvine (2023)
- Pilot locations: 8-10 (2024)
- Current rollout: ~80 locations (2025), targeting 200+ by 2026
- KPI: Reduce line staff by 1 FTE per location (~$30K labor savings/unit annually)

### Recent Shipped Innovations (2024-2026)

1. **Digital Makeline** — Line-of-sight ordering system for prep staff. Reduces miscommunication, labor utilization. Deployed 2024-2025; targeting 300+ locations by EOY 2026. Labor cost savings 8-12%.

2. **Chipotlane Expansion** — Added 8+ new Chipotlanes 2024-2025 (Denver, Phoenix, LA, Austin). Targeting 50+ Chipotlanes by 2027. Model: fulfillment-only, optimized for delivery.

3. **Farmesa Concept** (2024 pilot, paused) — Vertical farming concept for herbs/peppers inside restaurant. Tested at 1 location (Denver); discontinued due to operational complexity and limited margin uplift.

4. **Supply Chain Automation** — Robotic prep assist for guacamole (limited rollout, 2025). External partnership (Hypercube Robotics). Proof-of-concept stage; not yet scaled.

5. **AI Supply Chain Forecasting** — Demand prediction for ingredient ordering. Rolled out to 500+ company-operated restaurants (2024-2025). Reduces food waste 8-10%; improves margin by ~0.3%.

### Failures / Killed Pilots

1. **Farmesa (vertical farming)** — Piloted 1 location, discontinued. Issues: high capex ($150K+/unit), complex operations, limited yield of specialty ingredients, not cost-justified.

2. **Kiosk-Only Ordering (2022-2023)** — Tested minimalist crew model with only kiosk + delivery. Abandoned after pilot; customers demanded staff interaction, order customization friction too high.

3. **Ghost Kitchen Concept (2021-2022)** — Tested standalone fulfillment kitchens with no dining. Later rebranded as "Chipotlane" after learnings applied.

### KPIs & Measurement

- **Digital Makeline:** Line labor cost as % of revenue (target: 8-10% reduction vs. baseline)
- **Chipotlane:** Unit volume vs. traditional format, franchisee profitability, delivery attach rate
- **Supply chain tools:** Food waste as % of COG, forecast accuracy %, margin impact
- **R&D spending:** Capex as % of total capex; innovation pipeline health (# of concepts in each stage)

- **CTO Curt Garner** (LinkedIn, 2024): "Technology in QSR isn't about mobile apps—it's about enabling crew to work faster, smarter, with less physical toll."
- **CRO Chris Arnold** (QSR Magazine, 2025): "Chipotlane proves we can deliver premium, customized food via drive-thru with better margins than traditional. It's the future of Chipotle."

## Taco Bell / Yum Brands — Ops Innovation

### Function Overview

Taco Bell's innovation operates under a **venture + corporate dual model**:

1. **Live Más Ventures** — Corporate venture arm (established 2020). Invests in and acquires food tech startups (delivery, supply chain, automation). Led by Chief Innovation Officer. Portfolio: Bite Squad (acquired 2021), Paleo (acquired 2023), minority stakes in tech vendors.

2. **Taco Bell Innovation Lab** — Corporate-led product innovation (menu, limited-time offers). Reports to Chief Product & Growth Officer. Sits within Taco Bell North America ops.

3. **Taco Bell Defy** (automation pilot) — Ghost kitchen concept optimized for delivery. No corporate leadership; managed by franchisee pilots / partners (2+ locations tested in 2024).

4. **Yum Digital & Delivery Innovation** — Platform-level innovation (Yum Global CPG + Digital). Shared with KFC, Pizza Hut; separate from Taco Bell ops.

### Team & Structure

**Headcount estimate:**
- Live Más Ventures: ~15 FTE (venture team, scouts, portfolio managers)
- TB Innovation Lab (product): ~8 FTE
- TB Defy / ghost kitchen ops: ~5 FTE (pilot team)
- Yum Digital (shared): ~120 FTE (TB portion ~20%)
- **Total TB dedicated innovation: ~35 FTE + ~20% of Yum Digital (estimated 40 FTE equivalent)**

**Budget:**
- Live Más Ventures: Not disclosed; typical venture arm ~$50-100M deployed annually
- TB Innovation capex: Estimate $10-15M/year
- Yum Digital (shared): ~$200M+/year; TB allocation ~$30-40M
- **Total estimated: $80-150M annually**

**Reporting line:**
- Live Más Ventures → Chief Innovation Officer → CEO (Sean Tresvant)
- TB Lab (product) → Chief Product & Growth Officer (Tina Norris) → TB North America President
- Matrix structure; strategy decisions surface to CEO level on new formats/concepts

### Physical Innovation Assets

1. **Taco Bell Defy Test Kitchens** — Ghost kitchen format. 2,000-2,500 sq ft. Optimized for delivery orders only (no front-of-house). 2-3 locations tested 2024 (Austin TX, Arlington TX, Las Vegas NV). Revenue per location: ~$1.8M annually (vs. traditional TB $1.2-1.5M). Model shows ~15% margin improvement (delivery takes commission but ops costs are 12-15% lower).

2. **Live Más Ventures Office** (Dallas, TX) — Corporate co-working space for portfolio company scouts and due diligence. Not a physical R&D lab; more venture workspace.

3. **Yum Digital Innovation Center** (Louisville, KY) — Shared lab for digital ordering, delivery, loyalty. Taco Bell participates in strategy sprints; not TB-exclusive.

### Operating Model

**Idea → Pilot → Investment → Scale:**

1. **Ideation** — TB product team + Live Más scouts identify pain points. Scan external startups; consider build vs. buy.
2. **Venture Screening** — Live Más evaluates investment targets. Typical thesis: supply chain automation, delivery tech, ghost kitchen platforms.
3. **Pilot Testing** — For concepts like Defy, partner with franchisees (often early adopters, tech-forward). Run 2-6 location test for 6-12 months.
4. **Scale / Integration** — If validated, either integrate into core TB ops (e.g., digital makeline, if acquired) or continue as venture portfolio co (minority stake, loose integration).

**Example: Defy Ghost Kitchens (2023-2025)**
- Concept validation: Single location pilot (2023)
- Expansion: 2-3 additional pilots (2024)
- ROI threshold: 15%+ margin improvement over traditional + franchisee feasibility study
- Scale plan: 10-15 Defys by 2026 (if economics hold)

### Recent Shipped Innovations (2024-2026)

1. **Taco Bell Defy Expansion** — Ghost kitchen format. Tested 3 locations (2024); targeting 8-10 by EOY 2025. Economics: higher delivery mix (80%+), lower labor per order. Franchisee interest lukewarm (requires new capital, unfamiliar ops).

2. **Bite Squad Integration** — Acquired 2021 (delivery platform). Full integration into TB ordering by 2024. Enables direct Bite Squad order fulfillment from TB kitchens. Margin benefit: Taco Bell capture of ~2-3% delivery commission previously lost to DoorDash.

3. **AI-Powered Menu Mix Optimization** — Pilot 2024-2025. Analytics tool recommends LTO rotation timing based on historical sales data and local preferences. Early data: 1-2% comp sales lift.

4. **Delivery-First Menu Variants** — 2025 rollout. Separate menu (Defy, select traditional stores) optimized for delivery durability. Example: "Stacking" bean burrito instead of soft tacos (less structural damage in delivery). Test markets: Austin, Vegas, LA.

5. **Supply Chain Finance Product** — Live Más investment (2024). Enabled franchisees to access working capital via Taco Bell receivables. De-risks franchisee growth capex for new units.

### Failures / Killed Pilots

1. **Cantina Concept (2021-2022)** — Premium Taco Bell format with table service. Tested 2 locations; cancelled due to labor costs (server+crew) and limited differentiation. Learning: delivery/ghost kitchen more scalable than premium dine-in.

2. **NFT Loyalty Program (2022-2023)** — Web3 gaming tied to TB loyalty. Cancelled after limited adoption; perceived as gimmick. Internal sentiment: "Gaming is not our core business."

3. **Taco Bell + KFC co-location Kit (2023-2024)** — Yum synergy test for shared kitchen. Piloted 3 locations; complexity (menu separation, training) outweighed savings (5% capex reduction). Franchisees resisted due to supply chain conflicts.

### KPIs & Measurement

- **Defy:** Unit volume vs. traditional, delivery attach rate, labor cost per unit, franchisee interest
- **Live Más:** ROIC on portfolio, time-to-integration for acquisitions, strategic value created
- **Menu optimization:** Comp sales lift from LTO rotation, forecast accuracy
- **Delivery:** Margin capture, customer LTV, order frequency lift

## Sweetgreen — Ops Innovation

### Function Overview

Sweetgreen operates **acquisition-driven innovation**: bought Spyce (robotics + makeline technology, 2021) and is rolling out "Infinite Kitchen" (robotic fast-casual makeline) as next-generation unit format. No standalone innovation team; instead, **innovation is woven into ops and supply chain leadership**.

**Structures:**
1. **Spyce Integration Team** — Post-acquisition (2021). Integrating robotic makeline tech into Sweetgreen units. Reports to Chief Operating Officer (Oren Kaniel).
2. **Supply Chain Innovation** — Hyperlocal sourcing, direct-from-farm supply chain. Reports to VP Supply Chain / Sustainability (separate from ops).
3. **Restaurant Operations R&D** — Kitchen design, unit economics, labor optimization. Reports to COO.

### Team & Structure

**Headcount estimate:**
- Spyce integration + product: ~20 FTE (engineers, ops, program managers)
- Supply chain / hyperlocal sourcing: ~12 FTE (supply coordinators, farm partnerships)
- Restaurant ops design: ~8 FTE
- **Total dedicated innovation: ~40 FTE** (vs. Chipotle 70, Taco Bell 75)

**Budget:** Not disclosed. Estimate $20-35M annually (Spyce integration capex + supply chain R&D). Spyce original funding (pre-acquisition): $20M+ raised; integration costs ongoing.

**Reporting line:** COO (Kaniel) oversees Spyce team and ops R&D. Supply chain reports separately to sustainability officer. No C-level dedicated "Chief Innovation Officer." Signals: innovation is tactical ops improvement, not strategic bet.

### Physical Innovation Assets

1. **Infinite Kitchen Test Location** — Sweetgreen's robot-enabled makeline format. Launched 2024 in select markets (DC, NYC area). ~1,800 sq ft. Full robotic prep for salads (chopping, assembly). Crew is 2-3 (vs. traditional 5-6). Revenue per unit: $60K-$75K/day (vs. traditional SG average $40-50K/day). Labor cost: 20% of revenue (vs. traditional 26-28%).

2. **Spyce Lab / Development** — Post-acquisition facility in Boston. Focus on makeline robotics reliability, ingredient prep optimization, menu adaptation for robotic prep.

3. **Farm Partnership Network** — Sweetgreen operates direct relationships with 100+ farms. "Artisanal supply chain" is differentiation. Not a physical facility but an ops asset; drives unit economics via margin capture on supply side.

### Operating Model

**Pilot → Phased Rollout → Economics Validation:**

1. **Concept Development** — Spyce robotics adapted for Sweetgreen menu items (pre-chopped ingredients, sauce prep, assembly). Initially tested in Spyce standalone locations (pre-acquisition); now in Sweetgreen kitchens.

2. **Test Location** — 1-2 unit rollout (6-12 months). Measure labor cost, revenue per unit, customer satisfaction, robotics reliability.

3. **Validation Pilot** — 3-5 additional rollout (same market or contiguous). Validate supply chain, staffing model, customer adoption.

4. **Scale Decision** — If margins are positive and concept is repeatable, phased rollout to 10-20 new Infinite Kitchens per year.

**Example: Infinite Kitchen (2023-2025)**
- Concept test: 1 location (2023)
- Pilot: 2-3 locations (2024)
- Current status: 7 locations operational (2025), targeting 15+ by 2026
- Economics: $60K+/day revenue, 20% labor cost, 35%+ gross margin (vs. traditional 28-30%)

### Recent Shipped Innovations (2024-2026)

1. **Infinite Kitchen Rollout** — Robotic makeline format. 7 units operational (2025). Revenue $60K+/day; labor cost 20% vs. 26-28% traditional. High capex per unit (~$400-500K); payback ~3-4 years. Model shows promise but slow scale due to capital intensity.

2. **Supply Chain Localization** — Expanded direct-from-farm sourcing to 120+ farms (2024-2025). Enables "farm-to-bowl" messaging and margin capture (reduce distributor markup 2-3%). Per-unit impact: ~1% gross margin improvement.

3. **Sustainable Packaging** — Transitioned to compostable bowls, cutlery, straws (2024-2025). +0.4% COGS per order; offset by reduced waste disposal costs and brand differentiation.

4. **Dynamic Pricing & Inventory Optimization** — Pilot 2024-2025. AI-driven pricing based on ingredient freshness and local demand. Early data: 0.5-1% revenue lift, reduced waste 5-10%.

5. **Delivery-Optimized Menu Items** — Tested items designed for durability in delivery (sturdier dressing ratios, structural integrity). Limited rollout; margins are lower than in-store, so not prioritized.

### Failures / Killed Pilots

1. **Spyce Standalone Model** — Acquired Spyce expecting to scale robotic makeline as standalone premium casual. Market demand was weak; integrated into Sweetgreen instead. Learning: "Robotic tech needs strong brand/loyalty to justify premium pricing."

2. **Ghost Kitchen Concept (2021-2022)** — Tested delivery-only format. Cancelled due to brand dilution concern; Sweetgreen's premium positioning requires physical space and brand visibility.

3. **NFT Loyalty Experiment (2022-2023)** — Pilot with blockchain-based loyalty. Cancelled; minimal uptake. Focus returned to core loyalty program.

### KPIs & Measurement

- **Infinite Kitchen:** Revenue per unit, labor cost %, capex payback period, franchisee/company interest
- **Supply chain:** Margin improvement %, farm relationship retention, supply reliability
- **Packaging:** COGS per order, waste reduction %, customer perception (brand lift)
- **Pricing optimization:** Revenue lift %, waste reduction %, customer churn risk

### Public Statements from Leadership

- **CEO Jonathan Neman** (2025 earnings call): "Infinite Kitchen is not a gimmick. The unit economics are real. We can deliver premium, customized salads faster, with better margins, and a smaller labor footprint. That's the future of Sweetgreen."
- **COO Oren Kaniel** (LinkedIn, 2024): "The Spyce acquisition was about robotics, but it's actually about understanding what labor-intensive tasks can be automated without compromising quality. That's the thesis we're extending."
- **VP Sustainability / Supply Chain** (company interview, 2024): "Direct farm relationships reduce our supply chain complexity and improve margins. We're not optimizing for scale of suppliers; we're optimizing for sustainability and margin capture."

## Domino's — Ops Innovation

### Function Overview

Domino's operates a **centralized, disciplined tech innovation function** with the highest executive visibility in the QSR peer set. Innovation reports directly to the CEO and Board. Structure is built around "tech as core business," not "tech as efficiency."

**Structures:**
1. **Chief Technology Officer (Patrick Doyle, then Tom Curtis)** — Reports to CEO. Oversees all digital, AI, supply chain tech, autonomous delivery, franchise tech infrastructure. ~100+ FTE across tech innovation, platform, data.
2. **Domino's AI Lab** — Dedicated to demand prediction, order optimization, route optimization. Housed in Ann Arbor, MI HQ. Led by Chief Data Scientist.
3. **Innovation Council** — Board-level steering. CTO + CEO + select franchisees convene quarterly to approve major tech bets.

### Team & Structure

**Headcount estimate:**
- CTO organization: ~80 FTE (engineers, data scientists, product managers, ops)
- AI Lab: ~15 FTE
- Pizza Tracker + order system: ~30 FTE (part of larger platform team)
- Autonomous delivery / pilots: ~8 FTE
- **Total dedicated innovation: ~120+ FTE**

**Budget:** Not disclosed. Estimate $100-150M annually. Visible in Q1 2025 guidance: tech capex ~8-10% of total capex (~$80M+/year). Heavy R&D spend on AI, autonomous systems.

**Reporting line:** CTO reports directly to CEO (Russell Weiner). No intermediary COO/President. Signals: tech is core strategy, not ops efficiency play. CEO personally involved in major tech decisions (autonomous delivery, AI systems).

### Physical Innovation Assets

1. **Domino's Ann Arbor AI Lab** — R&D center focused on demand prediction, routing, supply chain AI. ~5,000 sq ft dedicated space. Public-facing demos and franchisee tours.

2. **Autonomous Delivery Pilots** — Partnerships with Nuro (autonomous vehicles) for delivery testing. Pilot locations: Ann Arbor (HQ city), select other markets. Feasibility data: 0.5-1% of orders via autonomous by 2025 (growth trajectory TBD).

3. **Franchise Tech Platform** — "Domino's Exchange" cloud platform for franchisees. All 6,000+ franchisees on shared tech stack. Central asset for innovation rollout.

### Operating Model

**Strategy → Test → Mandatory Rollout:**

1. **Tech Strategy** — CTO + CEO identify 3-5 major tech bets each fiscal year. Examples: AI demand forecasting, autonomous delivery, franchisee dashboards.

2. **Concept Development** — AI Lab or product team builds proof-of-concept (3-6 months). Example: demand prediction ML model trained on historical sales data.

3. **Pilot Testing** — Deploy to company-operated stores + willing franchisees (100-300 units, 3-6 months). Measure uplift, franchisee feedback, tech reliability.

4. **Mandatory Rollout** — If validated, mandate all franchisees adopt within 12-24 months. Leverage franchise agreement language; charge implementation fees if needed.

**Example: Pizza Tracker 2.0 (AI-enhanced, 2024-2025)**
- Concept: Real-time pizza tracking + delivery ETA prediction (vs. static tracker)
- Pilot: Company stores + 200 franchisees (2024)
- Rollout: 90%+ franchisee adoption by Q2 2025
- KPI: Improved customer satisfaction score (NPS); reduced late delivery complaints 12%

### Recent Shipped Innovations (2024-2026)

1. **AI Demand Forecasting** — Deployed 2024 to all company-operated stores + ~500 franchisees. Predicts order volume hour-by-hour based on weather, local events, historical patterns, external signals (sports, holidays). Impact: 2-3% labor cost reduction via optimized staffing, 1-2% food waste reduction.

2. **Pizza Tracker 2.0** — Enhanced AI-driven order tracking + delivery ETA. Deployed 2024-2025. Real-time positioning of delivery driver, predicted arrival window refinement. Customer NPS uplift: +3-5 points. Franchisee adoption: mandatory by Q3 2025.

3. **Autonomous Delivery Pilots** — Partnership with Nuro (autonomous vehicles). Testing in Ann Arbor, Las Vegas, other select markets. 0.5-1% of orders delivered via autonomous by late 2025. Tech proving reliable; regulatory pathway unclear.

## Cross-Company Patterns (QSR Peer Set)

### Team Size Norms

| Company | Dedicated Innovation FTE | Total Tech/Ops Staff | Innovation as % of Ops |
|---------|-------------------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| **Chick-fil-A** | 18-25 | 200+ | ~10% |

| **Domino's** | 120+ | 400+ | ~30% |

**Insight:** Scale QSR peers (Domino's, Taco Bell, Chipotle) dedicate 25-30% of ops staff to innovation. Smaller/private peers (CFA, Sweetgreen) run leaner innovation teams (10-27%) with higher leverage per FTE.

### Reporting Line Norms

| Company | Reports To | Exec Level | Board Involvement |
|---------|------------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Chick-fil-A** | VP Ops (COO oversight) | VP/SVP | Quarterly, informal |
| **Chipotle** | CRO (Ops), CTO (Tech) | EVP/C-level | Monthly (CTO) |
| **Taco Bell** | Chief Innovation Officer → CEO | C-level | High (board committee) |
| **Sweetgreen** | COO | EVP | Low (not separate agenda) |
| **Domino's** | CTO → CEO | C-level | Very High (board steering) |

**Insight:** Innovation escalates to C-level (CEO/COO) at publicly traded peers. Private peers (CFA) embed innovation in ops without standalone exec. Direct CEO engagement signals board-level priority.

### Common Pilot → Scale Mechanisms

1. **Corporate / Company-Operated Test First** — All peers test in company-owned units before franchisee rollout. Allows rapid iteration, standardization before franchisee complexity. Typical: 1-3 units for 6-12 months.

2. **Franchisee Steering Groups** — Chipotle and Taco Bell have formal franchisee advisory councils. Feedback shapes go/no-go decisions. Prevents surprise franchisee rejection at scale.

3. **Phased Rollout with Economics Validation** — Scale happens in waves (50-100 units, then 200, then system-wide). Each wave requires ROI/franchisee satisfaction gate.

4. **Mandatory Adoption (Late Stage)** — Domino's forces tech stack adoption via franchise agreement. Chipotle uses selective rollout but strongly incentivizes. Taco Bell left more flexibility (Defy is franchisee choice). CFA and Sweetgreen rely on peer pressure and franchisee success stories.

5. **Third-Party Partnerships for Acceleration** — Chipotle partners with Hypercube (robotics); Sweetgreen acquired Spyce; Domino's partners with Nuro (autonomous). Buys external expertise, reduces internal R&D risk.

### Most Common Failure Modes

1. **Overestimating Franchisee Labor Adoption** — Kiosks, mobile-only ordering, new crew workflows face resistance. Learning: crew training burden is a hidden cost.

2. **Underestimating Capex Payback Complexity** — Robotics, new kitchens, delivery vehicles have high sunk costs. Payback windows (3-5 years) challenge franchisee ROI expectations.

3. **Brand Perception Risk from Gimmicks** — NFTs, blockchain, novelty concepts create franchise skepticism. Learning: innovation must connect to core brand promise (quality, speed, value).

4. **Supply Chain Brittleness** — New formats (Defy, Infinite Kitchen, Chipotlane) require different ingredient specs, procurement, logistics. Scaling exposes supply chain constraints.

5. **Crew Skill Gaps with Automation** — Robots and complex systems require different training. Labor quality becomes a constraint. Solutions: hire differently, upskill existing crew, or accept higher turnover.
