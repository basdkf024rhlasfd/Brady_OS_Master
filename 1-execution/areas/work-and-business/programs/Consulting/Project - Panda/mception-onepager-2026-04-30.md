# mception
## An AI-native research and decision practice for operating leaders

**For** James Ku · CDO · Panda Restaurant Group  &nbsp;·&nbsp;  **From** Brady Smallwood  &nbsp;·&nbsp;  **April 30, 2026**

### What it is

mception is the consulting practice I run on top of an AI-native operating system I built for myself over the past two years. It exists because the work operating leaders actually need — peer benchmarks, KPI gap maps, problem decomposition, organizational option-mapping — is poorly served by the traditional model. Traditional consulting is too slow, too generalist, and produces decks rather than living research. mception is the inverse: fast, narrow, evidence-dense, and structured as a knowledge base you can keep using long after the engagement ends. The asset behind it is a research engine. The Panda whitepaper accompanying this brief is one example: 14 deep research threads, 3 supplementary focus dossiers, 480+ cited sources, ~600 pages of internal working notes, executed in eight days.

### How it can help — the current shape

Today, mception delivers research reports. One question, one operating leader, one fully cited synthesis. Examples:

- **Constraint mapping.** A real operating problem (digital order accuracy, wok automation, format capital efficiency) decomposed against peer benchmarks with addressable-value math, peer playbooks, pre-registered failure criteria.
- **Operating model option papers.** Three-option organizational comparisons with fit-to-context analysis (governance posture, capex envelope, talent market, peer precedent).
- **Industry deep-reads.** A specific peer set, a specific operational dimension, fully sourced — the kind of artifact a CFO would commission a McKinsey study for, delivered in days rather than months.
- **Decision-support synthesis.** A specific decision framed as inputs / options / consequences with the underlying evidence cited inline.

Every output is structured as a fully-cited document and written to be machine-readable: pasted into your LLM of choice, queried indefinitely. The research compounds rather than expires.

### Why this is worth keeping in view

The bar an outside research function has to clear to be worth using is high. Most senior operating leaders have learned not to bring consultants in for routine questions because the cost-to-insight ratio is bad. mception is built specifically to clear that bar — fast, narrow, fully cited, machine-queryable output, priced as a research deliverable rather than a multi-month engagement. The frame is "is this question worth $X to answer," not "do we have the budget for a strategy project." The Panda research package was an opening example. Whether the next question comes in three months, six months, a year, or never, the practice exists and the engine is being sharpened. If something comes up where you want a structured, evidence-dense outside read on a problem inside Panda's operating model — the kind of read that would be hard to commission internally without political weight — mception is configured to deliver it.
