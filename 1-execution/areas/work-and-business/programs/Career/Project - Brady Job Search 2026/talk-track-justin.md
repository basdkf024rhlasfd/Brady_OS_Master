# Talk Track: Next Conversation with Justin Woods

**Date prepared:** 2026-04-21
**Context:** Justin Woods joined 1915 South as President in 2025, after VP roles at Ashley Global Retail and Advance Auto Parts. He and Brady share background in retail analytics and pricing. Russell Turner is the CEO/owner who makes all major decisions. Brady's goal is to convert this relationship into an engagement (project, advisory, or role).

---

## Opening

Keep it warm and peer-to-peer. Justin knows the Walmart analytics world; Brady knows that world. Don't pitch — orient.

> "Hey Justin — I've been following what you're building at 1915 South. Jumping to the President seat at a company that's grown to 29 stores is a big move. I wanted to reconnect and hear what the first year's looked like from the inside. A lot of the stuff you were doing on omnichannel pricing at Ashley feels relevant to where retail is heading — curious what you're seeing now that you're running the full P&L."

**Goal of the open:** Signal that you've done your homework, position yourself as a peer (not a vendor), and get Justin talking about the challenges he's navigating.

---

## 5 Key Questions (grounded in research)

**1. What's the biggest operational gap you're running into now that you're at 29 stores?**

Why this works: 1915 South grew fast via acquisition (Jacksonville 2022, Yulee 2025). Rapid multi-site growth almost always creates fragmented ops systems and inconsistent store-level analytics. This question invites Justin to name the problem Brady already suspects is there — without Brady having to say it.

Listen for: inventory management pain, pricing consistency challenges across stores, distribution center coordination, manager-level analytics access.

---

**2. Ashley corporate is all-in on GMROI as a retailer success metric — are they pushing that framework down to franchisees actively?**

Why this works: Ashley published a major GMROI excellence framework in December 2024 and it's clearly a top-down priority from Todd Wanek. Most franchisees are getting this pressure but don't have the analytical infrastructure to respond well. Brady can position as someone who builds exactly that infrastructure.

Listen for: how much pressure they're getting from corporate, whether they have the reporting tools to respond, whether this is a source of stress for Justin or Russell.

---

**3. How are you thinking about AI right now — are you building internal capability or waiting for Ashley corporate to pipe something down?**

Why this works: Todd Wanek is an AI maximalist ("businesses have 3-5 years to figure this out or get left behind") and Ashley Industries is deploying significant technology. But the franchisee layer typically lags the manufacturer significantly. Justin's prior corporate career was analytics-heavy — he knows the gap is real. This question positions Brady as someone who knows the space and is already operating in it (mception.ai).

Listen for: whether they have any internal analytics/data team, whether they're relying entirely on corporate Ashley systems, whether Justin personally wants to move faster than the organization currently allows.

---

**4. How does Russell think about adding people to the leadership team — does he tend to promote from within, or is he open to bringing in outside operators at the senior level?**

Why this works: Understanding Russell's hiring philosophy before Brady ever pitches himself. This is intel-gathering, not a setup for an ask. It helps Brady understand whether a full-time role is even viable, and frames the conversation as Brady being thoughtful rather than desperate.

Listen for: Russell's leadership style, whether he's a builder who trusts outsiders or a culture-first owner who prefers organic growth, how Justin's own hiring process went.

---

**5. What does a win look like for 1915 South over the next 18 months — what would have to be true for Russell to feel like the company's really on track?**

Why this works: Gets Brady into Russell's priorities without having to be in the room with Russell yet. Also sets up the follow-on: "That's interesting — that's exactly the kind of work I've been doing with [relevant client/project]. Here's how we approached it."

Listen for: revenue targets, store count goals, technology/ops milestones, any M&A or exit considerations.

---

## Positioning Brady's Value (use after listening, not before)

Once Justin has described 1-2 real pain points, Brady can offer positioning without pitching:

> "That tracks with what I've seen building AI-driven ops systems for retail clients — the gap is almost always the same: the data is there, the analytics tools exist, but the org doesn't have the layer between the data and the store manager that makes it actionable. That's what I've been building at mception. I'd be curious whether that kind of capability is something you'd want to explore together — even if it starts as a scoped project rather than a big commitment."

Keep it lightweight. The goal of the first conversation is to get a second conversation with Russell in the room.

---

## What to Listen For (flags and signals)

| Signal | What it means |
|--------|--------------|
| Justin mentions specific ops problems (inventory, pricing consistency, analytics gaps) | Green light — Brady should offer to dig in |
| Justin talks about wanting to move faster than the org currently allows | Justin is the internal champion; Russell may be the bottleneck |
| Justin says Russell is "protective of the culture" or "likes to promote from within" | Full-time FTE path is harder; start with a project |
| Justin mentions any outside consultants or advisors already engaged | Brady has competition; differentiate on AI + retail ops combination |
| Justin asks Brady "what are you doing these days" with energy | Opening to pitch mception and the consulting engagement |
| Justin seems distracted or noncommittal | He may not be the actual decision-maker on adding resources; get to Russell faster |

---

## The Close (what to ask for at the end of the call)

Don't ask for a job. Ask for the next step:

> "I'd love to put together a quick framework on where I think 1915 South has the most opportunity — based on what you've shared and what I know from the Ashley ecosystem. Then maybe the three of us could get 30 minutes with Russell to pressure-test it. Would that be worth doing?"

This gives Justin something to bring back to Russell without Brady having to cold-call the owner. It positions Brady as someone who does the work first, then asks for the room — which is exactly the right sequence for a family-owned company.
