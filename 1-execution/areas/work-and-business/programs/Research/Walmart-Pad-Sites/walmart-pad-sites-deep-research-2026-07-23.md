# Pad Sites on Walmart Properties

### Where to Find Them, What's Actually Available, What They Cost, and Where the Traps Are

**Prepared for:** Brady Smallwood — mception.ai consulting practice
**Date:** July 23, 2026
**Depth:** Deep — 9 research tracks, each adversarially fact-checked, plus 3 gap-fill passes
**Sources analyzed:** 257 collected · ~180 unique · 90 cited

---

*The question: what pad sites (outparcels/outlots) are available for lease on Walmart properties across the country — where does that inventory actually live, how big is it, what do deals look like, and what should a tenant or developer watch out for? This report maps the entire channel structure (Walmart Realty, its two national brokers, the third-party shadow-pad market, and the separate in-store leasing program), enumerates the live inventory visible without a browser session, reconstructs deal economics from SEC filings and the few published comps, and stress-tests the risks. U.S. is the core; Canada and Mexico are covered because Walmart's international footprint is now U.S./Canada/Mexico/Central America/Chile plus minority positions.*

## Executive Summary

**Walmart does lease pad sites on its own properties across the country — but the inventory is deliberately quiet.** Walmart owns 3,728 of its 4,611 U.S. stores (80.9%) and typically the surrounding land [1], and its Bentonville real-estate arm, Walmart Realty, markets excess outlots, carve-outs, land tracts, and former stores nationwide through **walmartrealty.com** (the `realty.walmart.com` hostname is dead) [4][5] and two national broker channels: **CBRE's "Walmart Disposition Center"** (Midwest-led, three named brokers) [7] and **SRS Real Estate Partners' "Walmart U.S. Pad Site & Disposition Portfolio"** (national, sale and ground lease) [8]. Nearly everything is "contact broker for pricing" — there is no published rate card anywhere in the channel.

The verified floor of officially marketed Walmart pad/outlot inventory visible from search indexes is **30+ properties across 16+ states**, clustered in small and secondary Midwest/Plains markets (69% of indexed CBRE listings), typically 0.75–4 acres, offered flexibly "for sale or lease" [10]–[14][15]. The true portfolio is materially larger — the site itself showed **557 total listings of all types circa March 2023** [9] — but Walmart's portal and both broker portals are bot-blocked/JS-rendered, so a precise current census requires a 10-minute human browser session (the single highest-value follow-up from this research). Deal economics triangulate to roughly **$27K–$58K per acre per year** in ground rent depending entirely on the market (comps: Kapolei HI $350K/yr on 0.74 ac; Charleston SC $140K/yr on 1 ac; Walmart-as-tenant comps at $27K–$56K/ac/yr) [16][17][34][35], on ~20-year NNN ground leases with flat base rent and 5% bumps at 5-year options — and always subject to Walmart's recorded ECR covenants: sole-discretion site-plan/design approval, prohibited-use lists (no grocery, pharmacy, discount store, warehouse club), transfer rights, and easements in Walmart's favor [24].

Two structural facts should drive any go/no-go: **(1) Walmart's posture is steady-state disposition, not motivated selling** — the apparent FY2026 drop in owned-store share (87.7%→80.9%) is purely a reporting reclassification of ~360 owned buildings on ground-leased land, not sale-leasebacks (disposal proceeds actually *fell* to $106M; land holdings *grew* $1.4B) [1][2], and Walmart is now buying whole shopping centers ($118M across three in 2025) to act as landlord [44][45]. **(2) The tenant gets the anchor's traffic but none of the anchor's covenant** — Walmart pads carry no co-tenancy protection, closures come with little warning (269 stores in 2016; all 51 health centers on 45–90 days' notice in 2024), and Walmart's deed restrictions run 25–75 years and are litigated aggressively [24][26][28][49][82]. The opportunity is real, national, and accessible through three named CBRE brokers and SRS — but it is a relationship-and-LOI market priced deal-by-deal, not a browsable marketplace.

## Key Findings

1. **The official channel is Walmart Realty via walmartrealty.com — not realty.walmart.com, which no longer resolves.** The portal markets "outlots, carve outs, large land tracts, former retail stores, and shopping center spaces" for lease or sale nationwide with a map search and regularly updated listings [4][5]. Deals initiate on Walmart's own LOI template (`wmt-loi.DOC`, live on the site but unreadable by automated tools) [6].
2. **Two national brokerages are the practical front door.** CBRE runs a dedicated Walmart Disposition Center for "excess land sites, including outlots and large land tracts, and former retail stores" — contacts: Thomas Flynn (Akron, +1 330 670 4409), Jim Sakanich (Chicago, +1 312 297 7690), Yoni Zvi (Milwaukee, +1 414 274 1618) [7]. SRS maintains a national "Walmart U.S. Pad Site & Disposition Portfolio" with grid/list/map views and a "Download all" export [8].
3. **Verified live inventory floor: 30+ marketed Walmart-owned pad/outlot properties in 16+ states (July 2026), Midwest-heavy.** Search-index enumeration found 26 distinct CBRE "Walmart Outlot" listing pages in 13 states (NE 5, MN 5, IN 3, WI 2, MI 2, GA 2, plus IA/IL/KS/KY/PA/TN/VA), plus 9+ SRS listings (TX/FL-heavy) and regional-broker listings (IL, TN). This is a floor, not a census — the c.2023 anchor of 557 total listings (all property types) bounds the upper end [9]–[15].
4. **Pricing is almost never published.** Nearly every Walmart-owned listing reads "Contact Broker For Pricing." The exceptions and comps: Appomattox VA outlots asking $350–375K *for sale* (~$150–190K/acre) [11]; ground-lease asks of $350K/yr (Kapolei HI, 0.74 ac) [16] and $140K/yr (Charleston SC shadow pad, 1 ac) [17]; Walmart-as-tenant ground rents of ~$55.5K/ac/yr (Ocala FL) [34] and ~$27.3K/ac/yr (Hickory NC) [35]. There is no national rack rate — ground rent is set per-market off land value.
5. **The deal architecture is documented in SEC filings: Walmart keeps control after the deal.** The 2012 Walmart–Murphy Oil outlot agreement (the canonical public template) conveys pads as-is by special warranty deed subject to a recorded ECR: sole-and-absolute-discretion approval over site plans, building placement, parking, signage and any material change (30-day review loops), prohibited uses (grocery/supermarket, pharmacy, discount store, warehouse club, plus others), transfer restrictions with Walmart purchase rights (ROFR-style, per Murphy USA's 10-K), $5M liability insurance naming Walmart, a holiday construction blackout (Oct 31–Jan 31), and Walmart sign/utility easements *on* the pad [24].
6. **Walmart is also a landlord inside the box: 13,000+ inline spaces, 4,500+ stores with leased space, 350+ brands** via the separate "Your Shop At Walmart" program (leasing.walmart.com) — pop-ups (Popable partnership) through multi-year leases, governed by the ISO Handbook and 4th Wall buildout guidelines, with a stated 2–4 week application review [53]–[59]. Don't confuse this channel with pad sites; both get called "leasing space at Walmart."
7. **Supply outlook is steady-state, not a sell-off.** The FY26 owned-share drop is a reclassification (~311 Walmart U.S. stores + ~50 Sam's Clubs are owned buildings on ground-leased land, now reported as leased) — zero sale-leasebacks, disposal proceeds down to $106M, land up $1.4B [1][2]. New-store creation is slow (9 new U.S. stores in 2025; ~20 scheduled for 2026 vs. 650+ remodels), so pad supply comes overwhelmingly from the existing ~4,600-store base [41][42]. Meanwhile Walmart's own EV fast-charging build-out (73 locations/612 connectors by July 2026, targeting thousands of stores by 2030) competes internally for prime lot corners [48].
8. **Tenant demand is drive-thru QSR/coffee, banks, auto, medical — and the traffic case is quantified.** Recent named deals: Whataburger outparcel ground lease at a metro-Memphis Supercenter (14-yr, sold Oct 2024) [64]; Scooter's Coffee 10-yr NNN ground lease on an Avon Park FL pad (2024) [65]. Walmart's fleet drew +2.3% YoY visits in Q4 2025 with 34% of shoppers visiting 4+ times/month [68]. Inline, Charleys Cheesesteaks is converting hundreds of former Subway/McDonald's spaces; Jackson Hewitt runs 2,600+ kiosks [66][67].
9. **The risk stack is asymmetric: you pay anchor-adjacent pricing but hold no anchor covenant.** Pad tenants of a shadow anchor have no co-tenancy remedy if the store goes dark [39][40]; Walmart's 2016 closure of 269 stores and its 45–90-day shutdown of all 51 health centers in 2024 are the severity precedents [49][82]; deed restrictions (25–75 years) can kill a future concept pivot — though enforcement against successors is contestable (Walmart's covenant was *reversed* on appeal in *Bernard Court*, Ark. App. 2020) and Rhode Island banned new grocery deed restrictions effective July 1, 2026 [26][27][28].
10. **"Across the countries": Canada has no public pad portal (broker/relationship-driven; C$6.5B expansion + Nov 2024 waiver of restrictive property controls changes the terms landscape) [86][87]; Mexico does — Walmex runs an official Bienes Raíces portal covering land sales, locale rental, and "Excess property" land rental across 3,316 Mexico units [89].**

## 1. How the Program Works: Walmart Realty

Walmart is structurally unusual among big-box retailers: it is its own landlord. Per the FY2026 10-K, Walmart U.S. owned 3,728 of 4,611 retail units (80.9%), Sam's Club owned 464 of 601, and Walmart typically owns the land under and around its stores — 698.7M sq ft of Walmart U.S. retail space alone [1]. When Walmart assembles a Supercenter site, it habitually buys more land than the store needs, then sells or leases the surplus pads while controlling co-tenancy — a practice documented as far back as a 2004 trade profile of Walmart Realty ("so we don't have bars or pawnshops") [72]. Excess acreage on Walmart-owned parcels became so common that Walmart formalized the channel: it hired CBRE and built an interactive disposition website to sell or lease excess land sites and former stores [75].

**Walmart Realty** (Bentonville) is the operating unit. Its public surface is **walmartrealty.com** — note that `realty.walmart.com`, the hostname that appears in older references, no longer resolves in DNS. The portal's own description: "Walmart Realty has multiple excess properties available for lease or sale across the country, including outlots, carve outs, large land tracts, former retail stores, and shopping center spaces," with a map search filterable by Building vs. Land, transaction type (Own/Lease/Ground lease), and size bands; listings are "updated regularly and are subject to change" [4][5]. The site hosts Walmart's standard Letter of Intent template (`wmt-loi.DOC`) — deals start on Walmart's paper, not yours [6]. Circa March 2023 the portal showed **557 available listings** across all excess-property types — the only historical count on record, and an upper-bound anchor since it includes former stores and shopping-center space, not just pads [9].

Internally, this is an institutionalized, staffed function, not ad hoc: current Walmart job postings describe a Director-level asset-management role explicitly chartered with "shopping center sales, **outparcel dispositions**, and monetization of underperforming or non-strategic assets," a Tenant Leasing group, and regional portfolio managers each covering ~400 operating stores [61][62]. A 2004 profile of the program (dated, but structural) described 380 buildings and ~350 land parcels offered simultaneously, 240 outparcels sold in a single year, ~70% of dispositions broker-listed, and a weekly Deal Committee of Walmart Realty VPs approving transactions — with ground leases also done for billboards, ATMs, and solar [72]. Walmart has also historically cleared low-value pads at absolute auction (26 TX/LA outlots via Higgenbotham in 2010; a Norton, VA outparcel ran through a $1-reserve auction as recently as July 2025) [73][14].

One important distinction runs through everything: **Walmart-owned outlots vs. developer-owned "shadow-anchored" pads.** Most listings that surface when you search "Walmart outparcel for lease" are pads at Walmart-anchored or Walmart-adjacent centers owned by third parties (developers, strip-center owners, net-lease investors) — you'd lease from them, not Walmart, though Walmart's recorded REA/ECR restrictions usually still govern the dirt [24][80]. The Walmart-owned inventory routes through walmartrealty.com, CBRE, and SRS.

## 3. The Third-Party Channels

Four distinct populations of "Walmart pads" exist outside Walmart's own portal, and they behave differently:

1. **Walmart's listing brokers (official inventory).** CBRE (Disposition Center; Midwest-led team) and SRS (national pad-site portfolio). SRS separately describes the model: "Retail big boxes with large sites can carve out land from their parking lot to create a pad site which can be ground leased or sold to a complementary user" — and an SRS-bylined piece describes disposing stores/pads for an unnamed national discount retailer across 34 states: 150+ transactions, $400M+ recovered [7][8][74].
2. **Shadow-center consolidators (lease from them, not Walmart).** MRP Capital Group (St. Louis) is the self-described largest owner-operator of "Walmart Shadow Centers" in small-town America — 100+ properties, 2.6M SF, 26 states, ~$350M portfolio (self-reported, 2022) [76]. Others active 2025–26: Core Equity Partners (bought a multi-center Walmart shadow portfolio June 2025), RCG Ventures, Broad Reach Retail Partners [77]. Institutional ownership of this asset class dates back at least to a 2016 CMBS pool of 34 Walmart-shadow properties across 14 states [80].
3. **Net-lease investment brokers (completed pads as investments).** Marcus & Millichap, Boulder Group, SRS NNLG trade finished outparcels: a $5.2M Capital One + IHOP outparcel sale at a Baton Rouge Walmart shadow center (May 2025, part of a $12M break-up), raw pads on Crexi ~$425K, completed net-leased outparcels at 5.45–7.00% caps [78][79]. Parcelization/break-up of Walmart-anchored centers is a recurring 2025 strategy.
4. **Marketplaces (mixed population).** LoopNet/Crexi/CommercialSearch carry dozens of "Walmart outparcel" listings — always check who the landlord actually is.

A critical deal-terms reality documented in FrontView REIT's SEC filings: **Walmart's approval rights and use/size restrictions encumber pads even when third parties own them** — FrontView had to negotiate Walmart's consent (via a lease extension) just to carve a new 1-acre pad on a Walmart-leased site, and discovered an undisclosed Walmart-enforceable size restriction on a Buffalo Wild Wings outparcel it bought, which it had to get amended during due diligence [38]. The third-party channels are lead-generation surfaces; the restrictions are Walmart's either way.

## 4. Deal Structures, Terms & Economics

**Three documented structures:**

1. **Fee sale subject to a recorded ECR** ("Easements with Covenants and Restrictions Affecting Land"). The canonical public template is the SEC-filed 2012 Walmart–Murphy Oil Agreement of Sale covering a national outlot/carveout portfolio [24]: special warranty deed, AS-IS/WHERE-IS; ECR recorded immediately after each deed; Walmart plats/replats in "sole and absolute discretion"; buyer reimburses survey/plat costs above thresholds.
2. **Ground lease of the pad** — from Walmart Realty directly (via CBRE/SRS/local listing brokers) or from a third-party owner whose land sits under Walmart's REA/ECR. Observed lease type on CBRE listings: "Direct."
3. **Walmart as ground tenant on your land** — a different product entirely (investor owns land under a Walmart store), included here only because its pricing calibrates the market.

**What Walmart keeps (from the 2012 ECR/agreement — 14 years old, structural evidence, current forms may differ)** [24]:
- **Sole-and-absolute-discretion approval** of the buyer's entire development plan — site plan, setbacks, parking configuration and dimensions, driveways, building placement/size, architectural design, color scheme, landscaping, elevations, signage — *before* the buyer may even submit to a government authority, with a mandatory 30-day revise-and-resubmit loop that repeats until Walmart approves, and the same rights again on any material modification.
- **Use restrictions:** sole-permitted-use clauses plus prohibited-use lists — no discount store, wholesale/membership warehouse club, grocery store/supermarket, pharmacy/drug store; no theater, bowling alley, health spa/fitness; adult uses banned; Murphy's convenience kiosk capped at 1,200 SF. Restrictions can blanket whole developments: a ~20-year-old Walmart covenant at Centre Pointe (North Charleston, SC) bars any gas station, grocery, drug store, or 35,000+ SF department store across several hundred acres, a mile beyond the store [25].
- **Transfer control:** ECR purchase rights that operate as a right of first refusal / first offer on proposed transfers plus a ban on transfers to Walmart competitors (per Murphy USA's own 10-K disclosure — commonly mislabeled a "buyback option").
- **Operational encumbrances:** Walmart easements *on the pad* for its pylon/monument signs and lot lighting; $5M liability insurance naming Walmart (self-insurance only at $200M+ net worth); holiday construction blackout Oct 31–Jan 31; shared truck-easement maintenance (25% pad / 75% Walmart).

**Ground rent — the honest answer is "it's a per-market negotiation," and here is the full public comp set:**

At the CRE convention of ~6–9% ground-rent yield on land value, the sale asks imply ground rents bracketing the observed comps. **No evidence in asking data that Walmart pads command a systematic per-acre premium over other big-box-anchored pads** — brand credit and market land value drive pricing.

**Structure norms (converging across the Ocala OM, Hickory listing, Boulder Group data, and Net Lease Advisor):** ~20-year initial terms (15-yr typical on new construction per Boulder), absolute NNN, flat base rent, **5% escalations per 5-year option** (occasionally 10% on the first option), and very long option tails — up to 14 five-year options [30][33][34][35].

**Cap-rate context (for underwriting a build on a pad):** Boulder Group Q2 2026 — overall single-tenant net lease 6.82%, retail 6.60%; tightest ground leases (McDonald's, Chick-fil-A) asking ~4.45% [31]. Walmart-credit assets: Q1 2026 tenant profile (S&P AA, typical lease type: ground lease) asks 5.55–5.85% at 15-yr term, 6.00–6.30% at 10-yr, 6.45–6.75% at 5-yr; a Germantown WI Supercenter closed May 2026 at 6.05% with 6 years left [30][31]. Walmart pricing has widened ~100+ bps since the ZIRP era (5.15% in Feb 2020) [33].

**What we still can't see:** the contents of Walmart's LOI template (deposit, DD period, closing timeline — the file is live and opens in any browser/Word; a 30-second manual download closes this), and any executed Walmart-as-landlord pad ground lease with a full rent schedule. Every active Walmart-owned ground-lease listing found (Kapolei, Grand Island NE, Waukesha WI, Tullahoma TN, Carlyle IL, San Antonio TX) is quote-on-request [6][16][19]–[22].

## 5. Walmart's 2024–26 Real-Estate Strategy — What It Means for Pad Supply

**Growth is remodel-led, so pads come from the existing base.** January 2024: commit to build/convert 150+ stores over five years and remodel 650/year across 47 states + Puerto Rico ("Store of the Future") [41]. Reality: 9 new U.S. stores opened in 2025; ~20 grand openings scheduled 2026/early-2027 vs. 650+ remodels (which include "improved parking and landscaping" — i.e., lot reconfiguration) [42]. Capex is at historic highs ($23.8B FY25, guided 3–3.5% of sales) but aimed at remodels, new builds, and supply-chain automation; **no earnings-call discussion of outparcel or rent income exists — pad monetization runs quietly through Walmart Realty** [43].

**The ownership-share "drop" is an accounting artifact — and a diligence insight.** Walmart U.S. owned share fell 87.7% (FY25) → 80.9% (FY26) not from sale-leasebacks but from a new 10-K classification rule: "Owned retail units on leased land are reflected as leased locations." ~311 Walmart U.S. stores + ~50 Sam's Clubs moved columns while the fleet grew by 6 units; "sale-leaseback" appears zero times in the FY26 10-K; property-disposal proceeds *fell* to $106M (from $432M); land *rose* $1.4B to $20.75B; U.S. long-lived assets grew $13.1B [1][2]. Two implications: (a) supply-side — Walmart is not a motivated seller; expect ordinary-course dispositions, not a liquidation window; (b) deal-side — **~7–8% of U.S. stores sit on ground-leased land where Walmart cannot convey fee title to a pad** (sublease/assignment structures at best); confirm land tenure early in any site-specific pursuit.

**Walmart is becoming a landlord, which cuts both ways.** In calendar 2025 it spent $118.1M buying three shopping centers — Monroeville Mall PA ($34M, to be demolished for a ~780K SF Walmart-anchored mixed-use district with Cypress Equities, ~2029), Bethel Park PA ($39.6M), Walmart Center Norwalk CT ($44.5M, with Ulta/Panera/Aspen Dental as rent-paying co-tenants) [44]–[47]. Walmart calls these "one-off projects," but analysts read a Publix-style control-the-real-estate posture. For pad seekers: fewer fee sales at strategic sites, but potentially *more* leasable Walmart-controlled space over time. The 2018–19 "town center" parking-lot-conversion concept largely stalled — treat broker pitches citing it as history [51][52].

**Internal claimants on the parking lot.** Walmart's own 400kW EV fast-charging network (launched April 2025; ~73 locations/612 connectors by July 2026; target: thousands of stores by 2030) is Walmart's highest-priority use for prime lot corners — direct competition for the same real estate a third-party ground tenant wants [48]. Walmart Health's April 2024 shutdown (all 51 centers, 45–90 days' notice) redirected remodels toward Vision Centers/pharmacies and made third-party medical/dental the courted tenant archetype rather than a Walmart-operated one [49][50].

## 6. The Leasing Process — Practical Playbook

**For pad sites / outparcels (land):**
1. **Start with the brokers of record, not Walmart:** CBRE Walmart Disposition Center (Flynn/Sakanich/Zvi, contacts above) and the SRS Walmart portfolio page; add LoopNet/Crexi/CommercialSearch alerts for "Walmart outlot"/"Walmart outparcel" to catch regional-broker and third-party listings [7][8].
2. **Verify who owns the dirt** (Walmart Realty vs. shadow-center owner vs. small investor) and **pull the recorded ECR/REA before falling in love** — restrictions run with the land regardless of owner [24][38][81].
3. **Submit an LOI on Walmart's template** (`wmt-loi.DOC` from walmartrealty.com). Expect Walmart's paper, Walmart's process [6].
4. **Underwrite the approval gauntlet:** 30-day Walmart review loops on site plan and design (sole discretion), then normal municipal entitlement on top; budget the holiday construction blackout if your work touches Walmart's tract [24].
5. **Inquiries at stores with no active listing** route to Walmart Realty's regional portfolio managers (each covers ~400 stores) via the brokers [62].

**For inline space (inside the store) — a different program entirely:** search spaces by store at leasing.walmart.com ("Your Shop At Walmart"), apply against a specific store # and space # (business description, type, location count, annual sales); stated review 2–4 weeks (per a third-party scrape of Walmart's FAQ — verify on the live page); operate as an "Independent Service Operator" under the ISO Handbook (minimum hours: open by 10 a.m. Mon–Sat, 1 p.m. Sun; liquidated-damages exposure) and 4th Wall buildout guidelines (Walmart approves all plans/signage; ~600–2,200 SF spaces; non-competing uses) [53]–[59]. Pop-ups run through the Popable partnership (1 month–1 year, negotiated with Walmart) [56][57]; some emerging-brand access flows through master-lease intermediaries (Twin Towers Trading holds a Master Lease Agreement with Walmart; sister company Concept Development Solutions manages placements — vendor-claimed 2,500+ locations, unaudited) [63].

## 7. Who Leases Walmart Pads — and Why

**Outparcel tenant archetypes:** drive-thru QSR and coffee (the dominant category — Chick-fil-A, Raising Cane's, Dutch Bros, Scooter's, Whataburger), banks/credit unions, car wash, auto service, dental/medical, cellular. Named recent deals: **Whataburger** on a Walmart Supercenter outparcel in metro Memphis — 3,318 SF, 14-year corporate ground lease, sold Oct 2024 to a 1031 buyer; **Scooter's Coffee** — new-construction pad at Avon Park FL Walmart, 10-yr NNN ground lease commencing 2024 [64][65]. Note on auto: Walmart operates its own Auto Care Centers in 2,582 Supercenters — validation of demand, but also in-house competition for auto-service concepts [85].

**The traffic case:** 90% of Americans live within 10 miles of a Walmart; 38.5M people shop daily [53]. Placer.ai: Q4 2025 visits +2.3% YoY after a second-half surge; 34.0% of shoppers visit 4+ times/month (May–Jul 2025); the single busiest store (Sevierville, TN) drew 571.6K visits in June 2025 alone. Visits are essentials-driven and consistent across weekdays — exactly the pattern drive-thru and service tenants want [68][69]. Honest caveat: **no rigorous quantified sales-lift study for Walmart-shadow tenants exists publicly** — the performance case rests on traffic data and broker assertion.

**Inline tenant economy (context for the pad thesis):** Regal Nails ~1,000 salons; DaVi Nails 500+; Jackson Hewitt 2,600+ tax kiosks; Charleys Cheesesteaks converting hundreds of former Subway/McDonald's spaces through 2025; robot-run ghost kitchens (Richtech/Ghost Kitchens America, 20 more Walmart restaurants announced Oct 2024 — a high-churn category) [66][67][70]. The 2021 McDonald's exodus (peak ~1,000 in-store units → ~150) is what opened today's inline backfill wave.

## 8. Geography — U.S. Concentration and the International Picture

**The availability base:** 4,611 Walmart U.S. stores + 601 Sam's Clubs = 5,212 U.S. units (Jan 31, 2026; FY27 Q1 update: 4,614 / 5,215), in all 50 states, DC, and Puerto Rico [1][3]. Largest state footprints: Texas ~510, Florida ~345, California ~272 [71].

**Where marketed Walmart-owned pads cluster:** the Midwest/Plains, per CBRE's own language, its Midwest broker bench, and the 69% listing skew — consistent with the long tail of excess outlots left behind by decades of small-market Supercenter conversions, and with the historical pattern (the 2010 auction was entirely small-market TX/LA) [7][10]–[14][73]. Sun-belt "Walmart outlot" listings exist (GA, TN, VA, TX, FL via SRS) but skew third-party/shadow-anchored. **Growth-market pads at new stores do appear and move fast** — Lady Lake FL's five ground-lease pads were down to two premium pads within months [15].

**Canada (404 stores):** no public Walmart-Realty-style portal — the old walmart.ca "Lease Space" page is dead; excess-land monetization runs through Walmart Canada's Mississauga real-estate team and external brokers (SRS's portfolio filter notably includes Ontario) [8]. Two live dynamics: the **C$6.5B five-year investment announced Jan 30, 2025** ("dozens of new stores," first five Supercentres in ON/AB by 2027, incl. Taza Park, Tsuut'ina Nation) will create new pad supply at greenfield sites [86][88]; and on **Nov 22, 2024 Walmart Canada unilaterally waived all competitive property controls** in its leases and land agreements amid Competition Bureau scrutiny — dismantling the restrictive-covenant regime that governs Canadian co-tenancy [87].

**Mexico & Central America:** Walmex runs an official portal — **bienesraices.walmartmexico.com.mx** — covering land offers (*terrenos*), rental of commercial locales, and rental of excess land explicitly labeled "Excess property," across 3,316 Mexico units + 949 Central America units [89]. It's a landing page (listing depth unverified) but it is the official front door.

**Everything else:** after divesting the UK (Asda), Japan (Seiyu), and Argentina in 2020–21, Walmart's ownership footprint for any pad thesis is US/Canada/Mexico/Central America/Chile (403 Lider units; no public pad program found) plus China/India/Africa positions [90][3].

## 9. Risks & Pitfalls

1. **No co-tenancy remedy — ever — on the anchor you're paying for.** Walmart is a shadow anchor: it owns its own store and is not named in your pad lease, so if it goes dark you have no rent relief or termination right (CMBS risk-factor boilerplate flags exactly this; where Walmart co-tenancy protection exists it's in third-party landlord-owned inline space, not pads bought or leased from Walmart) [39][40]. Underwrite the store's traffic as a bonus you can lose, not a draw you control.
2. **Dark-store tail risk: low frequency, high severity, little warning.** Current closures are a trickle (~10–12 US stores/yr, 2024–26; Walmart has publicly disputed viral mass-closure lists) [85]. But the precedents are stark: January 2016 — 269 stores closed at once (154 US, all 102 Express), stranding small-town co-located businesses [82][83]; relocations strand neighbors just as effectively (Seabrook NH) [84]; and Walmart killed its entire 51-clinic health format on 45–90 days' notice in April 2024 [49]. Closures are portfolio optimization, not distress — they arrive healthy.
3. **Deed-restriction traps run for decades.** Walmart conveys outparcels and exits stores with 25–75-year restrictive covenants (no grocery, pharmacy, discount, etc.). A 1997 Jonesboro AR deed barred discount/grocery use for **50 years** and surfaced only when a successor tried to lease to Dirt Cheap in 2019 — though note the Arkansas Court of Appeals ultimately **reversed** enforcement against the successor (*Bernard Court*, 2020 Ark. App. 563): Walmart drafts restrictions to bind successors and litigates them, but enforcement is contestable [28]. The Woonsocket RI exit restriction (25 years; store vacant six years; contributed to a ~45,000-person food desert) drew FTC attention under Lina Khan and produced Rhode Island's first-in-nation ban on grocery deed restrictions — signed July 1, 2026, prospective-only [26][27]. Walmart also litigates asymmetrically — enforcing its own covenants while fighting competitors' covenants to the federal appeals level (*Dan's Super Market v. Wal-Mart*, 8th Cir.) [29]. **Diligence rule: pull the full chain of title and every recorded ECR/REA before contract.**
4. **The REA ambush.** Developers routinely discover months into a project that the recorded REA controls use, signage, parking, hours, and cost-sharing on their parcel, with Walmart holding approval rights over changes (Holland & Knight, March 2026; ICSC; ABA) [81]. FrontView REIT's SEC-disclosed Buffalo Wild Wings surprise is the cautionary case study [38].
5. **Financing friction on unsubordinated ground leases.** Walmart (or a center owner) will not subordinate its fee; lenders often require subordination, so expect leasehold-mortgage structures, more equity, and improvements reverting on default [37][38].
6. **Pricing risk.** Pad ground leases at big-box-anchored sites price at premium (low) cap rates (QSR pad stack ~4.4–6.8% vs. 6.82% market average) driven by tenant credit — but on a Walmart pad you're paying anchor-adjacent land pricing for traffic you cannot contractually keep (see #1). If the store goes dark, the premium evaporates with no remedy [30][31][32].
7. **Concept-fit gate.** Walmart's prohibited-use lists (grocery, pharmacy, fuel, discount) and its own expanding footprint (Auto Care, EV charging, financial services) pre-empt whole categories — check the ECR *and* Walmart's own roadmap before pursuing a site [24][48][85].

## Implications & Recommendations

- **If the goal is securing a pad (own use or client):** go straight to the named humans — CBRE's Flynn/Sakanich/Zvi and SRS's Walmart portfolio team — with a concept Walmart's ECRs permit (drive-thru QSR/coffee, bank, medical/dental, cellular, car wash). Expect LOI on Walmart's template, "contact for pricing," a 30-day sole-discretion design gauntlet, and a 20-year NNN ground lease with 5%-per-option bumps. Budget legal for ECR/REA review as a first-dollar cost, not a closing formality.
- **Three 10-minute manual actions would make this decision-grade:** (1) browse walmartrealty.com and record the live listing count/geography; (2) hit "Download all" on the SRS portfolio page; (3) download and read `wmt-loi.DOC` (deposit/DD/closing terms). All three are blocked to automation but trivial in a normal browser.
- **If the angle is investment:** the interesting asymmetry is small-market Midwest outlots (thin demand, negotiable pricing, sale-or-lease flexibility) vs. growth-market pads at new stores (Lady Lake-style, sell through in months). Shadow-center break-up plays (buy center, parcelize pads) are an active 2025 institutional strategy worth studying via MRP/Core Equity precedents.
- **If the angle is consulting IP:** this channel map (Walmart Realty → CBRE/SRS → LOI → ECR gauntlet) generalizes to every big-box owner-operator (Target, Home Depot, Lowe's, Costco run analogous excess-property programs). A "big-box pad-site access playbook" is a productizable deliverable, and the Walmart GC-insourcing whitepaper already in the Research program is a natural companion piece.
- **Watch items:** Walmart's landlord pivot (more center purchases would shift supply from fee sales to Walmart-controlled leasing); EV charging consuming lot corners; the FY27 10-K for whether the ground-lease reclassification stays stable; Rhode Island-style deed-restriction legislation spreading.

- **No authoritative current inventory census** — walmartrealty.com, the SRS export, and the CBRE map all require a human browser session; the ~30-listing floor vs. 557 (all types, March 2023) bounds are the best public evidence.

- **Current ECR form** — the 2012 Murphy template is the newest public example; current restriction durations/terms on pad ECRs are undocumented.

- **No quantified post-closure damage study** for pad tenants at dark Walmart sites (mechanism documented; magnitude not).
